## Scope

This atlas unifies the four completed retrospective mathematics volumes: 114 treatments for 2023, 237 for 2024, 351 for 2025 and 349 for 2026, for a total of 1,051 main treatments. Fourteen additional 2023 synthesis treatments are retained separately, giving 1,065 retained treatment bodies including supplements. These are treatments, not a count of unique conversations or distinct mathematical ideas. The source boundary is the four retrieved annual bundles, not a fresh complete export of every account conversation. The 2026 boundary is 21 October 2026; no later month is inferred. Historical dates identify the dated topics; revised proofs and examples are retrospective exposition and must not be read as discoveries made on those historical dates.

## Method

The chronological volumes remain intact. Above them, this report groups related material into domain chapters and canonical concept families. A family gives precise objects, a scoped theorem, a proof, a worked example, limits and concrete next questions. Its source identifiers allow the reader to return to every original treatment. Domain assignment is an editorial navigation aid, with secondary links for real cross-domain content; it is not an assertion that mathematical subjects have disjoint boundaries. Repeated annual treatments may support one family, and one treatment may support several families.

## Argument

The most productive organizing principle is a sequence of obligations: specify the object and admissible maps, identify the target, state hypotheses, derive a result, compute a checkable example, and expose what remains unresolved. Quotients ask which distinctions matter; cohomology asks whether local data glue; spectra quantify suppression and amplification; counting turns finite configuration spaces into exact laws and certificates; deformation tests whether a leading-order structure survives higher compatibility conditions; arithmetic reduction turns some infinite families into finite representative checks. These are connected through explicit constructions, not through a universal theory claimed to contain them all.

## Interactivity

The interactive companion has two different functions. Its catalogue provides search, domain/year/capability filters and the complete retained text of all 1,051 source treatments. Its ten labs implement small, explicitly specified mathematical models. A capability tag on a chapter means the mathematics supports that kind of operation; it does not mean a general solver for that domain has been implemented. Each lab identifies its inputs, arithmetic, output and limits. Exact rational or integer results are distinguished from floating evaluations, analytic upper bounds and conceptual research directions.

## Reading

Read the domain overview to orient the objects and questions, then a concept family for its theorem and proof. Follow a source identifier to inspect the original revised question and treatment. Use a lab to vary parameters inside a stated model. Use a cross-domain bridge only with its displayed hypotheses. The report is deliberately not another concatenation of the annual volumes: the full source bodies are retained in the interactive catalogue and portable bundle, while this document develops the shared architecture and the strongest concept families in depth.

## Verification

The four original annual verification suites were replayed successfully from fresh copies. They contain 61 verifier programs and 1,173 scoped records; records are heterogeneous and include a proof-only record, so these counts are not counts of formally proved theorems. New domain scripts check selected worked examples, and the lab engine is tested against independent exact fixtures and boundary cases. A passing finite check supports its named model and does not automatically certify an entire exposition or an unrecovered historical instance.
