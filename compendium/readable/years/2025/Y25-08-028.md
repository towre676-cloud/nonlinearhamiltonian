## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which of the recovered edge counts can describe a simple graph on the stated number of vertices?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The August15 project reported strong triangular-number fits and a high R². Exact combinatorics already finds inconsistent labels. A fit statistic cannot repair a value outside the feasible simple-graph range or an off-by-one triangular convention.

## Definitions

For a simple undirected graph on n≥2 vertices,0≤e≤C(n,2). Define completeness C=e/C(n,2) and residual r=e−C(n,2). Use the standard triangular number T_k=k(k+1)/2.

## Result name

Edge feasibility and corrected examples

## Result statement

A complete graph K_n has exactly n(n−1)/2 edges. Thus K₂₃ has253 edges, not231; K₁₀ has45 edges, so78 cannot be its simple-graph edge count. The counts528,946,4371 are correct for K₃₃,K₄₄,K₉₄.

## Proof

Every edge is an unordered pair of distinct vertices, and there are C(n,2) such pairs. A simple graph chooses a subset, proving both the maximum and equality criterion. For the stated examples direct multiplication gives23·22/2=253 and10·9/2=45. With e=231 at n=23, the residual is−22 and completeness is21/23; it is a possible incomplete graph, not K₂₃. With e=78 at n=10, completeness is26/15>1, impossible in the stated model. The value78 is the complete edge count for n=13. Calling both values triangular without fixing the indexing convention can hide this mismatch.
