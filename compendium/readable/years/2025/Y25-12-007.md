## Result name

Exact OU variance and discrete stability

## Result statement

The continuous stationary variance is εσ²/(2λ). The discrete chain has a finite stationary variance exactly when0<λ h<2, and then it is εσ²/(2λ−λ²h).

## Proof

Multiply the SDE bye^(λ t)and integrate to obtain X_t=e^(−λ t)X₀+√εσ∫₀ᵗe^(−λ(t−s))d W_s. The Itôisometry givesvariance εσ²(1−e^(−2λ t))/(2λ), whose limit is the stated value. For the discrete chain, seta=1−λ h. Independence givesv_(n+1)=a²v_n+ε h σ². A finite stable solution exists when|a|<1and equals ε h σ²/(1−a²)=εσ²/(2λ−λ²h). If|a|≥1, the accumulated noise variance Σ a^(2j)does not converge. The ratio to the continuous variance is1/[1−λ h/2], showing a systematic positive discretization bias.

## Worked example: calculation

The continuous variance is ε/2. The discrete variance is2ε/3, one third larger. As h→0the discrete formula tends to ε/2. A nonlinear rare-event calculation performed withh=1/2would therefore already inherit a known stationary error in this simplest test.

## Worked example: conclusion

The SDE and its discretization have exact conventions, a proved stability region, and a quantitative bias.

## Limits

The calculation does not prove a general rare-event asymptotic or validate a fitted rank-three throat, octonionic ratio, or neutrino claim. State-dependent diffusion needs the chosen Itô/Stratonovich convention and regularity assumptions.

## Extension
