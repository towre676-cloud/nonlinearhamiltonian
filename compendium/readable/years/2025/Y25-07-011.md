## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which consequence follows from the stated surface-metric evolution, and which monotonicity claims need additional equations?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The July10 TAG response proposed a smoothed-curvature flow and an entropy-like expression. A precise area identity follows directly from the metric equation. It does not justify independently prescribing the curvature’s heat equation or declaring the proposed functional monotone.

## Definitions

Let M be a closed smooth surface with time-dependent metric g. Let f=K_ε−mean_g(K_ε), where the mean is the area-weighted integral divided by total area. Assume g′=−2fg. The smoothing operator and its kernel still require their own definitions.

## Result name

Area preservation under mean-zero conformal velocity

## Result statement

The flow satisfies dA_g/dt=−2f dA_g and d Area(M,g)/dt=0. If K_ε is spatially constant, the metric is stationary.

## Proof

For a metric variation h, differentiating the volume density gives(d/dt)dA=(1/2)tr_g(h)dA. In dimension two, tr_g(−2fg)=−4f, hence the density derivative is−2f. Integrating yields−2∫f dA=0 by the definition of the mean. This calculation needs no special smoothing theorem. By contrast, Gaussian curvature is determined by g, and under this conformal velocity its evolution is K′=Δ_g f+2Kf. An independently stipulated equation K_ε′=Δ_gK_ε−μK_ε is therefore not automatically compatible with the metric flow, especially when the smoothing operator itself depends on time. Differentiating a proposed entropy must include all these metric and operator variations.

## Worked example: setup

Compare a round sphere with a spatially varying formal curvature input.

## Worked example: calculation

On a round sphere K_ε is constant for any normalized symmetry-preserving smoothing, so f=0 and g′=0. For a varying input the local area densities may grow or shrink, but their total derivative remains zero. Area preservation alone does not imply pointwise curvature decay.

## Worked example: conclusion

The actual proved geometric invariant is total area. Other desired dissipation claims remain named problems with missing hypotheses.

## Limits

No existence theorem, long-time regularity or empirical growth model is established. A kernel must be normalized relative to the changing measure before its mean-preserving properties can be used.

## Extension

Specify the smoothing operator on evolving metrics and derive the complete first variation of the proposed functional; then prove a sign or exhibit a counterexample.
