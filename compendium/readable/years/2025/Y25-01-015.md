## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which complex captures infinitesimal deformations and obstructions at a singular point?

## Status

Developed mathematical result with explicit hypotheses and checked example; historical claims repaired where necessary

## Summary

For a hypersurface the cotangent complex is an explicit two-term derivative map. At a double point it detects both a tangent direction and an obstruction, information not supplied byRHom(O_X,O_X).

## Definitions

Over a characteristic-zero fieldk letA=k[x]/(x²), with pointx=0.

For a hypersurface the cotangent complex is[I/I²→Ω_{k[x]/k}⊗A] in degrees−1,0. Here it is[A→A] with differential multiplication by2x.

The tangent complex at the point isRHom_A(L_{A/k},k), with cohomology in degrees zero and one.

## Result name

Double-point deformation and obstruction

## Result statement

Atx=0 the tangent-complex differential is zero, soH⁰≅k andH¹≅k. Every first-order displacementx=at satisfiesx²=0 modulot², but it extends tox=at+bt² modulot³ only ifa²=0.

## Proof

The relation generatorx² differentiates to2x dx, giving the two-term cotangent map. Tensoring with the residue field killsx, and dualizing givesk→k with zero differential in degrees0,1. For an explicit deformation, substituteat into the defining relation overk[t]/t²: its square vanishes automatically. Overk[t]/t³, the square ofat+bt² isa²t², so extension requiresa²=0. For a field this forcesa=0, exhibiting the nonlinear obstruction to a nonzero first-order tangent direction. The complex predicts a possible obstruction space; the equation computes the actual obstruction mapa↦a². By contrastRHom_A(A,A)=A in degree zero becauseA is a free module over itself, so it misses this deformation structure.

## Worked example: setup

Compare with the smooth hypersurfacek[x]/(x), whose derivative map is multiplication by one.

## Worked example: calculation

The smooth one-point scheme has zero tangent complex: the mapk→k is an isomorphism. The double point has the same underlying topological point but nontrivial tangent and obstruction spaces. Thus topology of the underlying set cannot recover its derived infinitesimal geometry.

## Worked example: conclusion

The correction supplies a concrete deformation problem and an actual failed lift, not only a replacement symbol.

## Limits

The displayed hypersurface model uses a regular-sequence presentation. General derived schemes require the full cotangent complex and derived base change. A nonzero obstruction space does not mean every deformation is obstructed.

## Extension

Forf₁,…,f_r forming a regular sequence, compute the Jacobian mapkⁿ→kʳ at a point. Its kernel and cokernel give tangent and first obstruction spaces; then compute higher-order lift equations to distinguish a potential obstruction from a nonzero one.
