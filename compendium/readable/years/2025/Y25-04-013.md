## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which real dilogarithm value can be proved exactly instead of numerically fitting an unexplained constant?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered passage mentions dilogarithmic approximations. A constructive replacement is an exact evaluation at the inverse golden ratio, linked to the same golden scaling used elsewhere that month. The proof uses functional identities derivable by differentiation.

## Definitions

For0≤x≤1 defineLi2(x)=Σ_(n≥1)x^n/n², withLi2′(x)=−log(1−x)/x on(0,1).

Letφ=(1+√5)/2 andr=1/φ, so r²=1−r and1+r=1/r.

## Result name

Exact golden dilogarithms

## Result statement

Li2(r)=π²/10−log²φ andLi2(r²)=π²/15−log²φ.

## Proof

The reflection identityLi2(x)+Li2(1−x)=π²/6−logxlog(1−x) follows by differentiating both sides and evaluating atx→0. AlsoLi2(x)+Li2(−x)=(1/2)Li2(x²) follows directly from the series, andLi2(x/(1+x))=−Li2(−x)−(1/2)log²(1+x) follows by differentiation and the value at0. PutA=Li2(r),B=Li2(r²),l=logr. Reflection givesA+B=π²/6−2l². The other two identities, usingr/(1+r)=r², giveA=(3/2)B+(1/2)l². Solving the two linear equations yieldsB=π²/15−l² andA=π²/10−l², equal to the stated forms because l²=log²φ.

## Worked example: setup

Evaluate both sides by a positive convergent series.

## Worked example: calculation

AfterN terms at0<x<1, the tail is at mostx^(N+1)/[(N+1)²(1−x)]. AtN=80 this bounds the error forx=r by less than10^(−19), enough to check many digits without an arbitrary fit.

## Worked example: conclusion

The special value follows from exact identities and a reproducible error bound, giving a genuine alternative to an unexplained numerical coincidence.

## Limits

These identities do not prove the recoveredΩ regulator formula or any physical constant relation. Real arguments avoid branch choices; complex dilogarithms need an explicit analytic continuation convention.

## Extension
