## Status

## Summary

A fitted score and a theorem about unseen samples are different mathematical objects. The source’s spectral PAC–Bayes claim does not provide its prior, posterior, sample law or loss bound. A standard finite-hypothesis theorem can be derived transparently and shows exactly where those assumptions enter.

## Definitions

Let H be finite, P a strictly positive prior fixed independently of an iid sample of size m, and ℓ(h,z)∈[0,1]. Define population loss L(h), empirical loss Lhat(h), and KL(Q||P)=ΣQ(h)log[Q(h)/P(h)].

Fix λ>0 andδ∈(0,1) before seeing the sample. Write L(Q),Lhat(Q) for posterior averages.

## Result name

A fixed-parameter PAC–Bayes–Hoeffding bound

## Result statement

With probability at least1−δ, simultaneously for every posterior Q, L(Q)≤Lhat(Q)+[KL(Q||P)+log(1/δ)]/λ+λ/(8m). This is a theorem under the declared iid bounded-loss model, not a consequence of R² or a spectral fit.

## Proof

Hoeffding’s bounded-variable lemma gives E_sample exp(λ[L(h)−Lhat(h)])≤exp(λ²/(8m)) for each h. Average over the fixed prior and apply Markov: with probability at least1−δ, log E_P exp(λ[L−Lhat])≤λ²/(8m)+log(1/δ). For any finite probability Q, Jensen’s inequality applied after writing the prior sum using Q gives λE_Q(L−Lhat)≤KL(Q||P)+log E_P exp(λ[L−Lhat]). Combining proves the bound uniformly in Q on that one event. Zero posterior masses are handled by continuity. Optimizing λ after observing Q requires a separate uniformization or parameter grid; the fixed-λ theorem does not silently supply it.

## Worked example: setup

Choose two hypotheses with uniform prior, a point-mass posterior, m=100,δ=.05,λ=20 and empirical loss.1.

Then KL=log2 and the penalty is log40/20+20/800≈.2094439727, giving L(Q)≤.3094439727 on the stated event. ## Worked example: conclusion

The proof makes its probability event, optimization domain and constants reviewable, while preserving the empirical report as a claim rather than a verified forecast.

## Limits

Prime-indexed observations are not automatically iid. Unbounded squared loss is outside the bounded-loss theorem unless explicitly controlled. A data-dependent prior needs another theorem. No cryptographic or cross-domain benefit follows from this certificate.

## Extension

Specify a dependency model, bounded or tail-controlled loss and a prior independent of the held-out data. A spectral hypothesis can enter only through a proved complexity or KL estimate, after which the actual numerical bound should be reported alongside the score.
