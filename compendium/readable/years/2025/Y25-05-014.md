## Revised question retained in the annual source

Reconstruction: Can contextual numerical closeness be equality in a field, and what happens if transitivity is imposed after the fact?

## Status

Developed mathematical repair or classical result; speculative source claims delimited

## Summary

The user’s infra-rational proposal explicitly allows non-transitive equality. That can be a useful tolerance relation, but it is incompatible with algebraic equality. The obstruction is elementary and remains even before multiplication, contexts or uncertainty propagation are introduced.

## Definitions

Fix δ>0 rational and define x∼y on Q by |x−y|<δ. A field’s equality relation must be reflexive, symmetric and transitive and must respect both operations.

The transitive closure of a relation declares two points equivalent whenever a finite chain of related points connects them.

## Result name

Nontransitivity and collapse of the naive quotient

## Result statement

The tolerance relation is not transitive. Its transitive closure on Q is the universal relation, so quotienting by that closure identifies every rational number, including zero and one.

## Proof

Take x=0,y=3δ/4,z=3δ/2. Both neighboring differences are3δ/4<δ, but |x−z|=3δ/2>δ. Thus the relation cannot be substituted for equality in a field. For any two rational a,b, choose an integer N>|b−a|/δ. The rational chain a+j(b−a)/N, j=0,…,N, has every step smaller thanδ. Transitive closure therefore equates a and b. A quotient with0=1 is not a field under the usual nontrivial-field axiom. Merely closing the relation under transitivity does not repair the intended numerical information; it erases it.

## Worked example: setup

Set δ=1 and use the explicit triple0,3/4,3/2.

## Worked example: calculation

The two true closeness tests and one false endpoint test are exact rational comparisons. A chain from zero to ten with twenty equal steps also shows why iterated tolerances can travel arbitrarily far. The verifier checks these examples and constructs chains between several rational endpoints.

## Worked example: conclusion

Tolerance should be a separately named relation whose inferential rules are controlled; it cannot inherit equality substitution automatically.

## Limits

This is a counterexample to a fixed positive tolerance. A context-dependent threshold may produce other relations, but a proposed quotient still needs an actual equivalence relation and congruence proof. At zero threshold the source’s strict inequality is not even reflexive.

## Extension

Use exact equality for values and store error bounds as additional data, or choose a declared rounding map q and define x≈y iff q(x)=q(y). The latter is an equivalence, but its arithmetic compatibility must still be tested rather than assumed.
