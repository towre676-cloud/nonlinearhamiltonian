## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which assumptions justify a proximal step or a residual certificate, rather than a generic solver-success label?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The August10 compiler listed several algorithms with validators. The mathematical contract must depend on the chosen solver. A proximal descent proof and an SPD residual bound show two distinct valid certificates, neither of which follows merely from matching array shapes.

## Definitions

Let f have L-Lipschitz gradient and let g be convex. A proximal step minimizes g(z)+(1/(2t))||z−x+t∇f(x)||². Separately, for Ax=b assume A symmetric positive definite and λ_min(A)≥a>0.

## Result name

Descent and a residual error bound

## Result statement

For0<t≤1/L, the proximal step satisfies F(x⁺)≤F(x)−(1/t−L/2)||x⁺−x||², F=f+g. For an approximate SPD solve with residual r=b−Ax, ||x−x*||_A≤||r||₂/√a.

## Proof

Optimality of the proximal minimizer gives s=−∇f(x)−(x⁺−x)/t∈∂g(x⁺). Convexity of g bounds its change by−∇f(x)·d−||d||²/t, where d=x⁺−x. The gradient descent lemma bounds f’s change by∇f(x)·d+(L/2)||d||². Adding proves descent. For the linear solve, e=x*−x=A⁻¹r, so||e||_A²=rᵀA⁻¹r≤||r||²/a by the spectral theorem. Nonsymmetric or indefinite matrices invalidate this SPD energy argument, even when an iterative routine returns a small-looking number.
