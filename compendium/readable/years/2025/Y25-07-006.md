## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which local data define a global Brauer class, and what is checked by the invariant sum?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The proposed Lean structures named cyclic algebras and local invariants but did not perform their arithmetic. Hamilton’s quaternion algebra provides genuine local data and an exact reciprocity check, separating an interface schema from a local splitting proof.

## Definitions

The quaternion algebra(a,b)_K has generators i²=a,j²=b,ij=−ji. A local split quaternion algebra has Brauer invariant0 and a division quaternion algebra has invariant1/2. The Albert–Brauer–Hasse–Noether theorem identifies global classes with finitely supported local invariant families summing to zero in Q/Z.

## Result name

Two ramified places for Hamilton’s algebra

## Result statement

For H=(−1,−1)_Q, the invariants are1/2 at2 and∞ and zero at every odd prime. Thus their sum is zero modulo one, while H itself is nontrivial.

## Proof

Over R its norm is a sum of four squares, anisotropic on nonzero vectors, so H is division. Over Q₂ a putative isotropic vector can be scaled to a primitive integral quadruple. Squares are0,1,4 modulo eight. If the number of odd coordinates is1,2,3 or4, their sum with the even squares cannot be0 modulo eight; hence the norm remains anisotropic. At an odd prime the local Hilbert-symbol formula for unit parameters gives(−1,−1)_p=1, hence splitting. The two nonsplit invariants sum to1=0 in Q/Z. The cited global injection detects the nontrivial class from either localization. This example checks the reciprocity constraint, rather than proving the full theorem from one case.

## Limits

This does not compute a Brauer–Manin set. That requires evaluating specified classes at adelic points, controlling all relevant places and proving those evaluations.

## Extension

For a concrete cyclic algebra, return valuation and residue certificates at every possible ramified place, then verify reciprocity before forming an obstruction pairing.
