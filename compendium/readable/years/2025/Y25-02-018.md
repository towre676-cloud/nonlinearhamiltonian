## Summary

A local factorization completely determines the singular part of a logarithmic derivative. It supplies a concrete analytic boundary to parameter formulas whose underlyingL-function, normalization or nonvanishing was not supplied.

## Definitions

LetL be holomorphic near s₀, not identically zero, with a zero of orderm≥0 there. ThenL(s)=(s−s₀)^m g(s),g(s₀)≠0.

A valueL′(s₀)/L(s₀) is an ordinary finite quotient only whenm=0.

A numerical expressionL(E,1),L(f,3/2) orL′(g,1)/L(g,1) must identify the curve/form, analytic normalization, conductor and required continuation.

## Result name

Exact local logarithmic-derivative decomposition

## Result statement

L′(s)/L(s)=m/(s−s₀)+g′(s)/g(s). Thus a positive-order zero produces a simple pole with residuem, and multiplyingL by a nonzero constant does not change its logarithmic derivative.

## Proof

Differentiate the product factorization and divide away froms₀. The quotient splits into the displayed terms. Sinceg is nonzero nearby,g′/g is holomorphic there. MultiplyingL byc≠0 cancels inL′/L, but multiplying it by an exponential e^{as} adds a. Therefore completed versus uncompletedL-functions can have different logarithmic derivatives even when they encode related arithmetic. Their gamma and conductor factors cannot be omitted from a numerical identification. For a contour enclosing onlys₀, integration gives(2πi)⁻¹∮L′/L=m, directly counting the zero order.

## Worked example: calculation

Its logarithmic derivative is2/(s−1)+3 and has no finite value ats=1. MultiplyingL by seven leaves that derivative unchanged; replacing it bye^{−s}L changes the constant term from three to two. These exact examples separate scaling invariance from normalization sensitivity.

## Limits

No source-defined curve or modular form was recovered for the historicalα,β,κ constants, so their asserted decimal values are not newly certified. The analytic lemma does not prove their conjectural physical interpretation.

## Extension

Supply the exact arithmetic object and completed-function convention, certify nonvanishing at the evaluation point and bound truncation/continuation error. If the point is a zero, use a stated regularized coefficient after subtracting the pole rather than an undefined quotient. The residue is stable under multiplication by any holomorphic nonvanishing factor because that factor contributes only a holomorphic logarithmic derivative. This makes zero order an invariant local quantity while the regular finite term remains normalization dependent. A proposed constant should specify which of those two pieces it intends to use.
