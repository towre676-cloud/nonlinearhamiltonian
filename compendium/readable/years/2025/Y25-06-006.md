## Summary

The interval and numerical setup of the reported eigenvalues were not recovered. A bounded-interval point-interaction model nevertheless supports precise domain, min–max and perturbation statements. Prime locations are just one admissible finite set of interior points; their arithmetic labels do not prove a zeta correspondence.

## Definitions

On(0,L), take finitely many distinct interior x_j andα_j≥0. On H₀¹ define q[u]=∫|u′|²dx+Σα_j|u(x_j)|².

The associated operator acts as−u″ between interaction points. Its domain has u continuous, piecewise H², Dirichlet endpoints and jumps u′(x_j+)−u′(x_j−)=α_ju(x_j).

## Result name

A closed positive form, spectral bracket and conditional perturbation derivative

## Result statement

The form defines a positive self-adjoint operator with discrete spectrum. With r interaction points, (nπ/L)²≤λ_n≤((n+r)π/L)², henceλ_n=(nπ/L)²+O(n). For a differentiable simple eigenbranch of q_ε=q₀+εΣα_j|u(x_j)|², λ_n′(0)=(2/L)Σα_j sin²(nπx_j/L).

## Proof

Point evaluation is continuous on H₀¹, for example|u(x)|≤√L||u′||₂. Thus the form norm is equivalent to the H₀¹ norm and is closed; the closed-form representation theorem and compact embedding into L² are the stated classical functional-analytic inputs. Integration by parts on each subinterval gives the jump conditions. The lower bound is min–max monotonicity. For the upper bound, intersect the span of the first n+r free sine modes with the r evaluation kernels; its dimension is at least n, and on it the interaction term vanishes, giving the bound. For the derivative formula, differentiate the normalized eigenvalue identity; the terms from differentiating the eigenvector cancel by the eigen-equation and normalization, leaving the form derivative evaluated on√(2/L)sin(nπx/L).

## Limits

The finite positive model does not imply an explicit formula for prime zeros. The first-order shift is a derivative at zero coupling, not an exact finite-strength correction. An unspecified interval cannot support a unique numerical spectrum.
