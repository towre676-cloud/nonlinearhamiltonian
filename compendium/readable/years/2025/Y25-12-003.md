## Summary

The recovered formalization correctly centered a weightedℓ¹space but alternated between row and column bounds. For theℓ¹norm, the relevant quantity is a weighted column sum. This gives a complete theorem and a sharp geometric-tail example.

## Definitions

For R>0, set||f||_(1, R)=Σ_j R^j|f_j|.

Let T_ijbe a finite or countable matrix with C=sup_j Σ_i R^(i−j)|T_ij|<∞.

## Result name

Weighted column theorem

## Result statement

Tf is absolutely defined forf∈ℓ¹_Rand||Tf||_(1, R)≤C||f||_(1, R). For causal T_ij=Aq^(i−j) when i≥j and zero otherwise, assume A≥0 and 0≤q<1/R; then C=A/(1−q R).

## Proof

Apply the triangle inequality and Tonelli to nonnegative terms:Σ_i R^i|Σ_j T_ijf_j|≤Σ_j|f_j|Σ_i R^i|T_ij|≤C Σ_j R^j|f_j|. Finite right side proves absolute convergence as well as the bound. For the causal kernel, putm=i−j≥0; every weighted column sum is A Σ_(m≥0)(q R)^m=A/(1−q R). Positivity shows this norm is attained on a coordinate vector in the infinite model, so the bound is sharp. Composing Loperators with the same bound gives norm at most C^Lby repeated application; this is worst-case amplification, not a prediction for typical vectors. A finite bandwidth Mchanges the sum to A[1−(q R)^(M+1)]/(1−q R).

## Worked example: setup
