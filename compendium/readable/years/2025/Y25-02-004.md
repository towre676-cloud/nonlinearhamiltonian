## Revised question retained in the annual source

When does a debt ratio fall, and what is missing from comparing growth with the interest rate alone?

## Status

Developed mathematical result with explicit hypotheses and checked example; historical claims repaired where necessary

## Summary

An exact recurrence separates inherited debt, interest, output growth and the primary flow. Its stable fixed point can be calculated, giving a constructive alternative to an unconditional growth claim.

## Definitions

LetD_{t+1}=(1+r)D_t+P_t andY_{t+1}=(1+g)Y_t withg>−1. Writeb_t=D_t/Y_t andp_t=P_t/Y_t.

PositiveP is a primary deficit under this sign convention; negativeP is a primary surplus.

For a fixed-flow model letp_t=p andr,g be constant.

## Result name

Exact ratio recurrence and fixed point

## Result statement

b_{t+1}=[(1+r)b_t+p]/(1+g). It decreases exactly whenp<(g−r)b_t. Ifg>r and1+r≥0, the fixed pointb*=p/(g−r) is attracting.

## Proof

Divide the debt equation by the new output level to obtain the recurrence. Subtractb_t and combine denominators to get[b_{t+1}−b_t]=[p−(g−r)b_t]/(1+g), proving the strict condition. Solveb*=((1+r)b*+p)/(1+g) to obtain the fixed point. Subtracting it givesb_{t+1}−b*=a(b_t−b*) witha=(1+r)/(1+g). Under the stated inequalities0≤a<1, so the difference decays geometrically. Thus a positive primary deficit can make the ratio increase despiteg>r, and the initial ratio’s position relative tob* determines the direction.

## Worked example: setup

Taker=1/50,g=1/25,b₀=1 and comparep=1/100 withp=1/20.

## Worked example: calculation

The first case givesb₁=103/104<1 and fixed point1/2. The second givesb₁=107/104>1 and fixed point5/2. The interest and growth rates are identical; only the primary flow changes. Repeating the first case givesb_t=1/2+(1/2)(51/52)^t exactly.

## Worked example: conclusion

The recurrence gives conditions and trajectories rather than an unsupported prediction.

## Limits

These are accounting dynamics under fixed rates, not financial advice or a forecast. Inflation, valuation changes, maturity structure and changing primary flows require additional terms.

## Extension

For bounded time-varyingr_t,g_t,p_t, derive product-sum bounds for the nonautonomous recurrence. Identify conditions giving a uniform contraction and an invariant interval for all admissible rate paths. With variable primary flows but fixed rates, b_t=a^t b₀+(1+g)⁻¹Σ_{j=0}^{t−1}a^{t−1−j}p_j. If 0≤a<1 and 0≤p_j≤p_max, the limiting upper bound is p_max/(g−r). This follows by summing a geometric series and makes the required bound on future flows explicit.
