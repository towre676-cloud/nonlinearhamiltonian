## Revised question retained in the annual source

What assumptions justify the recovered event-response formula and how can its rate be checked?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The historical response probability is exactly the first-arrival law of a homogeneous Poisson process. It is a mathematical model with strong assumptions: independent stationary increments and a constant rate. A formula alone does not validate the claimed speed of an implementation.

## Definitions

LetN(t) be a Poisson process of rateλ>0 andT=inf{t:N(t)≥1}.

Its survival probability isS(t)=P(T>t); the hazard rate ish(t)=−d(logS(t))/dt.

## Result name

Arrival probability and rate identification

## Result statement

P(T≤t)=1−e^(−λt), E[T]=1/λ, andh(t)=λ. Given an exact nontrivial probabilityp at timet>0, the unique compatible rate isλ=−log(1−p)/t.

## Proof

The eventT>t isN(t)=0. The Poisson mass formula givesP(N(t)=0)=e^(−λt), establishing the distribution. The tail integralE[T]=∫₀∞P(T>t)dt equals1/λ. DifferentiatinglogS yields the constant hazard, and solvingS(t)=1−p gives the identification formula. The memoryless identityP(T>s+t|T>s)=S(s+t)/S(s)=S(t) follows immediately. Conversely a claimed model whose conditional waiting probability depends on past waiting time is not this homogeneous arrival law.

## Worked example: setup

Useλ=2 in inverse-time units.

## Worked example: calculation

The median solvese^(−2t)=1/2, sot_med=(log2)/2, whereas the mean is1/2. At that median, the probability of at least two events is1−e^(−λt)(1+λt)=(1−log2)/2. First-arrival probability and event count are therefore different observables.

## Worked example: conclusion

The model yields exact quantities that can be compared with data only after the event and time unit are defined.

## Limits

Bursting, refractory periods, nonstationary rates and dependent arrivals violate the stated assumptions. None of the historical millisecond or energy numbers is certified by this distributional calculation.

## Extension

Test the constant-hazard prediction on declared time bins; for a nonhomogeneous rateλ(t), replaceλt by the integrated intensity∫₀ᵗλ(s)ds and derive the revised likelihood.
