## Revised question retained in the annual source

When does the recovered spectral-density model define a finite-variance process?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The historical phase-noise model is a polynomial in negative powers of frequency. Its integrability is a mathematical issue before any resonance or hardware interpretation. A finite bandwidth gives an exact variance formula and exposes the divergent idealization.

## Definitions

Use a one-sided spectral-density conventionVarφ=∫₀∞Sφ(f)df. On a declared band[f_L,f_H], assumeSφ≥0.

The coefficients have the units needed forSφ; negative values require a separate positivity check rather than termwise interpretation.

## Result name

Finite-band variance and low-frequency divergence

## Result statement

For0<f_L<f_H, the variance isΣ_(i≠−1)h_i(f_H^(i+1)−f_L^(i+1))/(i+1)+h_(−1)log(f_H/f_L). A nonzero nonnegativef^(−p) term withp≥1 diverges asf_L→0.

## Proof

The formula is direct integration of each power on a compact positive band. Forp=1 the integral is logarithmic; forp>1 it grows likef_L^(1−p)/(p−1). Therefore an ideal spectrum with such a positive leading low-frequency term cannot be the spectral density of a stationary finite-variance phase process over all frequencies. A flat positive term also diverges at infinite frequency unless a high-frequency cutoff or decay is supplied. These are consequences of finite covariance at zero, not a claim that finite-band models are unusable. Frequency noise or phase increments may admit different generalized-process interpretations.

## Worked example: setup

TakeS(f)=f^(−2) on[1,10] and zero elsewhere.

## Worked example: calculation

The variance is1−1/10=9/10. Extending the lower endpoint to1/100 gives100−1/10=999/10, a large change even though the high-frequency endpoint is fixed. TakingS(f)=1/f on[1,10] giveslog10 instead.

## Worked example: conclusion

A finite declared band makes the model quantitative. Omitting cutoffs can conceal an infinite variance.

## Limits

The calculation does not validate measured coefficients or predicted oscillator performance. PSD positivity and a chosen one-sided/two-sided convention must precede numerical comparison.

## Extension

Fit coefficients on a declared band with positivity constraints, propagate their intervals through the exact integral, and test whether a stationary phase or an increment model matches the intended observable.
