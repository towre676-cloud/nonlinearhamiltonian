## Summary

The proposed smoothed radical is an explicit positive function of a real parameter. Its calculus can be proved exactly. The source’s separate scalar-curvature and eigenvalue formulas are not consequences of that definition without a metric, domain and operator.

## Definitions

For fixed n>1 andσ≥0 define r_σ(n)=∏_{p|n}p^(1−p^(−σ)) and F(σ)=log r_σ=Σ_{p|n}(1−p^(−σ))log p.

For a valid ABC triple define E_σ=log c−σlog rad(abc). This uses the ordinary radical and is a separate affine function ofσ.

## Result name

Monotonicity, concavity and the exact zero of the energy surrogate

## Result statement

F′(σ)=Σp^(−σ)(log p)²>0 and F″(σ)=−Σp^(−σ)(log p)³<0. Also r₀=1 and r_σ→rad(n) asσ→∞. The source’s E_σ vanishes exactly atσ=q and has constant negative derivative−log rad(abc).

## Proof

Because the prime support is finite, differentiate each exponential term directly. All logarithms of primes are positive, giving the strict signs. Atσ=0 every exponent1−p^(−σ) vanishes; asσ increases each tends to one, proving the limits. Solving the affine equation E_σ=0 givesσ=log c/log rad(abc). Thus a crossing at the quality is built into the definition rather than independent evidence of a phase transition. A Riemannian scalar curvature depends on a declared metric and its first and second derivatives in coordinate directions; an eigenvalue depends on an operator domain and boundary conditions. Neither is determined merely by a scalar function F.

## Limits
