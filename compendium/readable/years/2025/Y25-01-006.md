## Revised question retained in the annual source

When does a non-Hermitian matrix become self-adjoint in a positive inner product, and which obstruction rules this out?

## Status

Developed mathematical result with explicit hypotheses and checked example; historical claims repaired where necessary

## Summary

An explicit similarity gives a positive metric and real spectrum for a triangular matrix. A nontrivial Jordan block cannot have such a metric, so pseudo-Hermitian notation alone is insufficient.

## Definitions

LetA_s=[[1,s],[0,2]] for reals andS=[[1,s],[0,1]], soA_s=Sdiag(1,2)S⁻¹.

A positive metric is a Hermitian positive-definite matrixη defining〈u,v〉_η=u*ηv. Self-adjointness in this inner product isA*η=ηA.

## Result name

Explicit quasi-Hermitian metric and its boundary

## Result statement

η_s=[[1,−s],[−s,1+s²]] satisfiesA_s*η_s=η_sA_s and is positive definite. A matrix with a nontrivial Jordan block and real eigenvalue admits no positive metric making it self-adjoint.

## Proof

Setη=(S⁻¹)*S⁻¹. Thenv*ηv=||S⁻¹v||²>0 for nonzerov. SubstitutingA=SDS⁻¹, withD real diagonal, provesA*η=ηA. For the obstruction, a positiveη has a positive square root. The transformed matrixB=η^{1/2}Aη^{−1/2} satisfiesB*=B, hence is unitarily diagonalizable and has real eigenvalues. Similarity then makesA diagonalizable too. Thus a Jordan defect cannot be hidden in a positive change of inner product. ForJ=[[1,1],[0,1]], writing a real symmetricη=[[a,b],[b,c]] givesJᵀη−ηJ=[[0,−a],[a,0]], forcinga=0 and contradicting positivity. The general similarity argument also covers complex Hermitian metrics.

## Worked example: calculation

Eigenvectorsv₁=(1,0),v₂=(1,1) have ordinary inner product one. Underη=[[1,−1],[−1,2]], their inner product is zero and both have metric norm one. The eigenvalues remain one and two because a change of inner product changes orthogonality, not the algebraic characteristic polynomial. In ordinary coordinates e^{itA_s} has off-diagonal entry s(e^{2it}−e^{it}). At t=π and s=1 its matrix is [[−1,2],[0,1]], whose Euclidean operator norm is 1+√2, although the evolution has norm one in the η metric. Thus real eigenvalues do not alone control Euclidean transient amplification.

## Worked example: conclusion

This is a full finite positive-metric construction with an explicit failure case, rather than a conjectural appeal to spectral reality.

## Limits

In infinite dimensions, a formal similarity may be unbounded, fail to preserve domains or lack a bounded inverse. The matrix theorem cannot silently be transferred to a proposed zeta Hamiltonian.

## Extension

Quantify stability through the condition number ofS orη. Even real spectra can have large transient Euclidean amplification in nonnormal coordinates; compare||e^{itA_s}|| with its unit norm in theη metric.
