## Revised question retained in the annual source

What mathematical relation does a modular Hamiltonian have to entropy, and which extra data are needed before inserting it into a geometric action?

## Status

Developed mathematical result with explicit hypotheses and checked example; historical claims repaired where necessary

## Summary

For a positive density matrix the modular Hamiltonian is−logρ. In a commuting finite model its entropy variation is exact to first order, and the negative second variation quantifies concavity. This is distinct from the weight transformation of a modular form.

## Definitions

Letρ=diag(p₁,…,p_n) withp_i>0 andΣp_i=1. SetK=−logρ andS(ρ)=−Tr(ρlogρ).

A commuting perturbation isρ(ε)=diag(p_i+εv_i), withΣv_i=0 and positivity for sufficiently smallε.

The word modular in this operator definition does not by itself mean a function transforms underSL₂(Z).

## Result name

Entropy first variation and strict concavity

## Result statement

S′(0)=Tr(δρK)=−Σv_i logp_i, andS″(0)=−Σv_i²/p_i. The second derivative is strictly negative for every nonzero trace-zero diagonal perturbation.

## Proof

Differentiate−(p_i+εv_i)log(p_i+εv_i). The derivative is−v_i(log(p_i+εv_i)+1). At zero the terms−Σv_i cancel by normalization, giving the first-law identity. Differentiating again gives the negative sum. It is strictly negative unless everyv_i is zero. For a Gibbs stateρ=e^{−βH}/Z, functional calculus givesK=βH+logZ·I, so the trace-zero perturbation law becomesδS=βδ〈H〉 whenβ,H are held fixed in that variation. These are consequences of the stated state and normalization, not a prescription for an arbitrary objectH_mod(R) in a gravitational action.

## Worked example: setup

Usep=(3/4,1/4) andv=(1,−1).

## Worked example: calculation

The entropy derivative is−log3, while the second derivative is−(4/3+4)=−16/3. A perturbation toward the more uneven distribution lowers entropy. The formula requiresp_i>0; at an eigenvalue zero,logρ is unbounded on that eigenspace and the differentiability argument changes.

## Worked example: conclusion

The construction provides a real operator and variation theorem while keeping two different uses of modular terminology separate.

## Limits

This finite commuting derivation does not define a modular gravitational action, renormalized black-hole entropy or a modular Hamiltonian for a quantum field region. Matrix positivity and trace normalization are essential.

## Extension

For noncommuting perturbations, use the Fréchet derivative oflogρ or its resolvent integral to derive the second variation. State a positive lower spectral bound to obtain quantitative continuity and concavity estimates. For the same diagonal family, relative entropy D(p+εv || p) has zero first derivative and second derivative Σv_i²/p_i. This positive quadratic form is the Fisher metric on trace-zero directions. It is the negative of the entropy Hessian here because the reference distribution is fixed in the relative-entropy expression.
