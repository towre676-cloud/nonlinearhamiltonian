## Summary

The proposed uncertainties resemble standard deviations of independent random errors. They are not universal interval laws, and ignoring dependence destroys algebraic cancellations. An exact independent-product variance calculation identifies a further term omitted by first-order propagation.

## Definitions

Let X,Y have means v,w and variances ε²,η². For independent variables assume finite second moments. For dependent variables write Cov(X,Y).

A stored pair (mean,standard deviation) does not record the joint law or whether two appearances refer to the same random variable.

## Result name

Exact variance identities and a missing second-order term

## Result statement

Var(X+Y)=ε²+η²+2Cov(X,Y). Under independence, Var(XY)=v²η²+w²ε²+ε²η². The source’s relative-error product formula retains only the first two terms and is undefined as written at zero means.

## Proof

Expand the centered square of X+Y and take expectations to obtain the covariance identity. For independent X,Y, E[X²Y²]=E[X²]E[Y²]=(v²+ε²)(w²+η²), while E[XY]=vw. Subtracting v²w² gives the exact product variance. The formula |vw|√[(ε/v)²+(η/w)²] has squared value w²ε²+v²η² when v,w≠0, omitting the nonnegative product term. For cancellation, using Y=−X gives covariance −ε² and exact zero variance. Treating those two appearances as independent instead reports2ε², demonstrating the lost dependence information.
