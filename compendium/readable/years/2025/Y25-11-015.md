## Revised question retained in the annual source

How can the recovered phase clustering be tested without confusing a fitted cone with a mathematical classification?

## Status

## Summary

The recovered plots were described as rigid motivic splitting across seeds. A circular resultant is a genuine invariant of phase concentration, but it cannot identify a motivic class without an independent map from the data to that class. A finite exact example exposes the statistic’s scope.

## Definitions

For phases θ_j define Z_n=n^(−1)Σ e^(i θ_j) and R_n=|Z_n|∈[0,1].

The von Mises model has densityexp(κ cos(θ−μ))/(2π I₀(κ)); it is an assumed family, not a consequence of plotting angles.

## Result name

Rotation invariance and a mixture ambiguity

## Result statement

R_n is invariant under a common phase rotation and equalsone exactly when all phases agree modulo2π. Equal populations at phases0 and π have R_n=0 despite being maximally concentrated in two separate directions.

## Proof

A common rotation multiplies Z_n by a unit complex number and leaves its modulus unchanged. The triangle inequality gives R_n≤1. Equality requires all nonzero unit summands to have the same argument, proving the equality characterization. For a two-point equal mixture, e^(i0)+e^(i π)=1−1=0, proving the ambiguity. The second harmonic Z_n^(2)=n^(−1)Σ e^(2i θ_j) is one for the same sample, detecting axial concentration. Thus a first-harmonic statistic can entirely miss a pair of opposing cones. Fisher-information or Cramér–Rao formulas require an actual parametric sampling model, independence assumptions, and differentiation under its likelihood; an empirical angular standard deviation is not automatically such a bound.

## Worked example: setup

Take ten observations, five at0 andfive at π; compare ten identical phases at π/4.

## Worked example: calculation

The first sample has R₁=0 and second-harmonic resultant R₂=1. The second has R₁=R₂=1. A tiny perturbation of each phase changes these values continuously but does not establish a discrete underlying category. ## Worked example: conclusion

The same data can support a concentration statement while leaving the proposed categorical interpretation completely unproved.

## Limits

## Extension
