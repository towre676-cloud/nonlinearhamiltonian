## Summary

## Result statement

## Proof

The transformation factors ofE₄³ andΔ both have exponent twelve, so their quotient factors cancel. This gives the invariance directly from the stated modular-form theorem. For the arithmetic calculation, E₄³=1+720q+179280q²+16954560q³+…. Writej=q⁻¹+c₀+c₁q+c₂q²+… and multiply byΔ. Matchingq terms givesc₀−24=720, soc₀=744. Matchingq² givesc₁−24·744+252=179280, yielding196884. Matchingq³ givesc₂−24·196884+252·744−1472=16954560, yielding21493760. Every step uses finite exact coefficient arithmetic; no floating-point fitting is involved.

## Limits

A finiteq-series match is not a proof of a global modular identity unless a theorem such as a dimension or Sturm bound supplies the upgrade. Modular invariance alone does not construct a positive Hilbert space, OPE or physical measurement model.

## Extension

For a proposed character vector, state its representation ofSL₂(Z), transformation law and pole orders. Then use an appropriate finite-dimensional modular-form bound to determine whether a certified finite coefficient comparison proves an identity. A stronger uniqueness argument is possible after compactification: if two modular functions on a compact modular curve have the same principal parts at every pole, their difference is holomorphic and hence constant by the maximum principle. Fixing one constant coefficient then determines the difference. The pole hypotheses are essential to this upgrade.
