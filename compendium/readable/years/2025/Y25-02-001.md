## Revised question retained in the annual source

What does the recovered integral equation imply, and which independent assumptions turn it into a predictive dynamical model?

## Status

Developed mathematical result with explicit hypotheses and checked example; historical claims repaired where necessary

## Summary

Integrating a flow defines a stock but does not determine the flow. An explicitly specified linear feedback gives a solvable growth law, a stable regime and a transparent accounting of units.

## Definitions

LetW(t)=W₀+∫₀ᵗ[αV(s)+βM(s)+γI(s)]ds. Each summand must have units of wealth per time.

For a new test model set the total flow equal toa+bW, wherea is constant wealth per time andb has inverse-time units.

The initial condition already fixes the integration constant; adding a second arbitrary constant would duplicate it.

## Result name

Exact affine feedback solution

## Result statement

Forb≠0,W(t)=(W₀+a/b)e^{bt}−a/b. Forb=0,W=W₀+at. Ifb<0 the equilibrium−a/b attracts exponentially; ifb>0 it is unstable.

## Proof

The fundamental theorem of calculus givesW′ equal to the integrand when that flow is continuous. Substituting the declared feedback givesW′−bW=a. Multiplication bye^{−bt} makes the left side the derivative ofe^{−bt}W; integrate from zero tot to obtain the formula. Perturbing the equilibrium byδ givesδ′=bδ, proving the stability statement. Thus the stock-flow identity and a causal feedback assumption are mathematically different premises. The integral by itself can represent every differentiableW after defining its flow asW′ and therefore cannot certify projected asset targets.

## Worked example: setup

TakeW₀=0,a=10,b=−1/5 in consistent units.

## Worked example: calculation

The solution isW(t)=50(1−e^{−t/5}), with initial flow ten and limiting stock fifty. In contrastb=1/5 gives50(e^{t/5}−1), with accelerating growth. Both start with the same value and derivative but give radically different long-run paths. A numerical projection must therefore report its feedback coefficient and not only its starting flow.

## Worked example: conclusion

The revised equation becomes a testable dynamical model once its flow is independently specified.

## Limits

No historic asset-growth forecast or multiplier is validated. IfV,M,I already contain overlapping receipts, summing them can double count; a common unit does not establish disjoint economic contributions.

## Extension

Add a capacity constraint or saturation term and establish an invariant nonnegative interval. Estimate parameters from independent flow observations and report whether the proposed long-run regime is identifiable from those data. If the coefficients are time dependent but b(t) is prescribed, the integrating factor gives W(t)=e^{B(t)}[W₀+∫₀ᵗe^{−B(s)}a(s)ds], where B(t)=∫₀ᵗb(s)ds. This formula separates observable input flows from amplification and exposes exactly which missing functions a numerical forecast would need.
