## Revised question retained in the annual source

What spectrum follows from the standard face and edge rules, without assigning motivic constants by analogy?

## Status

Classical or newly chosen explicit model developed with proof; historical conjectures delimited

## Summary

The recovered classical rewrite invoked Fourier blocks but asserted complex curvature eigenvalues. Deriving the ordinary Catmull–Clark block from its stencil gives a real discriminant. This is a constructive replacement that can be checked independently of any proposed atlas.

## Definitions

Around a valence-vvertex, label edge neighbors E_jand opposite face vertices F_jin cyclic order. A face point is F′_j=(P+E_j+F_j+E_(j+1))/4.

The edge rule is E′_j=(P+E_j+F′_(j−1)+F′_j)/4.

## Result name

Exact nonconstant Fourier block

## Result statement

For a nonzero modez=e^(2π ik/v), P=0, E_j=Ez^j, F_j=Fz^j, the block on(E, F)is M(z)=[[(3+c)/8,(1+z^(−1))/16],[(1+z)/4,1/4]], c=cos(2π k/v). Its eigenvalues are(5+c±√((c+9)(c+1)))/16.

## Proof

Substitute the modal values into the face rule to get F′=[(1+z)E+F]/4. In the edge rule, F′_(j−1)+F′_jcontributes(1+z^(−1))F′, so its E-coefficient is1/4+(2+z+z^(−1))/16=(3+c)/8and its F-coefficient is(1+z^(−1))/16. This proves the block. Its trace is(5+c)/8and determinant1/16. The quadratic formula gives the stated eigenvalues. Sincec∈[−1,1], the radicand(c+9)(c+1)is nonnegative; for this standard block the eigenvalues are real. Atz=−1the off-diagonal entries vanish and M=I/4, so the repeated eigenvalue there is semisimple.

## Worked example: setup

Takev=4, k=1, andv=6, k=1,2.

## Worked example: calculation

Forv=4, c=0, the eigenvalues are1/2and1/8. Forv=6, thek=1values are(11±√57)/32; thek=2values are(9±√17)/32. These exact formulas differ from the recovered ad hoc constants. Sixth roots of unity may put√3in matrix entries, but that does not force complex eigenvalues or a motivic interpretation.

## Worked example: conclusion

The spectrum now follows from explicit subdivision rules and a two-by-two characteristic polynomial.

## Limits

This is the nonconstant one-ring block, not a complete proof of global surface regularity or every extended stencil. Different modified schemes can have different blocks and must supply their own rules.

## Extension

Derive the constant-mode vertex block and characteristic map, then verify the eigenvalue ordering and regularity conditions required by a subdivision-smoothness theorem.
