## Summary

The August20 synthesis connected Hall algebras, Ext² and spectral sequences. Finite vector spaces provide a complete Hall counting example. It also distinguishes counting subobjects from detecting a nonsplit extension.

## Definitions

Work in finite-dimensional vector spaces over F_q. Let[n] denote an isomorphism class of dimension n. Use the Hall convention[a]*[b]=Σ_c g^a,b_c[c], where g counts b-dimensional subspaces with a-dimensional quotient.

## Result name

Gaussian binomial coefficients and flag associativity

## Result statement

[a]*[b]=GaussianBinomial(a+b,b)_q[a+b]. In particular[1]*[1]=(q+1)[2], and[1]^3=(q+1)(q²+q+1)[3].

## Proof

Only a space of dimension a+b can contribute. Count ordered linearly independent b-tuples in F_q^(a+b):there are∏_{j=0}^{b−1}(q^(a+b)−q^j). Divide by the number∏_{j=0}^{b−1}(q^b−q^j) of ordered bases of a fixed b-plane. This yields the Gaussian binomial. Associativity counts the same flags with two successive quotient dimensions in either parenthesization. For three one-dimensional factors, choose a line and then a plane containing it, giving(q²+q+1)(q+1). Every short exact sequence of vector spaces splits, so Ext¹=0 here despite nontrivial Hall coefficients.
