## Summary

A numerical quality is meaningful only after the integer identity and radical are checked. The recovered first example is valid, the second quality is wrong, and the third displayed triple fails the defining sum. Those finite corrections precede any curvature or asymptotic interpretation.

## Definitions

For positive coprime a,b with a+b=c, define rad(n)=∏_{p|n}p and q=log c/log rad(abc). Distinct prime factors are counted once.

The classical ABC statement quantified in the source allows a constant K_ε depending onε; a finite observed maximum is not a theorem bounding all qualities.

## Proof

The first factors are2,3^10·109 and23^5. Their distinct prime product is2·3·109·23=15042; pairwise coprimality follows from these disjoint prime factors. In the second example121=11²,109375=7·5^6, and109496=2³·13687, with13687 prime, giving the stated radical. Trial division through its square root certifies that primality. Exact addition verifies both sums and Euclid verifies coprimality. In the third, addition alone invalidates the setup, so its reported q cannot serve as an ABC quality. Logs are evaluated only after these integer certificates. If q>1+ε, rearrangement gives c>rad(abc)^(1+ε); this is an equivalence for one valid triple, not a proof of a universal finiteness statement.
