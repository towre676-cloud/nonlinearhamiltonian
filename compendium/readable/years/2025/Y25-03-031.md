## Revised question retained in the annual source

What does a concrete gradient loss prove about convergence and invisible directions?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered categorical-loss andE8-projection language needs a defined function. A least-squares loss gives a complete matrix theorem: energy decreases, but components in the kernel persist. It therefore does not automatically enforce every desired coherence condition.

## Definitions

LetL(x)=(1/2)||Wx−y||² for a real matrixW. Gradient flow isx′=−Wᵀ(Wx−y).

The observable subspace israngeWᵀ; the invisible subspace iskerW, its orthogonal complement.

## Result name

Exact dissipation and nullspace preservation

## Result statement

Along the flow,L′=−||∇L||²≤0. Every kernel component ofx is constant, while positive eigenmodes ofWᵀW decay exponentially toward a least-squares solution.

## Proof

The chain rule givesL′=∇L·x′=−||∇L||². Ifv∈kerW, thenv·x′=−(Wv)·(Wx−y)=0, so the projection onto the kernel is conserved. Diagonalize the symmetric positive semidefinite matrixWᵀW. Relative to any least-squares solutionx*, the error satisfiese′=−WᵀWe; its eigencomponent of eigenvalueλ evolves ase^(−λt). Thus positive modes decay and zero modes persist. Strict convexity and unique convergence occur only whenW has full column rank. A zero loss certifiesWx=y, not an unrelated associator or lattice constraint absent fromW.

## Worked example: setup

TakeW=[1,1] andy=2.
