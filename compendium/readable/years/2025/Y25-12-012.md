## Revised question retained in the annual source

What does Abel’s identity prove for the recovered two-component stress model?

## Status

Classical or newly chosen explicit model developed with proof; historical conjectures delimited

## Summary

The recovered application correctly described Abel–Liouvilleas structural rather than predictive. A two-dimensional linear system gives an exact determinant law and demonstrates why volume contraction does not imply stability of every direction.

## Definitions

Let X′=A(t)Xwith X(0)=Iand continuous A.

For a constant symmetric model use A=[[-α, κ],[κ,−β]].

## Result name

Liouville determinant and sharp two-mode stability

## Result statement

det X(t)=exp(∫₀ᵗtr A(s)ds). For α, β>0, the constant symmetric model is asymptotically stable exactly when κ²<αβ.

## Proof

Jacobi’s determinant formula givesd(det X)/dt=det X·tr(X^(−1)X′)=det X·tr Aas long as Xis invertible. Solving the scalar equation gives the displayed positive exponential, which also guarantees invertibility for the full interval and completes the argument. For the symmetric matrix, the eigenvalues are−(α+β)/2±√((α−β)²/4+κ²). Both are negative exactly when the square root is smaller than(α+β)/2, equivalently κ²<αβ. The trace is negative regardless of κ, so a negative volume-growth exponent alone does not exclude an unstable positive eigenvalue.

## Worked example: setup

Take α=0.015, β=0.020and κ=0.010in one consistent inverse-time unit.

## Worked example: calculation

κ²=0.0001<αβ=0.0003, so both eigenvalues are negative anddet X=e^(−0.035t). If κ is increased to0.030, the trace and determinant law remain the same but κ²=0.0009>0.0003and one eigenvalue is positive. This distinction is exact and can be checked without simulation.

## Worked example: conclusion

The identity supplies volume evolution and a separate spectral test supplies directional stability.

## Limits

These coefficients are historical illustrative assumptions, not verified fault parameters or a rupture forecast. Nonlinear switching, forcing and constitutive laws can invalidate conclusions drawn from one linearization.

## Extension

For time-varying coefficients, seek a common positive Lyapunov matrix or an exponential-dichotomy bound; the determinant alone cannot supply a uniform stability theorem.
