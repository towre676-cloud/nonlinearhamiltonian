## Summary

The analyzer names many rapidly growing sequences and exponents. A modular filter can be completely rigorous, but its scope is a particular equation, modulus and range. One simple obstruction already separates even and odd degrees rather than producing a universal threshold at seven.

## Definitions

For a modulus M≥2 define R_d(M)={x^d mod M:x∈Z/MZ}. If S(n)±k=m^d then its residue must belong to R_d(M).

Take S(n)=n!, k=1, n≥3. For a prime p, the nonzero dth powers form the image of the dth-power map on the cyclic group F_p×.

## Result name

An all-n factorial exclusion and an exact residue count

## Result statement

For every even d≥2 and n≥3, n!−1 is not a dth power of an integer. For a prime p, |R_d(p)|=1+(p−1)/gcd(d,p−1).

## Proof

Since3 divides n!, n!−1≡−1≡2 mod3. Every even power modulo3 is zero or one, giving the contradiction for all n in the stated range. For the residue count, write F_p× as a cyclic group of order p−1. Its dth-power homomorphism has kernel size gcd(d,p−1), so its image has(p−1)/gcd(d,p−1) elements; add zero. This theorem counts residues for uniformly ranging bases modulo p, not solutions along a factorial or tower sequence. Different sequence residue distributions and different signs can therefore have different exclusion fractions.

## Worked example: setup

Compare d=8 and d=7 modulo3, and inspect n=4.

## Worked example: calculation

R₈(3)={0,1}, so4!−1=23 is excluded. R₇(3)={0,1,2}, so this modulus excludes nothing for odd degree seven, although it does not prove23 is a seventh power. The positive equation4!+1=25=5² shows that changing the sign matters. The verifier exhaustively constructs residue sets and uses an exact integer-root search for finite examples.

## Worked example: conclusion

A modular rejection is a proof of impossibility; passing the filter is only a necessary condition and not a solution certificate.

## Limits

This does not classify n!+1, Fibonacci, Catalan, primorial or tower equations. The historicalρ=5/6 and d*=7 claims lack a defined sampling measure and proof. They are not consequences of this residue theorem.

## Extension

For a fixed named sequence and k,d, combine several coprime moduli by the Chinese remainder theorem and prove the sequence’s residue frequencies. Only then define and estimate a density with an explicit limiting procedure.
