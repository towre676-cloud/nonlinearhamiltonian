## Revised question retained in the annual source

How can canonical-height data certify independent points, and how does subgroup index affect a regulator?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The request for height refinement and BSD quantities becomes precise once a quadratic height and free subgroup are fixed. A Gram determinant can certify independence, but saturation is a separate issue and changes the regulator by a square factor.

## Definitions

Let q be canonical height modulo torsion, with q(nP)=n²q(P) and the parallelogram law. Define B(P,Q)=[q(P+Q)−q(P)−q(Q)]/2, so B(P,P)=q(P). For points P_i put G_ij=B(P_i,P_j).

## Result name

Positive height Gram matrices and index

## Result statement

Positive definiteness of G implies the P_i are independent modulo torsion. If their subgroup has finite index I in the full free Mordell–Weil lattice, detG is I² times the full regulator in this convention.

## Proof

The quadratic-height identities give bilinearity and q(Σc_iP_i)=cᵀGc. A torsion combination has height zero, whereas a positive-definite matrix gives a positive value whenever the integer vector c is nonzero. For the index formula express the P_i in a full lattice basis using an integer matrix C. Then G_P=CᵀG_fullC, so detG_P=(detC)²detG_full. The absolute determinant is the lattice index. The same equation shows why a numerically positive matrix does not alone certify a full regulator: it first needs rigorous error control and then an independent saturation argument.

## Worked example: setup

Use the stipulated Gram matrix[[2,1],[1,3]].

## Worked example: calculation

Its principal minors are2 and5. Its quadratic form is2a²+2ab+3b²=2(a+b/2)²+(5/2)b², positive for any nonzero pair. Hence the two points would be independent and their subgroup regulator would be5. If the subgroup index were two, the full regulator would instead be5/4.

## Worked example: conclusion

The proof explains which arithmetic conclusion a positive height certificate supports and exactly which information it leaves open.

## Limits

No specific elliptic points or verified height intervals were recovered for this example. A regulator is only one factor in a BSD leading term and does not prove that conjecture.

## Extension

Certify height intervals preserving positive definiteness and prove saturation at all candidate index primes before reporting the full regulator.
