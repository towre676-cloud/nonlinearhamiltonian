## Revised question retained in the annual source

What does a perversity allow near a singular point, and can it change the homology of a contractible cone?

## Status

Developed mathematical result with explicit hypotheses and checked example; historical claims repaired where necessary

## Summary

The open cone on a torus is contractible in ordinary topology, yet lower-middle intersection homology retains two first-homology classes. The discrepancy comes from which bounding chains may touch the cone point.

## Definitions

LetL=T² andX=cL be its open real three-dimensional cone, with the apex as a codimension-three singular stratum.

Use field coefficients and lower-middle perversity p(3)=0. An allowablei-chain may meet the apex only in dimension at mosti−3; its boundary must satisfy the analogous condition.

This is a fixed stratified pseudomanifold and perversity, not an unspecified objectIC(X).

## Result name

Explicit cone-formula instance

## Result statement

IH₀(X)=k,IH₁(X)=k² andIH_i(X)=0 fori≥2 under the stated perversity. OrdinaryH₁(X)=0 because the whole cone contracts.

## Proof

Allowable chains in dimensions zero,one and two cannot meet the apex: their permitted intersection dimensions are negative. Thus first cycles and their allowable bounding two-chains lie inX∖{apex}=T²×(0,∞), which retracts ontoT². Its two independent one-cycles therefore survive inIH₁. A two-cycle in the link can be coned to an allowable three-chain, because a three-chain may meet the apex at a zero-dimensional set. This kills the link’s fundamental two-cycle. More generally the standard cone contraction applies above the cutoff; the cone formula statesIH_i(cL)=IH_i(L) fori<dimL−p(3)=2 and zero above. The chain explanation proves the low-degree distinction directly and identifies why an ordinary apex-crossing disk is inadmissible.

## Worked example: conclusion

Perversity controls allowable fillings, so contractibility does not force this refined homology to vanish.

## Limits

Changing to upper-middle perversity p(3)=1 changes the cutoff and removes the retainedH₁. Shifts and indexing of an intersection complex must also be specified before equating its hypercohomology to anIH group.

## Extension

Construct an explicit triangulated link and its cone, label singular strata, and implement allowable-chain restrictions degree by degree. Compare the resulting homology with the stated cone formula before using it in a larger singular space.
