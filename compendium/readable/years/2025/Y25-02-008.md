## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which finite identities should a basis satisfy before its derivative arrays are used in a geometric solver?

## Summary

A quadratic Bernstein element gives exact partition of unity, polynomial reproduction and a positive-semidefinite stiffness matrix. These are local approximation facts that can be checked independently of a full relativity code.

## Definitions

On[0,1], useB₀=(1−x)²,B₁=2x(1−x),B₂=x², the degree-two Bernstein basis, also a single-spanB-spline basis.

The stiffness matrix isK_ij=∫₀¹B_i′B_j′dx. Its quadratic form is the derivative energy of the represented polynomial.

The mass matrixM_ij=∫B_iB_jdx is a Gram matrix and is positive definite because the basis functions are linearly independent.

## Result name

Exact local element identities

## Result statement

ΣB_i=1,x=(1/2)B₁+B₂ andx²=B₂. The stiffness matrix has diagonal4/3 and off-diagonal−2/3, hence eigenvalues0,2,2.

## Proof

Expand the three polynomials to prove the reproduction identities coefficient by coefficient. Their derivatives are−2+2x,2−4x,2x. Integrating each derivative product gives the stated matrix, equivalentlyK=2I−(2/3)11ᵀ. The constant coefficient vector(1,1,1) is in its kernel, reflecting zero derivative of a constant. Every vector orthogonal to it has eigenvalue two, proving positivity on nonconstant modes. Independently,cᵀKc=∫|(Σc_iB_i)′|²≥0; equality means the polynomial is constant, giving the same kernel without spectral algebra.

## Worked example: conclusion

These finite identities certify basis differentiation and an energy form, not a complete physical simulation.

## Limits

Multi-span splines need knot multiplicities and continuity specified. Curved geometry mappings add Jacobians, and boundary conditions determine whether the assembled stiffness becomes strictly positive.

## Extension

On an explicitly mapped element, verify a positive Jacobian and transform the stiffness integrand by the inverse metric. Assemble adjacent spans and test global constant reproduction, flux consistency and the expected null space. The exact null vector must be retained before boundary conditions are imposed. Artificially inverting the local stiffness matrix would fail because its determinant is zero. Removing a Dirichlet degree of freedom or passing to a quotient by constants changes the operator domain and can produce a positive-definite reduced system with a meaningful inverse.
