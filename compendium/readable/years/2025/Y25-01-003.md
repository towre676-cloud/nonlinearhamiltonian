## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which algebraic property separates jointly measurable finite observables, without discarding identity operators or symmetry?

## Status

Developed mathematical result with explicit hypotheses and checked example; historical claims repaired where necessary

## Summary

Commuting self-adjoint matrices admit a common orthonormal eigenbasis. Noncommuting examples retain an identity and can have exact unitary symmetries; their uncertainty is a quantitative statement about a state.

## Definitions

An observable in this finite model is a self-adjoint complex matrix. Joint diagonalization means one unitary basis makes every matrix diagonal.

Use PauliX=[[0,1],[1,0]],Z=diag(1,−1),Y=[[0,−i],[i,0]], and identityI.

For normalizedψ, variance is(ΔA)²=||(A−〈A〉)ψ||². A symmetry may act by unitary conjugation.

## Result name

Joint diagonalization and a sharp uncertainty example

## Result statement

A finite commuting family of self-adjoint matrices is simultaneously unitarily diagonalizable. Forψ=(1,i)/√2,ΔX=ΔZ=1 and|〈[X,Z]〉|/2=1, attaining the Robertson lower bound.

## Proof

Diagonalize one self-adjoint matrix. Every matrix commuting with it preserves each eigenspace, becauseA(Bv)=B(Av)=λBv. Restrict the remaining self-adjoint matrices to those invariant eigenspaces and iterate; their orthonormal eigenbases combine into the required basis. Conversely diagonal matrices commute. For the inequality putu=(A−〈A〉)ψ andv=(B−〈B〉)ψ. Cauchy–Schwarz gives||u||||v||≥|Im〈u,v〉|=|〈[A,B]〉|/2. With the statedψ,〈X〉=〈Z〉=0,X²=Z²=I and[X,Z]=−2iY whileYψ=ψ. All claimed numbers follow directly. The same noncommutative algebra still containsI, andXZX=−Z is an exact conjugation symmetry.

## Worked example: setup

CompareI,Z withX,Z and evaluate their products on the chosen state.

## Worked example: calculation

I andZ share the standard basis. In contrastXZ=−ZX, so no common eigenbasis exists. NeverthelessI commutes with everything, each Pauli matrix is unitary, and conjugation byX exchanges the twoZ eigenstates. Noncommutativity therefore does not imply absence of identity, symmetry or algebraic consistency.

## Worked example: conclusion

The original contrast becomes a theorem about simultaneous diagonalization and state-dependent variance.

## Limits

The finite result does not settle interpretations of quantum measurement or a Platonic ontology. Unbounded observables need common domains; zero commutator expectation in one state does not mean the operators commute.

## Extension

For an explicit matrix algebra, compute its center and commutant. These identify classical sectors and exact symmetries without assuming that every noncommutative system has the same observable structure. The commutator expectation can vanish in a particular state even for noncommuting operators. For the maximally mixed qubit state I/2, both Pauli variances equal one but the commutator has trace zero. The displayed inequality is then valid but not sharp; operator commutation is stronger than one state’s expectation test.
