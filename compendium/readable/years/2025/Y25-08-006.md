Which spectral assumptions justify a Chebyshev residual bound, and why is a generic preconditioner label insufficient?

## Summary

The solver narrative named Chebyshev spectrum shaping without recovered operators. A positive-definite matrix with a known interval admits an exact polynomial bound. The assumptions explain why the same formula cannot be transferred blindly to a nonnormal operator.

## Definitions

Let A be real symmetric positive definite with spectrum in[a,b],0<a<b. Set c=(a+b)/2,d=(b−a)/2 and p_m(λ)=T_m((c−λ)/d)/T_m(c/d), where T_m(cosθ)=cos(mθ). Then p_m(0)=1.

## Result name

Uniform residual polynomial bound

## Result statement

For e_m=p_m(A)e₀, ||e_m||₂≤|T_m(c/d)|⁻¹||e₀||₂≤2((√κ−1)/(√κ+1))^m||e₀||₂,κ=b/a.

## Proof

The affine map sends[a,b] to[−1,1], where|T_m|≤1. The spectral theorem turns the matrix norm into the maximum scalar modulus on its spectrum. For z>1, T_m(z)=(w^m+w^(−m))/2 with w=z+√(z²−1)>1, so its reciprocal is at most2w^(−m). At z=c/d, w=(√κ+1)/(√κ−1), giving the formula. A nonsymmetric right preconditioner can destroy symmetry in the Euclidean inner product; eigenvalue placement alone then does not justify the same norm equality. One must identify an appropriate energy inner product or use a different resolvent-based argument.
