## Summary

The historical series was assigned several automorphic interpretations without an explicit action. A classical weight-four lattice sum gives an actual transformation proof. This constructive example isolates the data that a higher-rank replacement would have to provide.

## Definitions

Forτ in the upper half-plane defineG4(τ)=Σ_((m,n)≠(0,0))(m+nτ)^(−4).

Letγ=[[a,b],[c,d]]∈SL2(Z) act byγτ=(aτ+b)/(cτ+d).

## Result name

Weight-four covariance

## Result statement

The series converges absolutely and locally uniformly, is holomorphic, and satisfiesG4(γτ)=(cτ+d)^4G4(τ).

## Proof

On any compact subset of the upper half-plane, |m+nτ| is bounded below by a positive constant times√(m²+n²). The fourth-power reciprocal is summable over the two-dimensional integer lattice, proving local uniform absolute convergence and holomorphy. Nextm+nγτ=[(md+nb)+(mc+na)τ]/(cτ+d). The integer pair(md+nb,mc+na) is a bijective reindexing because its determinant isad−bc=1. Absolute convergence permits rearrangement, producing the claimed factor. The proof depends on the precise lattice and its group action, not on the appearance ofζ-values in a numerical constant.

## Worked example: calculation

The first uses(m,n)↦(m+n,n) and leavesG4 unchanged. The second uses(m,n)↦(n,−m) and givesG4(−1/τ)=τ⁴G4(τ). Atτ=i this factor is1, consistent with the fixed point; for a hypothetical weight2 sum the corresponding factor would be−1 and would force vanishing at that point if covariance held.

## Worked example: conclusion

A transformation law is a proved identity with a defined group action. Merely addingSL3 orSL4 notation does not provide it.

## Limits
