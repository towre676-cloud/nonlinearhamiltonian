## Summary

The external May4 file contains a Mexican-hat formula and Fourier transform that do not match under the usual unnormalized forward transform. This repair fixes the convention before proving cancellation and admissibility. The file context is retained as T; no historical user prompt or completed wavelet theorem is invented.

## Definitions

Use F[f](ω)=∫_R f(x)e^(−iωx)dx. Let Gσ(x)=(√(2π)σ)^(−1)e^(−x²/(2σ²)), σ>0, so F[Gσ]=e^(−σ²ω²/2).

Define ψσ=−Gσ″=(√(2π)σ³)^(−1)(1−x²/σ²)e^(−x²/(2σ²)). These functions and their derivatives decay rapidly.

## Result name

A consistent pair, two vanishing moments and finite admissibility

## Result statement

F[ψσ](ω)=ω²e^(−σ²ω²/2), ∫ψσ=∫xψσ=0, and ∫_R|F[ψσ](ω)|²/|ω|dω=σ^(−4). The source coefficient 1/(2πσ³) would instead give an extra factor 1/√(2π) with this transform convention.

## Proof

Integration by parts gives F[f″]=−ω²F[f] when boundary terms vanish; apply it to Gσ. Integrating −Gσ″ directly gives zero. Integrating −xGσ″ by parts also gives zero because the remaining integral is a boundary difference of Gσ. Thus the Fourier transform vanishes to second order at frequency zero. For admissibility, symmetry reduces the integral to 2∫₀∞ω³e^(−σ²ω²)dω. With u=σ²ω² this is σ^(−4)∫₀∞ue^(−u)du=σ^(−4). All integrations converge absolutely. Finally the ratio of the source’s spatial coefficient to the corrected one is 1/√(2π); linearity of the transform forces the same ratio on the frequency side. A different Fourier convention could change constants, but it must be stated consistently on both sides.
