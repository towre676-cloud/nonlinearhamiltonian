## Summary

The categorical August20 discussion also named a Landau–Ginzburg energy. Its pointwise potential is solvable exactly. Separating that minimization from the gradient energy and statistical model prevents a formal minimum circle from being presented as a proved physical phase transition.

## Definitions

Let φ∈C,u>0 and V(φ)=r|φ|²+u|φ|⁴ with r real. For a spatial field one may add∫|∇φ|², but boundary conditions and function space must then be stated separately.

## Result name

Exact quartic minima

## Result statement

If r≥0,V has unique minimum atφ=0. If r<0, its minimum is−r²/(4u), attained exactly on|φ|=√(−r/(2u)).

## Proof

Set s=|φ|²≥0. Complete the square:rs+us²=u(s+r/(2u))²−r²/(4u). If r<0 the square vanishes at an allowed positive s, giving the circle. If r≥0 the function is increasing for s≥0 and minimized at zero. Along a nonzero minimum the phase direction costs no pointwise potential, while radial perturbations do. For a field with unconstrained periodic boundaries, constant minimizers also minimize the nonnegative gradient term. Nonconstant boundary conditions, topological sectors or fluctuations change the variational problem and require additional analysis.

## Worked example: setup

Use r=−2,u=1.

## Worked example: calculation

V=|φ|⁴−2|φ|²=(|φ|²−1)²−1. Every unit complex number minimizes it with value−1;φ=0 has value0 and is not a minimum. Fixing the field to zero on a boundary prevents a nonzero constant from meeting that boundary condition.

## Worked example: conclusion

The minimizer manifold is a proved feature of a declared polynomial, with the remaining physical interpretation separated.

## Limits

No quantum field theory, finite-temperature transition or categorical Ext obstruction is established by this elementary potential calculation.
