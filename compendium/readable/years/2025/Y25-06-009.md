## Summary

Several retrieved rules attempted to compute all motivic cohomology from a small inequality in p,q,n. The projective-bundle theorem instead shifts the motivic cohomology of the base field. On the Chow diagonal this simplifies beautifully, but it does not erase off-diagonal field information.

## Definitions

For a field k and standard integral motivic cohomology of smooth schemes, use H^{p,q}(X,Z)=CH^q(X,2q−p).

Let h be the hyperplane class of bidegree(2,1). The classical projective-bundle theorem is the stated input below.

## Result name

Correct projective decomposition and the Chow diagonal

## Result statement

H^{p,q}(P^n_k,Z)≅⊕_{i=0}^n H^{p−2i,q−i}(Spec k,Z). In particular CH^*(P^n_k)=Z[h]/(h^{n+1}); hence H^{2q,q}=Z for0≤q≤n and zero outside that Chow range. Other bidegrees are not specified by that diagonal rule.

## Proof

The projective-bundle theorem states thatΣh^iπ^* gives the displayed isomorphism, withπ:P^n→Spec k. The shifts follow from deg(h^i)=(2i,i). On the diagonal, H^{2r,r}(k)=CH^r(Spec k), which is Z for r=0 and zero for r>0 because a point has no positive-codimension cycles. Thus only i=q survives, yielding the polynomial Chow ring and the relation h^{n+1}=0. Off the diagonal, H^{1,1}(k)=k× is generally nonzero, so a computer rule returning merely zero or one Tate symbol loses genuine base-field data. The general projective-bundle theorem is cited rather than claimed as proved by finite tests; the diagonal and grading consequences are derived explicitly.

## Worked example: setup

For P² compute its Chow ranks and the product h·h.

## Worked example: calculation

The groups in bidegrees(0,0),(2,1),(4,2) are each Z, h² is the point class, and h³=0. For P¹, H^{1,1} contains k× even though its Chow ranks are only one in codimensions zero and one. The verifier implements only the declared truncated polynomial ring and checks degree shifts and products; it does not pretend to compute arbitrary field K-theory.

## Worked example: conclusion

A symbolic Tate-basis engine can be correct on a specified cellular Chow subtheory without being a universal motivic-cohomology algorithm.

## Limits

The source’s window formulas and parity tests are not accepted as general theorems. Coefficients, base field and cohomology model must be fixed before any computation is interpreted.

## Extension

Represent field cohomology as an explicit coefficient object with certified operations, then apply the projective decomposition functorially. Extend to products only with all bidegree summands and coefficient Tor information preserved.
