## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which exact invariants certify smoothness and good reduction, and what still requires a local reduction algorithm?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The historical request included Tate’s algorithm and conductor data. A discriminant calculation is substantial but does not supply all of that algorithm. A complete example identifies good primes and a singular-prime gap without confusing a model invariant with a conductor exponent.

## Definitions

For y²+a₁xy+a₃y=x³+a₂x²+a₄x+a₆, set b₂=a₁²+4a₂, b₄=2a₄+a₁a₃, b₆=a₃²+4a₆ and b₈=a₁²a₆+4a₂a₆−a₁a₃a₄+a₂a₃²−a₄². Set c₄=b₂²−24b₄ and c₆=−b₂³+36b₂b₄−216b₆.

## Result name

Exact discriminant identity

## Result statement

Δ=−b₂²b₈−8b₄³−27b₆²+9b₂b₄b₆ satisfies c₄³−c₆²=1728Δ. The Weierstrass cubic is smooth over a field exactly when its discriminant is nonzero there.

## Proof

Substitution proves the polynomial invariant identity. The discriminant condition results by eliminating common zeros of the cubic and its partial derivatives. In characteristic different from two and three, for y²=x³+Ax+B this reduces to Δ=−16(4A³+27B²): a repeated root r satisfies3r²+A=0 and r³+Ar+B=0, yielding4A³+27B²=0. An integral model with p-adic unit discriminant therefore has smooth reduction. A divisible discriminant still leaves minimality and reduction type to be checked, especially at small primes. Its valuation cannot simply be labeled the conductor exponent.

Use E:y²=x³−x.

## Worked example: calculation

Here b₂=0,b₄=−2,b₆=0,b₈=−1,c₄=48,c₆=0 and Δ=64. Thus j=48³/64=1728 and every odd prime is a good-reduction prime for this model. The identity48³=1728·64 holds exactly. The prime two remains exceptional and is not resolved by this calculation.

## Worked example: conclusion

The result gives a reproducible smoothness certificate with explicit limits on what a partial Tate routine can claim.

## Limits

No full Tate-algorithm branch at two is executed here. Coordinate changes can multiply a model discriminant by twelfth powers, making minimality essential.

## Extension

Return a certificate for each local coordinate change, residual polynomial and branch condition before reporting minimal discriminant and conductor data.
