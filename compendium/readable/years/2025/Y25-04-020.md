## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which mathematical stability statement follows from the recovered update rule?

## Status

## Summary

The recovered recurrence is elementary enough to solve exactly, but calling its iterates an attractor does not make them bounded. A precise stability analysis separates a positive growth multiplier from a contracting map with an actual fixed point.

## Definitions

LetA_(n+1)=aA_n witha=1+λr, where0<r<1 andλ real.

An equilibrium0 is asymptotically stable exactly when every sufficiently small initial condition tends to0.

## Result name

Exact linear stability

## Result statement

A_n=a^nA0. The equilibrium is asymptotically stable iff|1+λr|<1, equivalently−2/r<λ<0. Forλ>0 every nonzero magnitude grows exponentially.

## Proof

Induction gives the closed form. If|a|<1, powers tend0 and remain bounded by|A0|, proving stability and attraction. If|a|>1, arbitrarily small nonzero initial values eventually leave any fixed neighborhood, so stability fails. Ata=1 the iterates are constant and ata=−1 they alternate; neither attracts allnearby points to0. The real inequality−1<1+λr<1 solves to the stated interval because r>0. A parameter namedβ≈0.673 would be a contraction factor only if it actually bounds the derivative or multiplier of the defined map.

## Worked example: setup

Taker=1/φ. Withλ=1, a=1+r=φ.

## Worked example: calculation

A_n=φ^nA0 diverges in magnitude forA0≠0. Withλ=−1, a=1−r=r², soA_n=r^(2n)A0 tends0. Adding a constantc givesA_(n+1)=aA_n+c; when|a|<1 its unique fixed point isc/(1−a), and subtracting that point reduces to the same contraction proof.

## Worked example: conclusion

The proposed update has an exact parameter classification, not a generic stability guarantee.

## Limits

A bounded nonlinear state space, saturation or projection would define another map and require a new proof. No empirical stability threshold is inferred from the recovered decimalβ.

## Extension

Define the full nonlinear update and bound its derivative on an invariant interval. Use that bound to prove a fixed point and a quantitative convergence rate.
