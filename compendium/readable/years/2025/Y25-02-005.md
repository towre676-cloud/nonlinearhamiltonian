## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which distribution statistic and production identity are actually being compared, and what assumptions determine each?

## Status

Developed mathematical result with explicit hypotheses and checked example; historical claims repaired where necessary

## Summary

The finite-sample Gini is a normalized pairwise-difference sum. A Cobb–Douglas log differential is instead a production identity with a residual. Both can be computed exactly without validating a national comparison.

## Definitions

For nonnegative incomesx_i with positive sum, useG=Σ_{i,j}|x_i−x_j|/[2nΣ_i x_i].

For positive outputY=AK^αL^β, take logarithms. A growth-accounting residual isdlogA=dlogY−αdlogK−βdlogL.

A dependency ratio also needs an explicit numerator population and denominator population; changing either changes the statistic.

## Result name

Scale invariance and exact logarithmic decomposition

## Result statement

G is unchanged by multiplying every income by a positive constant. For(0,0,2,2),G=1/2; for(1,1,1,1),G=0. The production formula differentiates exactly todlogY=dlogA+αdlogK+βdlogL.

## Proof

Both numerator and denominator ofG scale linearly, proving invariance. In the unequal example eight ordered pairs cross between zero and two, each contributing two, so the numerator is sixteen and the denominator2·4·4=32. Equal values have zero pairwise differences. For production, logY=logA+αlogK+βlogL, so ordinary differentiation gives the identity whenα,β are fixed. A measured residual is therefore whatever remains under the chosen functional form and coefficients; the algebra does not prove that it measures a unique causal technology effect.

The revised comparison distinguishes inequality, growth accounting and representation arithmetic instead of treating them as interchangeable evidence.

## Limits

Zero total income makesG undefined. A finite Gini does not recover the whole distribution, and a growth residual depends on measurement and model choices. No country-specific data claim is established here.

## Extension

For an inequality-reducing transfer preserving total income, derive the change in the pairwise sum while accounting for rank crossings. For production data, compare alternative elasticities and report the resulting range of residual growth. Dividing each income by its mean leaves the Gini unchanged, but adding the same positive constant to every income generally lowers it: the pairwise numerator stays fixed while the total-income denominator increases. This exact distinction separates scale changes from a uniform transfer and shows why both should not be called normalization.
