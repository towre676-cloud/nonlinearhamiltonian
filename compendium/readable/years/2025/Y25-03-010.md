## Revised question retained in the annual source

What metric follows from an explicitly defined finite spectral triple?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered spectral-triple proposal contains a formal commutator expansion but no concrete operator domain. A two-point triple has no domain ambiguity and yields a complete distance calculation. It also shows directly how rescalingD changes the metric.

## Definitions

RepresentA=C⊕C diagonally onH=C², and letD=[[0,m],[conj(m),0]] withm≠0.

For the two evaluation statesδ1,δ2, define d(δ1,δ2)=sup{|a−b|:a,b real,||[D,diag(a,b)]||≤1}.

## Result name

Two-point spectral distance

## Result statement

The distance equals1/|m|. Ifm=0 the distance is infinite.

## Proof

The commutator has off-diagonal entriesm(b−a) andconj(m)(a−b). Its adjoint times itself is|m|²(a−b)²I, so its operator norm is|m||a−b|. The constraint is therefore exactly|a−b|≤1/|m|, and equality is attained by a suitable real diagonal element. Ifm=0, every commutator vanishes while|a−b| is unbounded, giving infinite distance. All spectral-triple conditions in this finite setting are immediate: the algebra is represented by bounded operators,D is self-adjoint, and its resolvent is compact becauseH is finite-dimensional. A formal gradient approximation was unnecessary.

## Worked example: setup

Takem=2 and then replaceD by3D.

## Worked example: calculation

The distances are1/2 and1/6. The two-point set and its algebra are unchanged, yet the metric changes. Conversely, choosingm purely imaginary with the same modulus leaves the distance unchanged because the operator norm depends only on|m|.

## Worked example: conclusion

The operator supplies concrete metric information that is not determined by the algebra’s name alone.

## Limits

This does not create a continuum Dirac operator, a hyperbolic quotient or a motivic structure. Infinite-dimensional spectral triples require domains, bounded commutators and compactness hypotheses.

## Extension

For a finite graph Dirac model, calculate the commutator norm and compare its induced metric with shortest-path distance; do not assume they agree without proof.
