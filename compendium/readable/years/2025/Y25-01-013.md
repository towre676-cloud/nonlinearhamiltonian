## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which derivative detects a singular level of a scalar wave field, and how is that different from the Jacobian determinant of a vector map?

## Status

Developed mathematical result with explicit hypotheses and checked example; historical claims repaired where necessary

## Summary

A scalar field onR³ has a gradient and Hessian. A determinant belongs to a square derivative of a declared three-component map. Two explicit examples distinguish critical values, Morse indices and fold singularities.

## Definitions

ForΦ:R³→R, a levelΦ=c is regular when∇Φ≠0 at every point on that level.

ForF:R³→R³, its derivative is3×3 anddetDF detects rank loss. These are different maps and cannot be denoted by an untyped determinant of one scalar.

OnT³ useΦ(x,y,z)=cosx+cosy+cosz, with coordinates modulo2π.

## Result name

Complete critical-point calculation and fold model

## Result statement

The potential has eight critical points, each coordinate zero orπ. Its critical values are3,1,−1,−3 and all eight points are Morse. The vector mapF(x,y,z)=(x²,y,z) has singular plane{x=0} and critical-value plane{u=0}.

## Proof

The gradient is(−sinx,−siny,−sinz), so it vanishes precisely at the eight listed points. The Hessian isdiag(−cosx,−cosy,−cosz), with every diagonal entry±1 there, so its determinant is nonzero. A zero coordinate contributes a negative eigenvalue; the index counts the number of zero coordinates. There arebinomial(3,k) points of indexk. Their alternating count1−3+3−1=0 matchesχ(T³). For the vector fold, DF=diag(2x,1,1), so rank drops exactly atx=0. An image point(u,v,w) has two preimages foru>0, one foru=0 and none foru<0. This is a complete local multiplicity change, obtained from a correctly typed determinant.

## Worked example: calculation

The potential maximum has value three and Hessian−I, so its scalar level becomes singular there. The fold map instead has derivative rank two at(0,v,w), with a one-dimensional kernel in thex direction. The two tests answer different questions even though both involve a loss of regular behavior.

Wave geometry is now expressed through explicit functions and regularity hypotheses rather than an undefined singular-locus symbol.

## Limits

The chosen cosine field is a new tractable example, not a recovered experimental reconstruction. Morse counts do not identify a derived stack, intersection complex or a sound-driven geometry.

## Extension

For a supplied finite wave sum, compute its gradient and Hessian symbolically, isolate all critical points and certify the Hessian signs. For a vector-valued measurement map, instead study its rank strata and the stability of inverse branches.
