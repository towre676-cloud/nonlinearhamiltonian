## Revised question retained in the annual source

What rigorous spectral estimate replaces an unexplained numerical transition inH^n?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered passage cites a hyperbolic Laplacian and numerical thresholds. A coordinate calculation gives a real theorem: the quadratic-form spectrum has a positive lower bound depending only on dimension and the curvature normalization.

## Definitions

Use the upper-half-space metricg=y^(−2)(dx²+dy²),y>0,onH^n. The Laplacian sign isΔ=divgrad.

Letb=−logy. Direct calculation gives|∇b|=1 andΔb=n−1. Test functionsu are real smooth and compactly supported.

## Result name

An exact Poincaré inequality

## Result statement

∫|∇u|²dV≥((n−1)²/4)∫u²dV. Thus the nonnegative operator−Δ has no spectrum below this quadratic-form bound.

## Proof

The coordinate expression isΔ=y²(Δ_x+∂y²)−(n−2)y∂y; substitutingb givesΔb=n−1, and the inverse metric gives|∇b|²=y²(1/y²)=1. For any reala, expand∫|∇u+a u∇b|². The cross term isa∫∇(u²)·∇b=−a∫u²Δb, with no boundary term becauseu is compactly supported. Therefore the nonnegative square equals∫|∇u|²+[a²−a(n−1)]∫u². Choosea=(n−1)/2 to minimize the bracket, yielding the inequality. The passage from the form inequality to the Friedrichs self-adjoint operator is standard; equality of the spectral bottom needs an additional approximating-sequence argument.

## Worked example: setup

InH³ the lower bound is1; inH² it is1/4.

## Worked example: calculation

Scaling the metric to curvature−κ² multiplies the operator byκ² and the lower bound becomesκ²(n−1)²/4. Thus a bare threshold20 has no invariant significance without metric units and a specified operator.

## Worked example: conclusion

The estimate is proved analytically and its scale dependence is explicit.

## Limits

The positive generalized functions y^s are not square-integrable eigenvectors on allH^n, so substituting them alone would not prove a spectral theorem. Quotients may have additional low discrete eigenvalues and require separate analysis.

## Extension

Construct compactly supported cutoff functions approaching the lower Rayleigh bound and prove spectral-bottom equality in this model, then study a declared quotient.
