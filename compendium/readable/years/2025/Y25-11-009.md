## Revised question retained in the annual source

What does a diagonal genus-two period matrix prove, and which Humbert discriminant describes the product locus?

## Status

Classical or newly chosen explicit model developed with proof; historical conjectures delimited

## Summary

The recovered source identified the product locus with H₄using a restricted Humbert relation. The standard principal-polarization convention assigns decomposable products to H₁. A lattice factorization proves the basic distinction and prevents a discriminant label from being silently changed.

## Definitions

Siegel upper half-space H₂ consists of symmetric2×2complex matrices Ω with positive definite imaginary part.

The principally polarized complex torus is A_Ω=C²/(Z²+Ω Z²); a primitive Humbert relation has integer coefficients and discriminant D.

## Result name

Diagonal periods give the H₁ product model

## Result statement

If Ω=diag(τ₁, τ₂), with Im τ_i>0, then A_Ω is the product E_(τ₁)×E_(τ₂)with product principal polarization. The relation Ω₁₂=0has coefficients(a, b, c)=(0,1,0)and D=b²−4ac=1.

## Proof

The period lattice is(Z+τ₁Z)⊕(Z+τ₂Z), so the coordinate map C²→C/(Z+τ₁Z)×C/(Z+τ₂Z)has exactly that kernel and induces the product isomorphism. The standard symplectic alternating form on the lattice splits into the sum of the two elliptic principal forms, identifying the product polarization. The indicated Humbert relation is primitive because its coefficients have gcdone. Writing2Ω₁₂=0would yield a formal discriminant4but multiplies the entire relation bytwo; it is not primitive and does not relabel the same locus as H₄. More general Humbert relations include quadratic termsd(Ω₁₂²−Ω₁₁Ω₂₂)+e, so a three-coefficient relation is only a special chart of the full definition.

## Worked example: calculation

The lattice is(Z+i Z)⊕(Z+2i Z). The first factor is an elliptic subtorus with principal restricted polarization of degreeone. In the standard convention H_(n²)instead describes a degree-nelliptic subcover/restricted polarization condition; n=2leads to H₄and does not mean the generic product polarization.

## Worked example: conclusion

The product statement follows from explicit lattices and the discriminant correction follows from primitiveness.

## Limits

The full moduli-theoretic characterization of Humbert surfaces is cited background. A period matrix or Humbert condition supplies no dynamics or zeta-zero theorem by itself.
