## Revised question retained in the annual source

What do degree growth and truncation say about the proposed finite-band composition operator?

## Status

## Summary

The recovered warm-up composed functions withx+x^dand suggested conjugation by the Stirling–Bernoulli matrix. Polynomial algebra gives exact triangular coefficients, but its bandwidth grows with degree, and the proposed Bernoulli matrix is singular.

## Definitions

## Result name

Explicit degree growth and the missing conjugacy

## Result statement

C_φ x^n=Σ_(j=0)^n C(n, j)x^(n+(d−1)j). Thus columnncontainsn+1nonzero terms spread over degreesnthroughdn, and there is no degree-independent finite bandwidth.

## Proof

Apply the ordinary binomial theorem to(x+x^d)^n=x^n(1+x^(d−1))^n. The lowest degree isnand the highest isdnwith nonzero coefficientone, proving both triangularity in degree and growing support. A projection to degrees≤Nproduces a finite triangular matrix, but its entries and omitted tail depend on N. A composition with an analytic nonpolynomial φ can have infinitely many nonzero coefficients even in a single column. An exact finite-band statement therefore needs an invariant basis and a proof, rather than an observation about one truncation. Further, the matrix A_nk=s(n, k)B_khas a zero column at k=3and no inverse on the full polynomial truncation, so A^(−1)B_φ Ais undefined there.
