## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which explicit kernel operation is an autoequivalence, and what distinguishes that theorem from merely adding a correction term?

## Status

Developed mathematical result with explicit hypotheses and checked example; historical claims repaired where necessary

## Summary

On a two-point space the bounded derived category can be classified completely. Equivalences permute the two simple objects and shift them; a shift-and-swap gives an exact involution.

## Definitions

Letk be a field andD=Dᵇ(Vec_k×Vec_k), represented by pairs of bounded finite-dimensional complexes.

Write[1] for the cohomological shift. Each complex over a field is isomorphic in the derived category to the direct sum of its cohomology spaces placed in their degrees.

The simple objects areS₁=(k,0),S₂=(0,k). Their shifts are the indecomposable objects, and different simples have no morphisms in any degree.

## Result name

Classification in a finite derived model

## Result statement

Everyk-linear triangulated equivalence ofD permutesS₁,S₂ and assigns an integer shift to each. In particularF(V,W)=(W[1],V[−1]) is an equivalence withF²≅Id.

## Proof

Split every differential into its image, a complement in its kernel and a remaining complement; the image/complement pairs form contractible two-term summands. This leaves precisely the graded cohomology, proving the decomposition. An equivalence preserves indecomposability and all graded morphism spaces. It therefore sends eachS_i to a shifted simple. The two images cannot be shifts of the same simple, since then some shifted morphism between them would be nonzero although everyHom(S₁,S₂[n]) vanishes. Essential surjectivity forces both simple types to occur. Conversely a permutation with prescribed shifts has an inverse permutation with the opposite shifts and acts componentwise on morphisms, so it is an equivalence. For the displayedF, applying it twice cancels both shifts.

## Worked example: setup

TakeV=k in degree zero andW=k² in degree zero.

## Worked example: calculation

F(V,W) has its two-dimensional first component shifted by one and its one-dimensional second component shifted by minus one. ApplyingF again restores both original complexes exactly up to the standard shift isomorphisms. The operationG(V,W)=(V⊕W,0), by contrast, loses the distinction betweenS₁ andS₂ and has no inverse.

## Worked example: conclusion

An equivalence is supported by a functor, an inverse and their composition identities, not by the existence of a suggestive kernel notation.

## Limits

This finite classification is not a Fourier–Mukai theorem for arbitrary varieties. Coherent sheaves on positive-dimensional spaces have extensions and support geometry absent here. The historical quantum-derived categories were not defined sufficiently to reconstruct them.

## Extension

For a genuine integral transformΦ_K, specify the source/target categories and convolution of kernels, then proveK*L andL*K are diagonal identity kernels. A computable finite model can test these identities before any geometric generalization is claimed.
