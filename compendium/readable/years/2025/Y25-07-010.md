## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which feedback minimizes a stated quadratic cost, and what proves both its stability and optimum?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

LQR was named in the architecture list. A normalized scalar example can be solved completely rather than supplying unexplained gains. Completion of squares also exposes the terminal condition needed for an infinite-horizon argument.

## Definitions

Let x′=x+u,x(0)=x₀ and J=∫₀∞(x²+u²)dt. Compare controls of finite cost with x(t)→0. Put P=1+√2, the positive root of2P−P²+1=0.

## Result name

Stabilizing Riccati solution

## Result statement

The feedback u=−Px gives x(t)=x₀e^(−√2t), achieves J=Px₀² and minimizes J in the declared admissible class.

## Proof

For V=Px², V′=2Px²+2Pxu. Since2P+1=P², x²+u²+V′=(u+Px)². Integration to T gives J_T=Px₀²−Px(T)²+∫₀ᵀ(u+Px)²dt. The terminal term tends to zero in the stated class, proving the lower bound. The feedback makes the square vanish and has decay rate1−P=−√2, so it attains equality. The other Riccati root1−√2 gives unstable feedback and is not a minimizing infinite-horizon certificate. Thus choosing the stabilizing solution is part of the theorem, not a numerical preference.

## Worked example: setup

Take x₀=1 and compare with u=−2x.

## Worked example: calculation

The optimal cost is1+√2≈2.414214. Under u=−2x, x=e^(−t) and J=∫5e^(−2t)dt=5/2, strictly larger. The gap is the integral of the nonzero completed square.

## Worked example: conclusion

The plant, cost, admissible class, stability rate and value are all explicit and independently checkable.

## Limits

This scalar model assumes exact state observation and no constraints or disturbances. It gives no operational prescription or performance guarantee for an original apparatus.

## Extension

Add a bounded-input constraint and derive the changed value equation and invariant region before comparing policies.
