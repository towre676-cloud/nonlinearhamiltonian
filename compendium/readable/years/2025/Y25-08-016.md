## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which operator annihilates the factorial period series, and how does a square-root gauge change its local monodromy?

## Summary

## Definitions

Let θ=z d/dz and a_n=(5n)!/(n!)⁵. Put F(z)=Σa_nz^n. A scalar gauge y=z^αv is interpreted on a chosen branch over a punctured neighborhood.

## Result name

Factorial recurrence and gauge shift

## Result statement

F solves[θ⁴−5z(5θ+1)(5θ+2)(5θ+3)(5θ+4)]F=0 formally. Gauge conjugation gives z^(−α)θz^α=θ+α.

## Proof

The factorial quotient gives n⁴a_n=5(5n−4)(5n−3)(5n−2)(5n−1)a_{n−1}; these are exactly the coefficient equations of the displayed operator. Its first coefficients are1,120,113400. An operator with−ζ rather than−5z uses a rescaled coordinateζ=5z and correspondingly rescaled coefficients; the two conventions cannot be mixed. The product rule givesθ(z^αv)=z^α(θ+α)v, proving conjugation for any polynomial inθ, with coefficient multiplication retained in its proper order. A loop around zero multiplies z^α by e^(2πiα), so a half-power gauge contributes−1 to monodromy.
