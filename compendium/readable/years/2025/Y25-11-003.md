## Revised question retained in the annual source

What extra condition is required before a scalar response is a reproducing kernel or a covariance?

## Status

Classical or newly chosen explicit model developed with proof; historical conjectures delimited

## Summary

The recovered kernel discussion moved from positive integrands to kernel methods and a conjectured spectral decay. A covariance or reproducing kernel must satisfy all finite Gram-matrix inequalities, a much stronger condition than having positive values.

## Definitions

A Hermitian kernel k(x, y) is positive definite if Σ overline c_i c_j k(x_i, x_j)≥0 for every finite point set and coefficients.

For a real translation kernel k(x, y)=f(x−y), Hermitian symmetry requires f(−t)=f(t).

## Result name

An exact quartic Gram counterexample

## Result statement

The pointwise positive even function f(t)=1/(1+t⁴) is not positive definite on R. Its Gram matrix at points−1/2,0,1/2 has negative determinant−157/1156.

## Proof

At adjacent separation1/2, f=16/17; at separation1, f=1/2. The Gram matrix is[[1, a, b],[a,1, a],[b, a,1]] witha=16/17, b=1/2. Expanding its determinant gives(1−b)(1+b−2a²). Here1+b−2a²=3/2−512/289=−157/578, so the determinant is−157/1156. A positive semidefinite Hermitian matrix has nonnegative determinant, proving failure. An even more immediate obstruction affects the original real asymmetric logistic factor: a translation kernel using it is not even, hence not Hermitian. On the other hand the quartic Fourier transform F(b)=∫e^(ibx)/(1+x⁴)dx is positive definite as a function of b: the Gram quadratic form equals∫|Σ c_j e^(ib_jx)|²/(1+x⁴)dx≥0. The response and its Fourier transform therefore play different roles.

## Worked example: setup

Compare f itself with F(b)=π e^(−|b|/√2)[cos(|b|/√2)+sin(|b|/√2)]/√2.

## Worked example: calculation

F can take negative values, yet its Gram forms remain nonnegative by the integral-square representation. Thus pointwise nonnegativity is neither necessary nor sufficient for positive definiteness. A valid covariance can have negative correlations, whereas a positive-valued response can fail the covariance test.

## Worked example: conclusion

The exact three-point certificate distinguishes two often-confused meanings of “positive kernel.”

## Limits

No spectrum λ_n can be assigned until an operator, domain, measure and boundary conditions are supplied. In particular the recovered(n logn)^(−4/3) claim is not certified by a scalar formula.

## Extension

Define an integral operator on a compact measured domain, prove self-adjointness and compactness, and derive its eigenvalue asymptotics from regularity and geometry rather than from a numerical fit.
