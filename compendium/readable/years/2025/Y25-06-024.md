## Revised question retained in the annual source

Reconstruction: When can an81-point test on F₃⁴ be a proof, and when is it merely evidence about a different polynomial claim?

## Status

## Summary

The source correctly distinguished computational verification from deductive proof. A finite-field identity theorem makes that distinction sharper: an exhaustive test can be a complete certificate within a specified reduced polynomial space. Without the degree restriction a nonzero polynomial can vanish at every test point.

## Definitions

Let f∈F₃[x₁,x₂,x₃,x₄] have degree at most two in each separate variable. Evaluate it on every point of F₃⁴.

The polynomial-function quotient is F₃[x₁,…,x₄]/(x₁³−x₁,…,x₄³−x₄). Each class has a unique representative with those separate degree bounds.

## Result name

A complete finite certificate and its exact counterexample

## Result statement

If the reduced f vanishes on all81 points, then f is the zero polynomial. Without the bound, x₁³−x₁ is a nonzero formal polynomial that vanishes at all81 points.

## Proof

A univariate polynomial of degree at most two over a field with three distinct roots is zero. View f as a polynomial in x₄ with coefficients in the first three variables. For any fixed values of those three variables, its three x₄ evaluations force all three coefficients to vanish at that triple. Repeat the argument in the remaining variables; induction makes every coefficient zero. Existence of a reduced representative follows by repeatedly replacing x_i³ by x_i, lowering degree; uniqueness follows from the just-proved evaluation injectivity. There are3^81 reduced polynomials and3^81 functions on an81-point set, so evaluation is also surjective. The counterexample violates the degree restriction and is zero only in the quotient of polynomial functions.

## Worked example: setup

Test the identity(x+y)³=x³+y³ over F₃ and distinguish it from the formal claim x³=x.

## Worked example: calculation

The first is a true polynomial identity because intermediate binomial coefficients are divisible by three. The second is true as a function on F₃ but false in the polynomial ring. The verifier evaluates every point and independently compares coefficient dictionaries, showing both the complete bounded certificate and the danger of an unstated quotient.

## Worked example: conclusion

A program can issue a deductive finite certificate when its theorem class and algebra are explicit.

## Limits

Testing a finite domain does not prove arbitrary integer, real, analytic or number-theoretic conjectures. The recovered program description does not provide its actual proof certificates, source code or a formal soundness theorem.
