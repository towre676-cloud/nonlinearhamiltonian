## Proof

Integrate W_nby parts using one factor sinx and the remaining sin^(n−1)x. The boundary term vanishes and gives W_n=(n−1)(W_(n−2)−W_n), hence the Wallis recurrence. Its initial values are W₀=π/2, W₁=1. For the differential equation, the coefficient ofz^nforn≥1isn(n−1/4)(n−3/4)c_n−n³c_(n−1), which vanishes exactly by the displayed rising-factorial ratio. The constant coefficient also vanishes. The ratio c_n/c_(n−1)=n²/[(n−1/4)(n−3/4)]tends toone, so the radius is one. This verifies the operator but not a ζ value. In fact the coefficients arepositive andgrow asymptotically likea constant timesn, so the unmodifiedseriesdiverges atz=1; a claimed finite value there needs an explicitly different solution, regularization or connection formula.

## Limits

Apéry’s irrationality theorem is established mathematics, but the recovered unspecified binomial sum and ζ(5+)ladder are not proved by this differential equation. Analytic continuation does not by itself prove a special-value identity.
