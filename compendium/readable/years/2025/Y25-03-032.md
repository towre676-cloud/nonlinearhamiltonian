## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which differential-form degrees make a proposed curvature action meaningful?

## Status

## Summary

The recovered expression integratesTr(R∧R) over a face. IfR is a curvature2-form andf is a2-dimensional face, its wedge square has degree4 and is not a surface volume form. A correctly typed four-dimensional calculation gives a constructive replacement.

## Definitions

A curvatureF is a matrix-valued2-form. Tr(F∧F) is a4-form and pulls back to zero on a smooth2-manifold.

On the unit4-torus, letω1=dx1∧dx2,ω2=dx3∧dx4 anduse a line-bundle curvatureF=2πi(ω1+ω2).

## Result name

An exact degree and characteristic-number calculation

## Result statement

The surface pullback ofTr(F∧F) iszero. OnT4, (1/2)∫(F/(2πi))²=1, whereas∫(F/(2πi))²=2.

## Proof

An alternating4-form vanishes on a tangent space of dimension2, because every four tangent vectors are linearly dependent. It therefore cannot supply a nonzero ordinary face integral. OnT4, ω1²=ω2²=0 andω1∧ω2=ω2∧ω1 because both degrees areeven. Hence(ω1+ω2)²=2dx1∧dx2∧dx3∧dx4, whose integral is2. The factor1/2 gives the degree-four Chern-character normalization1. Existence of a line bundle with this integral first-Chern class is classical; the wedge arithmetic and dimension check are explicit. A two-dimensional action could instead use a specified2-form, but that defines a different model.

## Limits

Discrete spin foams can use holonomies and combinatorial weights rather than differential forms, but those weights must be defined separately. No continuum quantum-gravity theory is constructed here.
