## Revised question retained in the annual source

Reconstruction: Which multiplicative convolution is intended, when do transforms turn it into a product, and what exact arithmetic identity can an implementation certify?

## Status

## Summary

The historical report’s multiplicative-function approximation and inversion claims lack their actual kernel and norms. The classical divisor convolution gives a well-defined replacement. It proves an exact transform product when absolute convergence permits regrouping, rather than asserting a universal approximation capability.

## Definitions

For arithmetic functions f,g:N→C, define(f*g)(n)=Σ_{d|n}f(d)g(n/d). Let1(n)=1 and ε(1)=1, ε(n)=0 for n>1.

A function is multiplicative if f(mn)=f(m)f(n) for coprime m,n and f(1)=1. Its Dirichlet series is D_f(s)=Σf(n)n^(−s) where absolutely convergent.

## Result name

Convolution, inversion and a transform product

## Result statement

Dirichlet convolution is associative and commutative with identity ε. The Möbius function satisfies μ*1=ε. Whenever D_f,D_g converge absolutely, D_(f*g)(s)=D_f(s)D_g(s). Multiplicative inputs give a multiplicative convolution.

## Proof

Associativity follows by writing either parenthesization as the finite sum overabc=n of f(a)g(b)h(c); commutativity swaps complementary divisors. For μ, summing μ(d) over divisors of n factors as∏_{p|n}(1−1), zero unless n=1. The Dirichlet-series product is the absolutely convergent double sumΣ_{a,b}f(a)g(b)(ab)^(−s); group terms by n=ab to obtain the formula. For coprime m,n, each divisor of mn decomposes uniquely into a divisor of m times a divisor of n, so the convolution sum factors. These are concrete algebraic and analytic hypotheses, not consequences of a neural architecture name.

## Worked example: setup

Take f=g=1, then invert the divisor-count function τ=1*1 by μ.

A finite implementation can certify these exact arithmetic identities while leaving approximation and extrapolation claims separate.

## Limits

This does not prove exponential approximation of arbitrary multiplicative functions, inversion of a noisy learned transform, or a number-theoretic theorem from finite training. Other notions of multiplicative convolution, such as Haar convolution on R_+, need different definitions.

## Extension

For a specified truncated divisor transform, compute its triangular matrix and exact inverse. Then derive a perturbation norm bound for noisy coefficients, making conditioning and truncation error explicit before claiming stable inversion.
