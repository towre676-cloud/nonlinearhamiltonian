## Result name

An exact observational collision

## Result statement

ρ_B andρ_M have identical computational-basis probabilities, identical one-qubit marginalsI/2 andZZ expectation one. ButTr(ρ_B XX)=1 andTr(ρ_M XX)=0, and their trace distance is1/2.

## Proof

Both states place probability one half on00 and11 and zero elsewhere. Every diagonal observable therefore has the same expectation. Partial trace removes the Bell cross terms, leaving the same marginalI/2. The operatorXX exchanges00 and11, so its expectation is the sum of those cross terms: one for the Bell state and zero for the mixture. Their difference is( |00⟩⟨11|+|11⟩⟨00| )/2, whose nonzero eigenvalues are±1/2. Its trace norm is one, giving distance one half. This proves that one class of observations can agree exactly while a different observable resolves the states.

## Worked example: setup

Use an equal mixture of the two states as a third candidate.

## Worked example: calculation

It retains every stated diagonal statistic but hasXX expectation1/2. In fact the entire familyρ(q) with coherenceq/2,0≤q≤1, shares those diagonal observations whileXX readsq. Repeating only the diagonal measurements at greater precision cannot identifyq.

## Worked example: conclusion

The mathematical issue is observation sufficiency, not merely the number of measurements.

## Limits

This example makes no factual claim about Microsoft hardware or Majorana modes. Topological invariants, lifetimes, coherent control and error rates require different defined observations and models.

## Extension

Specify a measurement family and compute the kernel of its linear map on Hermitian matrices. Add observables until the targeted parameter is identifiable, then derive finite-sample confidence bounds for those observables.
