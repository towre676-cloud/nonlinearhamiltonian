## Revised question retained in the annual source

Reconstruction: Which exact identities connect a finite positive spectrum, its heat trace and spectral zeta, and what makes the claimed small-eigenvalue density nonnormalizable without a cutoff?

## Status

Developed mathematical repair or classical result; speculative source claims delimited

## Summary

The report combines a learned spectral fit with a zeta-like series and transform inversion. These are different levels of claim. A finite positive spectrum gives exact analytic formulas; an asymptotic law requires a specified operator family, limiting measure and normalization.

## Definitions

Let0<λ₁,…,λ_r be positive eigenvalues counted with multiplicity. Define heat trace H(t)=Σe^(−tλ_i), t>0, and ζ_A(s)=Σλ_i^(−s). Exclude zero eigenvalues explicitly.

A proposed density ρ(λ)=Cλ^(−α) on(0,b] is integrable near zero only ifα<1. A finite lower cutoff changes this condition.

## Result name

A finite Mellin identity and an integrability obstruction

## Result statement

For Re(s)>0, Γ(s)ζ_A(s)=∫₀∞t^(s−1)H(t)dt. The finite spectral zeta itself is entire in s. A nonzero density proportional toλ^(−1.528) cannot be normalized on(0,b] without a cutoff or another modification.

## Proof

For one positive λ, substitute u=tλ in the gamma integral to obtain ∫₀∞t^(s−1)e^(−tλ)dt=λ^(−s)Γ(s). A finite sum can be interchanged with the integral, proving the identity. Each λ^(−s)=exp(−slogλ) is entire, so the finite sum is entire; the integral representation has its own restricted convergence region. For the density claim, ∫_ερ(λ)dλ=C[b^(1−α)−ε^(1−α)]/(1−α) whenα≠1. Ifα>1 this diverges asε→0. Therefore the fitted exponent does not define a probability density all the way to zero. Neither a finite fit nor this transform identity establishes a limiting law for an undefined arithmetic graph.

## Limits

An infinite spectrum requires convergence estimates before summing, integrating or analytically continuing. No λ₁ asymptotic, Riemann-type zero theorem or prime-gap prediction is obtained from a finite spectral definition.

## Extension
