## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which algebra, Hilbert space and operator define the proposed spectral geometry, and what distance do they produce?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The source named spectral triples and physics links without defining an operator. A complete two-point triple shows how an operator norm can encode a metric. It is a classical finite example, not a construction of the Standard Model or an arithmetic spectral realization.

## Definitions

Represent A=C² on H=C² by diag(a,b). Let D=[[0,m],[overline m,0]] with m≠0. For the two evaluation states δ₁,δ₂, define distance sup{|a−b|:a,b real,||[D,diag(a,b)]||≤1}.

## Result name

Exact two-point distance

## Result statement

The commutator norm equals|m||a−b|, so the spectral distance is1/|m|. D is self-adjoint and has compact resolvent because H is finite-dimensional.

## Proof

Matrix multiplication gives[D,diag(a,b)]=[[0,m(b−a)],[overline m(a−b),0]]. Its adjoint-times-itself is|m|²|a−b|²I, so both singular values equal|m||a−b|. The constraint is therefore|a−b|≤1/|m|, and equality is attained by a=1/|m|,b=0. The supremum is thus exact, not merely bounded. Scaling D by a positive factor c divides the distance by c. If m=0 the constraint imposes no restriction on a−b and the distance is infinite, illustrating why operator coupling is essential. More elaborate triples need domains, representation and regularity conditions that are trivial only in this finite case.

## Worked example: setup

Take m=2.

## Worked example: calculation

The allowed difference is at most1/2, and a=1/2,b=0 attains it. The eigenvalues of D are±2, while the distance is1/2; these are related by the chosen operator, not by a free numerical identification.

## Worked example: conclusion

Every component of the metric construction is now supplied and its consequence proved.

## Limits

No field theory, arithmetic L-function or higher-dimensional geometry follows from this two-point example. A named spectral triple without an algebra action and analytic domain is incomplete.

## Extension

For a finite weighted graph triple, derive its distance constraints and determine exactly when the result agrees with a chosen path metric.
