## Revised question retained in the annual source

What explicit assumption would justify the claimed improvement from quadratic to quartic refinement drift?

## Summary

The recovered cohomological-correction discussion reported ε²to ε⁴improvement and an exact-rationalk=35validation. No underlying certificate was recovered. A standard extrapolation theorem shows a constructive mechanism with the exact assumptions and residual coefficient visible.

## Definitions

Suppose F(h)=F₀+c h²+d h⁴+R(h), with|R(h)|≤M h⁶for0<h≤h₀.

Define the corrected estimate G(h)=[4F(h/2)−F(h)]/3.

## Result name

Explicit order-four extrapolation

## Result statement

G(h)=F₀−d h⁴/4+R_G(h), where|R_G(h)|≤(17/48)M h⁶. Thus theh²term cancels exactly and the remaining leading drift is quartic when d≠0.

## Proof

Substitute the expansion into4F(h/2)−F(h). The constant coefficient is3F₀; the quadratic coefficient is4c(h²/4)−ch²=0; the quartic coefficient is4d(h⁴/16)−dh⁴=−3dh⁴/4. Dividing bythree gives the formula. The remainder obeys|R_G|≤[4M(h/2)⁶+Mh⁶]/3=(17/48)Mh⁶. This is a complete cancellation proof. It also identifies a necessary structural assumption:the samec, dand F₀must govern the two scales. If the correction changes the problem or if an oddh³term is present, the claimed quartic order does not follow.

## Worked example: setup

Take F(h)=7+3h²+5h⁴exactly, and compareh=1andh=1/2.

## Worked example: calculation

F(1)=15, F(1/2)=7+3/4+5/16=129/16. Hence G(1)=[4·129/16−15]/3=23/4=7−5/4. The uncorrected quadratic coefficient3has disappeared, and G(h)−7=−5h⁴/4for everyh. Every value is rational, so no floating-point ambiguity is involved.

The improvement is a proved conditional approximation result with an exact example, rather than a claim inferred from a benchmark table.
