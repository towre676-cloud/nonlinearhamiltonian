## Revised question retained in the annual source

Under which finite-field hypotheses do the Paley spectral formulas apply, and what do their inverse moments count?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The August23 source supplied striking exact zeta values. They follow algebraically from a specific strongly regular graph spectrum, not from a general anomaly law. The smallest example is a five-cycle and can be checked without approximate eigenvalue fitting.

## Definitions

Let q be an odd prime power with q≡1 mod4. The Paley graph joins elements of F_q whose difference is a nonzero square. Its degree is d=(q−1)/2; the classical nontrivial adjacency eigenvalues are(−1±√q)/2, each with multiplicity d.

## Result name

First two Laplacian inverse moments

## Result statement

The positive Laplacian eigenvalues are(q±√q)/2, each with multiplicity d. Therefore ζ_L(1)=2 and ζ_L(2)=4(q+1)/(q(q−1)).

## Proof

Subtract the adjacency eigenvalues from d. Their positive Laplacian pair has sum q and product q(q−1)/4. The sum of reciprocals is4/(q−1); multiply by d to get two. The sum of reciprocal squares is(sum²−2product)/product²; simplifying and multiplying by d gives the second formula. The underlying adjacency spectrum is a classical quadratic-character/Gauss-sum theorem and is stated as such, rather than proved from these moments. The moments do not uniquely identify an arbitrary graph, since different spectra can share a few power sums.

## Worked example: setup

Use q=5.

## Worked example: calculation

The Paley graph is C₅. Its positive Laplacian values are(5±√5)/2, each twice. Hence ζ_L(1)=2 and ζ_L(2)=6/5. Kirchhoff’s matrix-tree formula gives five spanning trees, agreeing with the elementary fact that removing any one edge of a five-cycle produces a tree.

## Worked example: conclusion

The exact rational moments are explained by conjugate algebraic eigenvalues.

## Limits

The q≡1 mod4 condition ensures an undirected graph. These formulas are not a universal spectral signature or an arithmetic L-function theorem.

## Extension

Derive the adjacency spectrum from a specified additive-character convention and verify both moment and spanning-tree formulas independently.
