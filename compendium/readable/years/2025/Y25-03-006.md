## Result statement

The oscillatory series converges uniformly. Its tail afterN is at mostM N^(a−3)/(3−a). The denominator-series tail is at most(M/φ)N^(a−5)/(5−a), and reciprocal errors are bounded by that tail divided by the product of denominator lower bounds.

## Proof

Take absolute values termwise. The first majorant isMΣn^(a−4), convergent becausea−4<−1. The decreasing integral bound givesΣ_(n>N)n^(a−4)≤N^(a−3)/(3−a). The second follows in the same way with exponenta−6. IfB_N is the partial denominator, B_N⁻¹−B⁻¹=(B−B_N)/(B_NB), so lower bounds control inversion. These statements prove a limit; they do not establish modularity, duality, boundedness of unspecified operators or a fixed-point equation. Denominator nonvanishing is indispensable, because convergent denominator sums can still approach zero.
