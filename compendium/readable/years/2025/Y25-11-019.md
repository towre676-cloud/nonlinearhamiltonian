## Revised question retained in the annual source

When is an operator commuting with Xactually a scalar function of X, and what extra data defines a spectral triple?

## Status

Classical or newly chosen explicit model developed with proof; historical conjectures delimited

## Summary

The recovered assertion{X}′={f(X)}is false in the presence of spectral multiplicity. A finite counterexample and a two-point spectral metric show both the missing condition and the information needed beyond a list of eigenvalues.

## Definitions

For self-adjoint X, its commutant consists of all bounded operators Awith AX=XA.

On the two-point algebra C⊕C use π(a, b)=diag(a, b)and D=[[0, m],[m,0]], withm>0.

## Result name

Multiplicity and a two-point distance

## Result statement

For X=diag(0,0,1), its commutant is M₂(C)⊕C, whereasf(X)=diag(f(0)I₂, f(1)). In the two-point spectral triple, Connes’distance between the evaluation states is1/m.

## Proof

Commuting with a diagonal matrix forces A_ij=0whenever the two eigenvalues differ, but imposes no constraint within an equal-eigenvalue block. This gives the stated commutant. Scalar functional calculus assigns the same scalar to every vector in one eigenspace and therefore gives only the center of that commutant. For the two-point metric,[D, π(a, b)]=[[0, m(b−a)],[m(a−b),0]], whose norm ism|a−b|. The spectral distance is the supremum of|a−b|over self-adjoint(a, b)with commutator norm≤1, so it is exactly1/m. Finite dimensions make the resolvent compact automatically, and all commutators are bounded. This is a valid spectral-triple model, but its distance comes from Dand the represented algebra, not from an isolated spectral list.

## Worked example: calculation

The swap commutes with Xbut is notf(X), since its upper2×2block is not scalar. The two-point distance is1/2. Changingmchanges the distance while leaving the algebra C⊕Cunchanged, exhibiting the independent geometric role of D.

## Worked example: conclusion

The historical universal reduction fails already in three dimensions; a legitimate noncommutative-geometric model requires more structure.

## Limits

For a simple finite spectrum, commutant equality does hold by interpolation, but this special case cannot be assumed for arbitrary observables. No universe or QFT reconstruction follows.

## Extension

For an infinite self-adjoint operator, describe its direct-integral spectral multiplicity and identify exactly when the generated abelian von Neumann algebra is maximal abelian.
