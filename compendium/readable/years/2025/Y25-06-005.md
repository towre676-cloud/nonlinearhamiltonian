## Summary

The source imports a mean-field equation into an arithmetic narrative. The scalar equation itself has a classical exact analysis. Its threshold is a parameter valueλ=1; connecting that parameter to a Diophantine degree requires an additional justified mapλ(d).

## Definitions

Consider m∈(−1,1), λ≥0 and h∈R with m=tanh(λm+h). For h=0, symmetry permits m=0 and opposite pairs.

On a smooth solution branch define susceptibilityχ=∂m/∂h. Use the local expansion atanh m=m+m³/3+O(m⁵).

## Result name

A complete zero-field fixed-point classification

## Result statement

For0≤λ≤1 the only fixed point is zero. Forλ>1 there are exactly three:0 and±m_λ with0<m_λ<1. Nearλ=1⁺, m_λ∼√[3(λ−1)]. On the zero branch forλ<1,χ=(1−λ)^(−1).

## Proof

For m>0, the equation isλ=atanh(m)/m. This ratio tends to one at zero and infinity at one. Its derivative has numerator m/(1−m²)−atanh m, positive because atanh m=∫₀^m(1−t²)^(−1)dt<m/(1−m²). Hence the ratio is strictly increasing and crosses eachλ>1 exactly once. Negative solutions follow by symmetry. The Taylor expansion givesλ−1=m²/3+O(m⁴), proving the square-root behavior. Differentiating m=tanh(λm+h) givesχ=(1−m²)[λχ+1], soχ=(1−m²)/[1−λ(1−m²)] when the denominator is nonzero. Setting m=0 proves the stated susceptibility.

## Worked example: setup

## Worked example: conclusion
