## Summary

The source provides a piecewise ratio but not a justified physical interpretation. Its divisibility content is elementary and useful: prime-adic valuations determine exactly which branch applies and give a stable implementation without forming a gigantic factorial.

## Definitions

For positive integers n,k let g=gcd(k,n!). Defineρ(n,k)=n!/g if k divides n!, andρ(n,k)=g/k otherwise, following the retrieved branch convention.

For a prime p, v_p(n!)=Σ_{j≥1}floor(n/p^j), a finite sum. Factor k=∏p^{a_p}.

## Result name

Legendre valuations decide the branch exactly

## Result statement

g=∏_{p|k}p^{min(a_p,Σfloor(n/p^j))}; k divides n! exactly when each a_p is at most that factorial valuation. In the divisible branchρ=n!/k; otherwiseρ is the reciprocal of the positive integer k/g.

## Proof

Count each factor of p in1,…,n. Multiples of p contribute one, multiples of p² contribute an additional one, and so on, proving Legendre’s sum. The valuation of a gcd is the minimum of the two valuations, yielding the product formula. Divisibility is equivalent to the componentwise inequalities. If k|n!, then g=k and the first branch simplifies as stated. In the other branch k/g is an integer greater than one, soρ=g/k∈(0,1). The two branches therefore occupy distinct numerical ranges, with equality one possible at k=n! in the first branch. This is a chosen arithmetic encoding, not a continuous response law.
