## Revised question retained in the annual source

Can a Weyl algebra crossed with a reflection group have a real deformation parameter, contrary to a blanket rigidity claim?

## Status

Developed mathematical result with explicit hypotheses and checked example; historical claims repaired where necessary

## Summary

A rank-one Dunkl operator gives a concrete family satisfying a deformed commutator. The known Hochschild theorem for Weyl crossed products identifies the corresponding degree-two deformation direction.

## Definitions

OnC[x], letX multiply byx andsf(x)=f(−x). DefineD_κf=f′+κ( f(x)−f(−x))/(2x).

The quotient is polynomial because the odd part of a polynomial is divisible byx.

The Weyl algebra convention is[D₀,X]=I. The reflection anticommutes with bothX andD_κ.

## Result name

Exact deformed Weyl relation

## Result statement

[D_κ,X]=I+κs,s²=I,sX=−Xs,sD_κ=−D_κs. For the Weyl algebraA₁ crossed with{±I}, the standard Hochschild theorem givesHH²≅C, not zero.

## Proof

ApplyD_κ tomonomialxⁿ. The result is[n+(κ/2)(1−(−1)ⁿ)]x^{n−1}: even degrees have coefficientn and odd degrees coefficientn+κ. SubtractXD_κxⁿ fromD_κXxⁿ. For evenn the difference is1+κ, and for oddn it is1−κ, exactly(I+κs)xⁿ. Parity reversal of a derivative and of the odd-part quotient gives the anticommutation relations. These formulas prove a consistent represented deformation family. The cited general theorem computesHH^j(A(V)⋊G) by conjugacy classes withrank(g−I)=j for finite symplecticG; in dimension two the identity contributes degree zero and−I contributes degree two. That theorem is background, distinct from the polynomial certificate.

## Worked example: calculation

The outputs are3,2x,5x², whereas ordinary differentiation gives1,2x,3x². The commutator on1 is three and onx is−x, agreeing withI+2s. The parameter is not absent from the operator relations.

## Worked example: conclusion

This provides constructive deformation data rather than inferring rigidity from an unsupported zero cohomology assertion.

## Limits

The finite checks do not prove the full PBW or universal-deformation theorem. Hochschild rigidity also has no automatic implication for Berry curvature, which belongs to a specified parameterized state bundle.

## Extension

Establish the PBW basisx^a s^εD^b for the abstract algebra using a confluence proof, then classify which parameter values admit finite-dimensional quotients of the polynomial representation. A special value can change the polynomial representation. If κ is a negative odd integer, D_κ annihilates x^{−κ} as well as the constant. This follows immediately from the odd-degree coefficient n+κ and signals an additional singular vector. Such exceptional parameters should be recorded before asserting uniform representation-theoretic behavior.
