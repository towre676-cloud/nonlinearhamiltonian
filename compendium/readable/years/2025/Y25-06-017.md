## Revised question retained in the annual source

Reconstruction: What is the correct Euler equation for an L² reconstruction loss with H¹ regularization, and when is its minimizer unique?

## Status

Developed mathematical repair or classical result; speculative source claims delimited

## Summary

The source’s reconstruction and regularization terms are specific enough to motivate a rigorous linear inverse problem. Its update uses the adjoint of the forward operator, not the operator itself unless self-adjointness has been proved. Positivity then supplies a uniqueness certificate.

## Definitions

Let K:H→Y be a bounded linear map between real Hilbert spaces. In a finite-dimensional model define J(u)=||Ku−y||²/2+α||u||²/2 withα>0.

For a smooth field on a bounded domain, addβ∫|∇u|²/2 withβ≥0 and choose boundary conditions before integration by parts.

## Result name

A coercive normal equation and its gradient

## Result statement

The finite model has unique minimizer(K* K+αI)u=K*y. With an H¹ term its formal Euler equation is K*(Ku−y)+αu−βΔu=0, with the chosen natural or fixed boundary conditions.

## Proof

Differentiate J(u+εv) at zero to obtain⟨Ku−y,Kv⟩+α⟨u,v⟩=⟨K*(Ku−y)+αu,v⟩. Thus the derivative vanishes for every v exactly at the normal equation. The quadratic form of K*K+αI is||Kv||²+α||v||²≥α||v||², so the finite matrix is positive definite and invertible, proving existence and uniqueness. For the gradient term, integration by parts givesβ∫∇u·∇v=−β∫Δu v plus boundary flux; it vanishes for fixed variations or yields a Neumann condition in the natural problem. In infinite dimensions a corresponding coercive bilinear-form theorem supplies existence under stated domain assumptions, rather than a matrix inverse being assumed automatically.

## Worked example: setup

Take K=[[1,1],[0,1]], y=(1,0),α=1 andβ=0.

## Worked example: calculation

The normal matrix is[[2,1],[1,3]], its determinant is five, and K*y=(1,1). Therefore u=(2/5,1/5). The forward residual is(−2/5,1/5), and K*residual+u=(0,0). For any perturbation h, J(u+h)−J(u)=(||Kh||²+||h||²)/2≥||h||²/2, an exact global optimum certificate.

## Worked example: conclusion

The adjoint, coercivity constant and objective gap can all be verified independently of the proposed learning architecture.

## Limits

Optimizing activation parameters or enforcing nonlinear spectral constraints generally destroys this convexity. A CP tensor factorization has scaling ambiguities and is not unique merely because a reconstruction error is small.

## Extension

For a nonlinear forward map use its Fréchet derivative and adjoint in a Gauss–Newton step, then prove a local convergence region and a regularization-dependent error bound rather than extrapolating linear uniqueness globally.
