## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which matrix and convergence condition produce a claimed multiplier, and how does an input shock propagate through its network?

## Status

Developed mathematical result with explicit hypotheses and checked example; historical claims repaired where necessary

## Summary

A finite nonnegative input network has a convergent response series when its spectral radius is below one. A two-sector example yields an exact multiplier, showing why a universal scalar such as2.68 needs a declared model.

## Definitions

Letx=Ax+d, whereA has nonnegative entries andd is an external input. All coordinates use compatible units.

Assume spectral radiusρ(A)<1. Then(I−A)⁻¹=Σ_{n≥0}Aⁿ and the response to a shockδd isδx=(I−A)⁻¹δd.

A scalar multiplier also needs an aggregation rowwᵀ; it iswᵀδx divided by a stated input normalization.

## Result name

Exact two-sector propagation

## Result statement

ForA=[[0,1/4],[1/2,0]],(I−A)⁻¹=[[8/7,2/7],[4/7,8/7]]. A unit shock in sector one gives total output12/7; a unit shock in sector two gives10/7.

## Proof

A²=I/8, so even and odd powers form two geometric series andρ(A)=1/√8<1. Their sum is(I+A)/(1−1/8), giving the inverse directly. Multiplication byI−A independently verifies the identity. The two different column sums demonstrate that even one fixed network need not have a single shock-independent scalar multiplier. For a general finite matrix the Neumann series follows by Jordan form or an equivalent norm in which high powers decay; the spectral-radius hypothesis is essential to that series interpretation.

## Worked example: setup

Useδd=(1,0) and compare the first three response rounds.

## Worked example: calculation

The direct response is(1,0), the first spillover(0,1/2), and the next(1/8,0). Summing all rounds gives(8/7,4/7). Counting the direct input once and then adding the full inverse response again would double count the zeroth round.

## Worked example: conclusion

The mathematical multiplier is computable, directional and conditional on a network that must be independently justified.

## Limits

This is a linear propagation model, not evidence for any particular sectoral multiplier or monetary return. An inverse may exist even whenρ(A)≥1, but its Neumann-series and nonnegative-round interpretation can then fail.
