## Revised question retained in the annual source

Reconstruction: How do Fourier flux, series and parallel composites differ, and why do a filler fraction and two conductivities not determine one effective conductivity?

## Status

Developed mathematical repair or classical result; speculative source claims delimited

## Summary

Practical vector calculus is enough to expose the missing geometry. Parallel layers and series layers made from identical fractions have different exact effective conductivities. An interface resistance adds another independent parameter, so neither a nominal filler percentage nor a single multiplication validates the reported heat flux.

## Definitions

Use Fourier’s law q=−κ∇T withκ>0. On a stationary one-dimensional layered sample, constant flux and temperature drops determine its effective conductivity.

Let fractions f and1−f have conductivitiesκ₂ andκ₁. Parallel paths share a gradient; series layers share a flux. An interface resistance R_c≥0 gives temperature jump R_cq″.

## Result name

Two exact effective-conductivity formulas and a conservation identity

## Result statement

Parallel geometry givesκ_parallel=(1−f)κ₁+fκ₂; series geometry givesκ_series=[(1−f)/κ₁+f/κ₂]^(−1). For total thicknessℓ, contact resistance changes the series value toκ_eff=ℓ/[ℓ₁/κ₁+ℓ₂/κ₂+R_c]. Heat conservation isρc_pT_t=−div q+s.

## Proof

In parallel, sum area-weighted fluxes−κ_i∇T; the common gradient factors out. In series, each layer drops q″ℓ_i/κ_i, so the total drop is q″Σℓ_i/κ_i. Defining q″=κ_effΔT/ℓ yields the harmonic formula and the contact version after adding the jump. The arithmetic value exceeds the harmonic one by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality applied to positive weighted conductivities and reciprocals. Conservation follows by equating the rate of stored energy in any control volume with incoming boundary flux plus sources, then applying the divergence theorem. These arguments require no Hodge decomposition or unvalidated learned predictor.

## Worked example: conclusion

The same fractions support radically different exact values depending on transport geometry, so a reported κ_eff needs a declared model and data.

## Limits

The illustrativeκ₂ is not a material measurement. Arbitrary microstructures need a homogenization theorem or experiment; anisotropy requires a positive-definite conductivity tensor. Numerical metrics such as96.8% prediction accuracy or±2.8% variation are not validated here.
