## Summary

The notationM(a,b,c) was not defined in the recovered source. A precise conditional interpretation isf=x^a+y^b+z^c witha,b,c≥2. Its Jacobian algebra gives a directly countable Milnor number and distinguishes the singularity fiber from its three-dimensional link.

## Definitions

For an isolated polynomial critical point, the Jacobian algebra isQ_f=C[x,y,z]/(f_x,f_y,f_z); its finite dimension is the Milnor number.

The Milnor fiberf=ε in a small ball has complex dimension2; the linkf=0 intersected with a small5-sphere has real dimension3.

## Result name

Exact monomial basis

## Result statement

Forf=x^a+y^b+z^c, the monomialsx^iy^jz^k with0≤i≤a−2,0≤j≤b−2,0≤k≤c−2 form a basis, givingμ=(a−1)(b−1)(c−1).

## Proof

The partial derivatives are nonzero scalar multiples ofx^(a−1),y^(b−1),z^(c−1), so their ideal is generated by those three pure powers. Every monomial with an exponent beyond a bound vanishes in the quotient; the remaining monomials are linearly independent because a monomial ideal contains no nonzero linear combination supported on standard monomials. Counting the independent exponent choices proves the formula. The critical point is isolated because all three derivatives vanish simultaneously only at0. Identifying this algebraic number with the bouquet-of-spheres count of the Milnor fiber is Milnor’s structural theorem, not a new consequence of the count.

## Worked example: calculation

The first basis has4·11·1=44 elements. The second has40·839·1=33560. The Milnor fiber hasb2=μ and reduced homology in that middle degree under the classical theorem; it is not a closed3-manifold withb3=1.

## Worked example: conclusion

The recoveredμ=44 is correct in this explicit interpretation, while the topology and dimension of the associated object must be kept separate.
