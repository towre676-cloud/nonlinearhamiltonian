## Revised question retained in the annual source

Reconstruction: How does scalar approximation transfer to a symmetric matrix, and what local assumptions actually prove quadratic Newton convergence?

## Status

Developed mathematical repair or classical result; speculative source claims delimited

## Summary

Rational approximation and nonlinear iteration were named in the adaptive framework, but the retrieved exponential approximation bound is truncated. Two modest exact statements replace that missing certificate: a spectral maximum bound and a local Newton remainder estimate. Neither relies on an unrecorded Zolotarev constant.

## Definitions

Let A be real symmetric with spectrum in [a,b]. Let r be a rational function with no pole on that interval and f continuous there.

For Newton’s method let F be C² near a root α, assume |F′|≥m>0 and |F″|≤M on an interval containing α and the current iterate, and assume the next iterates remain in that interval.

## Result name

Spectral error transfer and a local quadratic recurrence

## Result statement

||f(A)−r(A)||₂=max_{λ∈spectrum(A)}|f(λ)−r(λ)|≤sup_[a,b]|f−r|. Newton’s update x⁺=x−F(x)/F′(x) satisfies |x⁺−α|≤[M/(2m)]|x−α|² under the stated local bounds.

## Proof

An orthogonal diagonalization A=Udiag(λ_i)Uᵀ makes f(A)−r(A) orthogonally similar to the diagonal matrix of scalar errors; its Euclidean operator norm is the largest absolute diagonal entry. The pole exclusion makes r(A) well defined. For Newton, Taylor-expand F(α)=F(x)+F′(x)(α−x)+F″(ξ)(α−x)²/2 for some intermediate ξ. Rearranging and using F(α)=0 gives x⁺−α=F″(ξ)(x−α)²/[2F′(x)], up to an irrelevant sign, proving the absolute bound. Remaining inside the interval is a real hypothesis: a local error estimate is not global convergence from every initial point.

## Worked example: setup

Use A=diag(1,2), f(λ)=1/λ and r(λ)=3/2−λ/2. Separately use F(x)=x²−2 and x₀=3/2.

## Worked example: calculation

The polynomial r is exact at both eigenvalues, so r(A)=A^(−1), although it is not exact throughout [1,2]. Newton gives x₁=17/12 and x₂=577/408. The exact residuals are x₁²−2=1/144 and x₂²−2=1/166464. These are also Pell-type rational certificates, computed without floating-point cancellation.

## Worked example: conclusion

A finite spectrum can make a simple approximation exact, while iteration convergence requires a separate nonlinear argument.

## Limits

No unrecovered uniform rational-approximation rate, prime-grid optimization complexity or arbitrary-start Newton guarantee is asserted. A residual-to-error bound additionally needs a derivative or inverse-conditioning estimate.

## Extension

For a prescribed positive interval [a,b], optimize a pole-free rational approximation and certify its scalar uniform error. That certificate then transfers immediately to every symmetric matrix with spectrum in the interval, independently of dimension.
