## Revised question retained in the annual source

Why is M=Π+R inconsistent with a Perron eigenvalueφ when Πr=r and Rr=0?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The August10 source simultaneously claimed λ≈1.618,Π=rℓᵀ,R=M−Π,Rr=0 and M^k→Π. These assertions cannot all hold. The correct decomposition is elementary and makes the normalization error decisive.

## Definitions

Let Mr=λr,ℓᵀM=λℓᵀ,ℓᵀr=1 and Π=rℓᵀ. Assume λ is simple and all other eigenvalues have modulus belowλ>0.

## Result name

Correct spectral projection decomposition

## Result statement

With R=M−λΠ, one has Π²=Π,ΠR=RΠ=0 and M^k=λ^kΠ+R^k for k≥1. Therefore(M/λ)^k→Π whenρ(R)<λ. Defining R=M−Π instead gives Rr=(λ−1)r.

## Proof

The normalization gives Π²=r(ℓᵀr)ℓᵀ=Π and MΠ=ΠM=λΠ. SubtractingλΠ yields the zero cross-products, so binomial expansion has only the two pure terms. Powers of R/λ tend to zero in finite dimension when its spectral radius is below one, including possible polynomial Jordan factors. The erroneous definition subtracts only r from Mr, leaving(λ−1)r, which directly contradicts the claimed annihilation unlessλ=1. Also a number1−ρ(R) is not a universal transient norm budget for a nonnormal matrix.

## Worked example: setup

Use the symmetric Fibonacci matrix M=[[1,1],[1,0]].

## Worked example: calculation

Its eigenvalues areφ and−1/φ. The orthogonal PF projection isΠ=(1/(φ²+1))[[φ²,φ],[φ,1]]. Here R=−φ⁻¹(I−Π), and normalized errors decay exactly asφ^(−2k). Unnormalized M^k grows along r rather than converging.

## Worked example: conclusion

A single exact eigenvector calculation exposes the historical inconsistency and supplies the corrected convergence theorem.

## Limits

General nonsymmetric matrices may have large transient constants. Spectral radius alone does not certify a numerical solver or perturbation margin.

## Extension

Add a proved norm or resolvent bound and distinguish asymptotic rate from finite-step amplification before claiming a stability budget.
