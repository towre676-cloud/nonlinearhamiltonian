## Revised question retained in the annual source

What computational saving and approximation error follow from a genuine rank-dfactorization?

## Status

## Summary

The recovered transformer discussion estimated enormous projection speedups from a small retained dimension. A rank factorization gives an arithmetic-count reduction for a specified linear map; whole-network speed, accuracy and energy claims require independent evidence.

## Definitions

Let W∈R^(n×n)have singular values σ₁≥…≥σ_n, and let W_dbe its truncated SVD.

A rank-dfactorization W_d=UV^Tusesn×dmatrices U, V.

## Result name

Optimal spectral residual and a product error bound

## Result statement

||W−W_d||₂=σ_(d+1). Applying UV^Tto a vector costs about2ndmultiply-adds instead ofn². For products with||A_j||,||B_j||≤Mand||A_j−B_j||≤ε, the difference of L-layerlinear products is at most L ε M^(L−1).

## Proof

Singular-value diagonalization shows the residual norm is σ_(d+1). Optimality follows because anyrank-dapproximation has a nonzero kernel vector within the span of the firstd+1right singular vectors; on that vector Wacts with norm at least σ_(d+1). The two matrix-vector multiplies V^Txand Uyuse2ndoperations to leading order. For products, insert one factor difference at a time:Π A−Π B=Σ A_L…A_(j+1)(A_j−B_j)B_(j−1)…B₁. Submultiplicativity bounds each term by ε M^(L−1), and summing proves the estimate. Thus small per-layer error can accumulate with depth and large operator norms.
