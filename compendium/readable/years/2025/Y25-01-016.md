## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which degree shift makes a local system perverse on a smooth complex curve, and what changes for a point-supported object?

## Status

## Summary

Middle perversity is a pair of support conditions, not a freely adjustable label. On a smooth complex curve a local system shifted by one is perverse, while a skyscraper is perverse in degree zero.

## Definitions

## Result name

Curve and point perverse objects

## Result statement

A finite-rank local systemL[1] is perverse onC. A skyscraperk_p is perverse. The unshifted nonzero local systemL is not perverse.

## Proof

The only nonzero ordinary cohomology sheaf ofL[1] lies in degree−1 and has one-dimensional support, exactly satisfying1≤−(−1). Its Verdier dual isL^∨[1], which satisfies the same inequality. A skyscraper has degree-zero cohomology supported in dimension zero and is self-dual up to vector-space dual, so both inequalities hold. The unshiftedL instead has degree-zero support of dimension one, violating1≤0. This computation checks both halves of the definition and demonstrates why ordinary cohomological degree and perverse degree differ. Direct sums of these two types remain perverse because support dimensions take maxima and duality distributes over finite sums.

## Worked example: calculation

H⁻¹(K)=k_C andH⁰(K)=k_p, with support dimensions one and zero. Both are allowed simultaneously. Moving the skyscraper to degree one would give a nonzero degree-one sheaf with support dimension zero, violating0≤−1. Thus even a tiny complex has a sharply constrained perverse grading.
