## Revised question retained in the annual source

Reconstruction: Which measure and convergence region make the proposed p-adic integral meaningful, and what exact rational function results for |x|_p^s?

## Status

## Summary

The source names an integral over p-adic integer points, but an integrand, measure and convergence claim still have to be specified. The monomial case supplies a complete local-zeta calculation: its rationality comes from a geometric series rather than from a speculative decoding interpretation.

## Definitions

Fix a prime p and normalize Haar measure μ(Z_p)=1. Then μ(p^nZ_p)=p^(−n), and |x|_p=p^(−v_p(x)) for x≠0.

For complex s define |x|_p^s=exp(s log|x|_p). The singleton zero has measure zero and may be assigned any value when the integral is finite.

## Result name

An exact local zeta integral

## Result statement

For Re(s)>−1, Z_p(s)=∫_{Z_p}|x|_p^s dμ=(1−p^(−1))/(1−p^(−s−1)). The integral is absolutely convergent exactly in this half-plane; the displayed rational-in-p^(−s) expression also supplies a meromorphic continuation.

## Proof

Partition nonzero Z_p into shells A_n=p^nZ_p∖p^(n+1)Z_p, n≥0. Their measures are (1−p^(−1))p^(−n), and the integrand is constant p^(−ns) on each shell. Thus Z_p(s)=(1−p^(−1))Σ_{n≥0}p^(−n(s+1)). Absolute convergence is equivalent to |p^(−s−1)|<1, or Re(s)>−1, and summation gives the formula. If Re(s)≤−1, the positive integral of the absolute value diverges by the same series. Analytic continuation is a statement about the resulting function; it does not make the original divergent integral convergent outside its initial half-plane.

## Worked example: setup

Choose p=3 and s=2, then compare shell integration with a finite residue approximation.

## Worked example: calculation

The exact answer is (2/3)/(1−1/27)=9/13. The first N shells sum to (9/13)(1−27^(−N)), with omitted tail (9/13)27^(−N). In a residue calculation modulo 3^N, each congruence class has measure 3^(−N); valuations below N reproduce those shell counts exactly. The unresolved zero class contributes a bounded tail rather than an arbitrary replacement value.

## Worked example: conclusion

The calculation includes its normalization, domain and an exact truncation error, making it suitable for a reproducible arithmetic implementation.

## Limits

A general variety X(Z_p), singular polynomial or oscillatory integrand needs additional geometry and convergence hypotheses. This one-variable proof is not a proof of the full rationality theory or of a physical detector.

## Extension

For a polynomial f, count solutions f(x)≡0 modulo p^N and relate those counts to the measure of valuation sublevel sets. A proved resolution or cell decomposition can then replace the elementary shell structure used here.
