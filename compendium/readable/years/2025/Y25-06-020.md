## Summary

The source’s assistant called a transportation analysis a mathematical proof and treated correlations as causal certainty. A structural counterexample establishes the missing inference exactly. Discounted scenario sums can also be computed, but their inputs remain assumptions rather than proved consequences of correlation.

## Definitions

Let U,V be independent mean-zero, variance-one variables. Define X=U and Y=−U+σV. An intervention do(X=x) replaces only the equation for X.

For a stipulated constant annual quantity b, discount r>0, horizon N and initial quantity C in compatible units, define V_N=Σ_{t=1}^N b/(1+r)^t−C.

## Result name

An exact confounding example and a geometric-sum formula

## Result statement

Corr(X,Y)=−1/√(1+σ²), yet do(X=x) leaves the distribution of Y unchanged. Choosingσ²=(1000/973)²−1 gives correlation−.973 exactly. Also V_N=b[1−(1+r)^(−N)]/r−C.

## Proof

Cov(X,Y)=−Var(U)=−1, Var(X)=1 and Var(Y)=1+σ², proving the correlation. The equation for Y contains U,V but no X, so intervening on X does not change it. Observational association can therefore be arbitrarily close to−1 with zero causal effect. For the sum, set a=(1+r)^(−1); Σ_{t=1}^Na^t=a(1−a^N)/(1−a)=[1−(1+r)^(−N)]/r. Multiplication by b and subtraction of C gives the expression. Its precision cannot validate b or the assumed intervention effect.

## Worked example: setup

Use N=30,r=1/20,b=1,C=10 as a new normalized scenario.

## Worked example: calculation

The finite geometric sum can be checked exactly as a rational number, then compared with its closed form. If a forecasted benefit b were actually zero under the confounded intervention model, the result would instead be−10. The verifier checks the correlation variance ratio and both sum forms.

## Worked example: conclusion

A causal model and an arithmetic projection are separate objects; a precise projection can still rest on an unjustified causal input.
