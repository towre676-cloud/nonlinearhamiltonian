## Revised question retained in the annual source

What exact algebraic example gives meaning to a prime-dependent chromatic label?

## Summary

The recovered source namesTMF and transchromatic structures but supplies no spectrum or formal group. The multiplicative formal group gives a complete algebraic calculation ofheight1 at every prime. It distinguishes a real invariant from assigning a prime to a framework label.

## Definitions

A one-dimensional formal group lawF(x,y) is an associative commutative power series withidentity0. Its[p]_F(x) addsx to itselfp times.

Over a field of characteristicp, finite heighth means the first nonzero term of[p]_F has degreep^h; if[p]_F=0 the height is infinite.

## Result name

Exact additive and multiplicative heights

## Result statement

ForF_m(x,y)=x+y+xy, [p](x)=(1+x)^p−1, reducing tox^p in characteristicp, so its height is1. The additive lawF_a=x+y has[p]=px=0 in characteristicp and infinite height.

## Proof

The identity1+F_m(x,y)=(1+x)(1+y) proves associativity, commutativity and the p-fold formula by ordinary multiplication. The inverse is−x/(1+x), a formal power series with zero constant term. In characteristicp, the binomial theorem removes all intermediate coefficients becausep divides binomial(p,j) for0<j<p, leavingx^p. The additive calculation is immediate. Thus two simple formal groups over the same characteristic have different heights. An invertible coordinate changeg(x)=ux+O(x²),u≠0, conjugates the p-series and preserves the lowest nonzero exponent, so this distinction is intrinsic to the formal-group isomorphism class.
