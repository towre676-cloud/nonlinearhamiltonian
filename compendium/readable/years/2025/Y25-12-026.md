## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which clearly specified deflation process has a complete finite-rank convergence theorem?

## Summary

The recovered iteration chose a top eigenspace of an accumulated covariance, which is different from simply removing the current largest eigenvalues. A precise current-covariance model has an exact theorem and clarifies why a nonzero residual or a fixed limiting subspace cannot be assumed.

## Result statement

After nsteps, Σ_n=Σ_(j>np)λ_je_j⊗e_jand||Σ_n||₁=Σ_(j>np)λ_j→0. In finite dimension the process terminates after at mostceiling(d/p)steps.

## Proof

The projection is diagonal in the same eigenbasis as Σ_nand removes exactly the selectedpterms. Induction gives the displayed expansion. For a positive trace-classoperator, the trace norm equals the sum of its nonnegative eigenvalues, so thetailformula follows. Summability of Σλ_jmakes that tailtend tozero. This proves a stronger convergence than strong-operator convergence. If the operator is merely compact, the largest remaining eigenvalue tends tozeroand operator-norm convergence still follows, but trace-norm statements need not make sense. Selecting the top eigenspace of an accumulatedsumcan repeatedly select already removed modes or requiretie rules; the samebatch formula cannot be copied to that different algorithm.

## Worked example: setup

Take Σ₀=diag(1,1/2,1/4,1/8,…), p=1.

## Worked example: calculation

After nsteps, the trace norm is Σ_(j>n)2^(1−j)=2^(1−n). Every residual direction is eventually removed, and the limit is zero. The selectedrank-oneprojectorsmove through the basis rather than converging in operator normto a fixedrank-oneprojector; their pairwise norm distance is one.

## Worked example: conclusion

The revised model has exact convergence and shows which extra claims about a limiting subspace need separate proofs.

## Limits
