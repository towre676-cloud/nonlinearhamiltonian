## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which operator domain makes an eigenvalue condition a theorem, and how does changing that domain change the spectrum?

## Status

Developed mathematical result with explicit hypotheses and checked example; historical claims repaired where necessary

## Summary

A differential expression alone does not specify an operator. The same second derivative has different spectra under Dirichlet and periodic domains, illustrating the domain gap in a proposed zeta Hamiltonian without pretending to resolve it.

## Definitions

OnL²(0,π), use the expression−u″. The Dirichlet domain isH²∩H₀¹; the periodic domain requiresu(0)=u(π),u′(0)=u′(π).

An eigenpair consists of a nonzero vector in the chosen domain satisfyingAu=λu. Self-adjointness includes equality with the adjoint domain, not merely a symmetric formal expression.

## Result name

Two domains, two exact spectra

## Result statement

The Dirichlet eigenvalues aren² forn≥1, with eigenfunctionssin(nx). The periodic eigenvalues are4n² forn∈Z, including zero, with eigenfunctionse^{2inx}.

## Proof

Integration by parts gives〈u,−v″〉−〈−u″,v〉=[u′ conjugate(v)−u conjugate(v′)]₀^π, with the precise conjugation placement adjusted to the inner-product convention. Either stated domain kills this boundary form. Solving−u″=λu gives trigonometric solutions forλ>0; Dirichlet endpoints force√λ to be a positive integer. Periodicity forces e^{i√λπ}=1, hence√λ=2|n|. The constant solves the periodic zero eigenvalue but is excluded by Dirichlet endpoints. Completeness follows from the classical sine and Fourier bases. These regular self-adjoint realizations demonstrate how domain information enters both the eigenvalue equation and its spectral interpretation.

## Worked example: calculation

Dirichlet has lowest eigenvalue one and no constant mode. Periodic has zero followed by four, with the nonzero frequencies occurring in sine/cosine pairs. The same expression therefore gives a different gap and multiplicity structure. A solution of a formal equation that violates an endpoint condition is not an eigenvector of the specified realization.

## Limits

The Bender–Brody–Müller proposal is a specific research construction, not a generic synonym for pseudo-Hermitian matrices. Its primary paper explicitly makes the self-adjointness argument conditional. This example proves neither that construction nor the Riemann hypothesis.

## Extension

For a proposed nonlocal Hamiltonian, state a dense invariant domain, prove closability and characterize the adjoint. Then prove that its boundary map both produces the desired zeros and excludes unwanted spectral points; these are separate implications.
