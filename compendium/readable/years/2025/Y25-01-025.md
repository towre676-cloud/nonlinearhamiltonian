## Revised question retained in the annual source

When does doubling produce6.02dB, and how do coherent amplitudes differ from independent power additions?

## Status

Developed mathematical result with explicit hypotheses and checked example; historical claims repaired where necessary

## Summary

The same physical ratio has different numerical logarithmic factors depending on whether one starts with power or amplitude. Coherent addition and independent addition are different hypotheses, with exact phase-dependent formulas.

## Definitions

For positive powers defineL=10log₁₀(P/P₀). IfP=cA² with fixed positivec, the same level difference is20log₁₀(A/A₀).

Two equal complex amplitudesA andAe^{iφ} sum coherently. Independent zero-mean signals with no cross covariance add their powers instead.

All logarithm arguments must be dimensionless ratios with the same reference convention.

## Result name

Exact doubling and interference laws

## Result statement

Doubling amplitude at fixed conversionc quadruples power and adds20log₁₀2≈6.02060dB. Doubling power adds10log₁₀2≈3.01030dB. Equal coherent amplitudes giveP_total/P_single=2+2cosφ.

## Proof

The amplitude-to-power law givesP₂/P₁=(A₂/A₁)², so ten times the logarithm of power becomes twenty times the amplitude logarithm. For coherent addition, |A+Ae^{iφ}|²=|A|²(2+e^{iφ}+e^{−iφ})=2|A|²(1+cosφ). Atφ=0 power is four times one signal; atφ=π it is zero. For independent or uncorrelated zero-mean signals, expectation of the cross term vanishes, leaving twice the single-signal power and the3.01dB result. These calculations identify exactly which statistical or phase assumption changes the reported gain.

## Worked example: calculation

The power ratios are four,two and zero. The first gives6.02060dB, the second3.01030dB, and exact cancellation has no finite logarithmic level relative to positiveP₀. Rounding6.02 does not convert an amplitude statement into a universal gain for two devices or two noisy sources.

## Limits

Changing impedance or another amplitude-to-power conversion factor changes the simple20log formula. The result does not validate any proposed phonon, curvature or material-protection mechanism.

## Extension

For unequal amplitudesA,B, deriveP=A²+B²+2ABcosφ and propagate a bounded phase error through it. The derivative−2ABsinφ shows where a phase-sensitive operating point is locally robust and where it is sensitive. Near destructive interference write φ=π+δ. Then P_total=2A²(1−cosδ)≤A²δ². The inequality 1−cosδ≤δ²/2 gives a global quadratic upper bound on leakage from a phase error, a stronger and more useful robustness statement than merely reporting the zero at perfect cancellation.
