## Revised question retained in the annual source

Reconstruction: What does a tensor-complex implementation need to preserve, and what higher class is lost by multiplying only degree-zero ranks?

## Status

Developed mathematical repair or classical result; speculative source claims delimited

## Summary

The source itself notes missing Künneth summands and signs. A two-term integer complex gives a compact counterexample to naive rank multiplication and a direct certificate for the required sign. This is a test of homological algebra rather than a claimed universal motivic solver.

## Definitions

Let C have Z in homological degrees one and zero, with differential multiplication by two. Thus H₀(C)=Z/2 and H₁(C)=0.

The tensor differential is d(x⊗y)=dx⊗y+(−1)^deg(x)x⊗dy. The classical Künneth framework uses derived tensor and Tor when coefficients are not flat.

## Result name

The tensor has an additional degree-one Z/2

## Result statement

For D=C⊗C, the chain groups are Z,Z²,Z in degrees two,one,zero, with d₂=(-2,2)ᵀ and d₁=(2,2). Hence H₀(D)=Z/2,H₁(D)=Z/2,H₂(D)=0.

## Proof

Choose the degree-one basis(e₁⊗e₀,e₀⊗e₁). The graded sign gives d(e₁⊗e₁)=2e₀⊗e₁−2e₁⊗e₀, the stated d₂. Each degree-one generator maps to2e₀⊗e₀, giving d₁. Their composition is−4+4=0. The kernel of d₁ is generated by(1,−1), while image(d₂) is twice that generator, so H₁ is Z/2. d₂ is injective, giving H₂=0, and image(d₁)=2Z gives H₀=Z/2. Omitting the sign would make d₁d₂=8 instead of zero. Multiplying only H₀ groups misses the degree-one Tor₁^Z(Z/2,Z/2)=Z/2 contribution.

## Worked example: setup

Repeat the calculation with differential multiplication by three.

## Worked example: calculation

The same argument gives H₀=Z/3,H₁=Z/3,H₂=0. The verifier checks matrix compositions and kernel/image indices for both examples exactly. Over a field the corresponding torsion obstruction disappears because vector spaces are flat; that is an extra coefficient hypothesis, not a reason to omit Tor over Z.

## Worked example: conclusion

A small torsion test catches both a broken differential and a missing Künneth summand.

## Limits

This example does not assert that every motivic Künneth spectral sequence collapses or that an arbitrary product motive is determined by a rank table. Its domain is an explicit tensor of free complexes.

## Extension

Use Smith normal forms and chain maps to certify integral homology and tensor products. A motivic layer should call that verified algebra only after providing its genuine complexes and comparison maps.
