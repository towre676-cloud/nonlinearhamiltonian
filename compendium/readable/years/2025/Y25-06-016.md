## Revised question retained in the annual source

Reconstruction: Which rank, composition and conservative-field properties follow from F(x)=a f(b·x), and how does this differ from a Clifford product?

## Status

Developed mathematical repair or classical result; speculative source claims delimited

## Summary

The g-dyad proposal gives an explicit nonlinear action. Its derivative and compositions can be developed without claiming that arbitrary nonlinear or hysteretic behavior is a tensor product. A conservative-field test also determines when the action can come from a scalar variational energy.

## Definitions

Let a,b∈R^d be fixed nonzero vectors and f:R→R be C¹. Define F(x)=a f(bᵀx). For composition let G(x)=c g(dᵀx).

A linear dyad abᵀ is a matrix. The geometric product uv=u·v+u∧v instead belongs to a specified Clifford algebra; it is a different algebraic construction.

## Result name

Derivative, composition and a potential criterion

## Result statement

DF(x)=f′(bᵀx)abᵀ has rank at most one. F∘G(x)=a f((bᵀc)g(dᵀx)). If f′ is nonzero somewhere, F is a gradient field on R^d exactly when a is parallel to b.

## Proof

Apply the chain rule componentwise to obtain the Jacobian and its rank bound. Substitution gives the composition, which generally leaves the original activation family unless it is closed under this scalar nesting. If F=∇V with V C², its Jacobian is symmetric, so abᵀ=baᵀ wherever f′≠0. For nonzero a,b this equality implies linear dependence: apply both sides to b to get a||b||²=b(a·b). Conversely if a=κb and F₀′=f, then V(x)=κF₀(bᵀx) has gradientκbf(bᵀx)=F(x). Thus a variational energy exists under an explicit alignment condition, not for every chosen dyad. For f(s)=s the composition reduces to the familiar rank-one matrix identity(abᵀ)(cdᵀ)=(bᵀc)adᵀ.

## Worked example: setup

Choose a=(1,0),b=(0,1),f(s)=s².

## Worked example: calculation

F(x,y)=(y²,0), with Jacobian[[0,2y],[0,0]]. At y≠0 it is nonsymmetric, so it cannot be the gradient of a C² potential. Choosing instead a=b=(1,0) gives F=(x²,0)=∇(x³/3). The verifier checks the derivative matrices, rank and linear composition identity exactly.

## Worked example: conclusion

The nonlinear action has useful precise structure, while bilinearity, energy and algebraic product claims need separate hypotheses.

## Limits

Hysteresis is not a single-valued memoryless f unless an internal state is added. A nonlocal kernel requires its own function spaces. This example does not prove a nonlinear Koopman closure or equivalence with geometric algebra.

## Extension

Define a finite sum of dyads, characterize symmetry of its full Jacobian, and derive a controlled low-rank approximation error. For state-dependent or hysteretic activations, enlarge the state space before analyzing spectra.
