## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which exact incidence identity proves the graph spectrum and the displayed Laplacian zeta function?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The August23 testbeds named Ramanujan graphs, graph zeta functions and primitive geodesics. A finite incidence construction gives a complete spectral certificate. It distinguishes the ordinary Laplacian zeta from Ihara’s nonbacktracking zeta.

## Definitions

Let B be the7×7 point-line incidence matrix of the Fano plane: each point lies on three lines and each distinct pair shares one line. Let A=[[0,B],[Bᵀ,0]] and L=3I−A. Remove the zero Laplacian eigenvalue from ζ_L(s).

## Result name

Exact Heawood spectrum and zeta

## Result statement

A has eigenvalues3,−3, and±√2 each with multiplicity six. Thus ζ_L(s)=6(3−√2)^(−s)+6(3+√2)^(−s)+6^(−s).

## Proof

The incidence property gives BBᵀ=2I+J. On the constant line this has eigenvalue9, while the six-dimensional orthogonal complement has eigenvalue2. The symmetric block adjacency matrix therefore has the positive and negative singular values3 and√2 with the stated multiplicities. Subtracting from3 gives Laplacian eigenvalues0,6,3±√2. Their power sum proves the zeta formula. The nontrivial adjacency values satisfy the cubic Ramanujan bound2√(3−1), but that does not identify graph zeta zeros with arithmetic L-function zeros. Ihara’s determinant formula uses det(I−uA+2u²I) and a separate factor(1−u²)^(21−14), not the Laplacian inverse-power sum.

## Worked example: calculation

The conjugate reciprocal sum is1/(3−√2)+1/(3+√2)=6/7, so ζ_L(1)=36/7+1/6=223/42. The graph has14 vertices and21 edges; its girth-six property is separately checked from its incidence structure.

## Worked example: conclusion

A complete finite operator identity supports the exact graph claims and keeps the two zeta constructions distinct.

## Limits

This does not prove a number-field Riemann hypothesis, spectral universality or a statistical law for arbitrary graphs.
