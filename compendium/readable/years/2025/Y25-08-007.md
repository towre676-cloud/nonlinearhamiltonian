## Revised question retained in the annual source

What hypotheses justify the cubic multiplier equations, and what can a stationary solution actually prove?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The August9 maximality solver repeatedly produced KKT roots and square-root formulas. Their algebra is a necessary first-order condition under regularity, not an automatic global certificate. A one-dimensional cubic constraint makes both sign branches and missing optima explicit.

## Definitions

Consider f(x)=cᵀx and equality constraints g_i(x)=Σ_jA_ijx_j³−b_i=0. With L=f+Σλ_ig_i, set S_j=Σ_iλ_iA_ij. Assume the constraint gradients are independent at the candidate.

## Result name

First-order stationarity and its scope

## Result statement

A regular constrained extremum satisfies c_j+3x_j²S_j=0. If S_j≠0, real solutions require−c_j/(3S_j)≥0 and generally have both signs. Stationarity alone implies neither existence nor global optimality.

## Proof

The multiplier theorem puts∇f in the span of the constraint gradients, yielding the displayed equations. Solving for x_j² gives the square-root condition; taking only the positive root excludes legitimate candidates unless x_j≥0 was separately imposed. If S_j=0, the equation requires c_j=0 and cannot be divided by S_j. Even all valid stationary points need not cover singular optima. Finally a continuous objective reaches a maximum on a nonempty compact feasible set, but arbitrary affine algebraic sets need not be compact; a cubic parametrization does not supply boundedness.

## Worked example: setup

Minimize or maximize x subject to x³=−1.

## Worked example: calculation

The only feasible point is x=−1. Its gradient constraint is3 and stationarity uses λ=−1/3. The positive-root-only expression would produce+1 and miss the feasible point. Separately f(x)=x on the unconstrained real line has no maximum at all.

## Worked example: conclusion

A solver needs feasibility, regularity/singular-case handling and a global comparison certificate after computing multipliers.

## Limits

Random surrogate restriction matrices and reported H¹ dimensions do not prove that a KKT optimum solves a geometric problem. The source matrices were not recovered.

## Extension

Restrict to a proved compact semialgebraic domain and certify candidate completeness and objective bounds with exact elimination or interval methods.
