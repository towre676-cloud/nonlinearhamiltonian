## Revised question retained in the annual source

Can the proposed ribbon operators satisfy[s,θ]=iℏI in a finite prototype, and what finite relation is actually possible?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The assistant promoted formal commutators into legitimate structures without an operator domain. A trace argument gives an immediate obstruction for finite matrices. Clock and shift matrices provide a precise alternative, with an explicit deformation phase rather than a false finite canonical pair.

## Definitions

For N×N matrices define[A,B]=AB−BA. Fix ℏ≠0. On C^N let ω=e^(2πi/N), Xe_j=e_{j+1 mod N} and Ze_j=ω^je_j.

## Result name

Trace obstruction and the finite Weyl pair

## Result statement

No finite matrices satisfy[A,B]=iℏI. The clock and shift instead satisfy ZX=ωXZ, X^N=Z^N=I, and are unitary.

## Proof

Cyclicity gives tr(AB−BA)=0, while tr(iℏI)=iℏN≠0, a contradiction. For the finite pair, ZXe_j=ω^(j+1)e_{j+1} and XZe_j=ω^je_{j+1}, proving the Weyl relation on a basis. Each map permutes or phases an orthonormal basis, so it is unitary. Iterating N times returns every basis vector. This multiplicative relation does not contradict the trace obstruction and should not be silently differentiated into an exact additive commutator of finite logarithms. Infinite-dimensional canonical operators require dense invariant domains and careful treatment of unbounded products.

## Worked example: setup

Take N=3.

## Worked example: calculation

The clock is diag(1,ω,ω²), and the shift is the cyclic permutation matrix. Their order-dependent product differs by the cube root of unity ω. Any claimed three-state realization of[A,B]=iℏI fails before its eigenvalues are computed.

## Worked example: conclusion

A prototype must choose a finite Weyl model, an approximation with quantified error, or a properly defined infinite-dimensional representation.

## Limits

This does not rule out canonical commutation relations on suitable infinite-dimensional spaces. It rules out the exact finite identity, not all discretizations or physical models.

## Extension

Choose an explicit discretization and bound its commutator error on a stated low-energy subspace instead of claiming an exact finite canonical relation.
