## Revised question retained in the annual source

What does the exponential of a skew matrix prove, and why do finite rotations about different axes fail to commute?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The August3 source named skew matrices, quaternions and BCH. Rodrigues’ formula supplies an exact operator, while a basis commutator identifies the first noncommutative correction. Neither implies a speculative model of an external system.

## Definitions

For a unit vector a∈R³ let Kv=a×v. Then Kᵀ=−K,K²=aaᵀ−I and K³=−K. Let J_x,J_y,J_z be the corresponding unit-axis matrices.

## Result name

Rodrigues formula and Lie bracket

## Result statement

exp(θK)=I+(sinθ)K+(1−cosθ)K² lies in SO(3). The generators satisfy[J_x,J_y]=J_z, with cyclic variants. Small rotations therefore commute only up to a second-order bracket term.

## Proof

The vector triple-product identity gives K²v=a(a·v)−v and hence K³=−K. Separate the exponential series into its odd and even powers to obtain Rodrigues’ expression. Since K is skew, exp(θK)ᵀexp(θK)=I; its determinant is exp(tr θK)=1. For the bracket, apply the two compositions to v and use a×(b×v)−b×(a×v)=(a×b)×v. Expanding exponentials gives e^(εJx)e^(εJy)−e^(εJy)e^(εJx)=ε²Jz+O(ε³), making noncommutativity quantitative. A quaternion representation must use the half-angle if it is to reproduce a spatial rotation of angleθ.

## Worked example: setup

Rotate e_z successively by right-angle rotations around x and y.

## Worked example: calculation

R_x(π/2)R_y(π/2)e_z=e_x, whereas R_y(π/2)R_x(π/2)e_z=−e_y. The order matters even though both factors individually preserve lengths and orientation.

## Worked example: conclusion

The group action, matrix convention and order of composition are now explicit.

## Limits

This calculation establishes rotation geometry, not a PDE, energy functional or physical coupling.

## Extension

Specify the representation and dynamics, then derive any symmetry reduction from actual commuting operators rather than axis labels.
