## Revised question retained in the annual source

What exact obstruction rules out the recovered finite determinant identity, and what valid finite determinant model remains?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The prior assistant asserted grand proofs from a finite384-dimensional determinant identity. IfH384 is an ordinary finite matrix andc≠0, the identity is impossible: a polynomial has finitely many zeros while the completed zeta function has infinitely many. A finite spectral model remains useful, but proves only its own spectral algebra.

## Definitions

For a384×384 matrixH, p_H(s)=det(sI−H) is a monic polynomial of degree384.

The completed xi function is entire and has the nontrivial zeta zeros; their infinitude is a classical theorem independent of RH.

## Result name

A finite-degree obstruction and a valid product identity

## Result statement

No ordinary finiteH and nonzeroc satisfy p_H(s)=cξ(s)^384 for allcomplexs. For a finite Hermitian matrix, the valid identity is p_H(s)=∏_j(s−λ_j), with realλ_j.

## Proof

The determinant expansion has leading terms^384 and therefore defines a nonzero polynomial with at most384 distinct roots. The right-hand side vanishes at every zero ofξ, an infinite set; equality would give infinitely many polynomial roots, a contradiction. Ifc=0, equality is also impossible becausep_H is monic. An infinite-dimensional regularized determinant is a different object and needs convergence, continuation and normalization; changing the word determinant does not supply those theorems. For the valid finite model, unitary diagonalizationH=Udiag(λ_j)U* and multiplicativity of determinant yield the product, whileHermiticity makesλ_j real.

## Worked example: setup

TakeH=diag(1,4).

## Worked example: calculation

p_H(s)=(s−1)(s−4)=s²−5s+4. The resolvent trace away from its poles is1/(s−1)+1/(s−4)=p_H′(s)/p_H(s). This exact identity encodes two eigenvalues and no assertion about all zeros of an arithmetic function.

## Worked example: conclusion

The finite construction can be checked, while the impossible entire-function identity is discarded.
