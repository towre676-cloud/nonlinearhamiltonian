## Revised question retained in the annual source

How can a capacity-based allocation be formulated so that its units, feasible set and fairness objective are explicit?

## Status

Developed mathematical result with explicit hypotheses and checked example; historical claims repaired where necessary

## Summary

A quotient of capacity by demand is not itself an allocation theorem. A concave two-recipient model yields a unique feasible optimum and an exact water-filling rule.

## Definitions

Letx_i≥0 be allocated units withΣx_i≤C,C>0. Let benefit beΣa_i log(1+x_i), witha_i>0.

A weighted index such as0.3A+0.3TC+0.2LP+0.2CE lies in[0,1] only after all four inputs are normalized to that interval.

A capacity ratioK/[θ(P+U)] requiresθ>0 andP+U>0. Its interpretation and rounding rule are additional modeling choices.

## Result name

Unique concave allocation

## Result statement

The log-utility problem has a unique optimumx_i=max(a_i/λ−1,0), withλ>0 chosen soΣx_i=C. For(a₁,a₂)=(2,1),C=2, the allocation is(5/3,1/3).

## Proof

The compact feasible simplex gives existence. The Hessian is diagonal with strictly negative entries−a_i/(1+x_i)², so the objective is strictly concave and has at most one maximizer. Each benefit increases, so capacity is exhausted. Lagrange optimality givesa_i/(1+x_i)=λ on positive coordinates and a_i≤λ on zero coordinates. These conditions yield the formula and are sufficient by concavity. For the example both coordinates are positive, so2/λ−1+1/λ−1=2 givesλ=3/4 and the displayed allocation.

## Worked example: conclusion

A feasible allocation and an aggregate index have explicit but different mathematical contracts.

## Limits

No choice of utility or normalization is scientifically or socially compelled by this calculation. Zero demand makes the quoted ratio undefined, and unbounded capacity claims cannot be repaired by optimization alone.

## Extension

Add lower guarantees, upper capacity bounds and multiple resource constraints. Derive the KKT multipliers and use complementary slackness to identify which resource actually limits expansion. The solution also has a precise activation threshold: recipient i receives a positive allocation exactly when a_i>λ. As capacity grows, λ decreases and additional recipients become active. This is a mathematical consequence of the specified concave utility, not a universal conclusion about participation or social value.
