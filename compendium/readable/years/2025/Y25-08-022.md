## Definitions

For fixed θ₀,u₀ define θ₁=θ₀−ηu₀−ηλθ₀. The direction u₀ is held fixed only for this partial-derivative example. Let L=||θ₁−y||²/2. In a multi-step optimizer, momentum and adaptive moments are part of the differentiated state.

## Result name

One-step outer derivatives

## Result statement

∂_ηθ₁=−u₀−λθ₀ and∂_λθ₁=−ηθ₀. Thus∂_ηL=(θ₁−y)·(−u₀−λθ₀) and∂_λL=(θ₁−y)·(−ηθ₀).

## Proof

Differentiate the explicit affine update while holding the stated inputs fixed, then apply the ordinary chain rule to the quadratic loss. For a full state recursion s_{t+1}=U_φ(s_t), its Jacobian obeys J_{t+1}=(∂_sU)J_t+∂_φU, not just the last-step partial. Removing a dependency with a detached value can make an automatic-differentiation gradient zero even when the mathematical derivative is nonzero. Conversely a true stationary point can also give zero; the value must be checked against the actual graph and an independent derivative calculation.

## Worked example: setup

Use θ₀=(1,−2),u₀=(1/2,−1/4),η=1/10,λ=1/5,y=0.

## Worked example: calculation

Then θ₁=(93/100,−387/200). The exact gradients are∂_ηL=−1527/800=−1.90875 and∂_λL=−12/25=−0.48. Centered finite differences of this quadratic reproduce them exactly in rational arithmetic.

## Worked example: conclusion

A nonzero analytic test is available, while the historical zero-gradient report remains unverified evidence.
