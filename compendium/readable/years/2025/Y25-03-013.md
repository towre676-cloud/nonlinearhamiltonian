## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which variation makes the recovered first law valid, and what remains at second order?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered first-law identity has a precise finite-state meaning. It is a linearized equality at a fixed faithful reference state, not equality of finite entropy changes. Relative entropy records the discrepancy beyond that first derivative.

## Definitions

Letσ>0 have trace1, letX=X* withTrX=0, andρ(ε)=σ+εX remain positive for smallε.

SetS(ρ)=−Trρlogρ andhσ=−logσ; relative entropy isD(ρ||σ)=Trρ(logρ−logσ).

## Result name

First law and a positive second-order example

## Result statement

Atε=0,dS(ρ(ε))/dε=Tr(Xhσ). Exactly, D(ρ||σ)=Tr[(ρ−σ)hσ]−[S(ρ)−S(σ)]. For a diagonal binary perturbation its second-order coefficient is positive.

## Proof

The trace derivative ofTrf(σ+εX) isTrf′(σ)X. In an eigenbasis, the diagonal derivative terms give this expression and off-diagonal first-order contributions have zero trace. Withf(u)=u logu, the entropy derivative is−TrX(logσ+I)=−TrXlogσ becauseTrX=0. Expanding the definition ofD gives the exact finite-change identity. Forσ=diag(p,1−p) andX=diag(1,−1), direct differentiation of the scalar relative entropy givesD′(0)=0 andD″(0)=1/p+1/(1−p)=1/[p(1−p)]>0. Thus finite changes agree only to first order in general.

## Worked example: setup

Takep=3/4 andε=1/20, soρ=diag(4/5,1/5).

## Worked example: calculation

The modular-energy change isεlog((1−p)/p)=−(log3)/20. The exact relative entropy is(4/5)log(16/15)+(1/5)log(4/5)>0. Its quadratic approximation is(8/3)ε²=1/150, while the exact finite identity gives the entropy change by subtractingD from the modular-energy change.

## Worked example: conclusion

The first law is now a proved derivative with a declared reference operator. Its second-order remainder can be checked explicitly.

## Limits

Faithfulness is essential for a bounded matrix logarithm; support changes can make relative entropy infinite. No Einstein equation or categorical entropy follows solely from this identity.
