## Revised question retained in the annual source

What finite certificate makes a family a frame, and which correction preserves its norm under a change of coordinates?

## Status

Developed mathematical result with explicit hypotheses and checked example; historical claims repaired where necessary

## Summary

A four-point Haar basis gives exact Parseval reconstruction. A redundant duplication is still a tight frame, and a Jacobian factor—not a name such as Möbius-scaled—controls unitarity of coordinate pullback.

## Definitions

InR⁴ useh₀=(1,1,1,1)/2,h₁=(1,1,−1,−1)/2,h₂=(1,−1,0,0)/√2,h₃=(0,0,1,−1)/√2.

A frame hasA||f||²≤Σ|〈f,ψ_j〉|²≤B||f||² withA>0. Its frame operator isSf=Σψ_j〈ψ_j,f〉.

For a smooth bijectiong in Euclidean coordinates, defineT_gf(x)=|detDg⁻¹(x)|^{1/2}f(g⁻¹x).

## Result name

Exact reconstruction and norm-preserving pullback

## Result statement

The fourh_j are orthonormal, sof=Σ〈f,h_j〉h_j and the frame bounds areA=B=1. Two copies of everyh_j scaled by1/√2 give a redundant tight frame with the same bounds. T_g is unitary onL².

## Proof

Direct dot products giveh_iᵀh_j=δ_ij, and four orthonormal vectors spanR⁴. Parseval and reconstruction follow. In the duplicate family each squared coefficient is halved but occurs twice, so their total remains||f||² andS=I; redundancy does not imply unique coefficients. For the pullback, substitute x=g(y) in∫|T_gf(x)|²dx. The inverse Jacobian cancels the change-of-variables Jacobian, leaving∫|f(y)|²dy. A hyperbolic isometry preserves its invariant volume measure and needs no such Euclidean-coordinate correction once that measure is used consistently.

## Limits

A finite Haar example does not construct a hyperbolic wavelet frame. Monte Carlo agreement with one signal cannot prove a uniform lower frame bound; a missing lower bound permits unrecoverable modes.

## Extension

For a finite discretization form the analysis matrixΨ and certify eigenvalue bounds forΨ*Ψ. Report discretization and quadrature errors separately from sampling variance and from GPU runtime. For uniform Monte Carlo sampling of this four-point signal, the mean is 3/2 and the population variance is 5/4. An average of m independent samples therefore has variance 5/(4m). This sampling uncertainty is distinct from the exact Parseval identity and does not decrease a deterministic missing-mode reconstruction error.
