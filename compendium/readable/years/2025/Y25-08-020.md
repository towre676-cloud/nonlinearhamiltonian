## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which algebraic condition preserves a positive inverse-Hessian estimate, and what does the recovered quadratic test actually verify?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The August10 narrative compiler included L-BFGS scaling and Armijo backtracking. A complete quadratic example checks its line search and scale. The inverse-update formula identifies the separate curvature safeguard required for positive-definite search directions.

## Definitions

Let H be symmetric positive definite,s=x⁺−x,y=∇f(x⁺)−∇f(x),ρ=(yᵀs)⁻¹. The inverse-BFGS update is H⁺=(I−ρsyᵀ)H(I−ρysᵀ)+ρssᵀ.

## Result name

Curvature condition and positive definiteness

## Result statement

If yᵀs>0, then H⁺ is positive definite. An Armijo decrease test alone does not guarantee this curvature condition.

## Proof

For any z≠0, zᵀH⁺z=wᵀHw+ρ(sᵀz)² with w=(I−ρysᵀ)z. Both terms are nonnegative. If sᵀz≠0 the second is positive; if it is zero then w=z and the first is positive. This proves the claim. Armijo bounds f(x+αp) against f(x)+c₁α∇fᵀp, but does not bound the new directional derivative; a curvature/Wolfe condition or an explicit yᵀs safeguard is additional. The scale H₀=(sᵀy)/(yᵀy)I is positive only under the same sign requirement and a nonzero denominator.

## Worked example: setup

Use f=(x₀−1)²+(x₁+2)² at x=(4,−6).

## Worked example: calculation

The gradient is(6,−8), initial objective25. A unit steepest-descent step leaves objective25 and fails Armijo at c₁=10⁻⁴. Step1/2 reaches(1,−2) with objective0. Then s=(−3,4),y=(−6,8),sᵀy=50,yᵀy=100, so the initial inverse scale is1/2.

## Worked example: conclusion

The test checks real derivative and update identities rather than merely a solver status.

## Limits

No global L-BFGS convergence theorem follows from one quadratic. Nonsmooth objectives and skipped or damped updates require their own hypotheses.

## Extension

Certify descent, curvature and sufficient-decrease conditions at every update, then compare finite-memory outputs with a direct inverse update on a small exact problem.
