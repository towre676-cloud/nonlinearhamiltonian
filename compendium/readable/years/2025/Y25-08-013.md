## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which cancellation and oscillation estimates support the named Hardy–BMO connection?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The August20 synthesis named Fefferman duality and controlled irregularity. A local atomic inequality can be proved completely and is useful without pretending to prove the full global duality theorem or identify Sobolev and Hardy spaces.

## Definitions

For an interval I define b_I=|I|⁻¹∫_Ib and osc_I(b)=|I|⁻¹∫_I|b−b_I|. A real Hardy atom a is supported on I, has mean zero and||a||∞≤1/|I|. The BMO seminorm is sup_I osc_I(b).

## Result name

Atomic pairing and bounded perturbation

## Result statement

|∫ab|≤osc_I(b). If b∈BMO and||e||∞≤ε, then b+e∈BMO and| ||b+e||_BMO−||b||_BMO |≤2ε.

## Proof

Mean zero gives∫ab=∫a(b−b_I); bounding a by1/|I| proves the pairing. For perturbations, osc_I(b+e)≤osc_I(b)+osc_I(e) by the triangle inequality. Since|e_I|≤ε, the second oscillation is at most2ε. Taking suprema and exchanging b with b+e proves the finite two-sided bound. These arguments identify exactly which norms control cancellation and perturbation. Full(H¹)*=BMO involves atomic decompositions and functional representation beyond this single estimate; merely choosing a function called entropy does not establish that theorem’s hypotheses.

## Worked example: setup

Take I=[−1,1],a=sign(x)/2 and b=x.

## Worked example: calculation

The atom has zero mean and sup norm1/2. Here b_I=0,osc_I(b)=1/2 and∫ab=(1/2)∫|x|dx=1/2, so the local constant is attained. The function x on all of R is not claimed globally BMO.

## Worked example: conclusion

The cancellation, coefficient convention and perturbation class make the irregularity claim mathematically testable.

## Limits
