## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which union-closed families admit a direct half-frequency proof, and what do small enumerations establish?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The July2 project studied Frankl’s conjecture with topological summaries and empirical counts. A simple injection gives a genuine theorem for a broad subclass. Exhaustive small-universe checks can then be certified, while correlations and reported Betti values remain distinct evidence.

## Definitions

A finite family F of subsets is union-closed if A∪B∈F whenever A,B∈F. Frankl’s conjecture asks whether every nontrivial finite such family has an element belonging to at least half its sets. Assume the union of the family is nonempty.

## Result name

Singleton certificate

## Result statement

If{a}∈F, then a belongs to at least|F|/2 members of F. The map A↦A∪{a} injects the members not containing a into those containing a.

## Proof

Union closure puts A∪{a} in F. If A and B both omit a and their unions with{a} agree, removing a gives A=B, proving injectivity. Thus the number omitting a is at most the number containing it, exactly the desired inequality. This proof makes no use of a topology or an entropy. It also explains its limitation: a family with no singleton does not admit this particular injection automatically. A complete search on a fixed finite universe can test all families by exact unions and frequencies, but it cannot prove the unbounded conjecture. Reported small Betti vectors additionally require the actual associated complex and coefficient convention.
