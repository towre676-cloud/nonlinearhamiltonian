## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which exact identities distinguish a creation algebra from a finite matrix approximation, and what does a Boolean Gaussian actually mean?

## Summary

Prefix creation on word space satisfies Cuntz–Toeplitz relations with a vacuum defect. Separately, the Boolean moment–cumulant recursion makes a symmetric two-point law Gaussian in the Boolean sense, unlike the classical Gaussian.

## Definitions

Let the full word Hilbert space have orthonormal basis|w⟩ over finite words in two letters, including the empty vacuum. DefineL_i|w⟩=|iw⟩.

Boolean cumulantsb_n satisfy m₀=1 andm_n=Σ_{j=1}ⁿb_jm_{n−j}, equivalent to summing products over interval partitions.

These constructions require stated notions of independence; a word such as Itô does not transfer classical stochastic rules to a different probability theory.

## Result name

Vacuum defect and exact Boolean cumulants

## Result statement

L_i*L_j=δ_ijI andΣ_iL_iL_i*=I−P_vac. For a symmetric variableX=±1,m_{2n}=1,m_{2n+1}=0 and its only nonzero Boolean cumulant isb₂=1.

## Proof

Removing the first letter undoes prefixing when the letters agree and kills it otherwise, proving the first relation. Every nonempty word begins with exactly one letter, while the vacuum lies in neither range, proving the defect formula. In a finite-dimensional space two exact isometries with orthogonal ranges cannot both exist: their ranges would each have the full ambient dimension. Finite truncations therefore add boundary defects. For cumulants, the moment series isM(z)=1/(1−z²). The recursion impliesM=1+B M, soB=1−1/M=z². Thus onlyb₂ survives. This does not give the classical Gaussian, whose fourth moment is three; the two-point law has fourth moment one.
