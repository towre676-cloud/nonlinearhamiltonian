## Revised question retained in the annual source

How does conditioning a Gaussian process change the calibration integral?

## Status

Classical or newly chosen explicit model developed with proof; historical conjectures delimited

## Summary

The recovered stochastic calibration used a kernel inside an OU/Wiener functional without a complete process specification. A Brownian bridge example gives an exact definition and reveals the covariance correction that is lost if a bridge is treated as an unconstrained Wiener process.

## Definitions

Let W_t be standard Brownian motion on[0, T] and B_t=W_t−(t/T)W_T.

For deterministicf∈L²(0, T), define its bridge functional J_f=∫₀ᵀf(t)d W_t−(W_T/T)∫₀ᵀf(t)dt.

## Result name

Gaussian bridge-functional variance

## Result statement

J_f is centered Gaussian and Var(J_f)=∫f²dt−T^(−1)(∫fdt)². Consequently a constantf produces zero, rather than Wiener variance T f².

## Proof

Approximatef by step functions to define the Wiener integral in mean square. Its mean is zero, its variance is∫f² by the Itô isometry, and its covariance with W_T=∫1d W is∫f. Expanding the variance of the displayed linear combination gives∫f²−2T^(−1)(∫f)²+T^(−1)(∫f)², proving the formula. It is nonnegative by Cauchy–Schwarz and vanishes exactly for almost-everywhere constantf. Gaussianity follows because the approximating functionals are finite linear combinations of a jointly Gaussian process and converge in L². This definition also avoids incorrectly using the original Brownian filtration to treat the anticipative representation of B as an ordinary Itô integrator.

## Worked example: setup

Take T=1 andf(t)=t, then comparef(t)=1.

## Worked example: calculation

Forf=t the variance is1/3−(1/2)²=1/12. Forf=1, J_f=W_1−W_1=0. An unconstrained Wiener functional∫₀¹td W has variance1/3, so conditioning removes a substantial component. For an OU bridge the same Gaussian conditional-covariance rule is C_br(s, t)=C(s, t)−C(s, T)C(T, t)/C(T, T), with the OU covariance explicitly supplied first.

## Worked example: conclusion

The stochastic object has a complete probability model, a proved variance, and an exact nontrivial example.

## Limits

Random or adapted kernel parameters require separate integrability and filtration conditions. A complex-valued kernel needs its Hermitian covariance convention. This finite-time construction does not validate a device or an unspecified stochastic calibration.

## Extension

For a deterministic observation kernel and a specified OU covariance, evaluate the double covariance integral and compare bridge and unconditioned uncertainties with rigorous quadrature errors.
