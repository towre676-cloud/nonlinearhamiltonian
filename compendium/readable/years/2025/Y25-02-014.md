## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which monodromy condition can be checked for a concrete second-order equation, and why is unipotent monodromy still nontrivial?

## Status

Developed mathematical result with explicit hypotheses and checked example; historical claims repaired where necessary

## Summary

A constant-potential equation on a periodic parameter has a complete monodromy calculation. The zero-potential case has only eigenvalue one but a nonidentity Jordan block, exposing a condition that spectral eigenvalues alone miss.

## Definitions

Consider y″=λy over a parameter interval of length one and writev=(y,y′). Thenv′=A_λv withA_λ=[[0,1],[λ,0]].

The monodromy isM_λ=e^{A_λ}; monodromy-free in this model meansM_λ=I.

This scalar constant-coefficient test is newly specified; a general oper includes geometric and gauge data not recovered here.

## Result name

Complete constant-potential monodromy test

## Result statement

Forλ=−4π²n² with nonzero integern,M_λ=I. Forλ=0,M₀=[[1,1],[0,1]]≠I although both eigenvalues are one.

## Proof

A_λ²=λI. Ifλ=−ω² withω≠0, the exponential isIcosω+(A_λ/ω)sinω. It is the identity whenω=2πn, giving the stated family. Atλ=0 the square ofA vanishes, so its exponential isI+A, proving the nontrivial unipotent case. For general complexλ≠0, diagonalization gives eigenvaluese^{±√λ}. Both are one only when√λ is a nonzero integer multiple of2πi, in which case the matrix is diagonalizable and isI. The exceptionalλ=0 case is precisely where that diagonalization fails; replacing matrix equality by eigenvalue equality loses it.

## Worked example: setup

Compare initial datav(0)=(0,1) atλ=0 andλ=−4π².

## Worked example: calculation

At zero potential,y(t)=t andv(1)=(1,1), so the initial vector is not restored. Atλ=−4π²,y(t)=sin(2πt)/(2π) andv(1)=(0,1), restoring every solution’s data after one period.

## Worked example: conclusion

The example supplies an exact monodromy condition rather than a label attached to an unspecified spectral support.

## Limits

It does not classify monodromy-free opers on arbitrary curves or justify the historical categorical equivalence. Gauge, singularity and global-bundle conditions matter.

## Extension

For a piecewise constant potential, multiply the exact transfer matrices and solve the full matrix identityM=I. Compare it withtrM=2, which also admits nontrivial unipotent matrices and is therefore weaker. The determinant of every monodromy matrix in this model is one because tr A_λ=0 and det e^{A_λ}=e^{tr A_λ}. Nevertheless determinant one and trace two do not force identity: the zero-potential Jordan matrix satisfies both. A geometric condition stated through traces alone must therefore address this nonsemisimple exception.
