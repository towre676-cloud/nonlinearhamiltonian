## Revised question retained in the annual source

Reconstruction: For the stated linear dispersion relation, what frequency and group velocity are actually implied, and does that establish recoverable power?

## Status

Developed mathematical repair or classical result; speculative source claims delimited

## Summary

A dispersion relation can support an exact propagation calculation without supporting the reported energy gains. The nonrotating constant-stratification model has a particularly clear geometry: its frequency is homogeneous of degree zero in wavevector, forcing phase and group directions to be perpendicular.

## Definitions

Take a stipulated N>0, horizontal wavenumber k>0 and vertical wavenumber m∈R. Use ω(k,m)=Nk/√(k²+m²), the positive branch of ω²=N²k²/(k²+m²).

Define group velocity c_g=∇_(k,m)ω and phase-velocity vector c_ph=ω(k,m)(k,m)/(k²+m²). This is the stated ideal linear nonrotating dispersion model.

## Result name

An exact group-velocity formula and orthogonality

## Result statement

c_g=(Nm²,−Nkm)/(k²+m²)^(3/2), and (k,m)·c_g=0. Hence c_ph·c_g=0. Also0<ω≤N, with equality only when m=0.

## Proof

Differentiate Nk(k²+m²)^(−1/2) in k to obtain N[(k²+m²)−k²]/(k²+m²)^(3/2)=Nm²/(k²+m²)^(3/2). Differentiating in m gives −Nkm/(k²+m²)^(3/2). Taking the wavevector dot product cancels the two numerator terms. Equivalently ω(sk,sm)=ω(k,m) for s>0, so Euler’s homogeneous-function identity forces that dot product to vanish. The frequency bound follows from k≤√(k²+m²). At m=0 the group velocity vanishes in this idealized expression; this limiting degeneracy must not be mistaken for a general transport prediction.

## Worked example: setup

Use nondimensional values N=5,k=3,m=4.

## Worked example: calculation

Then ω=3, c_g=(16/25,−12/25), and c_ph=(9/25,12/25). Their dot product is144/625−144/625=0. The wavevector dot product is48/25−48/25=0. If both wavenumbers are doubled to(6,8), the frequency stays three while both group-velocity components are halved. This follows from differentiating a degree-zero homogeneous function and provides a second scaling check. ## Worked example: conclusion

The geometry follows from a specified equation and can be certified independently of the harvesting proposal.

## Limits

Wave amplitude, energy density, dissipation, forcing and coupling are absent from a dispersion equation. It cannot determine watts per patch, total output or EROI. Rotation, variable stratification and boundaries require a different model.

## Extension

Supply a linear wave-energy density and boundary coupling operator, then prove an input-output energy inequality. Such an inequality would be the proper mathematical bridge from propagation to an upper bound on extractable energy.
