## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which finite code supplies an actual correction theorem rather than an asserted fidelity?

## Status

## Summary

The recovered HQECC material states stabilizer constraints and numerical errors without a noise model. A three-qubit repetition code gives a complete limited theorem: it corrects one bit flip, while explicitly failing to protect against a general phase error.

## Definitions

Use commuting checksS1=Z1Z2 andS2=Z2Z3. Their common+1 eigenspace isspan{|000〉,|111〉}.

Assume independent bit-flip errors with probabilityp on each qubit, followed by ideal syndrome measurement and correction.

## Result name

Exact syndrome and failure probability

## Result statement

The errorsI,X1,X2,X3 have syndromes(+,+),(−,+),(−,−),(+,−), respectively. Majority correction fails with probability3p²−2p³.

## Proof

PauliZ anticommutes withX on the same qubit and commutes on distinct ones. Applying that rule to each check gives the four displayed syndrome pairs, so each single flip can be identified and inverted. The checks act identically on both logical basis vectors and do not measure their superposition coefficients. With two or three independent flips, the syndrome-based single-flip correction instead changes the logical bit. The total probability isC(3,2)p²(1−p)+p³=3p²−2p³. At the subspace level,PE_a*E_bP=δ_abP for the four errors: diagonal products areI and distinct products take the code to an orthogonal syndrome subspace. This is the exact correction condition in this restricted error set.

## Limits

Measurement faults, correlated errors and phase noise change the result. No large holographic-code or hardware fidelity theorem follows from this small code.
