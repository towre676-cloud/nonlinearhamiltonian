## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which graph measure defines the curvature, and can a computed value justify a complexity or cryptographic claim?

## Summary

The July3–4 frameworks used curvature, spectral gaps and Betti numbers as proposed difficulty indicators. Those observables require precise constructions. A complete elementary transport calculation shows how curvature depends on the chosen neighborhood measure, while no security or hardness theorem is inferred.

## Definitions

For adjacent vertices x,y at graph distance one, choose probability measures m_x,m_y and define κ(x,y)=1−W₁(m_x,m_y). W₁ minimizesΣd(u,v)π(u,v) over couplings with the declared marginals. Here use the uniform measure on neighbors, with no idleness.

## Result name

Exact curvatures of two small graphs

## Result statement

For K₂ the unique edge has κ=0. For K₃ every edge has κ=1/2 under this measure convention.

## Proof

In K₂, the neighbor measures are point masses at opposite endpoints, so transporting unit mass costs one. In K₃ with vertices x,y,z, the measures are(δ_y+δ_z)/2 and(δ_x+δ_z)/2. Match the common z-mass to itself and move the remaining half from y to x, costing1/2. This is optimal because the target has half a unit at x and the source has none there; all possible source points are distance one from x. Hence W₁=1/2. A different idleness convention changes the measures and can change κ, so comparison requires locked definitions. Similarly complex cochain coefficients do not by themselves create new quantum Betti numbers: for integer boundary matrices the ranks over Q and C agree.
