## Definitions

Let k be a field, A=k[ε]/(ε²), and regard k=A/(ε) as an A-module. Ordinary tensor gives k⊗_Ak≅k.

Tor_n^A(k,k) is the nth homology of a free resolution of one copy of k after tensoring with the other. The derived tensor k⊗_A^Lk retains this whole homology sequence.

## Result name

An explicit resolution with a higher class in every degree

## Result statement

For every n≥0, Tor_n^A(k,k)≅k. Thus the derived tensor cannot be replaced by its degree-zero ordinary tensor without losing nonzero information in every positive degree.

## Proof

Use the augmented complex …→A→A→A→k→0, with every positive-degree differential multiplication by ε and the final map the quotient. Consecutive differentials compose to multiplication by ε²=0. In A, an element is a+bε; multiplying by ε gives aε, so the kernel and image of multiplication by ε are both the one-dimensional ideal εA. The kernel of A→k is also εA. Hence the complex is an exact free resolution. Tensoring with k sends ε to zero, so all resulting differentials vanish and each chain group is k. Its homology is therefore k in every nonnegative degree. This is a complete calculation rather than an invocation of derived language alone.

## Worked example: setup

Represent multiplication by ε on the basis (1,ε) as M=[[0,0],[1,0]].

## Worked example: calculation

Then M²=0, rank(M)=1 and kernel(M)=span(ε)=image(M). Reducing coefficients modulo ε turns M into zero on the tensor complex. The verifier checks these finite linear identities and the first eight homology dimensions. The written periodic argument proves the all-degree statement.

## Worked example: conclusion

Higher Tor is the information that ordinary tensor discards; a derived replacement must be justified by such an actual failure of exactness.

## Limits

This is a module calculation over dual numbers, not a motivic cohomology computation, an E∞ structure or a syntomic integration measure. Passing from complexes to those theories requires their own objects, maps and comparison theorems.
