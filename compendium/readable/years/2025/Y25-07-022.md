## Revised question retained in the annual source

What does the stated finite modulus actually mean, and which ray group follows without inventing infinite-place data?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The July25 arithmetic contains enough exact data for a real computation. Treat the modulus as the stated finite ideal, separately from later contradictory narrow-modulus assertions. This gives a class-group proof and an explicit ray-kernel calculation.

## Definitions

Let K=Q(√10), O=Z[√10], discriminant40, P₂=(2,√10), and m=(3,1+√10). Its quotient is F₃ through √10↦−1, so Norm(m)=3. The finite ray group uses principal generators congruent to1 modulo m, without a positivity condition.

## Result name

Finite ray group and ordinary class group

## Result statement

Cl(O)≅C₂ and Cl_m(O)≅Cl(O). Also narrow and ordinary class groups agree for modulus1 because the units realize all real sign patterns.

## Proof

Minkowski gives a representative of every class with norm≤√40/2<3.163. Thus norm-two and norm-three primes generate. P₂²=(2), while P₂ is nonprincipal: an element of norm±2 would require a²−10b²=±2, impossible modulo five. The element2+√10 has norm−6 and factors as P₂ times the norm-three prime with √10≡1. The conjugate norm-three prime has inverse class; hence both have the same order-two class as P₂. These exhaust the Minkowski generators, proving class number two. The ray exact sequence O×→(O/m)×→Cl_m→Cl→1 has trivial kernel term because−1 maps to the nontrivial unit of F₃. Finally3+√10 has norm−1 and signs(+,-); together with−1 it realizes all four real sign patterns, proving narrow=ordinary.

## Worked example: calculation

The map a+b√10↦a−b modulo three has kernel m. The two residue units are1,2, both images of global units1,−1. No extra ray factor survives. A computation treating m as norm9 would use the wrong quotient ring.

## Worked example: conclusion

The ray answer is now tied to a precise modulus and exact sequence rather than a guessed class field.

## Limits

Adding real places to this finite modulus changes the ray condition and requires recomputing the joint residue/sign image; the separate narrow-modulus1 result does not settle every combined modulus.

## Extension

Write the full finite and infinite modulus, compute its unit image, and construct its Artin map before claiming principalization or an inversion algorithm.
