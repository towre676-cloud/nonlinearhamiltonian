## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which Euclidean assumptions fail on the full hyperbolic space, and what spectral statement can be proved in a radial model?

## Status

## Summary

Hyperbolic three-space has exponential ball growth and is not globally doubling. A radial energy identity proves the bottom spectral value one and explains why a global discrete eigenbasis cannot simply be assumed.

## Definitions

For curvature−1 hyperbolic three-space, radial volume isdμ=4πsinh²r dr after angular integration.

For a radial compactly supported functionu, writeu(r)=v(r)/sinh r, withv(0)=0 and the endpoint behavior needed for integration by parts.

Use the nonnegative Laplacian convention−Δ and its Rayleigh quotient∫|∇u|²/∫|u|².

## Result name

Ball growth and exact radial spectral identity

## Result statement

V(R)=π(sinh2R−2R), soV(2R)/V(R) is unbounded. For radialu, its Rayleigh quotient is1+∫|v′|²dr/∫|v|²dr, whose infimum is one and is not attained by a nonzero radialL² eigenfunction at the bottom.

## Proof

Integrate4πsinh²r using sinh²r=(cosh2r−1)/2 to getV. Its leading exponential is(π/2)e^{2R}, proving the ratio divergence. For the energy,sinh²r·|u′|²=|v′−v coth r|². Integrate the cross term−(v²)′coth r and use(coth r)′=−csch²r; the remaining coefficient is coth²r−csch²r=1. Boundary terms vanish under the stated conditions. For general smooth compactly supportedu, apply the radial identity to each angular direction and add the nonnegative angular-gradient energy. This proves the lower bound one for all such functions, not only radial ones. Takingv(r)=sin(πr/R) on[0,R], with standard smooth approximation at the endpoint, gives quotient1+π²/R²→1. Equality would forcev′=0 almost everywhere; its zero endpoint andL² condition then forcev=0.

## Worked example: calculation

The volume-doubling ratio grows asymptotically ase^{2R}. Increasing a radial support length fromR to2R divides the excess Rayleigh quotient above one by four. These are geometric and spectral effects with exact formulas, not evidence of a finite discrete mode list.

## Limits

The radial calculation does not prove the entire spectral resolution or a weighted duality theorem. A bounded domain or compact quotient can have a discrete spectrum, unlike the complete noncompact model; boundary and quotient assumptions must be stated.

## Extension

Separate local balls of bounded radius from global geometry. Establish local doubling constants and compare local atomic spaces with global spaces before transferring a Euclidean proof or wavelet construction.
