## Summary

The detector used factors of the form(1−a_p²p^(−s))⁻¹. That is not the symmetric-square factor of a two-dimensional unramified arithmetic parameter. Writing its three eigenvalues explicitly yields a complete finite correction and an exact worked factor.

## Definitions

Use arithmetic parameters α,β satisfying α+β=a and αβ=p. On the symmetric square, the eigenvalues areα²,αβ,β².

Let t=p^(−s). This unnormalized convention keeps the middle eigenvalue p. Dividing α,β by√p instead gives the unitary normalization and changes the variable scaling.

## Result name

The symmetric-square factor needs all three eigenvalue products

## Result statement

The unramified symmetric-square denominator is(1−α²t)(1−pt)(1−β²t)=1−(a²−p)t+p(a²−p)t²−p³t³. For the exact p=2,a=−2 input, it equals1−2t+4t²−8t³.

## Proof

The trace of the three-dimensional representation is α²+αβ+β²=(α+β)²−αβ=a²−p. The pairwise eigenvalue products sum to p(α²+β²)+p²=p(a²−2p)+p²=p(a²−p). Their product is(αβ)³=p³. Expanding the three linear factors proves the cubic formula without choosing roots numerically. For p=2,a=−2, the roots of X²+2X+2 are−1±i, whose squares are∓2i. Their quadratic contribution is1+4t², and the middle factor is1−2t, giving the displayed product.

## Worked example: calculation

Inverting1−2t+4t²−8t³ gives coefficients1,2,0,0,16,…; direct recurrence or multiplication checks these exactly. The rank-one surrogate(1−4t)⁻¹ instead gives1,4,16,64,256,…, already disagreeing at the first local coefficient. The distinction is representation-theoretic: the trace of Sym² is a²−p, not the square a² of the original trace. A finite Euler product over selected primes additionally has only the analytic properties of that finite rational expression. At t=0 every local factor has constant coefficient one. This normalization is part of the representation: multiplying or averaging arbitrary observed values changes the local factor rather than preserving its arithmetic definition.
