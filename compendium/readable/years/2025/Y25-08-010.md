## Revised question retained in the annual source

What exactly does S^(−T)f^k(x)=x prove, and how does it differ from ordinary periodicity?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The August15 response supplied blinker and glider examples. The mathematical invariant is relative periodicity under a translation symmetry. Its iteration law can be proved without assuming that every repeated pattern is a new cohomological obstruction.

## Definitions

Let a deterministic map f commute with a translation action S^T,T∈Z^d. A state x is relatively periodic when f^k(x)=S^Tx for some k>0. Ordinary periodicity is the special case T=0.

## Result name

Iteration of a relative period

## Result statement

If f^k(x)=S^Tx and f commutes with translations, then f^(mk)(x)=S^(mT)x for every m≥0. On a finite periodic lattice, some multiple becomes an ordinary period.

## Proof

Induct on m. If the relation holds for m, apply f^k and commute it past S^(mT) to get S^(mT)f^k(x)=S^((m+1)T)x. On a finite translation group, T has finite order r, so f^(rk)(x)=x. On an infinite lattice, nonzero translation drift generally prevents an ordinary period unless the state has a matching spatial stabilizer. This makes time covers and moving frames precise. An observed visual repetition is not sufficient: the equality must hold for the full state under an explicitly defined update and boundary condition.

## Worked example: setup

Use the historical formal glider data k=4,T=(1,1) on an L×L periodic torus.

## Worked example: calculation

The iteration law gives f^(4m)(x)=S^(m,m)x. Since S^(L,L)=I,4L is an ordinary period bound, possibly not minimal if the pattern has extra symmetry. A blinker with k=2,T=0 is already periodic after two steps.

## Worked example: conclusion

The exact recurrence distinguishes drift, temporal period and boundary-induced return.
