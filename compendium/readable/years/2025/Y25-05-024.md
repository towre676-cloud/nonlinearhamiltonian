## Revised question retained in the annual source

Reconstruction: Under what compatibility and force-balance assumptions can interface thermal stress be calculated, and what remains unverifiable in the stated numerical claims?

## Status

Developed mathematical repair or classical result; speculative source claims delimited

## Summary

A stress value is not determined by thermal expansion coefficients alone. A simple bonded-bar model states its mechanical assumptions and produces an exact stress formula by energy minimization. It is an illustrative calculation rather than validation of the reported274.5MPa or an operational limit.

## Definitions

Two perfectly bonded axial layers share strainε and temperature changeΔT. Their areas are A_i>0, moduli E_i>0 and expansion coefficientsα_i. Assume no net external axial force, no bending and linear elasticity.

Their stresses areσ_i=E_i(ε−α_iΔT), and stored elastic energy per common length is U(ε)=ΣA_iE_i(ε−α_iΔT)²/2.

## Result name

Compatible strain as a unique convex minimizer

## Result statement

The common strain isε*=ΔT(ΣA_iE_iα_i)/(ΣA_iE_i), withΣA_iσ_i=0. For equal A and E, stresses are±E(α₂−α₁)ΔT/2. Changing boundary constraints changes the result.

## Proof

Differentiate U to obtain U′=ΣA_iE_i(ε−α_iΔT). Setting this to zero is exactly axial force balance. Since U″=ΣA_iE_i>0, the stationary point is the unique global minimizer. Substitution yields the strain formula and equal-layer stresses. If both layers were externally constrained toε=0, the stresses would instead be−E_iα_iΔT, showing why omitted compatibility data cannot be repaired by a more precise multiplication. The stress dimensions are modulus times dimensionless strain; expansion coefficients carry inverse-temperature units.

## Limits

Actual multilayers can bend, relax, creep, delaminate or have nonuniform temperature. The bar model is not an interface-failure criterion or a substitute for measurements. No strength, flight or deposition recommendation follows.

## Extension

For a specified laminate, minimize a quadratic energy over midplane strain and curvature and propagate interval material parameters to stress bounds. Compare those bounds with independently measured strength distributions rather than treating one nominal stress as a reliability probability.
