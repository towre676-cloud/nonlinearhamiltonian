## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which explicitly defined connection produces a nonzero topological invariant, and what normalization makes the integral an integer?

## Status

## Summary

A two-component state over a sphere gives a unit Chern number through a direct patch calculation. The example separates a geometric curvature theorem from unprovided links to phonemes, gravity or algebraic rigidity.

## Definitions

On a northern chart ofS² useu(θ,φ)=(cos(θ/2),e^{iφ}sin(θ/2)), withu†u=1.

Use Berry connectionA=−iu†du and curvatureF=dA. The first Chern number in this convention is(2π)⁻¹∫F.

Changing the local state by a phase changesA by an exact local differential and leavesF unchanged. A single chart need not extend over both poles.

## Result name

Explicit Chern-one line bundle

## Result statement

A=sin²(θ/2)dφ,F=(1/2)sinθ dθ∧dφ, and(2π)⁻¹∫_{S²}F=1. No globally smooth one-formA onS² can have this curvature.

## Proof

Differentiateu and take its inner product withu. Theθ contributions cancel and theφ contribution isi sin²(θ/2)dφ, givingA. Differentiation givesF. Integratingφ from zero to2π andθ from zero toπ yields2π. IfF=dA for a single smooth globalA, Stokes’ theorem on the closed sphere would force the integral to zero, a contradiction. The state therefore needs at least two phase charts. Multiplyingu bye^{−iφ} gives a southern choice whose potential differs by−dφ, encoding a winding-one transition. This is the local-to-global mechanism behind the integer, not an assumed physical protection law.
