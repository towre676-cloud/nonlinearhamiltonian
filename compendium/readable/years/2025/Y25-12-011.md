## Revised question retained in the annual source

What assumptions make the recovered probability-to-barrier inversion valid?

## Status

## Summary

The recovered scalarengine inverted an exponential exit law and identified its result with a geometric barrier. This is exact only for an assumed constant-hazard model, and a logarithm of a dimensional rate requires a reference prefactor.

## Definitions

For a constant rate λ>0, an exponential waiting time satisfies P_t=P(τ≤t)=1−e^(−λ t).

An Arrheniusmodel is λ=λ₀e^(−W/ε), with λ₀and λ having the same time-inverse units.

## Result name

Exact rate and barrier inversion

## Result statement

If0<P_t<1, then λ=−log(1−P_t)/tand W=−ε log[−log(1−P_t)/(t λ₀)]. The factor λ₀cannot be omitted unless a dimensionless time convention fixes it toone.

## Proof

Solving1−P_t=e^(−λ t)by logarithms gives the rate. Dividing λ by λ₀in the Arrheniusrelation gives a dimensionless quantity whose logarithm is−W/ε. This proves the formula, with no approximation. The exponential distribution has constant hazard because its survival ratio S(t+s)/S(t)=e^(−λ s)depends only ons. A general first-passage distribution need not have this memoryless property, and a small-noise exit asymptotic does not assert an exact exponential law at every time. The derivative of Wwith respect to P_tis−ε/[t λ₀·(−log(1−P_t)/(t λ₀))·(1−P_t)], showing ill-conditioning near the endpoints.
