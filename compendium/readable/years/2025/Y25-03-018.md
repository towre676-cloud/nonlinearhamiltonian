## Revised question retained in the annual source

What exact flatness equation can be derived before invoking higher brackets?

## Status

## Summary

The historical higher-categorical flow lists a Maurer–Cartan expression without bracket degrees or factorial conventions. A matrix-valued differential-form algebra gives a rigorous degree-one example, where flatness is a concrete commutator condition.

## Definitions

LetA be a matrix-valued1-form onR². In the differential graded algebra of forms defineF=dA+A∧A=dA+(1/2)[A,A].

The graded commutator uses the wedge product and matrix multiplication; for degree-oneA the two terms in[A,A] add.

## Result name

An exact constant-connection flatness criterion

## Result statement

ForA=Xdx+Ydy with constant matrices, F=[X,Y]dx∧dy. It is flat exactly whenX andY commute.

## Proof

Constant coefficients give dA=0. ExpandingA∧A removes thedx∧dx anddy∧dy terms; the cross terms areXYdx∧dy+YXdy∧dx=(XY−YX)dx∧dy. Thus the displayed formula is exact. More generally, the derivative and graded commutator satisfy the graded Leibniz and Jacobi identities, which imply the Bianchi identitydF+[A,F]=0. In anL∞ convention one commonly writesΣ_(n≥1)(1/n!)ℓ_n(A,…,A)=0 withℓ_n of degree2−n; a source using other normalization must define it. The symbols themselves do not establish those identities or define a scale-dependent category.

## Worked example: setup

TakeX=[[0,1],[0,0]] andY=[[0,0],[1,0]].

## Worked example: calculation

[X,Y]=diag(1,−1), so the connection has nonzero curvature. ReplacingY by2X gives zero curvature. Both examples involve simple constant coefficients, showing that flatness is algebraic compatibility rather than a generic consequence of naming a gauge field.

## Worked example: conclusion

The corrected equation has a definite degree, normalization and exact curvature test.
