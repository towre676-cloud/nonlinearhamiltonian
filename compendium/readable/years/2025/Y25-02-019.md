## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which error family is corrected by an explicit code, and why must code distance be an integer rather than a fitted real constant?

## Summary

The three-qubit repetition subspace corrects any single bit flip, but it does not correct a single phase flip and has quantum distance one. The exact distinction prevents classical redundancy from being reported as universal quantum protection.

## Definitions

LetC=span{|000⟩,|111⟩}, with projectorP. Consider errorsE={I,X₁,X₂,X₃}.

## Result name

Exact correction and exact uncorrectable error

## Result statement

For the four bit-flip errors,PE_a†E_bP=δ_abP, so they are jointly correctable. ButPZ₁P acts as logicaldiag(1,−1), not a scalar. Hence the code has parameters[[3,1,1]], despite its classical bit-flip distance three.

## Proof

Ifa=b, the error product isI and the condition givesP. Ifa≠b, the product flips one or two distinct bits. Applied to either codeword it lands in a basis state orthogonal to both codewords, making the projected product zero. The four resulting error subspaces are mutually orthogonal and fill the eight-dimensional ambient space; measuring their syndrome and undoing the indicated flip gives recovery. A phase flipZ₁ leaves the basis strings in place but reverses the phase of|111⟩, so its restriction is a nontrivial logicalZ. This weight-one logical operator proves distance one. The distinction follows from the actual error action, not a code-size analogy.

## Worked example: calculation
