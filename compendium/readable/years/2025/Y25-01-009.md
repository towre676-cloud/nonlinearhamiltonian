## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which phase factors define an associative projective representation, and how can one prove that they cannot be removed by rephasing?

## Status

Developed mathematical result with explicit hypotheses and checked example; historical claims repaired where necessary

## Summary

A two-cocycle onC₂×C₂ has an explicit Pauli realization and a nontrivial commutator phase. This supplies a rigorous model of projective symmetry instead of assuming a claimed Monster cohomology value.

## Definitions

LetG=F₂² under addition andσ((a,b),(c,d))=(−1)^{bc}. DefineU_(a,b)=X^aZ^b using the Pauli matrices.

A normalized two-cocycle satisfiesσ(g,h)σ(g+h,k)=σ(h,k)σ(g,h+k) andσ(0,g)=σ(g,0)=1.

RephasingU_g by a nonzero scalar changesσ by a coboundary. On an abelian group every scalar coboundary has symmetric values in its two arguments.

## Result name

A nontrivial projective representation with an exact certificate

## Result statement

U_gU_h=σ(g,h)U_(g+h), andσ is a two-cocycle that is not a coboundary. Thus the projective action cannot be made an ordinary representation by choosing new scalar phases.

## Proof

The relationZX=−XZ gives the displayed multiplier when movingZ^b pastX^c. For triple elementsg=(a,b),h=(c,d),k=(e,f), the cocycle exponents arebc+(b+d)e andde+b(c+e), equal modulo two. This proves associativity of the central-extension multiplication. For the obstruction chooseg=(1,0),h=(0,1). Their multiplier ratioσ(g,h)/σ(h,g)=−1. Replacingσ by a coboundary cannot change that ratio because the group is abelian and a coboundaryc(g)c(h)/c(g+h) is symmetric. Hence the class is nonzero. The eight matrices{±I,±X,±Z,±XZ} form the corresponding noncommutative central extension. The extension is non-split: a splitting would give commuting lifts of the two quotient generators, whereas every scalar rephasing leaves their commutator equal to −I. This demonstrates a group-level obstruction separately from the already checked cocycle identity.

## Worked example: calculation

BothX andZ square toI, butXZX⁻¹Z⁻¹=−I and(XZ)²=−I. Their images commute after quotienting by{±I}, exactly recoveringG. The lift group has order eight, while its quotient has four elements. ## Limits

This example concernsG=F₂². It does not establish the historical assertionH²(M,C*)=Z/2 for the Monster; a group-specific Schur-multiplier calculation must be cited separately. No continuous spacetime symmetry follows from this finite extension.

## Extension

Given a proposed finite multiplier table, test the cocycle identity exhaustively, compute its alternating commutator form and solve the rephasing equations. This separates a removable convention from a nontrivial projective class.
