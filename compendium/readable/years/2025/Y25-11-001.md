## Revised question retained in the annual source

Can parity remove the apparently asymmetric exponential denominator before contour integration?

## Status

Classical or newly chosen explicit model developed with proof; historical conjectures delimited

## Summary

The recovered integral was described as lacking an elementary closed form. Its asymmetry disappears under x↦−x, leaving the classical Fourier transform of a quartic rational function. The resulting formula is exact for every real modulation parameter.

## Definitions

Let a∈R and I(a)=∫R cos²(ax)/[(1+ix²)(1+4^x)(1−ix²)]dx.

Write w(x)=1/(1+4^x); then w(x)+w(−x)=1.

## Result name

Parity reduction and quartic transform

## Result statement

I(a)=π/(4√2)·[1+e^(−√2|a|)(cos(√2|a|)+sin(√2|a|))].

## Proof

The conjugate factors multiply to1+x⁴. The remaining factor f(x)=cos²(ax)/(1+x⁴) is even and integrable. Substitution x↦−x gives I=∫f(x)w(−x)dx, so adding yields2I=∫f. Now cos²(ax)=(1+cos2ax)/2. For b>0 close the contour for e^(ibz)/(1+z⁴) in the upper half-plane; the poles are e^(i π/4) and e^(3i π/4), with residues e^(ibz)/(4z³). Their sum, multiplied by2π i, is F(b)=π e^(−b/√2)[cos(b/√2)+sin(b/√2)]/√2. The large arc vanishes by the exponential factor and quartic denominator; b=0 follows directly or by dominated convergence. Evenness supplies F(−b)=F(b). Therefore I=(F(0)+F(2a))/4, proving the formula. Every exchange is justified by domination with1/(1+x⁴).

## Worked example: setup

Evaluate a=0, compare large|a|, and check local curvature at the origin.

## Worked example: calculation

I(0)=π/(2√2), while I(a)→π/(4√2) as|a|→∞. Differentiating under the integral twice is allowed since x²/(1+x⁴) is integrable, giving I″(0)=−π/√2. The third derivative cannot be justified by the same absolute-moment argument; the closed form indeed has an|a|³ term near zero.

## Worked example: conclusion

The recovered prototype is solved exactly, and its smoothness is linked to the decay of the quartic weight.

## Limits

This identity proves no eigenvalue law for an unspecified integral operator and no physical realization. A finite precision plot should be checked against this formula rather than treated as evidence for a new constant.

## Extension

Replace4^x by any positive-base exponential c^x with c>0, c≠1: the same symmetry gives exactly the same integral for every even integrable numerator.
