## Revised question retained in the annual source

What exact coefficient identity should be tested before interpreting prime-index correlations as Hecke symmetry?

## Status

Classical or newly chosen explicit model developed with proof; historical conjectures delimited

## Summary

The recovered CMB discussion reported prime-coherence significance and a Hecke analogy. A classical divisor-sum example supplies a complete arithmetic identity and shows the missing correction when the prime divides the index. Statistical claims additionally require the original data and covariance model.

## Definitions

Let σ₃(n)=Σ_{d|n}d³ and put σ₃(n/p)=0 whenp does not dividen.

The normalized weight-four Eisenstein eigenvalue at primep is1+p³.

## Result name

Exact prime-power coefficient recurrence

## Result statement

For every prime p and integern≥1, σ₃(pn)+p³σ₃(n/p)=(1+p³)σ₃(n). Thus σ₃(pn)/σ₃(n)=1+p³ only when the correction term vanishes.

## Proof

Write n=p^a m withgcd(p, m)=1. Divisor multiplicativity gives σ₃(n)=σ₃(m)Σ_{j=0}^a p^(3j). For a=0 the formula follows immediately. For a≥1, add Σ_{j=0}^{a+1}p^(3j) andp³Σ_{j=0}^{a−1}p^(3j); each interior power occurs twice, matching(1+p³)Σ_{j=0}^ap^(3j). This proves the identity without assuming a physical interpretation. It is the Fourier-coefficient form of the weight-four Hecke relation for E₄. Replacing these coefficients by an arbitrary angular power spectrum does not preserve the relation; the relevant modular form, weight, coefficient normalization, and prime-divisibility correction must be identified independently.

## Worked example: setup

Usep=2 atn=1 andn=2, then consider multiple prime tests.

## Worked example: calculation

σ₃(1)=1, σ₃(2)=9, σ₃(4)=73. For n=2 the identity is73+8=9·9, but the raw ratio is73/9 rather than9. If m separate null tests are searched, a Bonferroni bound gives P(any false rejection)≤Σ p_i; a single-test significance is not automatically a familywise significance. Shared data can also correlate the tests.

## Worked example: conclusion

The arithmetic claim has an exact benchmark, while the reported observational significance remains an unverified historical result.

## Limits
