## Revised question retained in the annual source

Does δ(g)dx equal the induced surface measure, or is a gradient factor missing?

## Status

## Summary

The August20 torus equation supports an exact correction. A delta distribution on a regular level set is weighted by the reciprocal gradient norm. The resulting factor is constant for this particular defining function, but changes when the equation is rescaled.

## Definitions

Let R>r>0,ρ=√(x²+y²),g=(R−ρ)²+z²−r². Its zero set is a smooth torus. Coarea gives∫fδ(g)dx=∫_{g=0}f/|∇g|dA for suitable test functions.

## Result name

Exact torus normalization

## Result statement

On g=0,|∇g|=2r. Hence∫fδ(g)dx=(1/(2r))∫_TfdA, whileδ(g)|∇g|dx represents dA. In particular∫δ(g)dx=2π²R.

## Proof

Differentiate in cylindrical coordinates:∇g has radial component2(ρ−R) and vertical component2z. Its squared norm on the torus is4[(ρ−R)²+z²]=4r², so the coarea factor is constant2r. Parametrize by((R+r cosv)cosu,(R+r cosv)sinu,r sinv). The area density is r(R+r cosv); integrating u,v over[0,2π] gives4π²Rr. Dividing by2r proves the delta integral. If g is replaced by cg,δ(cg)=δ(g)/|c|, whereas the zero set and its geometric area are unchanged. The compensating gradient factor is therefore indispensable.

## Limits

The delta is a distribution, not an ordinary pointwise function. Regularity fails for other level sets if∇g vanishes, and the present formula then needs replacement.

## Extension

Derive the appropriate coarea formula for the actual level-set model and test invariance under reparametrization before interpreting surface integrals.
