## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which algebraic invariants of the recovered commutator words can be proved before interpreting numerical heights?

## Status

## Summary

The recovered ladder mixed X=[ρ, ρ*], complex-valued outputs, inverse words, and motivic interpretations. Ordinary finite-dimensional traces impose exact constraints, while regularized traces require an explicitly different definition. A word identity gives a constructive invariant that can actually be checked.

## Definitions

For a finite complex matrix ρ let X=ρρ*−ρ*ρ.

For invertible X, Y, Z define W=XYX^(−1)ZY^(−1)Z^(−1), using ordinary matrix multiplication.

## Result name

Finite trace and determinant constraints

## Result statement

X is Hermitian and Tr X=0. Also W=[X, Y][Y, Z] anddet W=1. Thus a nonzero complex number cannot be the ordinary finite trace of[ρ, ρ*].

## Proof

Taking adjoints leaves ρρ* and ρ*ρ unchanged, proving Hermiticity. Cyclicity of finite trace gives Tr ρρ*=Tr ρ*ρ, so the trace is zero. Multiplying the two group commutators gives XYX^(−1)Y^(−1)·YZY^(−1)Z^(−1)=W after cancelling Y^(−1)Y. Determinants multiply and commute as scalars, so each group commutator has determinantone and so does W. Neither statement fixes Tr W or turns the word into an intrinsic “height-three” object. If a weighted or zeta-regularized trace is intended, it must be defined separately and cannot inherit cyclicity without proof.

## Worked example: setup

Take ρ=[[0,1],[0,0]] and a diagonal weight Q=diag(1,2).

## Worked example: calculation

X=diag(1,−1), with tracezero and real eigenvalues. For real s, Tr(Q^(−s)X)=1−2^(−s), generally nonzero, while at s=0 it is zero. This finite example shows exactly how a weight changes a trace functional. It does not produce a zeta anomaly because the finite expression is already entire in s.

## Worked example: conclusion

The output type and invariant are explicit: ordinary commutator trace, weighted trace, determinant and group word are different quantities.
