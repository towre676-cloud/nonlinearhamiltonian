## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which proposed constants are invariant under unit changes, and what can an integer framework actually determine?

## Summary

The recovered framework attempts to derive several dimensional constants from categorical integers and an angle. A precise unit-change argument distinguishes numerical conventions from dimensionless predictions. This is a mathematical consistency test, not a claim that constants cannot be related within a defined theory.

## Result statement

The only dimensionless monomial formed solely fromℏ,c,G is the trivial onea=b=d=0. Their three-dimensional unit-exponent matrix is invertible, so their numerical values can all be changed by a choice ofM,L,T units.

## Proof

The dimension equations area−d=0,2a+b+3d=0,−a−b−2d=0. The first givesd=a; substituting into the other two givesb=−5a andb=−3a, so2a=0 and all exponents vanish. Equivalently the determinant of the exponent matrix is nonzero. Thus a bare integer or angle cannot specify a unit-independent numerical value of one of these dimensional constants without a scale convention. Planck combinations are dimensionful definitions:√(ℏG/c³) has length,√(ℏG/c⁵) has time and√(ℏc/G) has mass.
