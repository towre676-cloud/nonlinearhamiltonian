## Revised question retained in the annual source

How does a simple finite generalized eigenvalue move, and how does its damping ratio differentiate?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The source reports descriptor intervention gradients and a numerical Fox–Kapoor agreement. The exact formula is derived here under the simple-eigenvalue hypothesis, with a fresh rational example. The reported benchmark and finite-difference residual are historical assertions rather than independently reproduced data.

## Definitions

Let A(t),E(t) be differentiable complex matrices, λ(t) a simple finite eigenvalue, Av=λEv and u* A=λu*E. Assumeu*Ev≠0. For λ=σ+iω≠0 defineζ=−σ/(σ²+ω²)^{1/2}.

## Result name

Generalized eigenvalue and damping derivative

## Result statement

λ′=u*(A′−λE′)v/(u*Ev). Writingλ′=σ′+iω′ givesζ′=(−ω²σ′+σωω′)/(σ²+ω²)^{3/2}.

## Proof

DifferentiateAv=λEv: A′v+Av′=λ′Ev+λE′v+λEv′. Multiply byu*. The terms involvingv′ cancel becauseu*A=λu*E, leavingλ′u*Ev=u*(A′−λE′)v. The denominator assumption permits division and reveals the normalization sensitivity. Differentiatingζ=−σ(σ²+ω²)^{-1/2} and collecting terms gives the damping formula. Scaling either eigenvector cancels from the quotient, so no Euclidean normalization is required. At a multiple or defective eigenvalue a differentiable branch need not exist; atλ=0 the damping ratio itself is undefined. A finite-difference comparison is useful verification but does not remove either hypothesis or certify a finite intervention remainder. The example has right eigenvectorv=(1,−i)ᵀ and left rowu*=(1,i)/2, givingu*Ev=1. Substitution returns the same derivative without finite differencing. The general sensitivity is bounded by ||u||||v||·||A′−λE′||/|u*Ev|, so a small denominator signals sensitivity even if all entries are modest. At the defective family[[0,1],[t,0]], the eigenvalues±√t show why differentiability can fail at a collision.

## Worked example: setup

LetE=I and A(t)=[[-1−t/2,−(2+t/4)],[2+t/4,−1−t/2]].

## Worked example: calculation

The upper-half-plane branch isλ(t)=−1−t/2+i(2+t/4), henceλ(0)=−1+2i andλ′(0)=−1/2+i/4 exactly. The damping ratio starts at1/√5 andζ′(0)=(2−1/2)/(5√5)=3/(10√5)>0. The same value follows from an explicit left/right eigenvector substitution.
