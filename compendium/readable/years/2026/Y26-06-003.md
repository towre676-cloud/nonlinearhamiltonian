## Status

## Summary

The source proposes S²=C and (ST)³=ξC. We prove these equations for the unnormalized-by-signature finite operators, so the phase is computed rather than hidden. A genuine representation may require a metaplectic lift or a compensating scalar.

## Definitions

Let (A, Q) be a finite quadratic module with nondegenerate symmetric polarization b. On C[A] set S_xy=|A|^{−1/2}e^{−2πib(x, y)} and T_xx=e^{2πiQ(x)}.

Let C send e_x to e_{−x}, and let γ=|A|^{−1/2}Σ_xe^{2πiQ(x)}. These conventions omit a possible signature multiplier on S.

## Result name

Finite Fourier and Gauss operators satisfy a projective modular relation with a computed phase

## Result statement

S is unitary, S²=C and (ST)³=γC. For A=Z/3Z, Q(x)=x²/3, γ=i. Direct sums multiply γ and tensor the S, T operators.

## Proof

Character orthogonality gives Σ_y e^{−2πib(x+z, y)}=|A| when z=−x and zero otherwise, proving S²=C and unitarity. Complete the square in the quadratic Gauss sum: Q(y)−b(y, a)=Q(y−a)−Q(a). Thus Σ_y e^{2πi(Q(y)−b(y, a))}=√|A|γ e^{−2πiQ(a)}. In the matrix product (ST)², take a=x+z to obtain γ|A|^{−1/2}e^{2πi(Q(z)−Q(x+z))}. Multiplying once more by ST leaves an orthogonality sum supported at the final index−x; the remaining Q terms cancel and yield γC. Since ST is unitary, the equation also forces |γ|=1. Under orthogonal direct sum the exponential and finite sum factor, proving the tensor and phase multiplication laws.

## Worked example: setup

For the order-three module put ζ=e^{2πi/3}.

## Worked example: calculation

T=diag(1,ζ,ζ), b(x, y)=2xy/3, and γ=(1+2ζ)/√3=i. Therefore (ST)³=iC rather than C. Squaring S reverses the labels1 and2. For a direct sum of two order-three modules the Gauss phase is i²=−1 and the module has nine elements; this can be computed either by a nine-term sum or by tensor factorization. The phase therefore carries information beyond the group cardinality.
