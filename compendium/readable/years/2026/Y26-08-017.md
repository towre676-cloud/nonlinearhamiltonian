## Revised question retained in the annual source

What exactly does a scalar residual on a six-cycle measure, and why does it not determine the dimension ofH¹?

## Status

## Summary

The August15 source explicitly distinguishes a complete simplex from a six-cycle and questions negligible harmonic circulation. This distinction can be proved with a full discrete Hodge decomposition on the cycle, without inferring topology from the size of one observed residual.

## Result statement

Every edge vectorg decomposes uniquely asD₀f+h after fixingΣf=0, whereh=(Σg_j/6)(1,…,1). ThusdimH¹=1 and||h||²=(Σg_j)²/6, which can be zero for a nonzero residual.

## Proof

The incidence matrixD₀ has kernel the constant vertex vectors, since equality of adjacent values propagates around the connected graph. It therefore has rank5. Every vectorD₀f has zero edge sum by telescoping; this five-dimensional image is exactly the zero-sum hyperplane. Its orthogonal complement is the span of the all-ones edge vector, giving the stated projection and the unique zero-mean potential. Consequently the topological dimension is one regardless of how small or largeΣg is. Attaching a two-cell filling the cycle kills this cohomology, and a full simplex is contractible; by contrast the graphK₆ with no filled faces hasβ₁=15−6+1=10. Calling every densely connected graph a simplex would confuse these complexes. Gauge removal of a scalar edge field means solvingD₀f=g. On the cycle this is possible exactly whenΣg=0; one can constructf recursively by cumulative sums, withthe final consistency equation giving the same condition. The zero-mean convention then subtracts a constant and changes no edge differences. This is an explicit global compatibility test.
