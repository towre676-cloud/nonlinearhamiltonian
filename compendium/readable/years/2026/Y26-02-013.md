## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which scaling actually preserves an anisotropic cone metric, and what sign belongs in its harmonic indicial equation?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

A coordinate calculation determines the correct homothety weights. On an ordinary metric cone, separation of variables derives the radial indicial roots with an explicit Laplacian sign convention, preventing spectral claims from being inferred from an undefined cone analogy.

## Definitions

Let g=dr²+r²g_Σ+∑_j r^{2α_j}dz_j² for r>0, with a fixed link metric and real α_j.

For the ordinary d-dimensional cone omit the z terms and use Δ_Σφ=−λφ, λ≥0. The Laplacian is ∂_r²+(d−1)r⁻¹∂_r+r⁻²Δ_Σ.

A homothety D_c must satisfy D_c* g=c²g; the coordinate scaling of z is part of the definition.

## Result name

Exact homothety and harmonic radial exponents

## Result statement

D_c(r,y,z_j)=(cr,y,c^{1−α_j}z_j) satisfies D_c* g=c²g. On the ordinary cone, r^sφ is harmonic exactly when s(s+d−2)−λ=0, giving s=−(d−2)/2±√((d−2)²/4+λ).

## Proof

Under the map, dr² and r²g_Σ gain c². Each vertical term gains c^{2α_j} from r and c^{2(1−α_j)} from dz_j², again c². This fixes the weights without identifying them with a singular-value exponent from another coordinate convention. For harmonic modes differentiate r^s twice and substitute the link eigenfunction: Δ(r^sφ)=r^{s−2}[s(s+d−2)−λ]φ. Setting the bracket to zero proves the roots. If the nonnegative Laplacian −Δ is used instead, its link eigenvalue convention must change consistently; writing a plus λ with the present Δ_Σ convention gives the wrong roots.

## Worked example: setup

On R³ away from zero, the link is S² and degree-ℓ spherical harmonics have λ=ℓ(ℓ+1).

## Worked example: calculation

The roots are s=ℓ and s=−ℓ−1. For ℓ=1 these give the harmonic functions rY₁, a linear coordinate, and r^{−2}Y₁, a dipole singularity. With an additional α=2 vertical coordinate, the homothety scales z by c⁻¹ while r grows by c.

## Worked example: conclusion

Both persistence of a mode and its singularity depend on a derived radial exponent and boundary conditions.

## Limits

No universal learning spectrum, secretless verification theorem or Delzant integrality follows from a conical chart. The full anisotropic metric has additional radial-volume terms, so the ordinary-cone indicial equation cannot be transferred unchanged.

## Extension

Compute the full anisotropic Laplacian, specify self-adjoint boundary conditions near r=0 and classify square-integrable radial modes. For torus actions, state the symplectic moment-polytope hypotheses before asking for integral lattice normals.
