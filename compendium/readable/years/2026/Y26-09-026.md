## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which branch is admissible, and how close is it to the discriminant-zero boundary?

## Status

Classical mathematics with newly authored derivation or explicitly defined worked model

## Summary

The nonlinear gradient-wind residual is a small exact inverse problem. Its algebra already shows why a residual can have several roots and why uncertainty can grow near a branch merger. A numerical root should therefore carry a branch and a sensitivity certificate.

## Definitions

For fixed R>0 and real f,G, consider V²/R+fV−G=0. Let Δ=f²+4G/R.

An admissibility rule may restrict V to a sign or interval; it is additional data, not part of the quadratic equation itself.

## Result name

The gradient-wind quadratic has two branches and a singular sensitivity boundary

## Result statement

Real roots exist exactly when Δ≥0 and are V_±=(R/2)(−f±√Δ). For a simple root, dV/dG=1/(2V/R+f). Its sensitivity diverges as Δ approaches zero from above. For G>0 the two roots have opposite signs.

## Proof

Complete the square:(V+fR/2)²=R²Δ/4. This gives existence and the root expressions. Their product is−RG, so G>0 forces opposite signs. Differentiate the residual with respect to G to obtain(2V/R+f)V′−1=0 whenever the derivative coefficient is nonzero. At the two roots that coefficient is±√Δ, proving the sensitivity magnitude1/√Δ. At Δ=0 the roots merge and the implicit-function theorem no longer supplies a smooth branch.

## Worked example: setup

Use the newly declared dimensionless fixture R=f=1.

## Worked example: calculation

At G=1 the roots are(−1±√5)/2, so an imposed positive-velocity rule selects the positive branch. At G=−1/4 the only root is−1/2 with zero derivative coefficient. Decreasing G below−1/4 removes all real roots, while a small positive increase splits them by2√(G+1/4). A solver that merely reports a small residual can therefore hide both branch ambiguity and proximity to this singular boundary.

## Worked example: conclusion

The nonlinear observation has an exact solution set and a sharp local condition number.

## Limits

This algebra does not choose a meteorologically appropriate branch or validate a balance approximation. R,f,G must have compatible units and a common coordinate/sign convention before applying the formula to data.
