## Result name

A Bézout identity certifies inconsistency, while Buchberger replay certifies the final ideal basis

## Result statement

For f₁=x²−y,f₂=xy−1, the set g₁=x−y²,g₂=y³−1 is a Gröbner basis of the same ideal. Separately the system xy−1=0,x=0 is inconsistent with certificate1=x·y−(xy−1).

## Proof

We have g₁=yf₁−xf₂ and g₂=f₂−y g₁, so g₁,g₂ belong to the original ideal. Conversely f₁=(x+y²)g₁+y g₂ and f₂=y g₁+g₂, proving ideal equality by exact polynomial identities. Their leading monomials are x and y³. The only S-polynomial is y³g₁−xg₂=x−y⁵. Subtract g₁ to leave y²−y⁵=−y²g₂, so it reduces to zero. Buchberger’s criterion proves the basis claim. Its standard monomials are1,y,y², so the quotient has dimension three as a Q-vector space. For the inconsistency witness, substitution of a common zero into the displayed identity would give1=0, impossible. This proof needs no numerical approximation and is valid in every field where the rational coefficients are defined.
