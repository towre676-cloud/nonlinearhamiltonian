## Revised question retained in the annual source

Does a pooled correlation threshold certify one common law across two domains?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The source proposes z-scored global correlation, quantile monotonicity and balanced domain correlations. A covariance decomposition shows why these are different summaries. A positive pooled coefficient can be driven entirely by group means.

## Definitions

For random variables X,Y and domain label G, the law of total covariance is Cov(X,Y)=E Cov(X,Y|G)+Cov(E[X|G],E[Y|G]).

Global z-scoring subtracts one overall mean and rescales by one overall standard deviation. It does not remove the between-domain covariance term.

## Result name

Exact decomposition and a pooling counterexample

## Result statement

Pooled correlation can be positive although each within-domain covariance is zero. Averaging within-domain correlations is a different estimator and is undefined for a domain with zero variance.

## Proof

Write X=(X−E[X|G])+E[X|G] and similarly for Y. Expand their centered product. Conditional expectation kills the cross terms, leaving the displayed identity. For an exact example, let G be equally likely zero or one, and let U,V be independent mean-zero variables of variance one, independent of G. Set X=10G+U,Y=10G+V. Within each domain, Cov(X,Y|G)=0, but the group means are both 10G, so the between-domain covariance is 100Var(G)=25. Both total variances are 26, giving pooled correlation 25/26.

## Worked example: setup

Use U,V independent uniform signs ±1.

## Worked example: calculation

The full distribution has eight equally likely triples (G,U,V). Each domain correlation is zero, while the globally standardized pooled correlation remains 25/26≈0.961538. It easily exceeds a threshold 0.10 without any within-domain relationship.

## Worked example: conclusion

A threshold on one pooled statistic cannot substitute for a common-law theorem.

## Limits

This is a constructed counterexample and does not assert that the source dataset has this confounding structure.

## Extension

Test within-domain slopes and interactions in a shared model, report the covariance decomposition, and use fixed-system interventions to distinguish changing domain composition from changing the proposed mechanism.
