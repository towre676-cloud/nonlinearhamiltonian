## Summary

The dated example gives three costs7.25, a shared cost2 and revenue10 per sale. Treating these as a hypothetical finite probability model yields an exact expectation threshold. It does not determine the probability of a positive outcome without information about dependence.

## Definitions

Let X₁,X₂,X₃∈{0,1} indicate three sales, S=X₁+X₂+X₃ and Π=10S−95/4.

Assume only equal marginal probabilities P(X_i=1)=p. Independence, when invoked, is an additional explicit hypothesis.

## Result name

Expectation threshold and sharp dependence bounds

## Result statement

EΠ=30p−95/4, so positive expected surplus is equivalent to p>19/24. Profit occurs exactly when all three items sell. Its probability satisfies max(0,3p−2)≤P(Π>0)≤p; under independence it is p³.

## Proof

The total fixed cost is3·29/4+2=95/4. Linearity of expectation gives E S=3p without any independence assumption, proving the expected-value formula and threshold. Since two sales return20<95/4 and three return30>95/4, the positive event is the intersection of the three sale events. An intersection cannot exceed any individual event, proving the upper bound. The union bound on the three failures gives P(all sales)=1−P(at least one failure)≥1−3(1−p)=3p−2, together with nonnegativity. These bounds are sharp: perfect positive dependence gives the upper bound, while suitably disjoint failure events give the lower bound when p≥2/3; analogous constructions give zero for smaller p. Independence instead multiplies the marginal probabilities.
