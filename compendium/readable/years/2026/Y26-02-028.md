## Revised question retained in the annual source

How do exact counts over F_p and F_{p²} reconstruct the genus-two local polynomial?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

Newton’s identities recover the middle coefficient from two point counts. A completely enumerated smooth hyperelliptic curve over F₃ supplies a certificate for the local polynomial, while global rank and endomorphism claims remain separate questions.

## Definitions

Let C/F_p be smooth projective of genus two and let N_r=#C(F_{p^r}). Its Frobenius eigenvalues α₁,…,α₄ satisfy N_r=p^r+1−∑α_i^r.

Write P_p(X)=∏(X−α_i)=X⁴−a₁X³+a₂X²−pa₁X+p². The reciprocal symmetry and |α_i|=√p are background Weil theorems for curves.

Use C:y²=x⁵−x+1 over F₃, with its unique point at infinity.

## Result name

Reconstruction and an exact finite-field example

## Result statement

a₁=p+1−N₁ and a₂=[N₂−(p²+1)+a₁²]/2. For the displayed curve N₁=7 and N₂=15, giving P₃(X)=X⁴+3X³+7X²+9X+9.

## Proof

The first count gives ∑α_i=a₁. Newton’s identity ∑α_i²=a₁²−2a₂ combined with N₂ gives the formula. For smoothness, in F₃ the derivative of f=x⁵−x+1 is 2(x⁴+1). Dividing f by x⁴+1 leaves x+1, and x⁴+1 evaluated at −1 is two, so the gcd is one. An odd-degree squarefree degree-five hyperelliptic equation in odd characteristic has smooth projective genus two with one point at infinity. Enumerate x,y in F₃ and in F₉=F₃[u]/(u²+1), count y²=f(x) and add that infinity point; this gives the stated counts.

## Worked example: calculation

For x=0,1,2 in F₃, f(x)=1, so each x has two y values and N₁=6+1=7. Over F₉ exhaustive enumeration gives 14 affine points and one at infinity. The polynomial factors over R as (X²+aX+3)(X²+bX+3), with a,b=(3±√5)/2. Both |a|,|b|<2√3, so each quadratic has conjugate roots of modulus √3.

## Worked example: conclusion

The exact point enumeration and independent root-size check support the local arithmetic calculation. The constant coefficient p² and reciprocal linear coefficient encode the genus-two duality symmetry, so checking only the first trace would leave necessary polynomial data undetermined.

## Limits

This does not prove a global Mordell–Weil rank formula, a Sato–Tate law, BSD or a Jacobian endomorphism classification. Those require global data and additional theorems.
