## Revised question retained in the annual source

What finite spectral calculation can be certified for an ADE lattice before interpreting numerical renormalization intercepts?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

The D₄ Cartan matrix has an elementary characteristic polynomial and an exact positive spectrum. Its adjacency spectral radius and Gaussian covariance are reproducible invariants, whereas a fitted intercept requires a separately defined model and estimator.

## Definitions

Let the D₄ graph have one central vertex joined to three leaves. Its adjacency matrix is B and its simply laced Cartan matrix is C=2I−B.

The root lattice is the integer span of a basis whose Gram matrix is C; positive definiteness makes xᵀCx a positive quadratic energy.

For a specified positive matrix C, the centered Gaussian with precision C has covariance C⁻¹.

## Result name

Exact D₄ spectrum and determinant

## Result statement

B has eigenvalues √3,−√3,0,0. Consequently C has eigenvalues 2−√3,2+√3,2,2, determinant 4 and inverse trace 5.

## Proof

The two-dimensional leaf-difference subspace consists of vectors with central coordinate zero and leaf coordinates summing to zero. B annihilates it. The orthogonal complement is spanned by the central basis vector e and normalized leaf-sum f. In that basis B acts as [[0,√3],[√3,0]], proving its remaining eigenvalues. Subtracting from 2 gives the Cartan eigenvalues and positivity. Their product is (4−3)·4=4. Their reciprocal sum is (2+√3)+(2−√3)+1=5. This also predicts E|X|²=5 for the specified Gaussian. Any deterministic renormalization applied to this matrix needs an explicit map; these spectral invariants do not choose a unique such map or intercept.

## Worked example: setup

Use vertex order center, leaf 1, leaf 2, leaf 3.

## Worked example: calculation

C=[[2,−1,−1,−1],[−1,2,0,0],[−1,0,2,0],[−1,0,0,2]]. Its inverse has center diagonal 2, center–leaf entries 1, leaf diagonal 1 and distinct leaf off-diagonal entries 1/2. Direct multiplication gives the identity. The least eigenvalue is approximately .267949, a precise coercivity constant.

## Worked example: conclusion

The graph, normalization and invariant are fully specified and independently checkable.

## Limits

The historical D₄/E₆/E₈ and CM/non-CM intercepts are reported experimental claims; neither a=1/4 nor an ordering is derived by this graph calculation. Elliptic-curve arithmetic and lattice Gaussian spectra require an explicitly proved connection before comparison.

## Extension

Extend the calculation to E₆ and E₈ using their exact Cartan matrices, prove determinant and spectral-radius identities, then state a concrete renormalization map and investigate whether its limit is invariant under graph relabeling and admissible normalization.
