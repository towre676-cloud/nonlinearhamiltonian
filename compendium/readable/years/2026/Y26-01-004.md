## Revised question retained in the annual source

How do trace, determinant and discriminant classify a real binary quadratic form, and what remains arithmetically finer?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

The symmetric-matrix convention determines the cross-term factor and exposes an arithmetic error in the recovered example. Diagonalization then gives exact positivity criteria, while integer representation demonstrates information that trace and determinant alone cannot encode.

## Definitions

For Q(x,y)=mx²+nxy+ℓy² use A=[[m,n/2],[n/2,ℓ]], so Q=vᵀAv.

Its trace is τ=m+ℓ, determinant δ=mℓ−n²/4, and classical discriminant is D=n²−4mℓ=−4δ.

If one instead writes mx²+2nxy+ℓy², the matrix entry is n and determinant mℓ−n². These are different coefficient conventions.

## Result name

Orthogonal classification and positivity

## Result statement

The real eigenvalues are (τ±√((m−ℓ)²+n²))/2. A is positive definite exactly when m>0 and δ>0. For the recovered tuple (m,n,ℓ)=(3,2,1), τ=4 and δ=2 in the displayed single-cross-term convention; neither τ=5 nor δ=5 follows.

## Proof

Expanding det(λI−A) gives λ²−τλ+δ, whose quadratic formula gives the eigenvalues. Completing the square yields Q=m(x+n y/(2m))²+(δ/m)y² when m≠0. Thus m>0 and δ>0 imply strict positivity away from zero. Conversely positivity on (1,0) gives m>0; minimizing over x at fixed nonzero y forces δ/m>0. Real orthogonal changes of coordinates diagonalize A and preserve its eigenvalue multiset. Integer changes are much more restrictive: a unimodular substitution preserves the set of represented integers as well as discriminant, so real eigenvalue data do not by themselves classify integral forms.

## Worked example: setup

Compare Q₁=x²+5y² and Q₂=2x²+2xy+3y².

## Worked example: calculation

Both have discriminant −20. Q₁ represents 1 at (±1,0). Completing the square for Q₂ gives 2(x+y/2)²+(5/2)y². If y≠0 its value is at least 5/2, hence at least 3 as an integer; if y=0 a nonzero value is at least 2. Therefore Q₂ never represents 1. No GL₂(Z) change can identify them, despite their identical discriminant.

## Worked example: conclusion

Matrix positivity and integral equivalence are separate, computable questions with different invariants.

## Limits

The source’s asserted obstruction is not repaired by silently changing its tuple or convention. The examples above are newly specified. A full integral class-group computation requires primitivity, discriminant and proper-equivalence conventions.

## Extension

For each negative discriminant D, enumerate reduced primitive positive forms |b|≤a≤c with b²−4ac=D, prove the reduction bound a≤√(|D|/3), and compare the resulting ideal classes with exact representation data.
