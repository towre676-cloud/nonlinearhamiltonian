## Revised question retained in the annual source

Does the coordinate change biject Z², and are all forward and backward domain restrictions preserved?

## Status

Classical mathematics with newly authored derivation or explicitly defined worked model

## Summary

The reconstruction asked for GL₂(Z) transport and explicit completeness status. The key theorem is a domain theorem as much as a polynomial identity. An invertible rational change can omit integer solutions unless its image lattice is retained.

## Definitions

Let F be a binary form with integer coefficients and T an integer2×2 matrix of nonzero determinant. Define F_T(x)=F(Tx).

Unimodular means detT=±1, so T⁻¹ has integer entries. For general T, its integer image is the sublattice T Z².

## Result name

A unimodular substitution preserves a Thue solution set; a nonunimodular one needs a lattice condition

## Result statement

If T is unimodular, x↦Tx bijects integer solutions of F_T(x)=M with integer solutions of F(y)=M. For arbitrary nonsingular integral T, it bijects the first set only with solutions satisfying y∈T Z².

## Proof

The identity F_T(x)=F(Tx) proves the forward implication. If detT=±1, the adjugate formula gives an integral inverse, supplying the unique backward integer x. In the general case, y has an integer preimage precisely when it lies in the image lattice; invertibility over Q only proves existence of a rational preimage. Extra congruence, positivity or primitive-pair conditions must also be transported explicitly rather than dropped during a polynomial rewrite.

## Worked example: setup

Use F(a,b)=a³−2b³ and first T=[[1,1],[0,1]].

## Worked example: calculation

The transformed form is x³+3x²y+3xy²−y³, and the inverse(a,b)↦(a−b,b) is integral. Now take T=diag(2,1). The transformed equation for M=1 is8x³−2y³=1, impossible by parity, while the original equation has(a,b)=(1,0). There is no contradiction: the original solution lies outside the required lattice of even first coordinates.

## Worked example: conclusion

The transport becomes a proved bijection with a checkable image domain.

## Limits

Classifying316 forms into79 asserted equivalence classes requires their actual matrices and representative lists, which were not recovered. A determinant or a matching invariant alone does not exhibit an equivalence.

## Extension

Emit each transport matrix, its integral inverse or Smith-lattice conditions, and a proof that all branch domains and primitive constraints are preserved in both directions.
