## Revised question retained in the annual source

How can an observation subspace move with time while still carrying an exact closed dynamical equation?

## Status

Classical mathematics or a newly chosen finite model developed with proof; historical claims delimited

## Summary

The recovered identity B′+BA=TB includes a term absent from a fixed quotient. It is both necessary and sufficient for exact closure on every initial state. A rotating example shows why ignoring B′ can incorrectly reject a valid reduction or accept an invalid one.

## Definitions

Let A(t),B(t),T(t) be continuous matrix functions, with B continuously differentiable. Assume x′=A(t)x and define y(t)=B(t)x(t).

All statements are on a fixed interval where the linear ODE has an invertible fundamental matrix.

## Result name

Exact moving-quotient criterion

## Result statement

Every solution satisfies y′=T(t)y if and only if B′(t)+B(t)A(t)=T(t)B(t) for every t. For full-row-rank B, some T exists at a given time exactly when ker B⊆ker(B′+BA).

## Proof

The product rule gives y′=(B′+BA)x. The displayed identity therefore proves sufficiency. For necessity, fix a time t: the invertible fundamental solution maps initial states onto every possible x(t), so equality of the two proposed derivatives for all solutions forces equality of the matrices. The kernel condition is the preceding linear factorization theorem applied to B and B′+BA. When B has a right inverse R, one may construct T=(B′+BA)R, provided the kernel condition holds; it ensures that the construction acts correctly on every state. For a fixed observation B, the B′ term vanishes and the condition reduces to invariance of ker B under A.

## Worked example: setup

Take A=[[0,−1],[1,0]] and B(t)=(cos t,sin t).

## Worked example: calculation

Here B′=(−sin t,cos t) and BA=(sin t,−cos t), so B′+BA=0. The moving scalar y is therefore constant, with T=0. Indeed x(t) is the counterclockwise rotation of x(0), and B(t) projects onto the transported first coordinate. A fixed B=(1,0) gives BA=(0,−1), which cannot equal a scalar multiple of B; the first fixed coordinate alone has no closed first-order scalar equation valid for all states.

## Worked example: conclusion

The moving frame cancels the hidden rotation exactly and yields a genuine one-dimensional quotient.

## Limits

An approximate residual B′+BA−TB gives an error term, not exact closure. Rank changes, singular frames and nonlinearity require separate treatment; no claim about the source’s unrecovered application matrices is inferred.

## Extension

Given a residual norm bound ε(t) and a bound on hidden states, apply variation of constants to quantify reduced-output error; optimize B and T against this certified bound rather than only a fitted trajectory.
