## Summary

The April foundational ladder lists identities, operations, quotients and functors. These structures require distinct axioms; none follows from the tautology a=a alone. The first isomorphism theorem gives a precise instance in which a quotient is forced and every invariant map factors through it.

## Definitions

Let φ:G→H be a group homomorphism and N=kerφ. Define g∼g′ when g⁻¹g′∈N. Since N is normal, the cosets G/N form a group.

A map f:G→K into a group is constant on N-cosets when N⊂ker f. The quotient projection is q(g)=gN.

## Result name

First isomorphism and universal factorization

## Result statement

The map gN↦φ(g) is an isomorphism G/N→imφ. Every homomorphism f with N⊂ker f factors uniquely as f=f̄∘q.

## Proof

If gN=g′N, then g⁻¹g′∈N and φ(g)=φ(g′), proving well-definedness. The induced map preserves multiplication, is onto the image, and has trivial kernel, proving the isomorphism. For a compatible f define f̄(gN)=f(g); the same calculation proves it is well-defined and multiplicative. Surjectivity of q forces uniqueness. This is a concrete universal property: it specifies the admissible maps and their unique factorizations, rather than only comparing a sequence of mathematical words.

## Worked example: setup

Use φ:Z→Z/6Z, n↦n mod6.

## Worked example: calculation

The kernel is 6Z and Z/6Z is the quotient. The parity homomorphism n↦n mod2 factors through it because 6Z⊂2Z. The map n↦n mod4 does not, since six maps to two modulo four although six is identified with zero in the quotient.
