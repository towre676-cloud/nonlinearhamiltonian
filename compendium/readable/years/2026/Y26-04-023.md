## Revised question retained in the annual source

When is the proposed constitutive update a unique projection, and when do multiple admissible branches remain?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The source connects competing admissible states with minimization. A precise projection theorem explains the convex case, while a two-point set demonstrates how branch multiplicity appears once convexity is lost. No material-specific constitutive law is inferred.

## Definitions

Let M be symmetric positive definite and ||x||_M²=xᵀMx. Let U be a nonempty closed convex subset of finite-dimensional Euclidean space.

The weighted projection of a trial point a minimizes 1/2||z−a||_M² over z∈U.

## Result name

Projection theorem and nonconvex failure

## Result statement

There is a unique minimizer p. It is characterized by ⟨M(p−a),z−p⟩≥0 for every z∈U. A nonconvex admissible set can have several minimizers.

## Proof

Coercivity of M makes every bounded-energy minimizing sequence bounded; closedness gives a convergent subsequence with a minimizer. Strict convexity of the quadratic on a convex set gives uniqueness. Differentiating along the feasible segment p+t(z−p) at t=0 gives the variational inequality. Conversely, expanding ||z−a||_M² about p shows it is at least ||p−a||_M² plus ||z−p||_M² when that inequality holds, proving minimality and uniqueness. If U is a union of convex branches, this argument holds inside each branch but the branch minima can tie.

## Worked example: setup

Use M=I and U={−1,1}⊂R, with trial point a=0.

## Worked example: calculation

Both admissible points have energy 1/2, so there are two minimizers. Replacing U by the interval [−1,1] gives unique projection p=0 and energy zero. A small positive a makes the discrete branch +1 uniquely preferred.

## Worked example: conclusion

Competing branches can be analyzed as an explicit geometry of the admissible set.

## Limits

The source’s steel state variables and energy terms require independent constitutive assumptions, units and calibration. They do not follow from the projection theorem.

## Extension

Specify each admissible branch and dissipation potential, prove existence of every branch minimizer and map the exact parameter set where their energies exchange order.
