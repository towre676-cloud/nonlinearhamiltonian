## Revised question retained in the annual source

When do exact cofactor identities combine into a conserved quantity, and what are the domain restrictions?

## Status

Classical mathematics or a newly chosen finite model developed with proof; historical claims delimited

## Summary

The source reports six invariant factors checked by their cofactors, but does not supply the six polynomials. A general cofactor theorem and a nontrivial explicit vector field provide a reproducible constructive replacement without pretending to replay those missing checks.

## Definitions

A polynomial vector field X acts as a derivation on polynomials. A nonzero polynomial H is Darboux with cofactor K when X(H)=KH.

A rational first integral I satisfies X(I)=0 wherever its denominator is nonzero.

## Result name

Cofactor cancellation and invariant hypersurfaces

## Result statement

If X(H_j)=K_jH_j and integers m_j satisfy Σm_jK_j=0, then I=∏H_j^(m_j) is a rational first integral. Each zero hypersurface H_j=0 is invariant under the local flow.

## Proof

The derivation product and quotient rules give X(I)=IΣm_jX(H_j)/H_j=IΣm_jK_j on the open set where all negative-power factors are nonzero; cancellation proves the rational identity. Along a solution trajectory, H_j obeys the scalar linear equation dH_j/dt=K_j(x(t))H_j. Its unique solution is the initial value times an exponential integral, so an initial zero remains zero while the trajectory exists. Thus the hypersurface statement follows without dividing by H_j. Polynomial identities X(H_j)−K_jH_j=0 can be checked coefficient by coefficient, providing exact finite certificates. Cofactor dependence is sufficient for an integral, but a collection of invariant factors need not have any nontrivial integer relation among its cofactors.

## Worked example: setup

Choose X=(x²−y²)∂_x+2xy∂_y.

## Worked example: calculation

For H₁=y, X(H₁)=2xy=2xH₁. For H₂=x²+y², X(H₂)=2x(x²−y²)+4xy²=2xH₂. The cofactors agree, so I=(x²+y²)/y is constant on trajectories with y≠0. Direct differentiation gives numerator y·2x(x²+y²)−(x²+y²)·2xy=0. The excluded line y=0 is itself invariant and carries x′=x², so its trajectories require a separate chart.

## Worked example: conclusion

The same algebra proves conservation and records exactly where the rational formula is defined.

## Limits

No completeness of all first integrals follows from finding several factors. Constants of motion do not guarantee global existence, and the missing historical six-factor calculation is not claimed verified.

## Extension

Search cofactors in a bounded-degree polynomial space, solve exact linear dependence relations, and use the resulting integrals to reduce a specified flow on regular level sets while analyzing singular divisors separately.
