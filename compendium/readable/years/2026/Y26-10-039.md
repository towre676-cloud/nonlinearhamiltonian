## Revised question retained in the annual source

What quantitative inequality turns congruence information about a determinant into an exact algebraic relation?

## Status

Classical mathematics or a newly chosen finite model developed with proof; historical claims delimited

## Summary

The source discusses integral kernels, divisibility and determinant-zero arguments. The essential step is a strict comparison between a proved integer divisibility modulus and a proved archimedean bound. Without both, a determinant method supplies a candidate relation rather than a certificate.

## Definitions

Let A be an r×r integer matrix. Let M≥1 be an integer with M dividing det A.

For its row vectors v_i, Hadamard’s bound is |det A|≤∏_i||v_i||₂. A simpler entry bound |A_ij|≤B gives |det A|≤r^(r/2)Bʳ.

## Result name

Divisibility–size vanishing certificate

## Result statement

If M>|det A| as bounded independently, then det A=0. In particular M>∏||v_i||₂ suffices. Equality of the bound with M is inconclusive, since det A may be ±M.

## Proof

Write det A=Mk with k∈Z. If |det A|<M then |k|<1, forcing k=0. For completeness, Hadamard’s bound follows by Gram–Schmidt on the real row vectors: elementary orthogonalization preserves determinant magnitude, the resulting orthogonal row lengths do not exceed the original lengths, and the determinant magnitude of the orthogonal matrix is their product. Thus an exact upper bound can be derived from rational squared row norms without approximating the determinant. Congruence information must establish divisibility of the determinant itself; entrywise patterns often do this by subtracting rows or extracting column factors, but their powers must be justified. Once a nonzero integer relation among evaluation columns is found, it must be checked on the intended points and its global scope proved separately.

## Worked example: calculation

Evaluate the functions1,x,y,x² at the four integer points (0,0),(1,1),(2,4),(3,9). The last two columns of the resulting matrix agree, so its determinant vanishes and the exact kernel vector (0,0,1,−1) records y−x²=0. A separate sharpness example A=[[M,0],[0,1]] has determinant M: M-divisibility and an upper bound of M do not force zero.

## Limits

A relation found on finitely many points need not hold on an entire curve without a degree/intersection or structural argument. Historical claims such as8² dividing a particular determinant require the actual matrix for replay.

## Extension

For a given bounded integer-point set, build a rational evaluation matrix, prove its p-adic determinant valuation and archimedean bound independently, and use a degree argument to turn the certified kernel into a complete auxiliary-curve covering.
