## Revised question retained in the annual source

Under exactly which assumptions do the recovered genus, curvature and local monodromy formulas hold?

## Status

Classical mathematics or a newly chosen finite model developed with proof; historical claims delimited

## Summary

The source gives a superelliptic genus formula and its Gauss–Bonnet counterpart. Both become rigorous on the smooth compact normalization of a squarefree equation. The point at infinity must be included: overlooking its gcd contribution changes the genus.

## Definitions

Let P∈C[x] be squarefree of degree m≥1, let d≥2, and let C be the smooth projective normalization of yᵈ=P(x). Set δ=gcd(d,m).

The map x:C→P¹ has degree d. Curvature below is the Gaussian curvature of any smooth Riemannian metric on the compact oriented surface C.

## Result name

Genus, curvature and branch monodromy

## Result statement

g(C)=[(d−1)(m−1)−δ+1]/2 and ∫_C K dA=2π[d+δ−m(d−1)]. Around a simple zero of P, analytic continuation multiplies a local branch of y by exp(2πi/d).

## Proof

Near a simple zero a, write P(x)=(x−a)u(x), u(a)≠0. A holomorphic dth root of u exists locally, so a local parameter t can be chosen with x−a=tᵈ. The ramification index is d, contributing d−1 at each of the m finite branch points. At infinity the equation has orders d·ord(y)=m·ord(x); dividing by δ shows that there are δ points above infinity, each with ramification index d/δ. Their total contribution is d−δ. Riemann–Hurwitz gives 2g−2=−2d+m(d−1)+(d−δ)=m(d−1)−d−δ, which rearranges to the stated formula. Gauss–Bonnet gives ∫K=2π(2−2g), yielding the curvature expression. Finally, moving x−a once counterclockwise adds 2π to its argument, so its dth root and therefore y acquire exp(2πi/d). This is local covering monodromy, distinct from an arbitrary flat-connection holonomy.

## Worked example: calculation

For y²=x³−x, d=2,m=3,δ=1 gives g=1 and total curvature zero. For y³=x⁴−1, d=3,m=4,δ=1 gives g=3 and total curvature −8π. In the latter, every finite root has local monodromy exp(2πi/3), while infinity is also totally ramified.

## Worked example: conclusion

The recovered formulas hold after the singular affine equation is replaced by its smooth compact curve.

## Limits

Repeated roots require revised local ramification data and can make the displayed genus formula false. Gauss–Bonnet controls total curvature, not a pointwise curvature sign, and an affine noncompact chart has boundary/end corrections.

## Extension

For yᵈ=∏(x−a_j)^(r_j), derive ramification contributions d−gcd(d,r_j), include infinity, and separate connected components when the equation is a nontrivial common power.
