## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which Fourier and entropy tests certify convergence to uniformity, and does the first mode alone detect every nonuniform state?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

The circle heat equation has an exact Fourier solution and entropy dissipation law. A second-harmonic density supplies a counterexample to treating first-mode pinning as a complete test for nonuniformity.

## Definitions

Let p(θ,t)≥0 integrate to one on [0,2π], be periodic and solve ∂_tp=D∂²_θp with constant D>0.

Define p̂_n=∫₀^{2π}e^{−inθ}p(θ)dθ and u=1/(2π). Relative entropy is KL(p||u)=∫p log(p/u).

The normalization makes p̂₀=1; it differs from Fourier conventions that put 1/(2π) in every coefficient.

## Result name

Mode decay, entropy and a partial observation bound

## Result statement

p̂_n(t)=e^{−Dn²t}p̂_n(0). Also d_tKL=−D∫p|∂_θlog p|²≤0 for positive smooth p. Pinsker’s inequality gives KL≥(1/2)||p−u||₁²≥(1/2)|p̂₁|². A vanishing first mode does not imply p=u.

## Proof

Multiply the PDE by e^{−inθ} and integrate twice by parts; periodicity gives the scalar ODE p̂_n′=−Dn²p̂_n. Differentiate entropy, use mass conservation and integrate by parts once to obtain dissipation. The Fourier observation inequality follows from |∫e^{−iθ}(p−u)|≤||p−u||₁ and natural-log Pinsker. The density p=(1+a cos2θ)/(2π), 0<|a|<1, has p̂₁=0 but p̂₂=a/2≠0, so a first-mode criterion sees only its declared observable. A nonzero liminf of |p̂₁| would obstruct convergence to uniformity; the converse is not valid without restrictions to a first-harmonic family.

## Worked example: setup

Start p=(1+.6 cosθ)/(2π) and choose D=2.

## Worked example: calculation

The exact solution is (1+.6e^{−2t}cosθ)/(2π), so p̂₁=.3e^{−2t} and KL≥.045e^{−4t}. For a pure second harmonic of the same amplitude, its coefficient decays as .3e^{−8t}. Fourier order changes the relaxation rate even though both examples have the same initial amplitude.

## Worked example: conclusion

The observation order and normalization are explicit, and their limits are independently visible. Checking every Fourier coefficient would determine the probability measure, whereas retaining one coefficient generally leaves many indistinguishable densities.

## Limits

A χ-dependent singular diffusion coefficient needs a specified χ trajectory and uniform analytic control. First-mode persistence is one operational definition of pinning, not a classification of all metastability or symmetry breaking.

## Extension

For variable positive diffusivity and a drift potential, derive the invariant density, prove a spectral-gap or log-Sobolev estimate and determine which finite set of Fourier observations robustly distinguishes the proposed regimes.
