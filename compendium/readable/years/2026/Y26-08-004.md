## Revised question retained in the annual source

When can a rank-one determinant ratio certify the number of characteristic roots in a contour?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The August4 source reports a larger gain window for a secant surrogate, but explicitly leaves the contour supremum and the surrogate-to-target remainder unresolved. A classical determinant-lemma theorem clarifies why structure helps and what still has to be proved.

## Definitions

LetF(z) be a square holomorphic matrix on and inside a simple closed contourΓ, withdetF nonzero onΓ. LetΔ(z)=u(z)v(z)ᵀ be holomorphic rank at most one; the transpose is algebraic, with no z-dependent complex conjugation.

## Result name

Structured root-count preservation

## Result statement

If sup_Γ|vᵀF^{-1}u|<1, then det(F+Δ) anddetF have the same number of zeros insideΓ, counting multiplicity. A further perturbationR needs its own certified bound, such as sup_Γ||(F+Δ)^{-1}R||<1.

## Proof

The matrix determinant lemma givesdet(F+tuvᵀ)=detF·(1+tq), q=vᵀF^{-1}u. If|q|<1 then1+tq≠0 for0≤t≤1 onΓ. The determinant is a continuous boundary-nonvanishing homotopy, so its winding number and argument-principle zero count remain constant. There are no poles becauseF+tΔ is holomorphic, even though the quotientq may have poles inside. For a full remainderR, the factorization(F+Δ+R)=(F+Δ)[I+(F+Δ)^{-1}R] and a Neumann-series bound give a second boundary-nonvanishing homotopy. A grid of contour samples is insufficient unless a derivative or interval bound fills the gaps. The argument counts algebraic multiplicities, so it remains useful for a cluster whose individual roots cannot be tracked differentiably. The count alone does not supply a differentiable branch for each individual root, or preserve a damping ordering within the cluster. The contour must remain fixed and avoid all zeros throughout the homotopy; otherwise its integer winding number can change.

## Worked example: setup

On|z|=1 takeF(z)=diag(z,z−3), Δ=δe₂e₂ᵀ with constantδ.

## Worked example: calculation

Hereq=δ/(z−3), so|q|≤|δ|/2. Every|δ|<2 preserves the count of one root inside the unit circle. The unstructured estimate||F^{-1}||||Δ||≤|δ| would only give|δ|<1. The improvement is exact and follows from the perturbation acting only on the second coordinate.

## Worked example: conclusion

A structure-aware bound can be much sharper while remaining a rigorous finite-contour theorem.

## Limits

The historical interval(10.4716,29.5284) is not certified here: its actual surrogate and uniform remainder were not recovered.

## Extension

Return interval contour panels, a rigorous bound forq on each panel and a second target-remainder bound; then certify the same root count for the actual model, not only its surrogate.
