## Summary

The HED source identifies an annealed statistic mislabeled as a Bowen root. A self-similar model makes the legitimate dimension equation precise, and Jensen’s inequality proves the direction of the annealed/quenched distinction in a random setting.

## Definitions

For a deterministic two-map similarity system with common ratio r∈(0,1/2), separated images and word length n, let Z_n(s)=2^n r^{ns}. Its pressure is P(s)=lim n^{−1}logZ_n(s).

For a random positive partition function Z_n, define finite annealed pressure n^{−1}logE Z_n and quenched pressure n^{−1}E logZ_n when both expectations exist.

## Result name

Pressure distinguishes a deterministic Bowen root from an annealed random statistic

## Result statement

The deterministic pressure is log2+slogr, with zero d=log2/log(1/r). Under the separation hypothesis this is the Hausdorff dimension of the self-similar set. For every random positive Z_n with integrable logarithm, quenched pressure is at most annealed pressure.

## Proof

The pressure formula follows by taking a logarithm of the exact product. For the dimension upper bound, cover the set by 2^n intervals of length r^n; for s>d their total s-content tends to zero. For the lower bound assign mass 2^{−n} to each cylinder. Separation gives a constant C such that every interval of length ℓ meets only boundedly many cylinders at the scale r^n comparable with ℓ, so its mass is at most Cℓ^d. The mass-distribution argument then forces Hausdorff dimension at least d. For the random inequality apply concavity of log: E logZ_n≤logE Z_n. Equality needs an essentially constant partition function at that n. Taking limits preserves the inequality when the limits exist. No equality of their zeros follows without extra assumptions.

## Worked example: setup

Take the separated middle-third system, r=1/3, and separately a one-step random variable Z equal to 1 or 9 with equal probability.

## Worked example: calculation

The deterministic dimension is log2/log3. In the random example E logZ=log3, whereas logE Z=log5, a strict difference log(5/3). These are not two numerical estimates of the same pressure. They answer different averaging questions, even though both are made from the same positive observations.

## Worked example: conclusion

The corrected workflow can return a pressure estimate without assigning an unsupported Bowen dimension.

## Limits

The lower-bound proof uses separation and exact similarity. Overlaps, conformal distortion, matrix products or random dynamics require their own pressure theorem; a bootstrap interval does not supply it.

## Extension

For the actual generator family, prove the required expansion/distortion/separation conditions, then estimate its deterministic pressure with controlled truncation before reporting a root and uncertainty.
