## Definitions

Let H=ℓ²(N_0) with orthonormal basis e_0,e_1,…, and let S be the unilateral shift Se_j=e_{j+1}. A bounded Fredholm operator has closed range and finite-dimensional kernel and cokernel.

Its index is ind T=dim ker T−dim(H/ran T). The adjoint satisfies S*e_0=0 and S*e_{j+1}=e_j.

## Result name

An index counts a kernel–cokernel defect in an explicit infinite model

## Result statement

S is Fredholm with index−1, S* has index1, and for every k≥1, ind(S^k)=−k. In particular a finite-dimensional square matrix cannot reproduce this nonzero index.

## Proof

S preserves norm, so its kernel is zero and its range is the closed hyperplane of sequences with zeroth coordinate zero. Its cokernel is spanned by e_0, giving index−1. For S*, the range is all H and the kernel is span(e_0), giving index1. The kth shift has range equal to the closed subspace whose first k coordinates vanish and has no kernel, proving index−k. For a linear endomorphism T of an n-dimensional space, rank-nullity gives dim kerT=n−rankT and dim cokerT=n−rankT, so its index is zero. Truncating an infinite shift to an n-by-n matrix therefore introduces an extra kernel or boundary effect that exactly cancels the infinite cokernel defect. This is a concrete reason that a finite spectral computation requires a separate theorem before being identified with a geometric or analytic index.

## Worked example: setup

Take S² and compare its n=4 truncation.

## Worked example: calculation

The infinite operator has kernel0 and cokernel span(e_0,e_1), so index−2. The four-by-four shift by two has kernel span(e_2,e_3) and cokernel span(e_0,e_1), so index0.

## Worked example: conclusion

Both defects are explicitly visible; they cannot be merged into one unspecified numerical invariant.
