## Summary

The source gives a graph resolvent and reciprocal diagonal constants. These admit a sharp finite-dimensional variational proof. It is important to distinguish quadratic regularization, used here, from the separately recovered series with the linear term λΣh_i.

## Definitions

For a graph on n≥2 vertices, let L be its Laplacian and λ>0. Put Q=L+λI, Π=I−11ᵀ/n and K=Q⁻¹−11ᵀ/(λn).

For real v, vᵀQv=Σ_(ij∈E)(v_i−v_j)²+λΣ_i v_i². Restrict to the mean-zero space1⊥.

## Result name

Sharp one-coordinate energy minimization

## Result statement

K=ΠQ⁻¹Π is positive definite on1⊥. For each vertex i and real a, min{vᵀQv:1ᵀv=0,v_i=a}=a²/K_ii, attained at v=aKe_i/K_ii. Thus the least energy for a coordinate of magnitude a is a²/max_iK_ii.

## Proof

Since L1=0, Q⁻¹1=λ⁻¹1, which proves the formula with Π. On1⊥, Q is positive definite and K is its inverse. For v in that space, e_iᵀv=(Ke_i)ᵀQv because QKe_i=Πe_i. Cauchy–Schwarz in the Q-inner product gives |v_i|²≤[(Ke_i)ᵀQ(Ke_i)]vᵀQv=K_ii vᵀQv. Equality occurs for a scalar multiple of Ke_i; its ith component fixes the scalar to a/K_ii. Since Πe_i is nonzero for n≥2, K_ii>0. Taking the best vertex proves the final statement. Uniqueness can also be checked without an inequality: every other admissible vector is v+u with u_i=0 and mean zero. The cross term vᵀQu=(a/K_ii)(Πe_i)ᵀu vanishes, leaving the strictly positive increment uᵀQu unless u=0. This is an exact Euler–Lagrange certificate for the constrained minimizer.

## Limits
