## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which exact computations on y²+y=x³−x prove that a visible point has infinite order, and what still requires a descent?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The July29 source reports multiples, a 2-descent and rank-one conclusions for the conductor37 curve. Exact group arithmetic and reductions modulo small primes give a self-contained rank lower bound. The stronger Selmer and Tate–Shafarevich claims remain conditional on an unavailable descent certificate.

## Definitions

Let E/Q be y²+y=x³−x with identity O at infinity and P=(0,0). Negation is−(x,y)=(x,−1−y). The discriminant is Δ=37. A prime is good when it does not divideΔ.

## Result name

Infinite order without adopting the reported descent

## Result statement

E(Q) has trivial torsion and P has infinite order; consequently its rank is at least one. The displayed calculation alone does not prove rank exactly one.

## Proof

For distinct points use slopeλ=(y₂−y₁)/(x₂−x₁); doubling uses λ=(3x₁²−1)/(2y₁+1). Then x₃=λ²−x₁−x₂ and y₃=−y₁−1+λ(x₁−x₃). These formulas give exact rational multiples. Counting solutions over F₃ and F₅ gives group orders7 and8, respectively. Good reduction injects the prime-to-p rational torsion into E(F_p). Any torsion prime other than3,5 would therefore divide gcd(7,8)=1. A 3-primary torsion point would inject at5 and require3|8; a 5-primary one would inject at3 and require5|7. Neither is possible, so the torsion subgroup is trivial. P is a finite point and hence nonzero, proving infinite order. This argument supplies no upper rank bound. A certified 2-Selmer calculation of dimension one, together with the found point and no rational2-torsion, would supply that missing bound.
