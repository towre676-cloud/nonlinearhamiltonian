## Revised question retained in the annual source

What exact scalar equation follows from a balanced gain–loss coupler, and when is it actually Mathieu’s equation?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

A direct elimination derives the scalar Hill coefficient and exposes both the gain derivative and its square. A periodic gain therefore generally creates more harmonics than Mathieu form permits; trace identities also constrain directional discriminant claims.

## Definitions

Consider a′=γ(t)a+iκb and b′=iκa−γ(t)b, with constant real κ≠0 and real differentiable periodic γ(t).

Hill form is y″+Q(t)y=0 with periodic Q. Mathieu form is the special Q(t)=A−2q cos2t, up to a declared phase and period rescaling.

The first-order coefficient matrix has trace zero, so its monodromy has determinant one by Liouville’s formula.

## Result name

Exact reduction and a directional trace restriction

## Result statement

The first component satisfies a″+[κ²−γ²−γ′]a=0. For γ=g cos2t this coefficient is κ²−g²/2−(g²/2)cos4t+2g sin2t, so it is generally Hill but not Mathieu. For any determinant-one 2×2 monodromy M, tr M⁻¹=tr M.

## Proof

Differentiate the first equation: a″=γ′a+γa′+iκb′. Substitute b′ and use iκb=a′−γa. The a′ terms cancel, leaving a″=(γ′+γ²−κ²)a. The harmonic expansion follows from cos²2t=(1+cos4t)/2 and γ′=−2g sin2t. The inverse trace identity follows from the explicit adjugate of a 2×2 matrix: when det M=1 its diagonal entries are exchanged under inversion. Thus merely traversing one fixed reciprocal evolution in reverse cannot generate unequal scalar traces; a directional asymmetry needs an explicitly changed dynamics or observable.

## Worked example: setup

Take constant γ=g and κ²>g².

## Worked example: calculation

Then a″+Ω²a=0 with Ω=√(κ²−g²). Over period T the scalar monodromy is [[cosΩT,sinΩT/Ω],[−ΩsinΩT,cosΩT]], determinant one and discriminant 2cosΩT. At κ=2,g=1,T=π/√3 it equals −I and has trace −2. At κ=g the limiting scalar equation is a″=0 and monodromy is a shear, so repeated eigenvalue one need not mean identity.

## Worked example: conclusion

The full coefficient and monodromy are derived before any small-parameter approximation is applied.

## Limits

The source’s complete original coupling equations were not recovered. This is a newly specified balanced coupler benchmark, not a reconstructed historical model. A claimed directional correction ±cθq requires separate equations and symmetry analysis.

## Extension

For the actual supplied periodic coefficients, derive the scalar equation without discarding harmonics, compute the determinant-preserving monodromy and compare perturbative traces with a convergent numerical integrator.
