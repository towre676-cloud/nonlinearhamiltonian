## Summary

The August22 source reports a103-support weighted row and an unfinished coefficient search. Its constituent configurations and rows are absent. A general dual-certificate theorem explains how such a result can be proved and why a few scalar frontier statistics cannot substitute for the omitted certificate.

## Definitions

Letx encode a finite configuration and suppose all allowedx satisfyAx≥b. A nonnegative vectory is a dual multiplier. For equal-cardinality finite setsK,L define swap distance as the least number of single-element removals and insertions transformingK intoL.

## Result name

Nonnegative row combination and exact swap metric

## Result statement

Everyy≥0 yieldsyᵀAx≥yᵀb. To prove a target inequalitycᵀx≥d by this method, it suffices to exhibit y withyᵀA=cᵀ andyᵀb≥d. For equal-size sets,d_swap(K,L)=|K\L|=|K△L|/2.

## Proof

Multiply each valid row by its nonnegative multiplier and sum; inequality direction is preserved. Exact equality of coefficients and comparison of constants provide a finite certificate even when the search that found it was complicated. A claimed support size103 is only a compression statistic until those103 rows and rational weights are available. For swap distance, every element ofK\L must be removed at least once, giving the lower bound. Pair its elements arbitrarily withL\K, which has the same size, and perform that many swaps to attain it. Thus a claimed lower bound of six requires an actual overlap bound or another verified obstruction for the source configurations.
