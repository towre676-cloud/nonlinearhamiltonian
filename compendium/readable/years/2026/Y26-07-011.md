## Summary

The July5 spectrum has a complete finite interpretation. Under the simple-graph hypothesis it identifies a five-vertex graph uniquely up to isomorphism. A separate graph-to-knot assignment is still required before this becomes a knot invariant.

## Definitions

Let A be the adjacency matrix of a simple undirected graph on five vertices with spectrum{1+√5,1−√5,−2,0,0}. For such graphs trA²=2|E| and trA³=6 times the triangle count.

## Result name

A small spectral-rigidity result

## Result statement

The graph is K₅ with two disjoint edges removed. Its characteristic polynomial is t²(t+2)(t²−2t−4)=t⁵−8t³−8t².

## Proof

The squared eigenvalues sum to16, so there are eight edges, exactly two fewer than K₅. Their cubes sum to24, so there are four triangles. Deleting two adjacent edges from K₅ destroys five triangles, leaving five, because the deleted edges share one triangle. Deleting disjoint edges destroys six, leaving four. Thus the two missing edges must be disjoint, proving uniqueness. Conversely pair the missing-edge endpoints into two groups of size two and leave the fifth vertex alone. Differences within either pair give two zero eigenvectors. The difference between the pair-constant vectors gives eigenvalue−2. On the remaining pair-symmetric/central subspace the quotient matrix[[2,1],[4,0]] has eigenvalues1±√5. This constructs the spectrum rather than only checking moments.
