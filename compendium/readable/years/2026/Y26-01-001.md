## Revised question retained in the annual source

When does adjoining saturating coordinates leave a finite system’s kernel unchanged, and what does identical input really guarantee?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

Truncated addition gives an associative finite monoid whose minimal ideal is a single absorbing state. Taking products with these monoids preserves the cardinality of another monoid’s minimal ideal, while trajectory invariance follows separately from deterministic recursion.

## Definitions

For an integer k≥0 let L_k={0,…,k} with a⊕b=min(a+b,k). A two-sided ideal I of a monoid S is nonempty and satisfies SI∪IS⊂I.

For a finite monoid S, its kernel means its unique minimal nonempty two-sided ideal K(S). This meaning must be distinguished from the preimage of zero under a homomorphism.

A deterministic driven system has x_{t+1}=T(x_t,u_t), with a specified initial state.

## Result name

Kernel of a product with saturating chains

## Result statement

For any finite monoid S and finitely many k_i, K(S×∏L_{k_i})=K(S)×∏{k_i}. Hence its cardinality is |K(S)|. Two executions with the same initial state and identical input streams have identical trajectories.

## Proof

Associativity follows because both (a⊕b)⊕c and a⊕(b⊕c) equal min(a+b+c,k); zero is the identity and k is absorbing. Every nonempty ideal of L_k contains k, obtained by multiplying any of its elements by k, so {k} is minimal. In S×L_k, K(S)×{k} is an ideal. Conversely, from any (s,a) in an ideal, multiplication by (1,k) produces (s,k). Since the ideal SsS contains K(S), further left and right multiplications produce K(S)×{k}. Thus this is the minimal ideal. Iterate for finitely many coordinates. For trajectories, equality at time zero is assumed; equality at time t and equality of u_t give equality at t+1 by applying the same function T. Induction proves the assertion without any need for a compiler-specific theorem.

## Worked example: setup

Let S be the two-element group C₂ and adjoin L₂×L₃.

## Worked example: calculation

The product has 2·3·4=24 states, but its minimal ideal is C₂×{2}×{3}, containing exactly two states. Starting its saturating coordinates at (0,0) and adding (1,2) repeatedly gives (0,0),(1,2),(2,3),(2,3),…. The group coordinate can continue changing after saturation.
