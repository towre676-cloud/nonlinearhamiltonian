## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which random-product model really yields a law of large numbers, a Gaussian fluctuation and a partition threshold?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

Independent exponential increments give an exact product model in which all three calculations can be checked. It illustrates the hypotheses needed before transferring a Furstenberg or Hagedorn-style statement to an unspecified dynamical measure.

## Definitions

Let X₁,X₂,… be independent exponential random variables of rate one, and define κ_k=exp(−∑_{j=1}^kX_j)∈(0,1).

Use natural logarithms, so −logκ_k is an additive sum with mean k and variance k.

For γ>0 and real β define the moment partition Z_k(β)=E[κ_k^{−βγ}], whenever finite.

## Result name

Product law, CLT and exact integrability boundary

## Result statement

k⁻¹logκ_k→−1 almost surely and (logκ_k+k)/√k converges in law to N(0,1). Moreover Z_k(β)=(1−βγ)^{−k} for βγ<1 and is infinite for βγ≥1. The sample-mean large-deviation rate is I(y)=y−1−log y for y>0.

## Proof

The strong law and classical independent finite-variance CLT apply to the X_j; multiplying their centered sum by minus one preserves the standard Gaussian limit. Direct integration gives E e^{tX}=∫₀^∞e^{−(1−t)x}dx=(1−t)⁻¹ for t<1, and divergence otherwise. Independence raises this to the kth power, proving the exact threshold. The Legendre transform of log E e^{tX}=−log(1−t) maximizes ty+log(1−t) at t=1−1/y, yielding the rate. Near y=1, Taylor expansion gives I(y)=(y−1)²/2−(y−1)³/3+…. The rate is centered at the actual mean, which must be specified before copying a quadratic expansion.

## Worked example: setup

Choose k=4, γ=2 and β=1/4.

## Worked example: calculation

Then βγ=1/2 and Z₄=16. At β=1/2 the integral diverges. The log product has exact mean −4 and variance four, whereas the product mean is Eκ₄=(E e^{−X})⁴=1/16. Exponentiating the mean log gives e^{−4}, a different statistic.

## Worked example: conclusion

The example distinguishes typical logarithmic behavior, ordinary moments and a genuine integrability threshold.

## Limits

Independence, the increment distribution and the moment definition are new explicit hypotheses, not recovered properties of a Furstenberg process. A physical partition sum with exponentially growing state counts can have the opposite β inequality.

## Extension

For a supplied stationary dependent process, prove ergodicity, mixing or a spectral-gap condition sufficient for a CLT, calculate its cumulant generating function and establish the exact domain of its partition function.
