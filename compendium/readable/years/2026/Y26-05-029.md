## Revised question retained in the annual source

Reconstruction: What exact class obstructs extending a first-order deformation to second order?

## Status

Classical result or newly stipulated finite model; historical claims explicitly delimited

## Summary

The homological followup contains the Maurer–Cartan equation and the class of one-half the bracket square. We state a differential graded Lie version with explicit signs and solve a finite obstruction example. This gives constructive deformation content without extending a finite arity check to an unrestricted L∞ theorem.

## Definitions

Work over Q in a differential graded Lie algebra (L,d,[,]) with d of degree+1. For a degree-one formal series α=t a+t²b+O(t³), the equation is dα+[α,α]/2=0.

The differential obeys d[x,y]=[dx,y]+(−1)^{deg x}[x,dy]. A first-order deformation has da=0.

## Result name

A second-order Maurer–Cartan obstruction is a cohomology class, not a numerical residual

## Result statement

A first-order a extends through order t² exactly when the degree-two cocycle [a,a]/2 is a boundary. Its obstruction class lies in H²(L). In a finite model with L¹=Qa⊕Qb, L²=Qc, db=c, da=0 and [a,a]=2c, the extension α=t a−t²b exists exactly.

## Proof

Expand the formal equation. The t coefficient is da. The t² coefficient is db+[a,a]/2, so b exists exactly when the bracket square is the negative of a boundary. Its differential vanishes because da=0 and the derivation identity gives d[a,a]=[da,a]−[a,da]=0. Thus it defines a cohomology class, not merely a vector norm. In the displayed algebra take every bracket involving b or c to be zero; all Jacobi identities then vanish because the only nonzero bracket lands in central c. The derivation identity also holds, including d[a,b]=0 and its right side−[a,c]=0. For α=t a−t²b, dα=−t²c and [α,α]/2=t²c, cancelling at every order. If instead db=0, c is a nonzero cohomology class and no coefficient can cancel it.

## Worked example: calculation

With db=c the second-order correction is−b and the exact deformation is polynomial in t. With d=0 the same [a,a]/2=c is nonzero in H², so even an arbitrarily small nonzero t cannot solve the formal equation beginning with a. Its norm can be made small by t, but the obstruction class remains algebraically nonzero.

## Worked example: conclusion

The obstruction criterion is exact and its solvable and obstructed cases are explicit.

## Limits

An L∞ algebra has higher brackets and additional higher-order obstructions. Verifying identities through arity three does not establish all arities or convergence of a formal series.

## Extension

For a specified finite filtered L∞ algebra, prove the relevant identities, compute successive obstruction classes and use nilpotence to show the correction process terminates.
