## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which continuation function defines Future, and when does a finite-horizon agreement certify the entire future?

## Status

Classical mathematics with newly authored derivation or explicitly defined worked model

## Summary

The TTL work repeatedly distinguishes state counts from the state semantics that produced them. A deterministic weighted transducer supplies an exact finite theorem: states are equivalent when every continuation has the same cost, and the equivalence can be computed by stable partition refinement.

## Definitions

Let X be a finite state set and A a finite input alphabet. Each state/input pair has a deterministic successor δ(x,a) and finite real immediate cost c(x,a).

For a word w, let C_x(w) be its accumulated cost from x, with C_x(empty)=0. Define x∼_L y when these costs agree for every word of length at most L.

## Result name

Future equivalence is a right congruence when continuation costs are retained

## Result statement

Full equality of all continuation costs is preserved by every input transition and gives a well-defined weighted quotient. The finite-horizon partitions refine monotonically; once two consecutive partitions agree, no longer word can separate them. There are at most|X|−1 strict refinements.

## Proof

The recursion is C_x(aw)=c(x,a)+C_(δ(x,a))(w). Thus equal full futures imply equal immediate costs and equal futures after each a, proving the congruence. The same recursion shows that∼_(L+1) is determined by immediate-cost equality and the∼_L classes of all successors. If∼_L=∼_(L+1), this recurrence makes every later refinement identical by induction. Every strict refinement increases the number of classes, beginning with one class at L=0 and ending with at most|X|, proving the bound. No enumeration count alone establishes that the implementation used this equivalence.

## Worked example: setup

Use a newly declared TTL counter with states0,1,2, a tick that decreases a positive counter, and a read that costs one only at zero and then resets to two.

## Worked example: calculation

States one and two have identical one-step read cost zero and tick cost zero. The word tick-read separates them: starting at one costs one after expiry, while starting at two costs zero. A quotient based only on current read cost therefore merges states with different continuations. The second refinement separates them, exactly recording persistent time-to-expiry.

## Worked example: conclusion

The Future object becomes an explicit continuation semantics with a finite completeness test for its quotient.

## Limits

This theorem assumes deterministic transitions and finite additive costs. Nondeterministic min-plus behavior, infinite costs, resets and joint allocation require an appropriately enriched semantics; the reportedlarge TTL quotients are not reproduced from absent tables.

## Extension

Define the actual TTL machine and its cost/terminal-observation semantics, emit a stable partition with transition witnesses, and certify every merge by the recursive congruence equations.
