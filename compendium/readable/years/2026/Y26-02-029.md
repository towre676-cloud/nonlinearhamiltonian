## Revised question retained in the annual source

What does the displayed Swan-conductor sum measure, and why is it not an automatic additive decomposition of arithmetic rank?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

A one-dimensional representation with a wild ramification break gives an explicit conductor while its dimension remains one. This separates a valid local invariant from an undefined global rank decomposition and records the exact hypotheses behind the sum.

## Definitions

Let L/K be a finite Galois extension of local fields, G₀ its inertia group and G_i its lower-numbered ramification groups for i≥1.

For a finite-dimensional characteristic-zero representation V, define Sw(V)=∑_{i≥1}|G_i|/|G₀|·codim V^{G_i}.

The Artin conductor is codim V^{G₀}+Sw(V). Only finitely many terms are nonzero because the ramification filtration eventually becomes trivial.

## Result name

A one-dimensional wild-break calculation

## Result statement

If G₀=G₁=…=G_b=C_p and G_{b+1}=1, and V is a nontrivial one-dimensional character of C_p, then dim V=1, Sw(V)=b and its Artin conductor is b+1. A nontrivial tame character has Swan conductor zero.

## Proof

For every 1≤i≤b the group acts nontrivially on the one-dimensional space, so V^{G_i}=0 and its codimension is one. The index factor |G_i|/|G₀| is also one. Summing b terms gives b. At i>b the acting group is trivial and the codimension is zero. Inertia invariants are zero, adding one to the Artin conductor. For a tame character the wild groups G_i, i≥1, act trivially, hence every term vanishes even though inertia itself may act nontrivially. Additivity of invariant codimensions under direct sums gives conductor additivity, which is a different statement from an additive formula for a global arithmetic rank.

## Worked example: setup

Use a characteristic-two Artin–Schreier model y²−y=t^{−3} over F₂((t)).

## Worked example: calculation

Its standard ramification theorem gives a single lower break b=3 for the nontrivial character. The preceding calculation then yields dimension one, Swan conductor three and Artin conductor four. The break theorem is cited background; the conductor sum itself is explicit once that filtration is specified.

## Worked example: conclusion

Dimension and ramification can vary independently, so each term in a proposed rank identity needs a separate definition and theorem.

## Limits

The retrieved λ+κ+ΣSw+δ_tor expression does not define its terms sufficiently to establish a universal rank formula. The example does not claim to prove the general ramification-break theorem from scratch.

## Extension

For a supplied local representation, compute the ramification filtration and fixed spaces, then compare conductor contributions with an actual global Euler–Poincaré formula or Selmer exact sequence under their full hypotheses.
