## Result name

Information lower bound and exact collision obligations

## Result statement

Four raw outputs cannot determine every state because b⁴=2¹⁰⁴<M. Five outputs have sufficient alphabet capacity but are not proved injective by that inequality. If every five-coordinate projection of a length-L code is injective, its minimum Hamming distance is at least L−4, allowing one error in seven or two errors in nine.

## Proof

The pigeonhole principle proves the lower bound by comparing M states with at most bᵏ output words. For a collision between states x and x′, write r_j=Aʲx mod M and r′_j=Aʲx′ mod M. Equal outputs mean r_j−r′_j is a multiple of b, while r_j−r′_j≡Aʲ(x−x′) mod M and |r_j−r′_j|<M. Thus collision differences lie in an explicitly defined integer lattice intersected with a bounded box; additionally they must be realizable by a common pair of states. Proving that there is no nonzero bounded lattice vector is sufficient for injectivity, while finding such a vector alone need not give a realizable collision. For the coding statement, two distinct codewords can agree in at most four positions; otherwise an injective five-coordinate projection would coincide. Their distance is therefore at least L−4. Disjoint Hamming balls of radius e follow when 2e is less than that distance.
