## Revised question retained in the annual source

How does entropy change under prefix refinement, and does a depth-increasing map necessarily create information?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

The chain rule bounds the information added by each digit. A deterministic prefix contraction can reduce finite-resolution entropy, while a fresh random prefix increases it, demonstrating that geometric closeness and stochastic information generation require different hypotheses.

## Definitions

Let X=(X₁,X₂,…) be a random sequence over b symbols and H_k=H(X₁,…,X_k), using natural logarithms.

A deterministic prefix map G(x)=0x prepends a fixed digit. In the metric a^{common-prefix length}, it contracts all distances by a.

A random prefix map instead prepends an independent uniform symbol U. Its output law includes new randomness not present in the deterministic map.

## Result name

Entropy refinement law and two contrasting generators

## Result statement

0≤H_{k+1}−H_k=H(X_{k+1}|X₁,…,X_k)≤log b. For fixed-prefix G, H_k(GX)=H_{k−1}(X). For a fresh independent uniform prefix, H_k(UX)=log b+H_{k−1}(X).

## Proof

Factor a joint probability into its prefix probability and conditional next-symbol probability inside the logarithm defining Shannon entropy. Summing gives the chain rule. Conditional entropy is a probability-weighted average of entropies on at most b symbols, each between zero and log b; Jensen’s inequality proves the upper bound. For a fixed first digit there is no entropy contribution at that position and the remaining observed coordinates are the first k−1 old ones. For an independent uniform first digit, add its entropy log b by independence. The deterministic map increases pairwise common-prefix length by one, yet its finite observation reveals one fewer old coordinate. Thus the metric refinement inequality alone does not establish uniqueness generation or an entropy source.

## Worked example: setup

Let X be an independent fair binary sequence and k=4.

## Worked example: calculation

Originally H₄=4log2. Prepending zero gives H₄=3log2; prepending a fresh fair bit leaves H₄=4log2. If X is deterministic instead, the fresh-prefix operation gives H₄=log2 while the deterministic operation remains zero. These exact counts distinguish information redistribution from genuine randomization.

## Worked example: conclusion

A proposed generator must specify both its action on sequences and the source of randomness.

## Limits

Thermodynamic energy costs do not follow from Shannon entropy change without a physical memory model, temperature and permitted operations. Prefix contraction is not an unconditional information-production theorem.

## Extension

For a declared stochastic transducer, derive its entropy-rate balance and the mutual information retained about the input. Combine it with an explicit channel or thermodynamic model only after identifying discarded, stored and newly randomized degrees of freedom.
