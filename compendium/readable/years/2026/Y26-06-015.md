## Summary

The source connects zonotope determinant mass to conductor compilation. We give a complete planar theorem and a worked exact example. It demonstrates useful geometry while keeping the proposed speedup ratio distinct from measured or proved algorithmic complexity.

## Definitions

For vectors v₁,…, v_m∈R², define Z=Σ_i[0, v_i], the Minkowski sum of segments. Parallel generators are allowed.

For a convex planar body K, adding a segment [0, v] increases area by |v| times the width of K perpendicular to v. A segment has zero area but contributes width.

## Result name

Planar zonotope area is a determinant sum, not a standalone runtime ratio

## Result statement

AreaZ=Σ_{i<j}|det(v_i, v_j)|. In particular equal area does not force equal numbers of generators, identical dependence patterns or identical implementation cost.

## Proof

Rotate coordinates so v is horizontal. Every horizontal slice of a convex body is an interval, and adding [0, v] increases each nonempty slice length by |v|. Integrating over the perpendicular coordinate increases area by |v| times the perpendicular projection length. For a zonotope generated by earlier segments, that projection is the interval sum of their projections, so its length is the sum of their absolute perpendicular projection lengths. Multiplication by |v| turns each into |det(v_i, v)|. Starting from one zero-area segment and adding generators proves the determinant formula by induction. This is an exact proof even with collinear vectors, whose pair determinants vanish. The higher-dimensional determinant-sum theorem is classical, but the planar result already suffices for the worked certificate here.

## Worked example: setup

Use generators v₁=(1,0), v₂=(0,1), v₃=(1,1).

## Worked example: calculation

The three absolute determinants are1,1,1, giving area three. The convex hull is the hexagon(0,0),(1,0),(2,1),(2,2),(1,2),(0,1), whose shoelace area is also three. Replacing v₁ by two collinear half-segments leaves Z unchanged and leaves the determinant sum three, while the generator list length increases from three to four. Therefore area does not by itself determine a count of controlled operations.

## Worked example: conclusion

The determinant mass has a rigorous geometric meaning and an exact polygon cross-check.
