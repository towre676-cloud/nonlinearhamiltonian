## Revised question retained in the annual source

Why does a proposed spectral-submanifold theorem need nonresonance and a regularity class in addition to a gap?

## Status

## Summary

The retrieved critique correctly identifies a distinction between nonresonance and small-divisor control. Even a two-dimensional polynomial map with a large normal gap can have infinitely many analytic tangent graphs if one exact resonance is present.

## Definitions

Take F(x,y)=(x/2,y/8+x²). The x-axis is the slow linear eigenspace and the y-direction contracts faster.

Seek analytic graphs through the origin tangent to the x-axis. The eigenvalues satisfy (1/2)³=1/8, an order-three resonance.

## Result name

Exact resonant nonuniqueness

## Result statement

For every real C, h_C(x)=8x²+Cx³ is an analytic invariant graph. All are tangent to the same slow eigenspace, and all have transverse contraction factor 1/8.

## Proof

Compute h_C(x/2)=2x²+(C/8)x³. On the other hand h_C(x)/8+x²=2x²+(C/8)x³, proving invariance. The graph derivative at zero is zero. Subtracting h_C(x/2) from y/8+x² gives (y−h_C(x))/8, so the same exact attraction identity holds for every member. In a power-series comparison, the quadratic coefficient is forced by 1/4−1/8=1/8, yielding eight, while the cubic equation is 0·h₃=0 and leaves C free. This is a direct obstruction to any theorem asserting unique analytic continuation from a spectral gap alone.

## Worked example: setup

Compare C=0 and C=1 at x=1/10.

## Worked example: calculation

The two graph values are 0.08 and 0.081, both invariant and tangent at zero. Their difference x³ is small near the origin but nonzero on every punctured neighborhood. The linear normal-to-tangential contraction ratio is (1/8)/(1/2)=1/4 in both cases.

## Worked example: conclusion

Normal attraction and graph uniqueness are logically distinct even in an exact polynomial setting.

## Limits

This example addresses resonance, not an infinite-dimensional small-divisor theorem. In many dimensions the relevant denominators are products or sums of selected eigenvalues, depending on discrete or continuous time.
