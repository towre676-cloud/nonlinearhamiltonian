## Revised question retained in the annual source

What does a triadic compatibility condition prove, and when can a finite-period cover trivialize a class?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The source uses local-state cochains, triangle conditions and period-k trivialization. A finite graph model makes the obstruction exact. It also shows that integer-valued nonzero periods are not automatically removed by passing to a finite cover.

## Definitions

For an oriented graph, let g_ij=−g_ji be an additive edge cochain with values in an abelian group A. A potential f gives the coboundary g_ij=f_j−f_i.

On a filled triangular overlap, the cocycle condition is g_ij+g_jk+g_ki=0. On a graph without filled faces, loop periods must be tested separately.

## Result name

Loop criterion and coefficient-dependent cover behavior

## Result statement

On a connected graph, g is a coboundary if and only if its sum around every closed walk is zero. On S¹ with integer coefficients, a degree-k cover multiplies a cohomology class by k, so no nonzero class is killed by a finite cover.

## Proof

Coboundary sums telescope around a loop. Conversely, fix a base vertex and define f(v) as the sum of g along any path from the base to v. Zero periods make this independent of path, and appending an edge proves g_ij=f_j−f_i. For a circle, the cohomology class is its total period n. A degree-k covering traverses the base loop k times, so the pulled-back period is kn. Over Z this is nonzero whenever k,n are nonzero. Over Z/mZ it can vanish, for example when k=m. Thus period trivialization is a theorem about coefficient torsion and the chosen cover, not a universal consequence of refinement.

## Worked example: setup

Use a three-edge loop with edge values (1,0,0).

## Worked example: calculation

The period is one, so no integer potential removes it. A double cover has period two, still nonzero over Z. With coefficients Z/2Z, that same doubled period is zero and a potential can be constructed on the cover.

## Worked example: conclusion

The local/global distinction and the role of coefficients are explicitly certified.

## Limits

General sheaf cohomology involves restriction maps and a cover or direct-limit construction; the graph proof does not identify every proposed temporal obstruction.

## Extension

Specify the coefficient sheaf and allowed refinements, prove compatibility of pullbacks with coboundaries, and determine the torsion subgroup on which finite-period trivialization can occur.
