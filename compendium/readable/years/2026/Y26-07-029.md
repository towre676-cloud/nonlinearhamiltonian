## Revised question retained in the annual source

What finite inequalities prove existence and uniqueness of a root in a chosen interval?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The late-July universal-solving discussion is made constructive by replacing an unqualified convergence promise with a local certificate. A fixed inverse-Jacobian approximation produces a contraction whose hypotheses can be checked with rational bounds.

## Definitions

Let f be continuously differentiable on I=[c−r,c+r], choose a nonzero constant C, and set T(x)=x−Cf(x). Assume sup_I|1−Cf′(x)|≤q<1.

## Result name

One-dimensional interval Newton–Banach certificate

## Result statement

If|Cf(c)|+qr≤r, then f has exactly one root inI. Starting anywhere inI, iteratingT converges to that root with geometric contraction factorq.

## Proof

The derivative bound and the mean-value theorem give|T(x)−T(y)|≤q|x−y|. Moreover|T(x)−c|≤|T(c)−c|+q|x−c|≤|Cf(c)|+qr≤r, soT maps the closed interval into itself. Completeness and the contraction theorem give a unique fixed point. SinceC≠0, fixed points are exactly zeros off. The same argument bounds the distance from an iterate to the root by|T(x)−x|/(1−q). Every hypothesis is local and explicit. A convergence plot or small residual is weaker: for example a nearly singular derivative can make a tiny residual coexist with a substantial root error. In several variables the analogous test uses a norm bound forI−CJF throughout a box and an inclusion test for its centered image. A practical certificate may choose a fixedC rather than recomputingf′(x)^{-1} at each step. This makes the map and its derivative bound uniform on the entire interval. Newton iteration with a changing inverse may converge faster, but its guarantee must be established separately or shown to stay inside the same certified domain.

## Worked example: setup

Certify the positive root off(x)=x²−2 usingc=7/5,C=5/14 andr=1/50.

## Worked example: calculation

OnI=[69/50,71/50], T′=1−5x/7 ranges from1/70 to−1/70, so q=1/70. Also|Cf(c)|=1/70. The inclusion bound is1/70+1/3500=51/3500<1/50=70/3500. Thus there is exactly one root in[1.38,1.42], and the fixed-inverse iteration converges from every point of this interval. The negative root lies outside it.

## Worked example: conclusion

Existence, uniqueness, enclosure and a convergence rate are certified by exact inequalities.

## Limits

This local theorem does not guarantee finding a valid box for every polynomial or remove global root-separation costs. The source’s universal algorithm was not retrieved as a complete implementation.

## Extension

Use an exact global root count to seed intervals, then attach such certificates to every isolated simple root and a separate multiplicity-aware certificate to repeated roots.
