## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which information is available even before noise, and what part of the target remains random after conditioning on it?

## Status

Classical mathematics with newly authored derivation or explicitly defined worked model

## Summary

The hidden-target floor can be stated as an exact Bayes-risk theorem. Noise-free observations may already identify too little of the target. Improving measurement precision then cannot remove that structural component, although additional kinds of observations may do so.

## Definitions

Let T∈L² be a scalar target and Z=h(X) the noise-free observation. Let Y be a noisy observation satisfying the Markov condition T→Z→Y, meaning conditional independence of T and Y given Z.

Under squared loss, the best estimator from an observation is its conditional mean, with risk EVar(T|observation).

## Result name

A hidden-target conditional variance is a genuine noise-free error floor

## Result statement

EVar(T|Y)=EVar(T|Z)+EVar(E[T|Z]|Y). In particular the optimal noisy-observation risk is at least the noise-free conditional-variance floor.

## Proof

Write m=E[T|Z] and e=T−m. Conditional independence gives E[e|Z,Y]=0, hence E[T|Y]=E[m|Y]. Expand T−E[T|Y]=e+(m−E[m|Y]). The cross term has expectation zero after conditioning on Z,Y. The squared expectation therefore splits into E e² and E(m−E[m|Y])², the two stated terms. The conditional-mean optimality follows from the same orthogonal decomposition with any competing estimator. This proves the information floor without assuming a linear model or Gaussian errors.

## Worked example: setup

Let X₁ and Z₃ be independent, with Z₃ centered Gaussian of varianceσ², and target T=X₁+Z₃². Observe X₁ exactly.

## Worked example: calculation

The best estimate is X₁+σ². The residual is Z₃²−σ² and its variance is2σ⁴, using E[Z₃⁴]=3σ⁴. Repeated noiseless measurements of X₁ do not reduce this error. Measuring a variable informative about Z₃ can reduce it, because it changes the information map rather than merely its precision. At the base value Z₃=0 the target derivative in that direction is zero, linking this probabilistic example to the finite-amplitude quotient test.

## Worked example: conclusion

The claimed floor has a defined loss, an exact decomposition and a worked nonlinear hidden-target model.

## Limits

The Markov condition matters: an extra sensor correlated with the hidden residual is new information, not mere noise added to Z. A minimax ambiguity width and a Bayes conditional variance are different quantities and should not share an unexplained numerical label.

## Extension

Specify the observation sigma-algebra and target distribution for each sensor set, then estimate or bound the irreducible and noise-induced risk terms separately.
