## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which sign makes two commuting differentials into a total differential, and why does the mapping cone test chain-map compatibility?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The retrieved July discussion includes double complexes, Koszul signs and mapping cones. These operations become rigorous only after fixing degrees and signs. A small rational example exposes the failure that an unsigned sum would conceal.

## Definitions

Let C^{p,q} have horizontal δ:C^{p,q}→C^{p+1,q} and vertical ∂:C^{p,q}→C^{p,q+1}, with δ²=∂²=0 and δ∂=∂δ. Total degree is p+q. For a cochain map f:C→D, Cone(f)^n=D^n⊕C^{n+1}.

## Result name

Signed totalization and cone criterion

## Result statement

D_tot=δ+(−1)^p∂ satisfies D_tot²=0. The cone differential d(d,c)=(d_Dd+fc,−d_Cc) squares to zero precisely when f commutes with the differentials.

## Proof

On bidegree(p,q), the mixed term from δ followed by the signed vertical map has sign(−1)^{p+1}, whereas the reverse mixed term has sign(−1)^p. They cancel because δ and ∂ commute; their pure squares vanish. In the cone, applying d twice gives first component d_D²d+(d_Df−fd_C)c and second component d_C²c. Thus the only possible obstruction is exactly the chain-map defect. The shift and its minus sign are indispensable. A cone records whether f is a quasi-isomorphism: its long exact cohomology sequence follows by taking the short exact sequence D→Cone(f)→C[1] and tracing cocycle representatives. In particular Cone(id) is contractible; an explicit homotopy is h(d,c)=(0,d), since dh+hd=(d,c). The characteristic matters to the diagnostic example: in characteristic two the unsigned product2 vanishes, so that particular numerical failure disappears. The sign convention nevertheless remains the correct degree bookkeeping over arbitrary rings and is necessary for uniform compatibility with shifts, tensor products and cone triangles. A rational example avoids concealing the issue behind2=0.

## Worked example: setup

Tensor two complexes Q --id→ Q in degrees0 and1.

## Worked example: calculation

The total groups have dimensions1,2,1. With degree-one coordinates ordered(horizontal,vertical), d⁰(1)=(1,1) and d¹(a,b)=−a+b. Their product is zero, and ker d¹=span(1,1)=im d⁰, so the total complex is acyclic. Using the unsigned map a+b instead gives d¹d⁰(1)=2, which fails over Q.

## Worked example: conclusion

Signs can be verified on exact matrices and give a real compatibility test rather than decorative notation.

## Limits

The convention assumes commuting raw differentials. If the starting bicomplex already anticommutes, one uses a different totalization convention. No full higher-coherence structure is recovered from this example.
