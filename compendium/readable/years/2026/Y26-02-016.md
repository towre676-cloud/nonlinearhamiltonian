## Revised question retained in the annual source

When does a contraction guarantee that a finite observation eventually stops changing, and what boundary condition is missing for prefix digits?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

A geometric error bound gives an explicit resolution time. Exact digit stabilization additionally requires the fixed point to lie inside an observation cell; convergence in an ordinary metric alone cannot rule out endless crossings of a cell boundary.

## Definitions

Let R be a q-contraction on a complete metric space, 0<q<1, with fixed point x_*. Then d(R^nx,x_*)≤q^n d(x,x_*).

Let O_k be a finite-resolution observation and assume O_k is constant on the open ball B(x_*,δ_k), with δ_k>0.

For a binary prefix metric, q=2^{−a} and initial depth ℓ₀ give depth at least ℓ₀+an when the metric contraction and encoding are genuinely compatible.

## Result name

Explicit resolution bound and a boundary counterexample

## Result statement

If q^n d(x,x_*)<δ_k, then O_k(R^nx)=O_k(x_*). Thus any integer n>log(d(x,x_*)/δ_k)/log(1/q) suffices. With a non-strict cell convention this becomes the familiar ceiling formula after checking equality cases.

## Proof

Iterate the contraction inequality and compare its right side with the stated observation radius. The radius assumption then gives exact equality of the finite observations; no limit interchange is needed. For binary depth, taking base-two logarithms of d_n≤2^{−ℓ₀}q^n gives the linear-depth lower bound and the source’s N_lin expression with the appropriate strictness convention. To see the missing hypothesis in Euclidean space, use R(x)=−x/2 and O(x)=1_{x≥0}. The fixed point zero lies on the observation boundary. Starting at any positive x, the iterates alternate sign forever while their distance to zero contracts by one half.

## Worked example: setup

Let q=1/2, initial distance one and δ_k=2^{−k}.

## Worked example: calculation

For the open-ball criterion, n=k+1 always suffices, while n=k only reaches the boundary. At k=12 a certified interior observation stabilizes by step13. The sign observation in the preceding counterexample never stabilizes at any step, despite exactly the same numerical contraction factor.

## Worked example: conclusion

Approximate convergence and exact stabilization become equivalent only after the observation topology is controlled.

## Limits

Stabilization of one orbit is not automatically the Mittag–Leffler stabilization of all tower images. A proof requires a uniform bound over each declared domain and compatible bonding maps; vanishing lim¹ alone does not imply contraction.

## Extension

For a concrete truncation tower, prove a uniform image-diameter bound and cell-interior condition at every level. Distinguish monotonicity of every register from an aggregate score, and include periodic-map analysis when the derivative has modulus one or greater.
