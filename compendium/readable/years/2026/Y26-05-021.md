## Revised question retained in the annual source

Reconstruction: How does an exponent species become a weighted partition, and which finite certificates control its count?

## Status

Classical result or newly stipulated finite model; historical claims explicitly delimited

## Summary

The May asymptotic source supplies the decisive reduction to primorial logarithms. We prove the bijection, construct the partition function, and derive a rigorous upper bound. This gives useful exact mathematics before any saddle approximation is invoked.

## Definitions

For a species a₁≥a₂≥⋯≥0 of finite support set d_j=a_j−a_{j+1}≥0. Let P_j=∏_{i≤j}p_i and w_j=logP_j.

Define S(t)=#{d of finite support:Σ_jd_jw_j≤t}. For s>0 set Z(s)=∏_{j≥1}(1−e^{−sw_j})^{−1} and F(s)=logZ(s).

## Result name

Species counting is an exact weighted partition problem with a convergent Euler product

## Result statement

The least representative satisfies m(a)=∏P_j^{d_j}; thus species with least representative at most N are exactly the partitions counted by S(logN). The Euler product converges for every s>0, and S(t)≤exp(F(s)+st).

## Proof

Because a_i=Σ_{j≥i}d_j, rearranging the finite sums gives logm=Σ_i a_i logp_i=Σ_jd_jΣ_{i≤j}logp_i. This proves the product identity and the bijection, since differences recover d uniquely. Also w_j≥jlog2, so Σ_je^{−sw_j} converges geometrically. For all sufficiently large j the terms are at most1/2, and −log(1−x)≤2x there; hence F(s) converges. Expanding each nonnegative geometric series gives Z(s)=Σ_dexp(−sΣd_jw_j), justified by monotone convergence. Every partition of weight at most t contributes at least e^{−st}; therefore S(t)e^{−st}≤Z(s), proving the bound. For a finite t only weights w_j≤t can occur, so S(t) can also be computed by a finite exact recursion using integer primorial products, avoiding logarithmic rounding.

## Worked example: setup

Count species whose least representative is at most twelve.

## Worked example: calculation

The representatives are1,2,4,6,8,12, giving six species: empty,(1),(2),(1,1),(3),(2,1). Here P₁=2,P₂=6,P₃=30>12, so only d₁,d₂ can be nonzero. The inequality2^{d₁}6^{d₂}≤12 yields four cases at d₂=0 and two at d₂=1. This is an exact integer enumeration.

## Limits

The saddle-point prefactor is not implied by the Euler product alone. Incommensurable logarithmic weights require a controlled local limit or cumulative Tauberian argument.
