## Revised question retained in the annual source

What error correction follows from the all-state observation theorem, and are the observation counts information-theoretically necessary?

## Status

## Summary

The finite-state trajectories define a code over the alphabet of26-bit words. The five-observation theorem controls its minimum Hamming distance without requiring linearity over a field. A puncturing argument then proves that the seven- and nine-word correction lengths cannot be improved for this full state population.

## Definitions

For5≤n≤9 let C_n={c_n(x):0≤x<M}, where c_n(x)=(y_0,…,y_{n−1}) is the raw trajectory. Hamming distance counts differing words, regardless of the bit distance within a corrupted word.

An arbitrary word corruption replaces one entire26-bit symbol. Unique correction oftcorruptions requires minimum code distance at least2t+1. Erasures at known positions are a different, easier observation model.

## Result name

Exact distance and length-optimal correction

## Result statement

For5≤n≤9 the minimum distance of C_n is exactlyn−4. Thus seven raw outputs correct one arbitrary word error, and nine correct two. No code representing all M states over this alphabet can correct one error with six words or two errors with eight words.

## Proof

If two distinct state codewords agreed at five positions in any n≤9, the preceding theorem would make the states equal. They therefore agree in at most four positions, giving distance at leastn−4. Conversely, for any code of minimum distanced, puncturing d−1fixed positions leaves an injective map: otherwise two original words differed only in those positions, contradicting the distance. Hence its population is at mostb^{n−d+1}. Since M>b^4, one has n−d+1≥5, or d≤n−4. This proves equality.

Two Hamming balls of radius t are disjoint when the minimum distance exceeds2t, by the triangle inequality; hence the stated seven- and nine-word codes uniquely correct the stated errors. An explicit decoder enumerates five-position subsets, applies the certified affine-lattice decoder, re-encodes each candidate and retains a state within the permitted Hamming radius. Enough clean positions remain to produce the true candidate, and the distance proof rules out a second one. Finally any code correcting t arbitrary errors has distance at least2t+1. Puncturing2t positions must be injective, giving M≤b^{n−2t}; sinceM>b^4, necessarily n≥5+2t. This proves the requested length lower bounds without a linear-code assumption.

## Worked example: conclusion

The finite decoder tests instantiate a globally proved distance theorem, and the puncturing bound proves that shorter full-population codes cannot have the same correction guarantee.

## Limits

This is a coding theorem for the stated deterministic raw trajectories. It does not assert cryptographic unpredictability, statistical randomness or a correction guarantee when output positions are hidden by rejection sampling.

## Extension

Study mixed errors and erasures: the same distance gives correction whenever2t+e<n−4. Implement a decoder that uses known erasure locations first, then prove exact failure/refusal conditions for inconsistent observations.
