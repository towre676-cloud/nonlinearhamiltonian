## Revised question retained in the annual source

What does the complex 0→L²₀→L²₀→0 with differential I−P actually classify?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered complex is a meaningful two-term linear object. Its degree-zero cohomology consists of fixed vectors and its degree-one cohomology is a quotient by the range. Infinite-dimensional closed-range issues must be handled before identifying that quotient with a stable inverse problem.

## Definitions

For a finite probability space let L²₀ be the mean-zero subspace. Suppose P preserves constants and the mean, so it restricts to L²₀. Let δ=I−P.

For the two-term complex, H⁰=kerδ and H¹=L²₀/imδ. There is no next nonzero differential, so no additional δ²=0 condition is required.

## Result name

Finite gap solves the coboundary equation

## Result statement

If ||P|| on L²₀ is at most q<1, then δ is invertible with δ⁻¹=Σ_(n≥0)P^n, ||δ⁻¹||≤1/(1−q), and both cohomology groups vanish.

## Proof

The geometric operator series converges in norm. Multiplying its Nth partial sum by I−P leaves I−P^(N+1), which tends to I, proving a two-sided inverse. The norm estimate is the scalar geometric sum. Consequently the kernel is zero and the range is the whole space. If q reaches one, neither conclusion follows from this estimate; a fixed mean-zero vector gives H⁰≠0, while an infinite-dimensional nonclosed range can make ordinary and reduced cohomology differ. The complex therefore encodes a definite solvability question rather than an automatic obstruction hierarchy.

## Worked example: setup

Take P=[[3/4,1/4],[1/4,3/4]] with the uniform measure.

## Worked example: calculation

On L²₀=span{(1,−1)}, P has eigenvalue 1/2. Hence δ acts by 1/2 and δ⁻¹ by 2. For g=(1,−1), the exact solution of (I−P)f=g is f=(2,−2). For P=I instead, δ=0 and both cohomology groups equal the one-dimensional mean-zero space.

## Worked example: conclusion

The gap controls a genuine cohomological inverse and identifies the precise failure at a fixed mode.
