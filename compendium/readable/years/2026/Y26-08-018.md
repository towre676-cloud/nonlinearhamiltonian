## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which transport quantities survive gauge change, and when does a small plaquette recover curvature?

## Summary

The August15 source asks for cohomology, holonomy, curvature and genuine two-dimensional refinement. Exact finite transports show why a one-dimensional ring can have nontrivial holonomy without a curvature two-form, and why matrix data are indispensable for nonabelian conclusions.

## Definitions

LetU_j transport vectors from vertexj toj+1. Under a basis changeh_j at each vertex, U_j↦h_{j+1}U_jh_j^{-1}. Loop holonomyH is the ordered product returning to the base vertex. For a smooth connection∇=d+A, curvature isF=dA+A∧A.

## Result name

Gauge covariance and infinitesimal commutator

## Result statement

H changes by conjugation at its base point, so its conjugacy class is gauge invariant. For constant matricesX,Y, the elementary commutator loop satisfies e^{εX}e^{εY}e^{−εX}e^{−εY}=I+ε²[X,Y]+O(ε³). A one-dimensional base supports no nonzero two-form curvature but can have nontrivial loop holonomy.

## Proof

In the product of transformedU_j, every adjacenth_j^{-1}h_j cancels, leavingh₀Hh₀^{-1}. Taylor-multiply the four exponentials through degree two: all linear terms cancel, as do the pureX² andY² terms, leavingXY−YX. In a smooth two-dimensional setting the analogous parallel-transport expansion includes derivative terms and recoversF times oriented area, with a sign depending on the stated transport convention. On a circle, all two-forms vanish by dimension, yet a flat connection can retain global monodromy because a loop need not contract. Thus neither scalar circulation on an arbitrary fitted graph nor a matrix commutator without an identified geometric base automatically equals curvature.

## Worked example: calculation

The circle holonomy ise^{iθ}, nontrivial unlessθ is a multiple of2π, despite zero two-form curvature. In the matrix example[X,Y]=diag(1,−1), so the infinitesimal commutator has a nonzero quadratic term. SinceX²=Y²=0, the four exponential factors are finite linear polynomials inε and their product can be checked exactly.
