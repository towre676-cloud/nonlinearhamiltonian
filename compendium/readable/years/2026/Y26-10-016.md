## Revised question retained in the annual source

How do local completion counts yield a global bijection between feasible objects and integer addresses?

## Status

Classical mathematics or a newly chosen finite model developed with proof; historical claims delimited

## Summary

The recovered query-space work emphasizes exact addresses, inverse lookup, pagination and uniform sampling. These are consequences of a single prefix-partition theorem. It is the disjoint completion population, not the apparent size of an ambient search box, that determines valid addresses.

## Definitions

Let Ω⊆{0,1}ⁿ be a finite nonempty population ordered lexicographically with 0 before 1. For a prefix q, let C(q) count its feasible completions.

The empty prefix has C(∅)=|Ω| and C(q)=C(q0)+C(q1); a feasible complete word has count one.

## Result name

Rank/unrank bijection and unbiased sampling

## Result statement

There is an exact bijection rank:Ω→{0,…,|Ω|−1}. At prefix q, all q0 completions occupy the first C(q0) addresses; the q1 block occupies the following C(q1). A uniform address therefore gives a uniform object.

## Proof

At a node q, split its contiguous address interval into disjoint initial and final blocks with those lengths. For unranking, compare the residual address r with C(q0): choose 0 when r<C(q0), otherwise choose 1 and replace r by r−C(q0). The chosen block has positive length and its residual address is valid; induction reaches exactly one feasible leaf. For ranking, scan a feasible word and add C(q0) whenever its next bit is 1. At each step these operations are inverse translations on the two disjoint blocks, proving the global bijection. Consequently P(unrank(R)=ω)=1/|Ω| when R is uniform. Conditional populations use the same construction below a fixed prefix. To draw an exact uniform integer from random bits, rejection sampling of a b-bit integer with 2ᵇ≥|Ω| is unbiased; reducing that integer modulo |Ω| is generally biased unless the modulus divides 2ᵇ.

## Worked example: setup

Rank the five feasible words from the three-project example.

## Worked example: calculation

The list is 000,001,010,100,110 with ranks 0,…,4; the prefix 1 has two completions. To sample one of five objects, generate three fair bits and reject addresses 5,6,7. Each valid address has conditional probability 1/5. Direct reduction of all eight bit strings modulo five instead gives probabilities (2,2,2,1,1)/8 and is not uniform.

## Worked example: conclusion

The population has a reversible exact index and a precisely stated sampling distribution.

## Limits

A deterministic seed supplies reproducibility, not by itself a proof of uniformity or cryptographic unpredictability. If distinct source objects were merged, the address bijection would index the quotient rather than the original population.

## Extension

Use counted prefixes as a persistent paginated API: each page is a half-open rank interval, and every update carries a versioned population definition so old addresses cannot silently refer to different objects.
