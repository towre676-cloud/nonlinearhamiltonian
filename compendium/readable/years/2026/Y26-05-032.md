## Summary

The final May sweep names the path-loop fibration and a polynomial claim for H*(ΩS²;Z). We preserve the transgression theme but correct the ring distinction. The following elementary graded argument and filtered-group example explain why a page certificate cannot close all extension questions.

## Definitions

In integral cohomology cup products are graded commutative: ab=(−1)^{deg a deg b}ba. The group H¹(ΩS²;Z) is Z, from π₁(ΩS²)=π₂(S²)=Z and the degree-one Hurewicz/universal-coefficient calculation.

For a filtered abelian group0⊂F₀⊂F₁=G, its associated graded group is F₀⊕G/F₀. An E∞ page records associated graded information, not a canonical splitting.

## Result name

A spectral-sequence page does not determine an integral ring or its extension data

## Result statement

A degree-one class a in H*(ΩS²;Z) has a²=0, so this cohomology ring is not an ordinary polynomial ring Z[a] on its degree-one generator. Separately Z/4 and Z/2⊕Z/2 have filtrations with the same associated graded group Z/2⊕Z/2, showing a genuine extension ambiguity.

## Proof

Graded commutativity gives a²=−a², hence2a²=0. The integral groups H^j(ΩS²;Z) are torsion free in this classical loop-space calculation, so a²=0. Even without using their complete ring, a polynomial claim with a degree-one variable would conflict with this vanishing. The correct familiar integral cohomology description is an exterior degree-one factor times a divided-power degree-two factor; that full published topology is identified as classical input, not proved from one d₂ arrow here. By contrast the Pontryagin homology algebra has a tensor-algebra description on a degree-one generator, and confusing homology product with cohomology cup product causes the source error. For extension ambiguity take G=Z/4,F₀={0,2}; both subquotients are Z/2. In G′=Z/2⊕Z/2 take its first summand as F₀′; again both subquotients are Z/2, but G has an element of order four and G′ does not, so they are not isomorphic.

## Limits
