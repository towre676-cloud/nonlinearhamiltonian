## Summary

The source proposes structured conductor SELECT and reports modeled speedups. The mathematical core of linear combinations of unitaries can be established exactly. It exposes the needed normalization λ and the distinction between a block identity and a complete simulation complexity theorem.

## Definitions

Let H=Σ_{j=0}^{m−1}a_jU_j with a_j≥0 and unitary U_j. Set λ=Σa_j>0 and prepare |a⟩=Σ√(a_j/λ)|j⟩.

Define SELECT=Σ|j⟩⟨j|⊗U_j. If PREP|0⟩=|a⟩, let W=(PREP†⊗I)SELECT(PREP⊗I).

## Result name

SELECT plus state preparation gives an exact linear-combination block

## Result statement

W is unitary and its ancilla-zero block is (⟨0|⊗I)W(|0⟩⊗I)=H/λ. In particular ||H||≤λ. Postselecting ancilla zero on input ψ succeeds with probability ||Hψ||²/λ².

## Proof

SELECT is block diagonal with unitary blocks, so it is unitary; multiplication by the preparation unitaries preserves this. Insert the expansion of |a⟩ on both sides of SELECT. Orthogonality of the label basis removes all cross terms, leaving Σ(a_j/λ)U_j. This proves the exact block identity. A subblock of a unitary is a contraction, yielding the norm inequality; it also follows from the triangle inequality. The ancilla-zero component of the output is Hψ/λ, whose squared norm is exactly the stated probability. Complex or negative coefficients can be included by absorbing their phases into the corresponding U_j. A structured implementation may reduce the cost of SELECT or PREP, but does not alter this normalization, and the complete cost depends on both operations.

## Worked example: setup

Take H=2X+Z on a qubit, with Pauli X and Z.

## Worked example: calculation

Here λ=3, |a⟩=√(2/3)|0⟩+√(1/3)|1⟩, and the block is(2X+Z)/3. Since XZ+ZX=0, H²=5I, so the success probability is5/9 for every normalized input. H has norm√5, strictly smaller than the coefficient sum three. This gap shows that λ is a construction parameter rather than automatically the exact spectral norm.
