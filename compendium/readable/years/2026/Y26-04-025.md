## Revised question retained in the annual source

Under which fixed parameters is the claimed ξ=1 minimizer of the compatibility equation mathematically justified?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The source gives a Euclidean/Lorentzian matching equation and a minimizing parameter pair. An algebraic constraint alone does not define minimization. Fixing the remaining parameter and explicitly minimizing the nonnegative variable ζ supplies a valid conditional statement.

## Definitions

Let a,b>0 and c>0 be fixed. Consider aζ²−b(1−ξ)²=c with ζ≥0 and ξ real.

The source coefficients correspond to a=0.1681,b=7.0225 and c=0.167918(2.15−0.05α), which is positive only on the declared α range.

## Result name

Conditional constrained minimum

## Result statement

The least possible ζ is √(c/a), attained uniquely at ξ=1. If c≤0 or if α is also free, the conclusion changes and must be reconsidered.

## Proof

Rearrange the constraint as ζ²=c/a+(b/a)(1−ξ)². Since b/a>0, the second term is nonnegative and vanishes exactly at ξ=1. Because ζ≥0 and square root is increasing, this gives the unique minimizing ξ and the stated ζ. At c=0 the minimum is zero at ξ=1. At c<0, feasibility instead requires |1−ξ|≥√(−c/b), and ζ=0 is attained at two distinct boundary points, showing the positivity assumption is substantive. If α varies, c varies, so optimizing over α requires an admissible interval and a separate boundary analysis.

## Worked example: setup

Use the source decimals with α=0.

## Worked example: calculation

c=0.167918·2.15=0.3610237. Thus ζ_min=√(0.3610237/0.1681)≈1.46549, attained at ξ=1. Moving ξ to zero increases ζ² by 7.0225/0.1681, so it cannot improve this objective.

## Worked example: conclusion

The minimizer is an exact consequence of a clearly chosen conditional optimization problem.

## Limits
