## Revised question retained in the annual source

How does the tower Z←×2 Z←×2… produce a nonzero lim¹, and why is that different from finite-depth instability?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

The standard product-difference model computes this derived limit as Z₂/Z. Its exact algebra identifies what a proposed geometric realization would need to supply, while separating infinite tower behavior from a finite register experiment.

## Definitions

For a countable tower A₀←f₀ A₁←f₁… of abelian groups define D:∏A_n→∏A_n by D(a)_n=a_n−f_n(a_{n+1}). Then lim A_n=ker D and lim¹ A_n=coker D.

For A_n=Z and f_n=×2, use the 2-adic integers Z₂ and map Ψ(b)=∑_{n≥0}2^n b_n, convergent 2-adically.

The Mittag–Leffler condition means that for each fixed level, images from sufficiently high levels eventually stop changing exactly.

## Result name

Exact obstruction group

## Result statement

For the doubling tower, lim A_n=0 and lim¹ A_n≅Z₂/Z. Its images at level zero are 2^mZ, so it is not Mittag–Leffler. A finite tower does not reproduce this infinite obstruction.

## Proof

Compatibility a_n=2a_{n+1} makes a₀ divisible by every power of two, forcing a₀=0 and then every a_n=0. For the cokernel, telescoping gives Ψ(Da)=a₀ in Z₂, so Ψ induces a homomorphism coker D→Z₂/Z. It is surjective because every 2-adic integer has binary digits, which may be used as b_n. If Ψ(b)=k is an ordinary integer, define a₀=k and recursively a_{n+1}=(a_n−b_n)/2. The congruences k≡∑_{j<n}2^j b_j mod2^n ensure every a_n is an integer; the recursion then gives Da=b. This proves injectivity of the induced map. The subgroup of ordinary integers is proper in Z₂, so the quotient is nonzero.

## Worked example: setup

Take b_n=1 for even n and zero for odd n.

## Worked example: calculation

Its 2-adic sum is 1+4+16+…=1/(1−4)=−1/3 in Z₂. This is not an ordinary integer, so it represents a nonzero lim¹ class. Every finite truncation of Da=b can still be solved backward after choosing its final a value, showing why finite solvability does not remove the infinite obstruction.

## Worked example: conclusion

The obstruction is an explicit quotient with a concrete nonzero element, not an inferred geometric phase.

## Limits

The recovered Heisenberg realization was conditional and its bonding maps were not established. A register with depth at most 64 may exhibit finite discrepancies, but cannot by itself instantiate an infinite nonzero derived limit.

## Extension

Construct the proposed geometric groups and bonding maps, prove they identify with this tower, and track the displayed class under that identification. For finite truncations, define a separate residual invariant rather than call it lim¹.
