## Revised question retained in the annual source

When do training time, sensitivity and algebraic tails follow from a spectral gap, and when are separate gaps independent?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered learning program proposes several co-closing gaps and inverse-gap laws. A diagonal gradient-flow model proves the mechanism while showing exactly what the spectrum and the forcing must contribute. Co-closing distinct gaps remains an additional hypothesis.

## Definitions

Let H be symmetric positive definite and consider x′=−Hx+b. Its gap is Δ=λ_min(H). The equilibrium is H⁻¹b.

For a spectral density cλ^(ν−1) on [0,1] with ν>0, consider the mode average C(t)=c∫₀¹e^(−tλ)λ^(ν−1)dλ.

## Result name

Gap, susceptibility and near-zero tails

## Result statement

The worst-case decay time is 1/Δ and ||H⁻¹||=1/Δ. If a continuous spectral measure has the stated density, C(t)∼cΓ(ν)t^(−ν), despite the absence of a positive gap.

## Proof

Diagonalize H in an orthonormal basis. Each error coordinate evolves as e^(−λ_jt), and the slowest allowed eigenvector attains the rate Δ. The equilibrium derivative with respect to b is H⁻¹, whose largest singular value is 1/Δ. In the integral set u=tλ, obtaining C(t)=ct^(−ν)∫₀^t e^(−u)u^(ν−1)du. The integral tends to Γ(ν) by monotone convergence. A spectral density alone does not specify an observed correlation unless the observable has that spectral measure; an observable orthogonal to slow modes can decay faster. Finally, independent diagonal blocks H₁=diag(ε,1) and H₂=I show one gap can close while another stays one.

## Worked example: setup

Take H=diag(1/10,2), b=(1,0), and ν=1,c=1.

## Worked example: calculation

The slow time is 10 and equilibrium is (10,0), with sensitivity 10 in the first direction. The continuum average is C(t)=(1−e^(−t))/t, so tC(t)→1. Replacing b by (0,1) removes the slow equilibrium response.

## Worked example: conclusion

Inverse-gap and power-law claims become testable statements about named modes and spectral weights.

## Limits

These linear results do not prove double-descent co-closing in a nonlinear learning model or identify its parameter, update and policy gaps.

## Extension

Specify operators defining each gap, exhibit a coupling theorem or counterexample, and estimate the observable-weighted near-zero density rather than only eigenvalue counts.
