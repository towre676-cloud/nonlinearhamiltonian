## Revised question retained in the annual source

How can an exact energy prove decay even when the ordinary Euclidean norm initially grows?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The August18 control discussion includes perturbations, Lyapunov stability, ISS and robustness. A nonnormal two-state system gives a compact exact proof linking these ideas. It also explains why eigenvalue signs alone do not bound every short-time norm response.

## Definitions

Considerẋ=Ax+Bu with A=[[-1,3],[0,−2]]. LetP=[[1/2,1/2],[1/2,1]] andV=xᵀPx. A positive definiteP defines an equivalent squared norm.

## Result name

Exact Lyapunov and forced-energy inequality

## Result statement

P is positive definite and AᵀP+PA=−I. Therefore unforced trajectories decay exponentially. With inputu, V̇≤−||x||²/2+2||PB||²||u||², giving a quantitative input-to-state energy bound.

## Proof

The leading principal minor1/2 and determinant1/4 are positive, provingP>0. Exact multiplication verifies the Lyapunov identity. Along a trajectoryV̇=−||x||²+2xᵀPBu. Cauchy–Schwarz and2ab≤a²/2+2b² give the stated inequality. SinceV≤λ_max(P)||x||², one obtainsV̇≤−V/(2λ_max(P))+2||PB||²||u||² and integrates the scalar differential inequality. The eigenvalues ofP are(3±√5)/4, so all constants are explicit. Withu=0 the stronger identity givesV(t)≤e^{−t/λ_max(P)}V(0). The Euclidean norm need not decrease: its derivative isxᵀ(A+Aᵀ)x, andA+Aᵀ is indefinite. This creates transient amplification without contradicting decay in the Lyapunov metric. The actual transition matrix is e^{At}=[[e^{−t},3(e^{−t}−e^{−2t})],[0,e^{−2t}]], as direct differentiation verifies. Its off-diagonal term transfers an initial second-coordinate excitation into the first coordinate before both decay. Comparing the minimum and maximum eigenvalues ofP also gives ||x(t)||≤√κ₂(P) e^{−t/(2λ_max(P))}||x(0)|| for zero input. The prefactor allows transient Euclidean amplification; the metric certificate and explicit trajectory agree.

## Worked example: conclusion

A positive definite matrix identity provides a replayable global stability certificate and a disturbance bound.

## Limits

This is a fixed linear system. Nonlinear, hybrid or parameter-varying systems need additional uniform hypotheses; a stable matrix at one operating point is insufficient.
