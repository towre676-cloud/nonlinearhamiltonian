## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which state really advances by a fixed residue increment, and does its observed period equal the period of that full state?

## Status

Classical mathematics with newly authored derivation or explicitly defined worked model

## Summary

The recovered experiment used the formula p=k/gcd(k,r) for a buffer-related residue. That formula has a complete elementary proof once the state and update are stated. It certifies an orbit of a cyclic permutation; it does not, by itself, certify the period of every hidden state in a processor or execution.

## Definitions

Fix integers k≥1 and r. The state space is Z/kZ, and the update is F(x)=x+r modk. Let d=gcd(k,r), taking a nonnegative gcd.

A period is a positive integer p with F^p(x)=x. The least such integer is the orbit length. An observation h can identify different states, so h(F^n(x)) may have a shorter period.

## Result name

A rotating residue has an exact period and a complete orbit decomposition

## Result statement

Every orbit of F has length p=k/d. There are exactly d orbits, each consisting of the residues in one congruence class modulo d. In particular, a genuine period two requires k even and r congruent to k/2 modulo k.

## Proof

Iteration gives F^n(x)=x+nr modk. Return to the initial state is equivalent to k dividing nr, independently of x. Write k=dk₀ and r=dr₀ with gcd(k₀,r₀)=1. Then k divides nr exactly when k₀ divides nr₀, which is equivalent to k₀ dividing n by coprimality. The least positive n is therefore k₀. Every increment is divisible by d, so an orbit remains in one class modulo d. That class has k/d elements, exactly the orbit length, proving equality and the d-orbit count. For length two, k/d=2 forces d=k/2 and hence r=(k/2) times an odd integer modulo k.

## Worked example: setup

Use the newly declared exact case k=12,r=8, then compare an observation that retains only parity.

## Worked example: calculation

Here d=4 and the orbit through zero is0→8→4→0. The remaining orbits are the other three congruence classes modulo four. The full residue has period three, but parity is constant along every one of these orbits, with observed period one. For a separate period-two case k=6,r=3, the orbit0→3→0 satisfies the theorem exactly. These integers illustrate the algebra; they are not asserted to be historical hardware parameters.

## Worked example: conclusion

The period formula becomes an exact state-transition theorem, while the distinction between state period and observation period remains explicit.

## Limits

The source bullet does not define the original k,r variables or all buffer and port components. A period in one counter cannot establish a full-state recurrence without proving that the update has this cyclic form and that the observation is sufficient.

## Extension

For a product of cyclic counters, derive the joint period from the least common multiple of the individual orbit lengths, then characterize which observation maps collapse that joint period.
