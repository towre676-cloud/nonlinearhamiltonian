## Revised question retained in the annual source

How does uniform fixed-size vertex sampling transform the Euler characteristic of a finite complex?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

Each simplex survives exactly when all of its vertices are sampled, giving a hypergeometric inclusion probability. Linearity of expectation then yields an exact polynomial transform of the face counts, without pretending that the alternating sum recovers a barcode.

## Definitions

Let K be a finite abstract simplicial complex on N vertices and f_{b−1} its number of faces with b vertices.

Choose an m-element vertex subset S uniformly and let K[S] contain exactly the faces of K whose vertices lie in S.

Use ordinary Euler characteristic χ(K[S])=∑_{b≥1}(−1)^{b−1}f_{b−1}(K[S]); the empty complex has χ=0.

## Result name

Exact fixed-size sampling identity

## Result statement

Eχ(K[S])=∑_{b≥1}(−1)^{b−1}f_{b−1}·C(m,b)/C(N,b), with the ratio zero for b>m. For independent vertex retention probability p the corresponding formula is ∑(−1)^{b−1}f_{b−1}p^b.

## Proof

Write the random face count as a sum of indicators over the original faces. A fixed b-vertex face lies in exactly C(N−b,m−b) of the C(N,m) possible samples. Their ratio equals C(m,b)/C(N,b) by factorial cancellation. Taking expectations and the alternating sum proves the identity; independence between faces is unnecessary. Under independent retention, a face’s inclusion probability is p^b instead. For fixed b and m/N=ρ bounded away from zero, expanding the product ∏_{j=0}^{b−1}(m−j)/(N−j) gives log Π=b log ρ−(1−ρ)b(b−1)/(2ρN)+O(N^{−2}); the constant may depend on b and the lower bound on ρ. In dimension three this becomes f₀p−f₁p²+f₂p³−f₃p⁴, with the same hypergeometric replacement for fixed-size sampling. This is an exact finite identity, not an asymptotic density argument.

## Worked example: setup

Take K to be the four-cycle graph, with N=4 and four edges, and sample m=2 vertices.

## Worked example: calculation

There are six samples. Four adjacent pairs induce an edge with χ=1; two opposite pairs induce isolated vertices with χ=2. Their mean is (4+4)/6=4/3. The formula gives 4·(2/4)−4·(1/6)=4/3. For m=3 each sample is a three-vertex path and χ=1.

## Worked example: conclusion

The full finite distribution checks the expectation and reveals the variability hidden by its mean.

## Limits

The same Euler characteristic can arise from different Betti numbers and different persistence intervals. A real geographic dataset or operational benefit is not established by this identity; the retrieved source explicitly reported no dataset execution.

## Extension

Derive the variance by classifying pairs of faces according to their union size, using their joint survival probability C(m,|σ∪τ|)/C(N,|σ∪τ|). Determine which additional observations identify Betti curves beyond the Euler projection.
