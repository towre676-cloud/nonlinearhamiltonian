## Revised question retained in the annual source

If an observable depends on β and t only through α=β^(−t), which parameter combinations are identifiable?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered puzzle contains several parameter pairs sharing the same exponent. This is an exact quotient phenomenon, not a numerical coincidence: a one-dimensional observable cannot recover two free parameters when it factors through their product in logarithmic coordinates.

## Definitions

Take β>1 and t>0 and define α=exp(−t logβ), so 0<α<1. Let an observable be J(β,t)=j(α) with differentiable scalar j.

Set u=logβ>0. The map to the exponent is α=e^(−tu), and its level sets are the hyperbolas tu=constant in the positive quadrant.

## Result name

Exact fibers and rank-one information

## Result statement

Two pairs give the same α exactly when t logβ agrees. The differential dα=−α(t du+u dt) has one-dimensional kernel, and any scalar Fisher matrix built from j(α) and one scalar noise variance has rank at most one.

## Proof

The real exponential is injective, proving the fiber characterization. Differentiating the exponent gives the displayed covector. Its kernel is spanned by (u,−t), which is nonzero on the domain. If noise variance is σ² and the mean is j(α), the local information matrix is σ⁻²∇J∇Jᵀ, an outer product, so its rank is at most one regardless of measurement precision. When j′(α)≠0 it is exactly one; if j′ vanishes it may fall to zero. A parameter convention or a genuinely independent observable is needed to remove the remaining ambiguity.

## Worked example: setup

Compare (β,t)=(2,1) and (4,1/2).

## Worked example: calculation

Both have t logβ=log2 and α=1/2. For the proved integral j(α)=π/[2(log2)^α], both yield π/(2√log2). The point (β,t)=(e²,1/2) instead gives α=e^(−1).

## Worked example: conclusion

Equality of the exponent is an exact algebraic explanation for equality of every observable that factors through it.

## Limits

This does not show that every quantity in the original program depends only on α. An additional observable can distinguish the pairs if it uses β or t separately.

## Extension

Choose a second measurement k(β,t), compute the determinant of the combined two-by-two Jacobian, and certify an open region on which both parameters are locally identifiable.
