## Revised question retained in the annual source

When is a multiplier reserve a valid stability certificate for gradient descent?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The final sweep recovers a direct optimizer connection. For a positive quadratic objective the boundary is exact, including its oscillatory character. A reserve near zero is interpretable only relative to a defined norm and eigenmode model.

## Definitions

Let f(x)=xᵀHx/2 with H symmetric positive definite and eigenvalues 0<λ_min≤λ_i≤λ_max. Gradient descent is x_(n+1)=(I−ηH)x_n with η>0.

Define q=max_i|1−ηλ_i| and reserve ρ=1−q. This worst-mode reserve differs from monitoring a single observed multiplier.

## Result name

Exact quadratic stability interval

## Result statement

Every initial condition converges to zero if and only if 0<η<2/λ_max. The top mode has multiplier −1 at η=2/λ_max, so the endpoint gives undamped sign alternation rather than convergence.

## Proof

Orthogonally diagonalize H. Each coordinate satisfies x_(n+1,i)=(1−ηλ_i)x_(n,i), which converges for every input exactly when all multipliers have modulus below one. The inequalities −1<1−ηλ_i<1 reduce to 0<ηλ_i<2 and hence the interval stated. The convergence bound is ||x_n||≤q^n||x₀|| and is attained on an extremal mode. Balancing 1−ηλ_min with −(1−ηλ_max) gives the optimal constant step η=2/(λ_min+λ_max), with q=(κ−1)/(κ+1), κ=λ_max/λ_min. Thus a top-mode flip boundary and a best overall step are different quantities.

## Worked example: setup

Use H=diag(1,4).

## Worked example: calculation

The stability interval is 0<η<1/2. At η=1/2 the multipliers are 1/2 and −1, so the second coordinate alternates forever. The optimal step is 2/5, giving multipliers 3/5 and −3/5, q=3/5 and reserve 2/5.

## Worked example: conclusion

The monitoring quantity has an exact linear interpretation and a transparent failure at the boundary.

## Limits

Nonlinear objectives, stochastic updates and time-varying steps require additional hypotheses; a fitted local multiplier does not certify every training mode.

## Extension

Bound the Hessian uniformly on an invariant sublevel set and compare a reserve-based adaptive rule with a proved variable-step contraction theorem.
