## Summary

The recovered halving and tripling discussion correctly points toward kernel cosets and possible missing rational preimages. A general group argument proves the fibre structure. The explicit doubling formula supplies an exact rational witness while showing why a quartic root alone is not a complete lift.

## Definitions

Let E:y²=x³+ax+b be nonsingular over a field K of characteristic different from2 and3. Write O for its identity.

For n≥1, [n] denotes multiplication in E(K), and E(K)[n] its rational n-torsion subgroup.

## Result name

Fibre-coset theorem and halving candidate equation

## Result statement

If [n]P₀=Q, then [n]⁻¹(Q)∩E(K)=P₀+E(K)[n]; otherwise the fibre is empty. For a finite P=(u,v) with v≠0 and x(2P)=X, u satisfies u⁴−4Xu³−2au²−(8b+4aX)u+a²−4bX=0.

## Proof

For any other rational preimage P, the homomorphism law gives [n](P−P₀)=O, so P−P₀ is rational n-torsion. Conversely adding such a torsion point preserves the image; translation is a bijection, proving the coset theorem. For doubling, the tangent slope is m=(3u²+a)/(2v), obtained by differentiating the curve equation. Intersecting the tangent line with the cubic and reflecting the third point gives X=m²−2u. Substituting v²=u³+au+b and clearing its nonzero denominator yields X=(u⁴−2au²−8bu+a²)/[4(u³+au+b)], which rearranges to the quartic. A root u must still admit v∈K, have a valid denominator and yield the prescribed y-coordinate of Q; the x-coordinate equation also accepts −Q.

## Limits

Having rational Q does not imply it has a rational n-preimage. A bounded saturation computation proves only its stated bound unless linked to a global divisibility theorem; generic tripling claims require their actual rational maps and exceptional cases.
