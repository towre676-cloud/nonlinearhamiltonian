## Revised question retained in the annual source

What does Lindemann–Weierstrass actually exclude in the recovered exponential master equation?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The source equation can be cleared of denominators and interpreted algebraically. Its transcendence consequence is strong only when all coefficients are independently algebraic. Coefficients defined from the same unknown solution do not meet that hypothesis.

## Definitions

For v>0 consider e^v=(C₁²/4)v²+(1+2C₁D)+4D²/v². Set T=C₁v/2+2D/v, so the right side is 1+T².

Assume C₁ and D are algebraic real numbers when invoking the algebraic-number consequence. They may instead be endogenous in the historical model, in which case that consequence is conditional.

## Result name

Conic reduction and algebraic exclusion

## Result statement

The equation is equivalent to 4v²e^v−C₁²v⁴−4(1+2C₁D)v²−16D²=0. If C₁,D are algebraic, no nonzero algebraic v can satisfy it.

## Proof

Expand T²=C₁²v²/4+2C₁D+4D²/v² and multiply by 4v², which is nonzero. If v,C₁,D were algebraic, the rational expression on the right would be algebraic, while e^v is transcendental for nonzero algebraic v by the Hermite–Lindemann consequence of Lindemann–Weierstrass. This contradiction proves the stated exclusion. If C₁ or D is chosen from v and e^v, the right side need not be algebraic, and the argument no longer applies. The conic identity itself is exact but does not confer an independently defined root-system or chamber classification.

## Worked example: setup

Take C₁=0 and D=1/2.

## Worked example: calculation

The equation becomes e^v=1+1/v², equivalently v²(e^v−1)=1. At v=1/2 the left side is about 0.16218; at v=1 it is e−1>1. Its derivative 2v(e^v−1)+v²e^v is positive, giving exactly one positive root. That root is transcendental by the theorem.

## Worked example: conclusion

An explicit monotone example yields both unique existence and a valid transcendence result.

## Limits

This proves nothing about algebraicity of source parameters built from the same flip candidate unless their independent algebraicity is established.

## Extension

Identify which coefficients are fixed in a specified algebraic field and obtain monotonicity or zero-counting bounds for that exact parameter family.
