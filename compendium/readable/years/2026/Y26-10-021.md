## Revised question retained in the annual source

When are two hidden states indistinguishable under every permitted experiment, and how can an additional action refine that quotient?

## Status

Classical mathematics or a newly chosen finite model developed with proof; historical claims delimited

## Summary

The recovered future-state refinement is stronger than comparing current outputs. The relevant equivalence depends on the full action alphabet. A finite partition-refinement proof supplies an exact stopping criterion and a concrete example in which reset changes identifiability.

## Definitions

Let S be a finite state set, o:S→O an output map, and F_a:S→S deterministic actions for a in a finite alphabet.

Two states are future-equivalent if o(F_w(s))=o(F_w(t)) for every finite action word w, including the empty word.

## Result name

Greatest observable congruence by partition refinement

## Result statement

Start with the partition by current output and repeatedly split blocks according to the blocks containing every action successor. The process stabilizes after at most |S|−1 strict refinements, and its final blocks are precisely the future-equivalence classes.

## Proof

At stage n, induction shows that two states share a block exactly when their outputs agree after every action word of length at most n: the next stage tests the empty word and then one action followed by a word of length at most n. Each strict refinement increases the number of nonempty blocks, which cannot exceed |S|. At a fixed point the partition respects both current output and every action, so induction over arbitrary word length gives future equivalence. Conversely states agreeing on all words are never separated at any finite stage. This proves the algorithm computes the greatest action-compatible quotient preserving observations. A new action can only refine this relation because it enlarges the set of possible experiments.

## Worked example: setup

Use S={a,b,c,d}, outputs o(a)=o(b)=o(c)=0 and o(d)=1.

## Worked example: calculation

With only an identity action, a and b remain indistinguishable forever. Add reset with reset(a)=c, reset(b)=d, reset(c)=c and reset(d)=d. The one-letter experiment reset now separates a and b. Thus a controller that omits reset may collapse states that a richer experiment vocabulary can distinguish.

## Worked example: conclusion

Identifiability is an exact property of the stated action-and-output system.

## Limits

Current-output equality alone is insufficient. Conversely, a finite test suite proves equivalence only if it is known to include a distinguishing basis or a stabilized refinement. Continuous-state systems need additional finiteness or algebraic structure.

## Extension

Attach a shortest distinguishing action word to every split; the resulting quotient certificate can support an adaptive experiment tree and reveal exactly which additional action reduces the unresolved state population.
