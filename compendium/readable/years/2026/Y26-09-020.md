## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which covariance defines the geometry, and can two candidate states be separated under that same geometry?

## Summary

The correlated-evidence passage used Mahalanobis separation and confidence-fiber intersection. These have exact finite certificates: whitening reduces them to Euclidean ball geometry, and Cauchy–Schwarz supplies matching primal and dual target bounds.

## Definitions

Let Q be symmetric positive definite and E={x:(x−x₀)ᵀQ(x−x₀)≤c²}, c≥0. For observations with injective H and covariance R>0 one often has Q=HᵀR⁻¹H.

The target is gᵀx. Two ellipsoids with common covariance R and radius c have centers a,b and the same Mahalanobis metric.

## Result name

A correlated confidence ellipsoid has exact target extrema and an intersection test

## Result statement

The target extrema on E are gᵀx₀±c√(gᵀQ⁻¹g). For g≠0 the bounds are attained at x₀±cQ⁻¹g/√(gᵀQ⁻¹g). Two equal-radius ellipsoids intersect exactly when(a−b)ᵀR⁻¹(a−b)≤4c².

## Proof

Write z=Q^(1/2)(x−x₀). The feasible set becomes||z||≤c and the target displacement is(Q^(−1/2)g)ᵀz. Cauchy–Schwarz gives the bound, and choosing z parallel or antiparallel to that vector proves attainment. Whitening both ellipsoid centers by R^(−1/2) turns them into equal balls. Their triangle inequality requires center distance at most2c; conversely the midpoint belongs to both whenever that inequality holds.
