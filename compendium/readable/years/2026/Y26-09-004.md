## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which randomization makes the paired differences exchangeable, and what can a small exact p-value establish without a model for effect size?

## Status

Classical mathematics with newly authored derivation or explicitly defined worked model

## Summary

The campaign description included correctness gates and randomized paired timing. The mathematical content is strongest when the randomization is explicit: under a sharp label-invariant null, swapping assignments within each pair gives a finite reference distribution. This is a theorem about a declared experiment, not evidence that the reported throughput gains occurred.

## Definitions

For m pairs, condition on nonnegative magnitudes a₁,…,a_m. Under the null assume independent fair signs ε_i∈{−1,1}, giving statistic S(ε)=Σε_i a_i.

For an observed sign vector ε define the one-sided randomization p-value p(ε)=2^(−m)#{η:S(η)≥S(ε)}. Ties are counted in the conservative direction.

## Result name

A paired sign-randomization test has a finite exact null certificate

## Result statement

For every0≤α≤1, the null probability P(p≤α) is at most α. With eight equal positive magnitudes and all observed signs positive, the one-sided p-value is1/256 and the absolute-value two-sided p-value is1/128.

## Proof

There are N=2^m equally likely sign vectors. Order their statistic values from greatest to least, grouping ties. For a vector in a tie group, Np is the number of values at least as large, namely that group’s ending rank. Every vector satisfying p≤α must therefore lie within at most floor(αN) of the ordered outcomes. Its probability is at most floor(αN)/N≤α. With equal positive magnitudes, the sum is uniquely maximized by the all-positive vector, giving one upper-tail outcome; its absolute value is maximized by all-positive and all-negative vectors, giving two outcomes.

## Worked example: setup

Use the newly declared illustrative magnitudes a_i=1 for i=1,…,8.

## Worked example: calculation

Enumerating all256 sign vectors gives the exact reference distribution. The maximum S=8 has multiplicity one and S=−8 also has multiplicity one. The example contains no fitted variance, normal approximation or asymptotic limit. Multiplying every magnitude by the same positive constant leaves these p-values unchanged, so a p-value does not determine the practical size of an improvement.

## Worked example: conclusion

The randomized comparison has a finite type-I-error guarantee under its explicit exchangeability assumption.

## Limits

Historical raw pairs and assignments were not recovered. Pairing alone does not make signs independent and fair. A correctness or selection gate depending on the observed timing sign can destroy the null distribution: retaining only positive differences makes positivity certain even under the original fair-sign null.

## Extension

Specify the assignment mechanism, preregister a statistic and preserve every pair. Add a separately justified confidence interval for an effect parameter rather than treating a small randomization p-value as an effect-size or hardware-correctness certificate.
