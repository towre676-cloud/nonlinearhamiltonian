## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which transport minimization gives the contraction factor (1+λτ)⁻¹, and what does it fail to prove about discrete prefixes?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

A quadratic potential on probability measures admits a closed-form proximal map. Completing the square inside a coupling proves contraction exactly, providing a rigorous transport model while preserving the distinction between metric convergence and exact tower stabilization.

## Definitions

Let μ have finite second moment on R^d, λ>0 and τ>0. Minimize J(ν)=(λ/2)∫|x|²dν+W₂²(ν,μ)/(2τ).

W₂² is the infimum of ∫|x−y|² over couplings with x-law ν and y-law μ.

Set q=(1+λτ)⁻¹ and T(y)=qy.

## Result name

Exact minimizer and contraction

## Result statement

The unique minimizer is ν=T_#μ. For two input measures μ,η, their outputs satisfy W₂(T_#μ,T_#η)=qW₂(μ,η). Repeated proximal steps therefore contract at rate q^n toward δ₀.

## Proof

Combine the energy and transport cost in one joint integral. Pointwise in y, (λ/2)|x|²+|x−y|²/(2τ) equals (λ+τ⁻¹)|x−qy|²/2 plus λ|y|²/[2(1+λτ)]. The second term is fixed by μ, and the first is nonnegative, vanishing only when x=qy. The deterministic coupling therefore attains the minimum and fixes the unique output law. Scaling any coupling by q gives the upper W₂ bound. Conversely rescale an output coupling by 1/q to recover an input coupling, giving the lower bound and equality. Iteration yields q^n scaling, and W₂(q^n_#μ,δ₀)=q^n(∫|y|²dμ)^{1/2}.

## Worked example: setup

Take λ=2,τ=1/2 and μ=N(2,9) on R.

## Worked example: calculation

Then q=1/2, so one step gives N(1,9/4); n steps give N(2^{1−n},9·4^{−n}). Distance to δ₀ starts at √13 and becomes 2^{−n}√13. The contraction and all moments are exact rather than inferred from an empirical fit.

## Worked example: conclusion

A precise variational problem yields the historical contraction factor with a complete proof. In particular its contraction rate is uniform over all finite-second-moment inputs, rather than fitted to one Gaussian trajectory.

## Limits

This energy contains no entropy term; adding entropy changes the proximal solution. Metric contraction alone does not prove exact equality of finite prefixes, Mittag–Leffler stabilization or a vanishing derived-limit obstruction without compatible observation hypotheses.

## Extension

Add relative entropy and derive the corresponding JKO Euler equation, then prove a quantitative convergence theorem under explicit displacement convexity. Combine it with the observation-cell condition from the finite-resolution entry before making a tower-stability claim.
