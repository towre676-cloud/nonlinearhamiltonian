## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which differential connections and integration cycles genuinely produce e−1 and √π?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

Elementary rank-one connections give explicit horizontal sections and convergent periods. Their construction separates a valid period identity from an unproved claim of algebraic independence or motivic rigidity.

## Definitions

For a polynomial Q(z), the rank-one connection ∇=d−dQ on the trivial line bundle has horizontal section e^Q, because d(e^Q)=e^QdQ.

Use Q=x on the finite real segment [0,1], and Q=−x² on the whole real line. The latter cycle is noncompact and requires decay.

A period identity specifies its integrand, path, endpoints and convergence convention; changing any of them changes the value.

## Result name

Two exact exponential periods and a decay sector

## Result statement

∫₀¹e^x dx=e−1 and ∫_R e^{−x²}dx=√π. For complex z=re^{iθ}, |e^{−z²}|=e^{−r²cos2θ}, which decays at least e^{−r²/√2} on |θ|≤π/8.

## Proof

The first identity follows from the antiderivative e^x. To evaluate the Gaussian, square its positive integral, apply Tonelli’s theorem and switch to polar coordinates: I²=∫_{R²}e^{−x²−y²}dxdy=2π∫₀^∞re^{−r²}dr=π. Positivity gives I=√π. The sector bound follows from cos2θ≥cos(π/4)=1/√2. The recovered polynomial x²−x can also be handled exactly: completing the square gives x²−x=(x−1/2)²−1/4, hence ∫_R e^{−(x²−x)}dx=e^{1/4}√π. Its associated horizontal connection must therefore use Q=−x²+x, not the opposite sign, if decay on the real axis is intended.

## Worked example: setup

Compare the segment and real-line periods with a shifted Gaussian.

## Worked example: calculation

The values are approximately 1.7182818, 1.7724539 and 2.2758758 respectively. The shifted Gaussian is finite because its completed square is negative at infinity. The integral of e^{x²−x} on R diverges, despite using the same polynomial with the other sign.

## Worked example: conclusion

An exact connection and cycle justify the value and its convergence; they do not supply transcendence relations among different values.

## Limits

No theorem here proves algebraic independence of e−1 and √π, nor the historical exponential-motivic rigidity program. A visual frame can illustrate the connection but cannot replace an independence proof.

## Extension

Specify a family of exponential integrals, derive its parameter differential equation and rapid-decay cycles, and compute connection matrices across decay sectors. State any proposed algebraic-independence theorem separately with its exact field and known status.
