## Revised question retained in the annual source

What can be proved about transient growth when all eigenvalues are stable, and which additional observations measure that growth?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

A two-dimensional Jordan block has exponentially decaying eigenmodes yet can amplify a vector before decaying. Its propagator, commutator and resolvent are explicit, separating eigenvalue stability from finite-time norm growth and pseudospectral sensitivity.

## Definitions

Take A=[[-1,s],[0,−1]], with real s≥0, and solve x′=Ax in Euclidean norm.

Normality means AAᵀ=AᵀA. The commutator C=AAᵀ−AᵀA measures failure of this identity.

The ε-pseudospectrum consists of complex z for which the smallest singular value of zI−A is below ε, equivalently the resolvent norm exceeds 1/ε off the spectrum.

## Result name

Exact propagation and a quantitative growth witness

## Result statement

e^{tA}=e^{−t}[[1,st],[0,1]]. Hence the trajectory from e₂ has norm e^{−t}√(1+s²t²), and the operator norm at t=1 is at least √(1+s²)/e. For s>√(e²−1), finite-time amplification exceeds one despite spectrum {−1}.

## Proof

Write A=−I+sN with N²=0; commuting exponentials truncate to the displayed formula. Acting on e₂ gives the norm formula. Squaring A and Aᵀ products gives C=diag(s²,−s²), so tr(C²)=2s⁴. The propagator nevertheless tends to zero because every polynomial in t is dominated by e^t. For z≠−1, (zI−A)⁻¹=[[1/(z+1),s/(z+1)²],[0,1/(z+1)]]. Its action on e₂ has norm at least s/|z+1|². Therefore every z with |z+1|<√(sε) satisfies the resolvent threshold. This lower inclusion is enough to demonstrate sensitivity; it does not assert that this disk is the exact pseudospectrum.

## Worked example: setup

Let s=4 and t=1.

## Worked example: calculation

The input norm is one and the output norm is √17/e≈1.5168. The commutator has diagonal entries ±16 and tr(C²)=512. For ε=.01, the resolvent witness contains |z+1|<.2. When s=0 the commutator vanishes and every trajectory norm decays exactly as e^{−t}.

## Worked example: conclusion

The example gives directly testable finite-time evidence rather than a universal scalar transition threshold.

## Limits

A commutator magnitude depends on normalization and does not alone determine transient amplification for all matrices. The historical Π≈1/π and grokking ratios are not mathematical consequences of this Jordan calculation.

## Extension

For a supplied time-varying Jacobian, estimate the propagator’s largest singular value and compare it with resolvent and numerical-abscissa bounds. Establish a perturbation theorem with declared norms, rather than infer training divergence from one spectral statistic.
