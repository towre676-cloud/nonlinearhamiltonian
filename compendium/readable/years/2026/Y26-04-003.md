## Revised question retained in the annual source

How does the corrected cycle formula determine its diagonal Gram entry and possible rank?

## Status

## Summary

The source explicitly corrects a cycle-norm exponent. Independence gives the answer immediately once the random variables and coefficient are declared. A diagonal norm calculation does not by itself prove that an arbitrary family has full rank.

## Definitions

Let ξ_e be independent centered Gaussians with Eξ_e²=A_e⁻¹, A_e>0. For distinct finite edge sets C define P_C=−(Σ_(e∈C)A_e)∏_(e∈C)ξ_e.

Let G_CD=E[P_CP_D]. Distinct simple edge sets are assumed; repeating an observable creates a repeated row.

## Result name

Exact Gaussian cycle Gram formula

## Result statement

For this model G_CC=(Σ_(e∈C)A_e)²∏_(e∈C)A_e⁻¹. Distinct edge sets are mutually orthogonal, so every nonzero such monomial gives one positive Gram eigenvalue.

## Proof

Squaring P_C squares its scalar coefficient once and yields ξ_e² for every edge in C. Independence factors the expectation into ∏A_e⁻¹, proving the diagonal formula. If C≠D, at least one index lies in their symmetric difference and occurs to the first power in P_CP_D. Its centered expectation is zero independently of all other factors, giving G_CD=0. Each diagonal is strictly positive. Therefore the Gram matrix is positive definite for pairwise distinct sets under the declared independent covariance model. A source divergence polynomial may contain additional terms or correlated variables, and then this orthogonality proof must be replaced by a genuine linear-independence argument.

## Worked example: calculation

The sum is six and the variance product is 1/6, hence G_CC=6²/6=6. Raising the sum to the cycle length would instead give 6³/6=36, contradicting direct expectation. Two copies of the same P_C produce the singular matrix [[6,6],[6,6]].

## Worked example: conclusion

The corrected coefficient power is exact, and full rank has explicit sufficient hypotheses.
