## Revised question retained in the annual source

What information do the power sums determine, and why can padding create tiny complex roots?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The July20 cluster reported three roots and a padded degree-five inversion with a tiny complex pair. Newton identities explain an exact failure mode. It is a reconstruction artifact unless the analytic root count and moment enclosures independently justify the larger degree.

## Definitions

For N roots r_j, power sums are s_k=Σr_j^k, including s₀=N. Newton identities recursively give ke_k=Σ_{i=1}^k(−1)^(i−1)e_{k−i}s_i,e₀=1. The monic polynomial is t^N−e₁t^(N−1)+⋯+(−1)^Ne_N.

## Result name

Exact reconstruction and padding instability

## Result statement

The first N positive power sums and the correct N determine the polynomial. Multiplying it by t^m leaves every positive power sum unchanged but adds m zero roots; ignoring s₀ therefore loses degree information.

## Proof

Newton’s recursion derives by comparing the logarithmic derivative of∏(1+r_jz) with its power-series expansion, or by multiplying the root equations and collecting symmetric terms. Appending zero roots changes no s_k for k≥1 but changes s₀, proving the ambiguity. Repeated padded zeros are ill-conditioned: t(t²−1)(t²+ε) differs from the padded polynomial t³(t²−1) by coefficients of orderε but has an extra pair±i√ε. A small coefficient or moment error can therefore produce a much larger imaginary displacement. A separate transport law multiplying a moment error by4^m amplifies its absolute enclosure by that factor unless correlations are explicitly retained.

## Worked example: calculation

Their moments are s₀=3,s₁=0,s₂=2,s₃=0. Degree three yields t³−t. Degree five with the same positive moments introduces extra zeros. Atε=10⁻⁸ the perturbed padded factor gives imaginary roots±10⁻⁴i, despite coefficient changes of size10⁻⁸.

## Worked example: conclusion

A complex pair in a padded reconstruction is not evidence of off-axis zeros of the original analytic function.
