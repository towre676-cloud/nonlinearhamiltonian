## Revised question retained in the annual source

How does a local angular defect recover Euler characteristic on a triangulated surface?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The July25 finite-topology register names Gauss–Bonnet alongside homology and transport. Its simplest complete version is a piecewise Euclidean surface theorem. It connects local geometric data with an independently countable topological invariant and supplies an exact baseline for any richer representation.

## Definitions

Let a finite triangulation of a closed surface haveV vertices,E edges andF faces. Give each face a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle metric, with matching lengths across glued edges. At a vertexv define angular defectκ_v=2π−Σ_{f∋v}θ_{f,v}. Euler characteristic isχ=V−E+F.

## Result name

Polyhedral Gauss–Bonnet

## Result statement

The total angular defect isΣ_vκ_v=2πχ. No orientability assumption is needed for this counting form of the theorem.

## Proof

Every Euclidean face contributes exactlyπ when its three angles are added. ThusΣ_vκ_v=2πV−πF. Because the surface is closed, every edge is incident to two triangular faces, so3F=2E. Substitute this relation into2π(V−E+F) to obtain2πV−πF, proving the identity. Both the local-angle sum and global incidence counts are essential. A mesh with boundary has boundary edges incident to one face and requires an additional boundary turning term; the closed formula cannot simply be copied. The theorem is insensitive to how curvature is redistributed among vertices while the incidence topology stays fixed. It does not say that every assignment of defects summing to2πχ is realizable by a chosen edge-length metric.

## Worked example: setup

Take the boundary of a regular tetrahedron, with four equilateral triangular faces.

## Worked example: calculation

HereV=4,E=6,F=4 andχ=2. Exactly three face anglesπ/3 meet at each vertex, so each angular defect isπ. The total is4π=2π·2. Subdividing a flat face by adding an interior vertex creates no new total defect: the interior angle sum is2π and the surrounding old angles still add to the original three face angles.

A topology–geometry bridge is certified by elementary incidence and angle identities rather than a visual resemblance.
