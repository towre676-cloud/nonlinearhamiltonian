## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which eigenvalue sum and curvature normalization make the edge identity an exact Taylor theorem?

## Status

Classical mathematics with newly authored derivation or explicitly defined worked model

## Summary

The source stated a weighted Ky Fan edge identity. Its elementary finite-dimensional mechanism is useful on its own: sums of leading eigenvalues of an affine Hermitian family are convex, and their nonlinear excess over a tangent line is an integral of curvature. The normalization must be declared before matching historical symbols.

## Definitions

Let A(s)=A₀+sV be Hermitian and F(s) the sum of its n largest eigenvalues. Assume F is twice continuously differentiable on[0,1].

Define the edge remainder B=F(1)−F(0)−F′(0) and curvature kernel K(s)=F″(s)/2.

## Result name

A Ky Fan edge remainder is a weighted curvature integral

## Result statement

F is convex and B=2∫₀¹(1−s)K(s)ds≥0. The identity is exact for the stated normalization and does not depend on a numerical finite difference.

## Proof

The Ky Fan variational formula is F(s)=max_(P=P*=P²,rankP=n)Tr(PA(s)). To prove it, diagonalize A with decreasing eigenvalues. The diagonal entries of an orthogonal rank-n projection lie in[0,1] and sum to n, so its weighted eigenvalue sum is at most the sum of the n largest; the top-eigenspace projection attains it. Every fixed P gives an affine function of s, and their supremum is convex. Integrating F″ twice gives F(1)=F(0)+F′(0)+∫₀¹(1−s)F″(s)ds. Convexity makes F″≥0 where it exists, proving the sign.

## Worked example: setup

Take A(s)=[[s,1],[1,−s]] and n=1.

## Worked example: calculation

Its leading eigenvalue is F(s)=√(1+s²), so F(0)=1,F′(0)=0 and F″=(1+s²)^(−3/2). The remainder is√2−1. The weighted integral equals∫₀¹(1−s)/(1+s²)^(3/2)ds=√2−1 by elementary antiderivatives. This gives an exact2×2 certificate of the edge mechanism. No negative remainder is compatible with these stated hypotheses.

## Worked example: conclusion

The spectral remainder has a proved sign and a normalized curvature representation.
