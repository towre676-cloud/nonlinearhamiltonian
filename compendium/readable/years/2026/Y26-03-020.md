## Revised question retained in the annual source

What can be deduced exactly from B(k)=B₀k²/(k²+w²) without importing a physical running law?

## Status

## Summary

The recovered scale model is mathematically elementary but its integration measure matters. Integrating against log k produces a logarithm, while integrating against k gives a different quantity. Declaring the differential equation makes the claimed suppression factor reproducible.

## Definitions

Let k,w>0 and B(k)=B₀k²/(k²+w²), with constant B₀. Define a scalar running model d logλ/d log k=−B(k), with λ(k₀)>0.

This is an explicitly chosen ODE. Its sign, coefficient and boundary condition are not derived from the recovered physical discussion.

## Result name

Exact integration of the declared model

## Result statement

For k₁>k₀>0, ∫_(k₀)^(k₁)B(k)d log k=(B₀/2)log[(k₁²+w²)/(k₀²+w²)]. Consequently λ(k₁)/λ(k₀)=[(k₀²+w²)/(k₁²+w²)]^(B₀/2).

## Proof

Use d log k=dk/k. The integrand becomes B₀k/(k²+w²), whose antiderivative is (B₀/2)log(k²+w²). Integrating the declared differential equation then gives the ratio. If B₀≥0 the accumulated area and suppression are monotone in the upper endpoint; B₀=0 gives no running. The expression is dimensionally valid because the logarithm takes a ratio of squared scales. Solving for w is possible only if the desired area lies in the range allowed by the endpoints and B₀; it is not guaranteed by assigning a preferred target.

## Worked example: setup

Use B₀=2, w=1, k₀=1 and k₁=3.
