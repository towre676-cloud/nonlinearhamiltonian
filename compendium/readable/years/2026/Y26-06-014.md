Reconstruction: Which exact lattice invariants survive a basis change, and how does a half-open cell avoid overlap?

## Summary

The June lattice material gives a fundamental parallelotope, covolume and successive minima. We prove the tiling and basis invariance explicitly and compute a nonorthogonal example. The result provides genuine geometry without implying a compiler speedup.

## Definitions

Let B be an invertible real n-by-n matrix, L=BZ^n and P=B[0,1)^n. Its covolume is Lebesgue volume of P.

A unimodular integer matrix U has determinant±1 and inverse integral. Successive minima λ_i(L) are the least radii of Euclidean balls containing i linearly independent lattice vectors.

## Result name

A lattice parallelotope tiles space and basis changes preserve covolume

## Result statement

The translates P+ℓ,ℓ∈L, partition R^n uniquely. VolP=|detB|. Two bases B and BU with U unimodular generate the same lattice and have the same covolume; their successive minima also coincide because these depend on the lattice set.

## Proof

For any x, write y=B^{−1}x and decompose each coordinate uniquely as its integer floor plus a fractional part in[0,1). This gives x=Bk+Bf with k∈Z^n and f∈[0,1)^n. Uniqueness of integer and fractional parts gives a genuine partition, including boundary points under the half-open convention. Linear change of variables multiplies volume by |detB|. If U is unimodular, UZ^n=Z^n, so BU generates the same set. Conversely if B and B′ generate the same lattice, U=B^{−1}B′ and U^{−1} have integer entries, forcing detU=±1. The successive-minimum statement then follows from equality of the lattice sets, not from a false claim that a nonorthogonal basis preserves the lengths of individual coefficient vectors.

## Worked example: setup

Take B=[[2,1],[0,1]].

## Worked example: calculation

The covolume is two. L consists of integer vectors(x, y) with x−y even. The shortest nonzero vectors are(1,1),(1,−1) and their negatives, all of length√2; the first two are independent, so λ₁=λ₂=√2. Multiplying B by U=[[1,1],[0,1]] gives a visually different skew cell but the same lattice and area. The displayed original basis vector(2,0) is longer than λ₁, illustrating why a basis column need not be a shortest vector.

## Worked example: conclusion

The lattice model has exact tiling, determinant and shortest-vector certificates.

## Limits

No determinant ratio alone proves a runtime or arithmetic-operation reduction. An application must specify which lattice and which algorithm use the basis.

## Extension

For a conductor lattice, derive its exact integer basis and use a proved reduction algorithm to compare basis conditioning, enumeration cost and invariant covolume separately.
