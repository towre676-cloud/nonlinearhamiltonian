## Summary

The followup supplies an explicit hybrid bound and effective margin. We derive it with all metric-jump and counting assumptions present. The factor one-half is explained by working with squared norms rather than norms.

## Definitions

Along continuous pieces let q(t)≥0 obey q′≤−2α_min q. At each jump let q(t⁺)≤ρq(t⁻), with ρ≥1. Suppose the number of jumps through time t satisfies N(t)≤N₀+t/τ_min.

A dwell time τ_min>0 is a lower bound on the time between successive jumps; the offset N₀ permits a prescribed initial burst.

## Result name

Hybrid switching consumes a logarithmic metric budget at each jump

## Result statement

q(t)≤ρ^{N₀}exp[−2(α_min−logρ/(2τ_min))t]q(0). Hence a positive effective margin α_eff=α_min−logρ/(2τ_min) proves exponential norm decay, up to the initial factor ρ^{N₀/2}.

## Proof

Integrate the differential inequality on each continuous interval, multiply the resulting exponential factors and the at-most-ρ factors at jumps, and use that the continuous interval lengths add to t. This gives q(t)≤e^{−2α_min t}ρ^{N(t)}q(0). Because ρ≥1, the jump-count upper bound can be substituted in the increasing function ρ^N, yielding the displayed expression. Taking square roots converts the squared-energy rate to a norm rate. If ρ<1, jumps are contractive and a lower rather than upper bound on their number would be needed to extract a negative logarithmic improvement; without that, simply ignore their favorable factors. If dwell time is absent, arbitrarily fast harmful switching can overwhelm a positive continuous margin.

## Worked example: setup

Let α_min=1, ρ=4 and τ_min=1.

## Worked example: calculation

Then α_eff=1−log2≈0.306853, still positive. With one jump each unit time and equality in every bound, q(n)=4^n e^{−2n}q(0), so the formula is attained on integer times. Reducing τ_min to1/2 changes the margin to1−log4<0, and the same scalar construction grows instead of decaying.

## Worked example: conclusion

The formula is a proved stability theorem with a sharp scalar example.

## Limits

An LMI for each mode does not by itself establish the jump comparison ρ between their metrics or the dwell-time count. A numerical eigenvalue tolerance is separate from a rigorous residual enclosure.

## Extension

For finitely many certified mode metrics, bound each transition ratio by a generalized eigenvalue calculation, then optimize dwell constraints along the actual transition graph rather than using a worst-case global ρ.
