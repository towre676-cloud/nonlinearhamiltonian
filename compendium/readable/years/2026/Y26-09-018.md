## Summary

The late-September synthesis identified target-relative observation as the central object. In finite dimensions the factorization and operator inequality have a complete proof. This is stronger than demanding a full-rank state observer and more precise than checking that one derivative happens to vanish.

## Definitions

Let O:Rⁿ→Rᵐ be linear observations and G:Rⁿ→Rᵏ a linear target. All spaces carry their Euclidean norms.

A bounded recovery map is a linear L with G=LO. The quadratic inequality GᵀG≼λOᵀO means||Gx||²≤λ||Ox||² for every x.

## Result name

A target is observable exactly when it factors through the observation map

## Result statement

The following are equivalent: kerO⊂kerG; G=LO for some L; and GᵀG≼λOᵀO for some finiteλ≥0. The optimal λ is the square of the smallest possible recovery norm.

## Proof

If kerO⊂kerG, define L on rangeO by L(Ox)=Gx. Equal observations differ by a vector in kerO, so this is well defined. Extend L by zero on the orthogonal complement of rangeO; finite dimensionality makes it bounded. Its norm gives the quadratic inequality. Conversely that inequality forces Gx=0 whenever Ox=0, recovering the kernel condition. Every factorization gives the inequality with λ=||L||², while the smallest possible norm on rangeO is exactly sup_(Ox≠0)||Gx||/||Ox||. This proves the optimal constant as well as existence.

## Worked example: setup

Take O=diag(1,1,0) and G=(1,2,0).

## Worked example: calculation

The third coordinate is completely invisible, but irrelevant to this target. Recovery is L(y)=y₁+2y₂, with optimal squared norm five. The matrix5OᵀO−GᵀG has upper block[[4,−2],[−2,1]], a positive-semidefinite rank-one matrix. Changing the target to G′=(0,0,1) destroys the kernel inclusion and makes every finite quadratic bound impossible. In a linear dynamical system, O can instead stack C,CA,…,CA^(n−1); the same theorem then identifies target-relevant unobservable directions.

## Worked example: conclusion

The observer can be judged against the actual target, with an exact recovery and noise-amplification certificate.

## Limits

For infinite-dimensional operators, mere kernel inclusion need not yield a bounded factor; closed-range or majorization conditions become essential. The recovered Douglas correction explicitly addresses that distinction, and the finite proof is not silently extended.

## Extension

Compute the minimum-norm factor on the real observation space, bound its singular values and determine how additional sensors improve target recovery without requiring complete state identifiability.
