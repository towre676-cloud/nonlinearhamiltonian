## Summary

The monograph supplies the array {6,5,1;1,1,6} and a relation B²=X+6I. These are useful exact constraints, but their operators must not be silently identified. The following result derives everything forced by the array under the explicit distance-regularity hypothesis.

## Definitions

Let A be the adjacency matrix of a connected distance-regular graph with intersection array {6,5,1;1,1,6}.

For a fixed vertex, b_i and c_i count neighbors moving outward and inward between its distance shells; a_i=6−b_i−c_i counts neighbors staying in the same shell.

## Result name

Conditional forty-two-vertex spectrum

## Result statement

Such a graph has 42 vertices and spectrum 6¹,2²¹,(−1)⁶,(−3)¹⁴. Its shell quotient is [[0,6,0,0],[1,0,5,0],[0,1,4,1],[0,0,6,0]], whose characteristic polynomial is (t−6)(t−2)(t+1)(t+3).

## Proof

Counting edges between consecutive shells gives k_(i+1)c_(i+1)=k_i b_i. Starting k₀=1 produces shell sizes 1,6,30,5, hence 42 vertices. The stated quotient follows from the intersection numbers. The distance matrices A₀=I,A₁=A,A₂,A₃ satisfy a three-term multiplication recurrence; each A_i is a degree-i polynomial in A, and the final recurrence annihilates A with the quotient characteristic polynomial. The four nonempty disjoint distance matrices are linearly independent, so A has exactly four eigenvalues, precisely the quotient roots. Connected regularity makes eigenvalue six simple. If the other multiplicities are m₂,m_−1,m_−3, their sum is41, while tr A=0 and tr A²=42·6 give 6+2m₂−m_−1−3m_−3=0 and36+4m₂+m_−1+9m_−3=252. Solving yields21,6,14.
