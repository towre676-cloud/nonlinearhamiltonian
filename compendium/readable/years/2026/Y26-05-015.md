## Revised question retained in the annual source

Reconstruction: What exact example separates small-time divergence from trace class for every positive heat time?

## Status

Classical result or newly stipulated finite model; historical claims explicitly delimited

## Summary

The HED audit correctly separates asymptotic information about a spectral diagnostic from an operator-ideal assertion. A diagonal heat semigroup makes the distinction precise. It also supplies error bounds that can replace an unreachable absolute threshold.

## Definitions

On ℓ²(N₀), let A have diagonal eigenvalues n, n≥0. The heat operator H_t=e^{−tA} has eigenvalues e^{−tn}, t>0.

A positive diagonal operator is trace class exactly when its diagonal entries have a finite sum. Small-time heat behavior concerns t→0 rather than a fixed t.

## Result name

A heat-trace asymptotic is different from a trace-class statement at a fixed time

## Result statement

H_t is trace class for every t>0, with TrH_t=1/(1−e^{−t})=t^{−1}+1/2+O(t) as t→0. Its trace diverges as t→0, and H_t converges strongly to the noncompact identity. The trace-norm truncation tail after n=N is e^{−t(N+1)}/(1−e^{−t}).

## Proof

The geometric series gives the trace and exact tail, all terms being nonnegative. Taylor expansion 1−e^{−t}=t−t²/2+O(t³) and division give the small-time expansion. For a fixed vector x∈ℓ², ||(H_t−I)x||²=Σ_n|e^{−tn}−1|²|x_n|² tends to zero by dominated convergence, since the multiplier is bounded by one. Identity is not compact because the standard basis has no norm-convergent subsequence. Thus trace-class operators can converge strongly to a noncompact operator while their trace norms diverge. This does not contradict trace-norm closedness because convergence is only strong. For a trace difference, a cancellation of leading asymptotic coefficients likewise says nothing by itself about whether the corresponding operator difference is trace class at a fixed parameter.

## Worked example: setup

At t=log2 the heat eigenvalues are 2^{−n}.

## Worked example: calculation

The full trace is two. Keeping indices n=0,…,9 gives trace 2−2^{−9}, and the exact positive tail is 2^{−9}=1/512. At t=log2/2 the trace is 1/(1−1/√2)=2+√2, showing sensitivity to the time scale without any failure of trace class.

## Worked example: conclusion

The model gives separately a summability theorem, an asymptotic formula and an explicit numerical cutoff certificate.

## Limits

This example does not identify the historical Δ₁ operator or prove trace-class behavior for an unspecified geometric generator. A scalar statistic and a trace norm must not be interchanged.

## Extension

For the actual operator, derive eigenvalue or singular-value bounds uniform in the proposed parameter range, and choose truncation N(t,ε) from a proven tail estimate.
