## Definitions

Consider f_μ(x)=−(1+μ)x+x³ for real μ near zero, with complex μ allowed when discussing analytic branch continuation.

The fixed branch x=0 exists for every μ. For μ>0, let x_±=±√μ. The two-cycle multiplier is the product of derivatives at its two points.

## Result name

Exact square-root cycle and branch exchange

## Result statement

f_μ(x_+)=x_- and f_μ(x_-)=x_+. For 0<μ<1 the two-cycle is attracting, since its multiplier is (−1+2μ)²<1. Continuing μ once around zero interchanges x_+ and x_-, while the fixed branch remains zero.

## Proof

Substitute x²=μ: f_μ(x)=−(1+μ)x+μx=−x. Nonzero μ gives two distinct cycle points. Differentiate f′=−1−μ+3x², which becomes −1+2μ at either point, proving the multiplier formula. At zero, the fixed-point multiplier is −1−μ and crosses −1 transversely with derivative −1. The algebraic curve x²=μ is parametrized by x, and over a loop μ=re^(it), a local square root is √r e^(it/2). At t=2π it changes sign, establishing a two-sheet branch exchange. The zero fixed branch is a globally single-valued analytic function of μ and has no such exchange.

## Worked example: calculation
