## Summary

The explicit wall operator is a tractable Pöschl–Teller problem. Factorization proves stability and identifies its discrete modes. An intertwining argument excludes any additional bound state; the mathematical result does not certify the source’s physical mass or lifetime estimates.

## Definitions

Let k>0 and L=k²[−d²/dx²+4−6 sech²x] on L²(R), with domain H²(R).

Use the L² adjoint of d/dx, namely −d/dx, and write A=d/dx+2tanh x and B=d/dx+tanh x.

## Result name

Exact discrete and essential spectrum

## Result statement

L/k²=A* A≥0. Its discrete eigenvalues are 0 and 3, each simple, with eigenfunctions sech²x and sech x·tanh x respectively; its essential spectrum is [4,∞).

## Proof

Using (tanh x)′=sech²x and tanh²x=1−sech²x gives A*A=−d²+4−6sech²x. The kernel solves Af=0, hence f=C sech²x, which is square-integrable and unique. The partner AA*=−d²+4−2sech²x equals B*B+3, while BB*=−d²+1. The kernel of B is span(sech x), giving an eigenvalue three of AA*. Applying A* yields a nonzero multiple of sech x·tanh x, hence the second eigenfunction of A*A. If A*A had any other positive eigenvalue λ<4, A would transport its eigenfunction to an eigenfunction of B*B with eigenvalue λ−3. Positivity excludes λ<3. For 3<λ<4, applying B would produce an L² eigenfunction of −d²+1 below one, impossible by its Fourier transform. At λ=3 the kernel of B is one-dimensional. The decaying bounded potential is a relatively compact perturbation of −d²+4, so the essential spectrum remains [4,∞); equivalently, the standard resolvent compactness follows by local Rellich compactness and a small sup-norm tail.
