## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which binary matrix computes the Pauli symmetries of a declared Hamiltonian?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The July4 sector calculations have a precise finite-field core. Once the actual nonzero Pauli support is specified, its centralizer follows from one symplectic nullspace. This constructive theorem states what the reported chemical, spin and lattice dimensions would need to certify without adopting unseen matrices.

## Definitions

Encode an n-qubit Pauli up to phase by v=(x,z)∈F₂^(2n). Define ω(v,w)=x·z′+z·x′. Let H=Σ_{s∈S}h_sP(s), with distinct support labels and all h_s≠0.

## Result name

Centralizer formula

## Result statement

P(v) commutes with H exactly when ω(v,s)=0 for every s∈S. Thus C(H)=span(S)^⊥ has dimension2n−rank_F2(S).

## Proof

The single-qubit relation XZ=−ZX gives P(v)P(s)=(−1)^ω(v,s)P(s)P(v). If all pairings vanish, each term commutes. Conversely a nonzero commutator is a sum of nonzero multiples of Pauli matrices labeled v+s for the anticommuting support labels. Those labels are distinct, so linear independence of the Pauli basis prevents cancellation. Therefore all pairings must vanish. The symplectic form is nondegenerate, so the annihilator dimension is the ambient dimension minus the support span dimension. This proof also explains why one must first combine duplicate Pauli terms and remove coefficients that cancel to zero. Each independent binary commutation equation halves the number of solutions. If the support span has rankr, the kernel therefore contains exactly2^{2n−r} Pauli labels. This is a count of Pauli symmetries modulo scalar phase, not automatically the dimension of the full operator commutant ofH; degeneracies may create further linear combinations of operators commuting withH.

## Worked example: setup

Take n=2,H=Z₁+Z₂.

## Worked example: calculation

The support equations force x₁=x₂=0 and leave z₁,z₂ free. The Pauli centralizer has dimension two and consists of I,Z₁,Z₂,Z₁Z₂ up to phase. Replacing H by X₁+Z₁+X₂+Z₂ makes the four binary support directions span everything, so the Pauli centralizer is zero-dimensional.

## Worked example: conclusion

Exact GF₂ linear algebra yields the symmetry space; interpreting it as a commuting sector algebra additionally requires an isotropic choice.

## Limits

A Pauli centralizer is not the entire real or complex operator commutant. The source’s particular Hamiltonian support tables were not recovered, so their dimension reports remain qualified.
