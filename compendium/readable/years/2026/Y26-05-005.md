## Revised question retained in the annual source

Reconstruction: Under which conventions does a real-quadratic regulator produce the stated factor four in a hyperbolic length?

## Status

## Summary

The source states a relation between a regulator, a geodesic length and a fourth-power unit factor. We give the precise determinant-one matrix convention that makes such a relation true, while avoiding the unrecovered normalization of the source energy coefficients.

## Definitions

Let φ=(1+√5)/2, a unit of norm −1 in Q(√5), and define R=logφ. The Fibonacci matrix A=[[1,1],[1,0]] has eigenvalues φ and −φ^{−1}.

The determinant-one hyperbolic matrix M=A²=[[2,1],[1,1]] acts on the upper half-plane by Möbius transformations. For a positive hyperbolic matrix with eigenvalues λ,λ^{−1}, λ>1, its translation length is 2logλ.

## Result name

Fibonacci matrices connect units, geodesic length and an exact fourth-power decay

## Result statement

M has translation length 4R, and its eigenvalue contraction ratio is φ^{−4}. Moreover φ^{−2}=2−φ. These are exact algebraic identities; they do not determine a quotient-energy normalization.

## Proof

The characteristic polynomial of A is t²−t−1. Squaring its eigenvalues gives M eigenvalues φ² and φ^{−2}, both positive and reciprocal. Conjugating a hyperbolic Möbius transformation to z↦λ²z moves points along its vertical invariant geodesic. The hyperbolic line element |dz|/Imz integrates from i to λ²i as logλ²=2logλ, proving the length formula. With λ=φ² this is 4logφ. The ratio of the stable and unstable eigenvalues is φ^{−2}/φ²=φ^{−4}, exactly exp(−length). Finally φ²=φ+1 implies φ(φ−1)=1, and squaring φ−1 gives φ^{−2}=φ²−2φ+1=2−φ. A different choice of fundamental norm-positive unit would use φ² and regulator 2logφ; the same length would then be twice that regulator. The factor four therefore records a convention, not an invariant independent of unit normalization.

## Worked example: calculation

TrM=3, and 2cosh(length/2)=3. The projective eigenvalue ratio after m iterations is φ^{−4m}. At m=1, φ^{−4}=(7−3√5)/2≈0.145898. The fixed boundary points solve x=(2x+1)/(x+1), hence x²−x−1=0, recovering φ and −φ^{−1}.
