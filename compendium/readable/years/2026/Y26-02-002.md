## Revised question retained in the annual source

When can the birth distribution of a persistent cycle be derived exactly from its support?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

A four-cycle with independent vertex arrival times gives a complete random persistence interval. The example distinguishes a necessary support-survival law from claims about arbitrary cycle detection, where alternative representatives and earlier fillings change the answer.

## Definitions

Give each vertex of a four-cycle an independent Uniform[0,1] birth time U_i. An edge enters when both its endpoints have arrived.

At time one, add one diagonal and the two triangles filling the square. Before that time the filtration contains only the induced cycle graph.

Work with homology over a field. Let B=max(U₁,U₂,U₃,U₄).

## Result name

A random interval and its expected length

## Result statement

Almost surely the degree-one barcode consists of the single interval [B,1). Its birth cumulative distribution is P(B≤t)=t⁴ for 0≤t≤1, expected length is 1/5, and the expected first Betti number is t⁴ for 0≤t<1.

## Proof

Before all four vertices arrive, every induced subgraph is a forest and therefore has H₁=0. Once they all arrive, the full cycle is present and has one independent cycle because m−n+1=1. At time one the two triangles and their diagonal form a disk, killing that class. Independence gives P(B≤t)=∏P(U_i≤t)=t⁴. Its density is 4t³, hence E B=∫₀¹4t⁴dt=4/5 and E(1−B)=1/5. Alternatively the layer-cake identity gives expected bar length ∫₀¹P(B≤t)dt=1/5. These two calculations independently connect a random barcode with its expected Betti curve.

## Worked example: setup

Compare observation at t=1/2 with sampling exactly three of the four vertices.

## Worked example: calculation

Under independent arrival, the cycle has appeared with probability (1/2)⁴=1/16 at t=.5. Under a uniform three-vertex sample the cycle is never present. For a uniform m-element subset of a larger N-vertex system, a specified b-vertex support survives with probability C(m,b)/C(N,b), agreeing with the separate hypergeometric formula.

## Worked example: conclusion

A fully declared filtration permits an exact persistence distribution rather than a generic random-barcode slogan.

## Limits

In a larger complex, a homology class may have several representatives or be filled before all vertices of a chosen support arrive. Support inclusion is then not automatically equivalent to persistence detection.

## Extension

For a supplied complex, enumerate minimal cycle supports and fillings, use inclusion–exclusion for alternative representatives, and derive a confidence bound for detecting every bar longer than a stated threshold.
