## Revised question retained in the annual source

What exactly does the proposed Mann–Whitney AUROC measure, and how can it be checked without threshold sampling?

## Status

## Summary

The source adds pairwise AUROC for two groups. Its exact formula is a finite combinatorial count. It is invariant under strictly increasing score transformations, while group balancing and causal intervention are separate questions.

## Definitions

Let positive scores p₁,…,p_m and negative scores n₁,…,n_k be real. Assume both sample sizes are nonzero. Count a strict win as one and a tie as one half.

Define U=Σ_(i,j)[1_(p_i>n_j)+1/2·1_(p_i=n_j)] and AUROC=U/(mk).

## Result name

Exact pairwise interpretation

## Result statement

AUROC is the probability that a uniformly chosen positive has larger score than an independently uniformly chosen negative, with ties split equally. It is unchanged by any strictly increasing transformation of all scores.

## Proof

There are exactly mk equiprobable positive-negative pairs; summing their declared win values and dividing gives the probability statement. A strictly increasing function preserves strict inequalities and equalities, so every pair contribution remains unchanged. The half-tie convention ensures that if every score is equal, AUROC is exactly 1/2. A decreasing transformation reverses wins and losses and changes the value to 1−AUROC. None of these rank identities controls calibration, prevalence-specific precision or intervention effects.

## Worked example: setup

Use positive scores (2,3) and negative scores (1,2).

## Worked example: calculation

The four pair contributions are 1,1/2,1,1, summing to 7/2. Thus AUROC=7/8=0.875. Applying exp to every score leaves 7/8; negating every score gives 1/8.

## Worked example: conclusion

The requested statistic can be certified by exact finite counting rather than a numerical ROC grid.
