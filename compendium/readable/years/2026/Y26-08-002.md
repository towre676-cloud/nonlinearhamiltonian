## Revised question retained in the annual source

What can bounded-degree nullities actually prove about a singular pencil?

## Status

## Summary

The retrieved descriptor campaign reports left- and right-index-one controls through degree five. A complete exact toy pencil explains the nullity sequence and its finite-difference interpretation, while keeping bounded searches distinct from an unqualified completeness claim.

## Definitions

For a polynomial matrix F(λ), letν_d be the dimension over k of polynomial vectors v(λ) of degree at most d satisfyingFv=0; setν_{−1}=0. A minimal polynomial basis has right minimal indices ε₁,…,ε_s.

## Result name

Nullity growth certificate

## Result statement

For a minimal polynomial basis, ν_d=Σ_i max(0,d−ε_i+1). Henceν_d−ν_{d−1} counts indices at most d, and its next difference counts indices exactly d. For F(λ)=[−λ,1], the only right minimal index is1 and there is no left polynomial nullspace.

## Proof

The displayed pencil equation is−λv₁+v₂=0, so every polynomial solution is h(λ)(1,λ). Its degree isdeg h+1 whenever h≠0, givingν₀=0 andν_d=d for d≥1. The generator has no common polynomial factor and cannot be replaced by a degree-zero nonzero vector, proving minimal index one directly. A left polynomial vector is a scalar h withh[−λ,1]=0, forcingh=0. In general a minimal basis has the predictable-degree property: polynomial combinations have degree governed by the largest coefficient-degree plus corresponding minimal index. Counting allowed polynomial coefficients gives the nullity formula. The direct calculation above needs no canonical-form algorithm. For a black-box finite-degree search, stabilization only proves absence of further indices when a suitable dimension or degree bound has also been established; an arbitrary stopping rule is not such a bound.
