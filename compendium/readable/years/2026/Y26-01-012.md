## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which exact formula connects the curvature of an optimization trajectory to its objective’s Hessian?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

A gradient trajectory turns when the Hessian sends the gradient partly away from its own span. The resulting formula is invariant under time reparametrization of the same regular curve and yields explicit estimates, without importing a fluid regularity theorem into learning.

## Definitions

Let V:R^d→R be C² and x′=−g with g=∇V(x), H=∇²V(x). Work where g≠0 so the trajectory is regular.

Let P_{g⊥}=I−ggᵀ/|g|². The curvature of a regular parametrized curve is κ=|P_{x′⊥}x″|/|x′|².

Arc length s satisfies ds/dt=|g|, while V′=−|g|².

## Result name

Exact curvature and an objective decay estimate

## Result statement

κ=|P_{g⊥}Hg|/|g|². In particular κ≤||H||/|g|. If V is μ-strongly convex, its gap along the flow satisfies V(x(t))−V_*≤e^{−2μt}(V(x(0))−V_*).

## Proof

Differentiate x′=−g to get x″=−Hx′=Hg. The normal projection is unchanged when replacing x′ by −g, giving the formula. Tangential Hessian action changes speed; only its perpendicular component bends the curve. For decay, strong convexity implies V_*≥V(x)+g·(x_*−x)+(μ/2)|x_*−x|². Minimize the right-side quadratic over its displacement to obtain V−V_*≤|g|²/(2μ). Thus the gap derivative −|g|² is at most −2μ times the gap; integration proves the exponential bound. The curvature estimate can blow up near a critical point even though objective decay is well controlled, demonstrating that these statements concern different features.

## Worked example: setup

Take V(x,y)=(x²+2y²)/2 and initial point (1,1).

## Worked example: calculation

The solution is (e^{−t},e^{−2t}); at t=0, g=(1,2), Hg=(1,4), |g|²=5 and det(g,Hg)=2. Hence |P_{g⊥}Hg|=2/√5 and κ=2/(5√5). The objective is .5e^{−2t}+e^{−4t}≤1.5e^{−2t}, agreeing with μ=1.

## Worked example: conclusion

The normal Hessian action gives a precise measurable turning statistic alongside a separate energy inequality. If H is a scalar multiple of the identity, this normal action vanishes and every regular trajectory segment is straight.

## Limits

No curvature identity alone proves generalization, relevance, a learning phase transition or a Navier–Stokes correspondence. At a stationary point the curve formula needs a limiting analysis rather than division by zero.

## Extension

For preconditioned flow x′=−G(x)⁻¹∇V, derive covariant acceleration in the metric G and distinguish coordinate curvature from intrinsic curvature. Formulate a controller with an explicit Lyapunov inequality before asserting a stability improvement.
