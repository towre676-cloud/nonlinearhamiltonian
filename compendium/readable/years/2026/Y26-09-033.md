## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which kernel and weight make the coordinate identity exact, and what summability is sufficient for its determinant regularization?

## Summary

The spectral program supplied a Green-coordinate factorization and a Hilbert–Schmidt determinant. A complete Dirichlet model exhibits both mechanisms without importing the claimed RH-dependent weighted estimates. The distinction between trace class and Hilbert–Schmidt class remains essential.

## Definitions

For the inverse Dirichlet Laplacian on(0,1), let G(u,v)=min(u,v)(1−max(u,v)), g(u)=u(1−u), and τ(u)=log[u/(1−u)].

For eigenvalues λ_n of a positive Hilbert–Schmidt operator, define det₂(I+T)=∏_n(1+λ_n)e^(−λ_n) when this product converges.

## Result name

A Green-coordinate identity gives an exact Hilbert–Schmidt model and explains det₂

## Result statement

G(u,v)²=g(u)g(v)e^(−|τ(u)−τ(v)|). The operator with kernel G has trace1/6 and Hilbert–Schmidt squared norm1/90. The regularized product converges wheneverΣλ_n²<∞, even ifΣλ_n diverges.

## Proof

For u≤v, multiplying g(u)g(v) by e^(τ(u)−τ(v)) cancels to u²(1−v)²=G²; symmetry handles the other half. The diagonal integral is∫u(1−u)du=1/6. The squared-kernel integral is2∫₀¹(1−v)²∫₀ᵛu²du dv=1/90. Equivalently the eigenvalues are1/(π²n²), from the sine eigenfunctions. For det₂, log(1+x)−x=O(x²) as x→0; a finite number of exceptional factors is harmless and the square-summable tail makes the logarithmic series absolutely convergent. This proves the regularization mechanism in this positive spectral setting.

## Worked example: calculation

It is Hilbert–Schmidt but not trace class. The ordinary partial determinant is∏_(n=1)^N(1+1/n)=N+1 and diverges. Its regularized version is(N+1)e^(−H_N), tending to e^(−γ), where γ=lim(H_N−logN). Thus det₂ removes a precisely identified divergent linear term, not an arbitrary numerical offset. For the Green kernel, an additional bounded nonnegative weight q gives a positive weighted operator; its actual trace and HS bounds depend on that weight. A bounded weight q has squared-kernel norm at most its supremum squared times1/90, directly from the positive integrand.

## Limits
