## Definitions

A complex matrixY is accretive whenRe(x*Yx)≥0 for allx, equivalently(Y+Y*)/2≥0. For positive definiteR define||x||_{R^{-1}}²=x*R^{-1}x.

## Result statement

IfY is accretive andr>0, then||(I+rY)^{-1}||₂≤1. For positive definiteR, the same claim holds forI+RY in theR^{-1} norm. Its Euclidean norm is at most√κ₂(R), rather than automatically one.

## Proof

For anyx, Re[x*(I+rY)x]≥||x||². Cauchy–Schwarz gives||(I+rY)x||||x||≥||x||², so the smallest singular value is at least1 and the matrix is invertible. For matrixR, conjugateI+RY byR^{-1/2}: R^{-1/2}(I+RY)R^{1/2}=I+R^{1/2}YR^{1/2}. The transformed admittance is accretive because its quadratic form evaluatesY onR^{1/2}x. Applying the scalar-r=1 result proves contraction after this coordinate change, exactly theR^{-1} norm. Returning to Euclidean coordinates introduces||R^{1/2}||||R^{-1/2}||=√κ₂(R). This proof states a sufficient condition; arbitrary complexY with a resonance need not satisfy it. If the stronger bound(Y+Y*)/2≥mI holds withm>0, then the scalar-resistance argument gives||(I+rY)^{-1}||≤1/(1+rm). This follows by replacing the real-part lower bound||x||² with(1+rm)||x||² before applying Cauchy–Schwarz. It quantifies a strict contraction margin and shows precisely which positive Hermitian-part bound would be needed to improve the passive estimate.
