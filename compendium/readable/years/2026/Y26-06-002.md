## Revised question retained in the annual source

Reconstruction: Which bilinear form can a quadratic refinement polarize, and what must be corrected in a symplectic-looking source definition?

## Status

## Summary

The source writes q(x+y)−q(x)−q(y)=ω(x, y) for a finite phase module. The left side is symmetric in x and y. At odd order it therefore cannot be a nonzero alternating form. This elementary obstruction is foundational for choosing consistent Weil and Heisenberg data.

## Definitions

Let A be an abelian group and Q:A→Q/Z with Q(0)=0. Define b_Q(x, y)=Q(x+y)−Q(x)−Q(y). A quadratic form additionally has bilinear b_Q and Q(nx)=n²Q(x).

An alternating form ω has ω(x, x)=0 and ω(y, x)=−ω(x, y). The Heisenberg commutator form and a quadratic module’s symmetric bilinear form play different roles.

## Result name

Quadratic polarization is symmetric and cannot equal an alternating form at odd order

## Result statement

b_Q is always symmetric. If b_Q is also alternating and A has odd order, then b_Q=0. For A=Z/pZ with odd prime p, Q_a(x)=ax²/p mod1 has b_Q(x, y)=2axy/p and is nondegenerate exactly when p∤a.

## Proof

Symmetry follows immediately from commutativity of x+y. If a form is symmetric and skew-symmetric, then2b(x, y)=0. For finite odd-order A, each b(x, y) has odd order dividing the order of x, so it cannot have order two unless it is zero. This proves the obstruction. The cyclic example is well defined because replacing x by x+p changes ax²/p by2ax+ap, an integer. Expansion gives the displayed polarization. If p∤a and b_Q(x, y)=0 for every y, choose y=1; then p divides2ax, and oddness implies x=0. If p|a the form is zero. The same calculation shows why Q(x)=x²/(2L) must be assigned a correct underlying cyclic group and parity condition rather than copied to any Z/LZ: changing x by L contributes x+L/2, which is integral only when L is even.

Use A=Z/3Z and Q(x)=x²/3.
