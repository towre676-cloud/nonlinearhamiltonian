## Revised question retained in the annual source

What proves that a reduced state model preserves every output and that no smaller exact recurrence exists?

## Status

Classical mathematics or a newly chosen finite model developed with proof; historical claims delimited

## Summary

The source reports a six-to-three-state reduction with a nonsingular Hankel witness. Since its matrices are absent, the following explicit six-state model is newly chosen. It illustrates both halves of a complete certificate: an all-time intertwiner for correctness and a Hankel minor for minimality.

## Definitions

For a sequence y_n=cAⁿb over Q, its Hankel matrix is H_ij=y_(i+j), i,j≥0.

A forward recurrence of order r means y_(n+r)=Σ_(j=0)^(r−1)a_j y_(n+j) for every n≥0, with constant coefficients.

## Result name

Intertwining plus rank gives exact minimality

## Result statement

If RA=GR, Rb=β and c=dR, then y_n=dGⁿβ for every n≥0. Any r-state realization or order-r recurrence has Hankel rank at most r. Thus a nonzero r×r Hankel minor together with an r-state realization proves minimal dimension and recurrence order exactly r.

## Proof

From RA=GR, induction gives RAⁿ=GⁿR; the input and output identities then yield the full sequence equality, not merely agreement on a sampled prefix. For an s-state realization, H_ij=(cAⁱ)(Aʲb), so every finite Hankel block factors through an s-dimensional space and has rank at most s. An order-s recurrence similarly expresses every later Hankel column as a fixed combination of the preceding s columns. Hence either model needs at least the size of a nonzero minor. Conversely, an r-dimensional realization supplies an order at most r through its characteristic polynomial by Cayley–Hamilton. This suffices for the certificate used here; general minimal realization can be constructed from the shift action on the finite-dimensional span of Hankel columns.

## Worked example: setup

Take A=diag(1,2,3,4,5,6), b=(1,1,1,0,0,0)ᵀ and c=bᵀ.

## Worked example: calculation

Projection R onto the first three coordinates gives G=diag(1,2,3), β=(1,1,1)ᵀ and d=(1,1,1). Thus y_n=1+2ⁿ+3ⁿ, starting 3,6,14,36,98. Its leading 3×3 Hankel determinant is four, the squared Vandermonde determinant on 1,2,3. The recurrence is y_(n+3)=6y_(n+2)−11y_(n+1)+6y_n, from (t−1)(t−2)(t−3). Therefore three is exact.

## Worked example: conclusion

A global matrix identity and a finite nonzero determinant jointly certify the reduction and its optimality.

## Limits

No finite prefix alone rules out a later perturbation unless a model class or global recurrence is already proved. Approximate low rank and a small singular value do not establish exact state equivalence.

## Extension

For a supplied rational automaton, compute reachable and observable quotients over Q, emit the intertwining matrices and a nonsingular Hankel minor, and connect the certificate to every original input and output channel.
