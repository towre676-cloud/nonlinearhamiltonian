## Revised question retained in the annual source

What is the minimum guaranteed measurement cost for determining a target on a finite uncertainty set?

## Status

Classical mathematics or a newly chosen finite model developed with proof; historical claims delimited

## Summary

The recovered decision recurrence becomes rigorous once terminal conditions, deterministic outcomes and admissible progress are specified. Its proof is an exact decision-tree induction. This gives a constructive optimum rather than an assertion that one greedy panel is best.

## Definitions

Let S be a finite set of possible states and g:S→D the target decision. An experiment e has positive cost c_e and partitions S into outcome fibres S_(e,y).

A terminal set is one on which g is constant. Ignore experiments whose only nonempty outcome is S itself; they cannot improve a worst-case exact decision.

## Result name

Bellman minimax recursion

## Result statement

If the available tests separate every pair with different target values, V(S)=0 on terminal sets and otherwise V(S)=min_e[c_e+max_(y:S_(e,y)≠∅)V(S_(e,y))], restricting to experiments with all nonempty fibres proper subsets. This equals the minimum worst-case cost of an exact adaptive decision tree.

## Proof

A decision tree must choose some experiment e first. An adversarial outcome can choose the most expensive remaining fibre, so its worst-case cost is at least c_e+max_y V(S_(e,y)). Minimizing over the first choice gives a lower bound on every tree. Conversely, choose an experiment attaining the finite minimum and attach optimal subtrees on its proper fibres. Induction on |S| constructs a tree achieving exactly that cost. Pairwise separability ensures a nonterminal set has a useful splitting test and a finite exact tree; positive costs make a non-splitting first experiment wasteful. This proves both existence and optimality. For noisy observations the fibres would be replaced by feasible posterior sets and terminal robustness would need a separate definition.

## Worked example: setup

Identify one of three states a,b,c.

## Worked example: calculation

Experiment e₁ costs one and partitions {a} from {b,c}; e₂ costs two and partitions {b} from {a,c}; a direct three-way experiment costs four. Performing e₁ first and, only if necessary, e₂ has worst-case cost three. Every one-test cheaper choice leaves a two-state ambiguity requiring the other test, so the Bellman optimum is three. The early a outcome costs only one, showing the adaptive branch structure.

## Worked example: conclusion

The decision policy and its lower bound arise from the same exact recurrence.

## Limits

A worst-case optimum need not minimize expected cost under a prior. Historical panel speedups or prediction accuracies cannot be inferred from this finite theorem without the original states, tests and evaluation data.

## Extension

Enrich each outcome fibre with a target-separation certificate and an admissible lower bound on its remaining cost; branch-and-bound can then find an optimum while retaining a verifiable proof tree.
