## Revised question retained in the annual source

Can the recovered weight-two cancellation and logarithmic constant be derived exactly from the specified kernel?

## Status

## Summary

The kernel formula is sufficiently explicit for a full proof. Its cancellation comes from a divisor sum, not from a numerical integration or an assumed E₈ interpretation. Differentiating the Mellin transform also derives the stated logarithmic companion.

## Definitions

Let Φ₃₀(x)=x⁸+x⁷−x⁵−x⁴−x³+x+1 and K(x)=240x²⁹/(1−x³⁰)−Φ₃₀′(x)/Φ₃₀(x),0<x<1.

Let c₃₀(n)=Σ_(d|gcd(30,n))dμ(30/d). For Re(s)>1 set D(s)=Σ_(n≥1)[c₃₀(n)+240·1_(30|n)]/nˢ.

## Result name

Exact zero and logarithmic Mellin derivative

## Result statement

∫₀¹(−log x)K(x)dx=0 and ∫₀¹(−log x)log(−log x)K(x)dx=−π²[16log2+12log3+10log5]/180.

## Proof

From the product of Φ₃₀ over its primitive roots, logarithmic differentiation gives −Φ′/Φ=Σ_(n≥1)c₃₀(n)x^(n−1) for |x|<1. The other kernel term contributes240 precisely when30 divides n. The coefficients are bounded, so integrating against (−log x)^(s−1) is justified absolutely for Re(s)>1 and gives Γ(s)D(s). Summing the divisor formula yields D(s)=ζ(s)F(s), where F(s)=240·30^(−s)+∏_(p∈{2,3,5})(p^(1−s)−1). At s=2, the product is−4/15 and240/900=4/15, hence F(2)=D(2)=0. Differentiating under the integral is legitimate near s=2 by the same absolute bound with an additional |log(−log x)| factor. The Γ′ term vanishes because D(2)=0. Convergence can also be seen directly: Φ₃₀(1)=1, so the logarithmic derivative stays bounded near one while the first kernel term is asymptotic to8/(1−x). Multiplication by−log x removes this pole, and the extra logarithm leaves an integrable log(1−x) singularity. Near zero the kernel is bounded, so its logarithmic moments are integrable there as well. Logarithmic differentiation of the finite product gives F′(2)=−(4/15)[log30+log2+½log3+¼log5]. Multiplying by ζ(2)=π²/6 produces the displayed companion.

## Worked example: calculation

The eight divisors of30 give Σ_(d|30)μ(30/d)/d=−1+1/2+1/3+1/5−1/6−1/10−1/15+1/30=−4/15. The exact added coefficient240/30² is4/15. Their sum vanishes before any transcendental evaluation. ## Limits

The name E₈ for this scalar kernel requires a separate Coxeter/lattice identification. This proof does not certify every reported cancellation family or infer a physical spectrum from a Mellin zero.

## Extension

For a prescribed conductor N and weight s₀>1, choose the compensating coefficient C=−N^(s₀)Σ_(d|N)μ(N/d)d^(1−s₀); then derive the exact zero and its higher logarithmic derivatives from the finite divisor polynomial.
