## Summary

The retrieved OGL statistics describe finite dimensions and a later weakening of a proposed Haar lemma. They do not supply all observation channels. We formulate a newly stipulated linear observation model whose recovery and blindness statements are exact, and leave the historical composite statistics empirical.

## Definitions

Let A:R^n→R^m be linear, with observed data y=Ax+e and ||e||≤ε. For a subspace V of admissible displacements define σ_V=inf{||Av||:v∈V,||v||=1}.

Recovery is considered modulo an identified symmetry subspace K⊂kerA; choose V=K⊥. A positive σ_V is a restricted coercivity constant.

## Result name

Observation geometry has exact invisible subspaces and a noise-to-state bound

## Result statement

Two admissible states x,z with x−z∈V and both residuals at most ε satisfy ||x−z||≤2ε/σ_V when σ_V>0. Exact recovery modulo K holds if and only if kerA=K. If σ_V=0 in finite dimension, a nonzero invisible displacement remains.

## Proof

The triangle inequality gives ||A(x−z)||≤||Ax−y||+||Az−y||≤2ε. The definition of σ_V then proves the bound. In finite dimension the unit sphere in V is compact, so the infimum is attained. It is positive exactly when A has no nonzero kernel vector in V. Because K⊂kerA and every vector decomposes orthogonally into K and V, this is equivalent to kerA=K. If there is a unit v in kerA∩V, then every x+tv has the same data for all real t, so no finite uniform state-error bound can hold. Thus a valley in a numerical score is not itself a recovery theorem: the crucial mathematical object is the least restricted singular value, with its declared admissible subspace and normalization.

## Worked example: setup

Take A(x₁,x₂,x₃)=(x₁,x₂) and K=span(e₃).

## Worked example: calculation

On V=span(e₁,e₂), σ_V=1, so the identified coordinates differ by at most 2ε. Full-state recovery is impossible because x₃ is invisible. Adding a third channel δx₃ with δ>0 yields singular values 1,1,δ and full-state error at most 2ε/min(1,δ). At δ=1/100, noise can be amplified by a factor one hundred in the weakest direction.

## Worked example: conclusion

The model gives a constructive geometry of recovery, symmetry blindness and conditioning.
