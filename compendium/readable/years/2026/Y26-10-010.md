## Revised question retained in the annual source

When does eliminating T from T²=D and A(x)+TB(x)=0 preserve the desired solution branch?

## Status

Classical mathematics or a newly chosen finite model developed with proof; historical claims delimited

## Summary

The source describes norm elimination, reconstruction and a B=0 branch. The exact theorem clarifies why an eliminated polynomial gives necessary candidates rather than an automatic bijection. It also explains how a large resultant can contain both conjugate sheets and repeated exceptional factors.

## Definitions

Let K be a characteristic-zero field, D∈K, and A,B∈K[x]. Work first with the algebra K[T]/(T²−D).

The norm polynomial is N(x)=A(x)²−DB(x)², the determinant of multiplication by A+TB on the basis 1,T.

## Result name

Elimination and reconstruction with all branches

## Result statement

Every solution of T²=D and A+TB=0 satisfies N=0. If B(x)≠0, the converse holds with the unique reconstruction T=−A(x)/B(x). If B(x)=0, a solution exists exactly when A(x)=0 and T²=D; this branch must be handled separately. A prescribed real sign of T requires an additional sign test.

## Proof

The conjugate product (A+TB)(A−TB)=A²−DB² proves necessity. For B≠0, N=0 gives (−A/B)²=D, and substitution gives A+(−A/B)B=0, proving the converse. For B=0 the original relation reduces to A=0, which cannot be inferred by dividing by B. If one starts with the fixed real constant T=√D rather than a free algebraic variable, N=0 combines both signs of √D. Reconstruction decides which sign a candidate belongs to. Over a quotient by another polynomial p, gcd(p,B) separates precisely the potentially singular components when p is squarefree. If p has nilpotents, pointwise branches alone do not capture the quotient algebra.

## Worked example: setup

Use D=2, A=(x−1)(x−2), B=x−1.

## Worked example: calculation

The eliminant is (x−1)²[(x−2)²−2]. For T=√2, the original equation is (x−1)(x−2+√2)=0, so its roots are x=1 and x=2−√2. The eliminant also contains x=2+√2, which reconstructs T=−√2 and must be rejected on the positive sheet. At x=1, A=B=0, so both signs of T satisfy the equation although the rational reconstruction is undefined.

## Worked example: conclusion

The example exhibits both a genuine exceptional branch and a conjugate extraneous candidate.

## Limits

The recovered 26-term and 100-term counts cannot be replayed without the actual polynomials. Squaring or taking a norm can enlarge a prescribed real solution set, and integer reconstruction further requires exact divisibility and integrality tests.

## Extension

Attach to every eliminant root an exact algebraic sign or isolating interval for −A/B; split gcd components first and record reconstruction identities in the quotient ring, producing a reversible elimination certificate.
