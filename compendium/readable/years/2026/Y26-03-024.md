## Revised question retained in the annual source

Can the source’s scalar reduction be solved exactly for a comparison family?

## Status

## Summary

The recovered exponential siblings offer a case in which fixed-point and multiplier conditions determine the parameters without numerical elimination. This gives a useful calibration for claims about the more complicated logarithmic-cosine kernel.

## Definitions

Let c,r>0 and Kα(x)=exp(−αcx^r), with x>0 and α>0. Work in a neighborhood where the real power x^r is smooth.

A flip candidate satisfies Kα(x)=x and Kα′(x)=−1. Assume c and r are nonzero algebraic numbers only for the arithmetic conclusion.

## Result name

Explicit fixed-point multiplier-minus-one solution

## Result statement

There is exactly one positive pair solving these two equations: x*=e^(−1/r), α*=e/(cr). If c,r are positive algebraic numbers, both x* and α* are transcendental.

## Proof

The fixed-point equation gives logx=−αcx^r, so x<1. The derivative at a fixed point is −αcrx^r. Setting it equal to −1 gives αcx^r=1/r. Substitute into the logarithmic equation to get logx=−1/r, determining x uniquely. Then x^r=e⁻¹ yields α=e/(cr), which is positive and satisfies both equations directly. If r is algebraic and nonzero, −1/r is a nonzero algebraic exponent, so e^(−1/r) is transcendental by Hermite–Lindemann. A nonzero algebraic multiple of e is transcendental, proving the statement for α. This arithmetic proof uses independently algebraic coefficients, exactly the hypothesis missing in an endogenous-coefficient argument.

## Worked example: setup

Take c=r=1.

## Worked example: calculation

x*=1/e≈0.36787944 and α*=e≈2.71828183. The fixed equation is exp(−e/e)=1/e. The derivative is −e exp(−1)=−1.

## Worked example: conclusion

The comparison family has a complete exact candidate theorem and a valid transcendence proof.

## Limits

A multiplier-minus-one pair alone is not a full local period-doubling theorem; that needs the usual parameter transversality and nonlinear coefficient.

## Extension

Compute those two flip coefficients symbolically for general r, and determine the parameter range for which a stable two-cycle is born.
