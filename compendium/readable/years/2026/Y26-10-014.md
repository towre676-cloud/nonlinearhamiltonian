## Revised question retained in the annual source

What exact hypotheses make a finite-jet linearization unique, and why can early jets fail to identify later coefficients?

## Status

## Summary

The dated synthesis lists jets and Schröder equations, while another passage warns that a seventh-order coefficient leaves six lower orders unchanged. A formal coefficient argument explains both phenomena. The chosen example is explicitly solvable and does not assume convergence of a general formal conjugacy.

## Definitions

Let f(x)=λx+Σ_(n≥2)f_nxⁿ over a characteristic-zero field, λ≠0. Seek h(x)=x+Σ_(n≥2)h_nxⁿ with h(f(x))=λh(x).

The N-jet is a power series modulo x^(N+1). A resonance at degree n means λⁿ=λ.

## Result name

Nonresonant jet recursion

## Result statement

If λⁿ≠λ for 2≤n≤N, the normalized Schröder equation has exactly one N-jet solution. Each h_n depends only on f₂,…,f_n; changing f_(N+1) cannot alter the solution through degree N.

## Proof

Assume h₂,…,h_(n−1) have been determined. In degree n, the contribution of h_nf(x)ⁿ is λⁿh_n; the right side contributes λh_n. Every remaining term uses only lower coefficients of h and coefficients of f through degree n. Thus (λⁿ−λ)h_n is a known scalar, uniquely determining h_n in the nonresonant case. Induction proves existence and uniqueness to every requested finite order and the triangular dependency. At a resonance the known scalar must vanish for solvability; if it vanishes, h_n is not determined at that order. This proves a precise obstruction rather than a general linearization promise. In particular, two maps differing first at degree seven have identical six-jets, so no procedure using only those six coefficients can distinguish them.

## Worked example: setup

Take f(x)=2x+x².

## Worked example: calculation

Here 1+f(x)=(1+x)², so h(x)=log(1+x) gives h(f(x))=2h(x) as a formal identity. Its first coefficients are x−x²/2+x³/3−x⁴/4. Direct degree-two comparison gives 1+4h₂=2h₂, hence h₂=−1/2; degree three gives 4h₂+8h₃=2h₃, hence h₃=1/3. For contrast, λ=−1 resonates at every odd degree greater than one.

The example links an exact conjugacy to the finite triangular calculation and makes the identifiability limit explicit.
