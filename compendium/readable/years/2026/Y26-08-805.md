## Revised question retained in the annual source

What separator invariant makes frontier optimization exact, and when does a repeated-layer recurrence follow?

## Status

## Summary

The source combines fixed-height frontier DP, route occupancy and repeated min-plus transfer. A frontier is mathematically sufficient only when all interaction between processed and unprocessed choices passes through its retained state. A small transfer matrix proves an additive period law exactly.

## Definitions

Consider a finite constraint-and-cost problem with variables processed in a fixed order, each with at most D values. At a cut retain all assigned variables that occur in a factor involving an unassigned variable, together with any extra resource/connectivity state used by future factors.

Assume the retained boundary has at most κ variables and all forgotten factors have already been checked and charged exactly once. For a repeated layer let M_ij be the least additional cost of moving from boundary statei to statej, or+∞ if impossible.

## Result name

Exact separator recursion and repeated-layer transfer

## Result statement

For each frontier signature, keeping the minimum accumulated cost preserves the global optimum. There are at most D^κ assignments before auxiliary states. Repeated identical layers satisfy v_(L+1)=v_L⊗M, where (v⊗M)_j=min_i(v_i+M_ij); hence v_L=v₀⊗M^⊗L.

## Proof

Any future factor sees the processed region only through the retained variables and resources. Two partial assignments with the same signature therefore admit exactly the same continuations and add the same future costs. Keeping their cheaper prefix is valid by direct substitution, and induction over the processing order proves the recursion. The D^κ bound counts boundary assignments, not extra partitions or capacities. For repeated layers, concatenate an optimal prefix to each one-layer transition and minimize the intermediate boundary state; this is precisely min-plus multiplication. Associativity follows because both bracketings minimize the same sum over all intermediate indices. An eventual period law is additional structure, not a consequence of observing several values.

## Worked example: setup

Use two boundary states and M=[[+∞,2],[3,+∞]], with v₀=(0,+∞).

## Worked example: calculation

M^⊗2=[[5,+∞],[+∞,5]], so M^⊗(L+2)=5+M^⊗L for every L≥0. The minimum terminal cost sequence is d₀=0,d₁=2,d₂=5,d₃=7,d₄=10, and d_(L+2)=d_L+5 exactly. This law follows from an exact matrix identity rather than fitting a short sequence.
