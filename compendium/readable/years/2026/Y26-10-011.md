## Revised question retained in the annual source

How can a denominator be inverted exactly on the valid components of a polynomial quotient without losing its exceptional components?

## Status

Classical mathematics or a newly chosen finite model developed with proof; historical claims delimited

## Summary

The recovered gcd branch splitting has a clean Chinese-remainder proof. It turns an informal instruction to avoid zero denominators into an exact decomposition of a finite algebra. The squarefree hypothesis is the structural reason the branches separate.

## Definitions

Let K be a field, p∈K[t] squarefree, and a∈K[t]. Set g=gcd(p,a), h=p/g, with monic choices.

Write R=K[t]/(p). A component is regular for a when multiplication by a is invertible on it.

## Result name

Exact regular/exceptional decomposition

## Result statement

g and h are coprime, and R≅K[t]/(g)×K[t]/(h). The image of a is zero in the first factor and invertible in the second; a Bézout identity supplies its inverse there.

## Proof

Because p is squarefree, no irreducible factor dividing g can also divide h. The Chinese remainder map therefore has inverse constructed from Bézout polynomials ug+vh=1, proving the algebra isomorphism. Every factor of g divides a, so a is zero modulo g. Conversely, any common divisor of a and h would divide p and a, hence g, contradicting coprimality of g and h. Thus gcd(a,h)=1, and extended Euclid gives ra+sh=1; r is an exact inverse of a modulo h. No root approximations or heuristic branch labels enter. When g=1 there is no exceptional component; when h=1 every component is exceptional. These cases are naturally interpreted using the zero quotient factor.

## Worked example: setup

Take K=Q, p=(t−1)(t+1)(t−2) and a=t−1.

## Worked example: calculation

Here g=t−1 and h=t²−t−2. In K[t]/(h), (t−1)(t/2)=(t²−t)/2=1, so t/2 is the regular inverse. In the factor t=1, a is zero. Hence an expression b/a can be evaluated using bt/2 only on the h component; the t=1 component still requires its original undivided equation. For contrast, if p=(t−1)², then g=t−1 and h=t−1 are not coprime: the quotient contains a nonzero nilpotent and cannot be replaced by a product of two point factors.

## Worked example: conclusion

The decomposition makes every denominator decision and exceptional branch auditable.

## Limits

This is an algebra decomposition, not a guarantee that all real roots are physically or arithmetically admissible. Repeated factors require primary or local-algebra methods, and real sign conditions remain additional data.

## Extension

Combine this split with Sturm isolating intervals and norm reconstruction, so each real component carries both an exact inverse certificate and a proof of its sign and multiplicity.
