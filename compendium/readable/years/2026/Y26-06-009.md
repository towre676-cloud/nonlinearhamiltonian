## Status

## Summary

The knot source identifies a cubic with discriminant−23 and states that an abelian cyclotomic degeneration cannot generate it. That assertion has a precise Galois-theoretic proof. It does not establish a full state-integral or knot-triangulation comparison.

## Definitions

Let f(u)=u³−u+1. A cubic over Q is irreducible if it has no rational root. For u³+pu+q its discriminant is−4p³−27q².

A cyclotomic field Q(ζ_m) is finite Galois with abelian Galois group. Every intermediate field of an abelian Galois extension is itself Galois over Q.

## Result name

The discriminant-minus-twenty-three cubic has S₃ closure and cannot lie in an abelian cyclotomic field

## Result statement

f is irreducible with discriminant−23, its splitting field has Galois group S₃, and no root belongs to any cyclotomic field.

## Proof

The rational-root theorem restricts a rational root to±1, and both give f=1, so f is irreducible. The discriminant formula gives4−27=−23. The Galois group of an irreducible cubic is a transitive subgroup of S₃, hence either A₃ or S₃. The product of the three root differences changes by the sign character; its square is the discriminant. Consequently the group lies in A₃ exactly when the discriminant is a rational square. Here it is not, so the group is S₃. The single-root field has degree three and is not normal: its normal closure has degree six. If a root lay in a cyclotomic field, its degree-three subfield would be an intermediate field of an abelian Galois extension, hence normal, a contradiction. Equivalently every subfield corresponds to a normal subgroup because the ambient Galois group is abelian. This rules out even an unramified choice of a much larger cyclotomic conductor; it is a structural obstruction rather than a small-conductor search failure.

## Worked example: conclusion

The source’s abelian-versus-S₃ barrier is a proved arithmetic theorem.

## Limits

The specific knot interpretation, its volume and the proposed Potts bridge require independent gluing and branch data. A Galois obstruction alone does not prove that a particular analytic degeneration is impossible after arbitrary enlargement beyond abelian fields.
