## Revised question retained in the annual source

How can linear Diophantine lifting and prime-exponent constraints yield a complete, exactly countable population?

## Status

Classical mathematics or a newly chosen finite model developed with proof; historical claims delimited

## Summary

The retrieved examples illustrate two forms of integer lifting: an affine line of solutions and a finite product of exponent lattices. Both require divisibility conditions, rather than a real-valued inverse followed by rounding. The exponent formulation is also the correct setting for Smith normal form compatibility tests.

## Definitions

A positive integer has a unique vector of nonnegative prime exponents.

A unimodular integer change of variables has determinant ±1 and preserves the integer lattice; Smith normal form diagonalizes an integer linear map using such changes.

## Result name

Complete affine and multiplicative solution sets

## Result statement

All integer solutions of 2x+3y=1 are (x,y)=(−1+3t,1−2t), t∈Z. The equation x²y³=2²⁰3¹² has exactly twelve positive integer solutions, indexed by 0≤r≤3 and 0≤s≤2 as x=2^(10−3r)3^(6−3s), y=2^(2r)3^(2s).

## Proof

Reducing 2x+3y=1 modulo three gives x≡−1 mod 3, so x=−1+3t; substitution forces y=1−2t. Every t works, proving completeness and injectivity. For the multiplicative equation, no prime other than two or three can divide x or y, because all exponents are nonnegative. Write x=2ᵃ3ᶜ and y=2ᵇ3ᵈ. Unique factorization gives 2a+3b=20 and 2c+3d=12. The first equation forces b even, b=2r, and then a=10−3r≥0, so r=0,1,2,3. The second similarly gives d=2s and c=6−3s≥0, so s=0,1,2. These choices are independent and unique, producing 4·3=12 solutions. More generally, if UAV=diag(d₁,…,d_r,0,…) is a Smith decomposition of an exponent map, integer solvability requires d_i to divide (Ub)_i and zero coordinates of Ub to vanish; nonnegativity is an additional constraint after applying V.

## Worked example: calculation

At (r,s)=(0,0), (x,y)=(2¹⁰3⁶,1); at (3,2), (x,y)=(2,2⁶3⁴). The address j=3r+s runs from zero to eleven; division with remainder recovers r=⌊j/3⌋ and s=j mod 3. Thus the count and inverse lookup are both exact.

## Worked example: conclusion

Prime-exponent reduction proves every listed point and excludes every omitted positive point.

## Limits

Negative or zero solutions need separate sign and support cases. Smith invariant factors alone do not enforce nonnegativity, and a claimed perfect-cube compatibility obstruction requires the actual lattice map, not just a numerical determinant.

## Extension

Combine Smith coordinates with polyhedral lattice-point enumeration to count bounded exponent populations and optimize a stated linear cost in the original exponents, preserving a certificate of every divisibility and sign condition.
