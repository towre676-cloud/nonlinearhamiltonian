## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which arithmetic weights preserve kernel positivity, and how can a gcd-kernel multiply a vector without forming every matrix entry?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

A nonnegative divisor expansion makes the gcd matrix a Gram matrix. Combining it with the reciprocal-maximum covariance yields a positive kernel and an exact divisor-sieve matvec; neither positivity nor speed requires a guessed spectral law.

## Definitions

Let w(n)=∑_{d|n}a_d, with real a_d≥0. Equivalently a=w*μ under Dirichlet convolution.

Set G(i,j)=w(gcd(i,j)) and K(i,j)=G(i,j)/max(i,j), for 1≤i,j≤N.

For every d define v_d(i)=1 if d divides i and 0 otherwise.

## Result name

Positive divisor decomposition and exact matvec

## Result statement

G=∑_{d≤N}a_d v_dv_dᵀ is positive semidefinite. K is also positive semidefinite. Its action is (Kx)_i=∑_{d|i}a_d[(1/i)∑_{j≤i,d|j}x_j+∑_{j>i,d|j}x_j/j]. All inner sums can be computed in O(N log N) arithmetic operations.

## Proof

A divisor divides gcd(i,j) exactly when it divides both indices, proving the G decomposition entry by entry. For K, replace each v_d(i) by the L² function v_d(i)1_[0,1/i](t); integrating pairwise products and summing with a_d gives exactly K. This directly proves positivity without invoking a black-box product theorem. Expand the divisor sum and split the inner j sum at i to obtain the matvec. For each d, traverse d,2d,…,⌊N/d⌋d once in each direction, recording prefix sums of x_j and suffix sums of x_j/j. The total number of visits is ∑_{d≤N}⌊N/d⌋≤N(1+log N), proving the operation bound. Actual bit complexity additionally depends on coefficient growth and arithmetic precision.

## Worked example: setup

Choose w(n)=n, so a_d=φ(d), and N=3.

## Worked example: calculation

The gcd matrix is [[1,1,1],[1,2,1],[1,1,3]], and K=[[1,1/2,1/3],[1/2,1,1/3],[1/3,1/3,1]]. On x=(1,2,3), direct multiplication gives (3,7/2,4). The divisor formula gives the same values using a₁=1,a₂=1,a₃=2. The determinant is 23/36>0.

## Limits

Nonnegative values of w alone do not imply nonnegative divisor coefficients or positive semidefiniteness. The bounded infinite constant-weight operator theorem does not automatically extend to growing w such as w(n)=n.

## Extension

Characterize admissible weights through a_d, establish weighted ℓ² Schur bounds, and quantify approximation error when the divisor expansion is truncated at d≤D. Compare predictive performance only under a specified train/test protocol.
