## Revised question retained in the annual source

How can the same rational series diverge in the real metric yet converge p-adically, and how is a p-adic root built exactly?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The July31 representation discussion includes rationals, reals and p-adics. A valuation makes this distinction precise: it changes which approximations are small, while preserving ordinary algebraic equalities on the embedded rational field. Root lifting provides a constructive rather than purely metaphorical example.

## Definitions

For a primep and nonzero rationalx, letv_p(x) be the exponent ofp in its factorization and|x|_p=p^{-v_p(x)}; set|0|_p=0. Q_p is the completion ofQ in this metric. Congruence modulo p^n records an error ofp-adic norm at mostp^{-n}.

## Result name

Geometric convergence and simple-root lifting

## Result statement

The seriesΣ_{j≥0}p^j converges inQ_p to1/(1−p). If an integer polynomialf hasf(r₁)≡0 modp andf′(r₁)≠0 modp, its root lifts uniquely through compatible congruencesr_n modulo p^n, producing a root inZ_p.

## Proof

The finite geometric identity(1−p)Σ_{j=0}^{N−1}p^j=1−p^N is valid overQ. Since|p^N|_p=p^{-N}→0, completeness gives the stated limit; the real terms instead grow and their partial sums diverge. For lifting supposef(r_n)≡0 modp^n andtryr_{n+1}=r_n+t p^n with0≤t<p. Taylor expansion modulo p^{n+1} givesf(r_{n+1})≡f(r_n)+t p^n f′(r_n). Dividing byp^n leaves one linear equation fort modulop. The derivative is a unit modulo p, so that equation has exactly one solution. The compatible sequence is Cauchy and continuity of polynomial evaluation gives a root. Uniqueness applies to the root in the chosen residue class, not every root ofthe polynomial.

## Worked example: setup

Takep=5 andf(x)=x²−6, starting withr₁=1.

## Worked example: calculation

The successive least residues are r₂=16 modulo25, r₃=16 modulo125 andr₄=516 modulo625. Their squares are6 modulo the respective moduli, and each new residue agrees with the previous one. The other root begins in the residue class−1, so the construction does not claim global uniqueness.
