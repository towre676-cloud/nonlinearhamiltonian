## Summary

The source gives a two-sector failure product and fitted threshold candidates. We replace an implicit independence assumption with an exact joint-probability formula, then add a finite confidence calculation. Threshold fitting is kept distinct from a theorem about arbitrarily large devices.

## Definitions

Let X and Z be failure events with probabilities p_X, p_Z and intersection r. Their combined failure is the union. Feasibility requires max(0, p_X+p_Z−1)≤r≤min(p_X, p_Z).

If n independent identically distributed trials each have failure probability p, the probability of zero observed failures is(1−p)^n. Independence of trials is separate from independence of sectors within one trial.

## Result name

Two-sector failure has a correlation parameter and a rigorous confidence envelope

## Result statement

P(X∪Z)=p_X+p_Z−r, hence max(p_X, p_Z)≤P(X∪Z)≤min(1, p_X+p_Z). The product formula1−(1−p_X)(1−p_Z) holds exactly when r=p_Xp_Z. After zero failures in n trials, the one-sided level1−δ upper confidence bound is p≤1−δ^{1/n}.

## Proof

The union identity follows by partitioning into the three disjoint regions X\Z, Z\X and X∩Z. The intersection bounds follow from nonnegative probabilities of all four cells; conversely those bounds make the four cell probabilities r, p_X−r, p_Z−r,1−p_X−p_Z+r nonnegative and summing to one, so the bounds are sharp. Sector independence is exactly the additional equality r=p_Xp_Z. For the zero-failure bound, a candidate p above1−δ^{1/n} makes the zero-count event have probability belowδ. Inverting this binomial test gives the stated confidence procedure; it is a coverage statement about repeated sampling, not a posterior probability that the fixed parameter lies in the interval.

## Worked example: setup

Take p_X=p_Z=1/10 and n=100 independent trials with zero observed combined failures.

## Worked example: calculation

Independent sectors give combined failure19/100. Perfectly coincident sectors give1/10, and disjoint sectors give1/5. At δ=1/20 the zero-failure confidence upper bound is1−(1/20)^{1/100}≈0.029513. A point estimate zero is therefore not a proof of zero failure probability.

## Limits

The historical threshold candidates and scaling-fit R² are not independently reproduced. A finite confidence interval does not prove an asymptotic threshold or a uniform QRAM envelope.
