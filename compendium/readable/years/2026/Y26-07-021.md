## Revised question retained in the annual source

How do integer normal forms and the boundary–coboundary pairing turn a topological calculation into a checkable certificate?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The recovered Morphonic discussion includes integral homology, Smith normal form and discrete Stokes. Their constructive content is an integer chain complex together with unimodular change-of-basis witnesses. Rational ranks recover Betti numbers, but cannot by themselves recover finite torsion.

## Definitions

A chain complex consists of free abelian groups C_k and integer matrices ∂_k satisfying ∂_k∂_{k+1}=0. Its homology is ker∂_k/im∂_{k+1}. A unimodular matrix has determinant±1 and an integer inverse. Cochains C^k=Hom(C_k,Z) have coboundary δφ=φ∘∂.

## Result name

Integer cokernel and discrete Stokes certificates

## Result statement

If PMQ=diag(d₁,…,d_r,0), with P,Q unimodular and d_i dividing d_{i+1}, then cokerM≅⊕Z/d_i⊕Z^{m−r}. For every cochainφ and chainc, ⟨δφ,c⟩=⟨φ,∂c⟩ exactly.

## Proof

Changing domain basis by Q and codomain basis by P preserves the image quotient. For a diagonal matrix the quotient separates coordinate by coordinate, proving the cokernel formula directly. The invariant factors arise from integer Euclidean row and column operations; floating elimination cannot replace their divisibility data. The pairing identity follows immediately from the definition of δ, so discrete Stokes is compatible with the actual integer boundary operator rather than a numerical approximation. For general homology, first compute an integer basis of ker∂_k, express im∂_{k+1} in that basis, and take its cokernel. Applying Smith form to an unrelated boundary matrix without this kernel step can give the wrong group.

## Worked example: setup

Take M=[[2,4],[6,8]], P=[[1,0],[3,−1]] and Q=[[1,−2],[0,1]]. Also consider the cellular complex of RP².

## Worked example: calculation

Both P and Q are unimodular and PMQ=diag(2,4), hence cokerM≅Z/2⊕Z/4. For RP² the complex is Z --2→ Z --0→ Z, giving H₁=Z/2, H₂=0 and H₀=Z. Over Q its first homology vanishes: the torsion was erased by changing coefficients.

## Worked example: conclusion

The integer matrix products and divisibility conditions provide a compact independently replayable certificate.
