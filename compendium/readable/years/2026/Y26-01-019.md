## Revised question retained in the annual source

How can one construct an exact affine coordinate for one-loop and two-loop flow, and what does it say about a Landau boundary?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

Separating variables gives a coordinate that advances at unit speed along a positive coupling flow. Its finite limiting value yields an exact remaining-time function, while an independently selected metric may put the same boundary infinitely far away.

## Definitions

Let μ(ℓ)>0 solve μ′=bμ²+b₁μ³ with b>0, b₁≥0 and μ(ℓ₀)=μ₀>0. All logarithms use dimensionless positive arguments.

For b₁>0 define R(μ)=−1/(bμ)+(b₁/b²)log((b+b₁μ)/μ).

The one-loop special case b₁=0 has reciprocal coordinate y=1/μ.

## Result name

Exact affine invariant and remaining flow time

## Result statement

R(μ(ℓ))−ℓ is constant. The blowup time is ℓ_*−ℓ₀=∫_{μ₀}^∞dμ/(bμ²+b₁μ³)<∞. With τ=R(∞)−R(μ), one has τ′=−1. At one loop μ=μ₀/[1−bμ₀(ℓ−ℓ₀)].

## Proof

Differentiation gives R′(μ)=1/[μ²(b+b₁μ)], so the chain rule gives dR/dℓ=1. The vector field is positive, making μ strictly increasing, and the separated integral to infinity is bounded above by ∫_{μ₀}^∞dμ/(bμ²)=1/(bμ₀). Thus its maximal positive solution reaches an infinite-coupling boundary in finite ℓ. For b₁>0 the limit is R(∞)=(b₁/b²)log b₁, yielding the claimed τ. In the one-loop case y′=−b integrates directly. A separately chosen metric g=c²dμ²/μ² has distance c|log(μ/μ₀)|, divergent as μ→∞, despite finite flow time. Time to the boundary and metric distance are therefore not interchangeable notions.

## Worked example: setup

Set b=b₁=μ₀=1 and ℓ₀=0.

## Worked example: calculation

Here R(1)=−1+log2 and R(∞)=0. The blowup time is 1−log2≈.306853. At μ=2, elapsed time R(2)−R(1)=1/2+log(3/4)≈.212318, leaving .094535. Their sum is exactly 1−log2.

## Worked example: conclusion

The boundary coordinate and its rate are obtained from a solved ODE, rather than presumed from geometric terminology. Strict positivity of the beta function also makes R invertible on the chosen positive branch, giving a unique implicit solution before the blowup time.

## Limits

The beta polynomial is an assumed model. The calculation does not prove that a physical theory remains valid to arbitrarily large coupling, nor select a unique metric, anomalous dimension or cylinder governor.

## Extension

For a supplied beta function with zeros and poles, classify maximal flow intervals and the convergence of ∫dμ/β. Determine which coordinate changes extend smoothly to the boundary and which metric completeness properties survive them.
