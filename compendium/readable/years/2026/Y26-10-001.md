## Definitions

Work in Q[u]. A root has multiplicity m when (u−α)^m divides f and the next power does not.

For nonzero f in characteristic zero, its squarefree part is q=f/gcd(f,f′), with monic gcd. A Sturm chain starts q₀=q, q₁=q′ and q_(j+1)=−rem(q_(j−1),q_j). At a real endpoint, delete zero entries before counting sign variations V.

## Result name

Squarefree reduction and a complete cubic certificate

## Result statement

For f(u)=(u−1)²(u+2)=u³−3u+2, gcd(f,f′)=u−1 and q=u²+u−2. The intervals (−5/2,−3/2) and (1/2,3/2) each contain exactly one distinct real root. Their roots are −2 with multiplicity one and 1 with multiplicity two.

## Proof

If f=(u−α)^m g with g(α)≠0, differentiating gives f′=(u−α)^(m−1)[m g+(u−α)g′]. In characteristic zero the bracket is nonzero at α, so the gcd removes exactly m−1 copies. Thus q has precisely the same roots, all simple. Here f′=3(u−1)(u+1), giving the displayed gcd and q. Euclidean division yields the Sturm chain (u²+u−2,2u+1,9/4). At a zero of an interior chain member, its two neighbors have opposite signs by the negative-remainder recurrence, so the number of variations does not change. At a simple zero of q the sign of q crosses in the direction of q′, reducing V by one as u increases. Therefore V(a)−V(b) counts roots between nonroot endpoints. The four endpoint variations are respectively 2,1,1,0, proving both interval claims. Since q has degree two, these account for every root; exact division of f then supplies their multiplicities.

## Worked example: calculation

f(1/2)=5/8 and f(3/2)=7/8 are both positive, although f(1)=0. The squarefree q changes sign across that interval. Dividing f by u−1 twice gives u+2, and its value at 1 is 3, proving multiplicity exactly two. A dyadic endpoint that is itself a root can be recorded and deflated before further bisection; it should never be silently discarded by an endpoint convention.

## Limits

An arbitrary interval with opposite endpoint signs need not contain exactly one root, and no sign change does not imply no root. This fixture proves no correctness claim about unrecovered repository code, nor does it cover positive-characteristic inseparability.
