## Revised question retained in the annual source

When is JΣJᵀ a valid uncertainty approximation, and what happens at a singular derivative?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

The exact pushforward law survives where first-order covariance propagation fails. A squared Gaussian variable gives an explicit nonzero uncertainty invisible to its zero Jacobian, clarifying why a conical or singular coordinate map needs more than a linear error bar.

## Definitions

For a random vector X with law μ and a measurable map f, the exact output law is f_#μ, defined by P(f(X)∈A)=μ(f⁻¹A).

Near a deterministic x₀, write X=x₀+εZ with E Z=0 and covariance Σ. For differentiable f, its linear approximation has covariance ε²JΣJᵀ.

A higher-order covariance estimate needs moment bounds and control of Taylor remainders, not differentiability alone.

## Result name

A complete singular-map example

## Result statement

If X∼N(0,σ²) and Y=X², then E Y=σ² and Var Y=2σ⁴, although f′(0)=0 makes the linear covariance prediction zero. For y>0 its density is e^{−y/(2σ²)}/[√(2π)σ√y].

## Proof

The Gaussian moments are E X²=σ² and E X⁴=3σ⁴, so the variance is 3σ⁴−σ⁴. For the density, each y>0 has preimages ±√y; summing the two Gaussian densities multiplied by the inverse derivative 1/(2√y) gives the stated formula. Its integrable y^{−1/2} singularity reflects the fold at zero. Away from the singularity, write X=x₀+εZ and Taylor expand X²=x₀²+2x₀εZ+ε²Z²; the linear term correctly gives leading variance 4x₀²ε²Var Z when x₀≠0. At x₀=0 the next order becomes leading instead.

## Limits

Coordinate covariance depends on the coordinate chart and noise model. From a metric term r^{2α}dz² one cannot infer Var(z)∼r^{2α} without specifying whether noise is isotropic in coordinates, an orthonormal frame or a pushforward model.
