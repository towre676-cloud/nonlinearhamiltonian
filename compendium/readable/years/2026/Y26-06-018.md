## Result name

## Result statement

[f, g]_M=Σ_{1≤k≤κ, k odd} (−1)^{(k−1)/2}ℏ^{k−1}S_k q^{a+c−k}p^{b+d−k}/(2^{k−1}k!). The k=1 term is the Poisson bracket (ad−bc)q^{a+c−1}p^{b+d−1}. Equality holds exactly when every active odd S_k for k≥3 vanishes.

## Proof

Expand the exponential and use the binomial theorem on the commuting left-right derivative operators. A term choosing r copies of−∂_p on the first input and∂_q on the second gives exactly the falling-factorial product and sign in S_k. Swapping inputs changes the order-k bidifferential by(−1)^k, so the commutator cancels every even k and doubles odd k. Dividing2(iℏ/2)^k by iℏ yields the stated coefficient, including ℏ^{k−1}; a source power ℏ^{(k−1)/2} would be a different parameter convention and would not match its own ℏ² examples. Derivative orders vanish beyond total input degree, making the sum finite. Distinct odd k have distinct formal powers ofℏ, so equality to the Poisson bracket forces each coefficient separately to vanish.
