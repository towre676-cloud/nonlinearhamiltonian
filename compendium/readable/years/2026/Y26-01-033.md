## Revised question retained in the annual source

What does a max-normalized barrier certify, and how much measurement error can its decision tolerate?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

The recovered maximum of scaled residuals is a weighted infinity norm. Its acceptance region and perturbation margin are exact, while labels such as canonicalization or refusal acquire meaning only through the residual definitions.

## Definitions

Let r_i≥0 be declared residuals and ε_i>0 their allowed tolerances. Define B(r)=max_i r_i/ε_i.

Accept when B(r)≤1. Suppose measured values r̃_i have certified errors |r̃_i−r_i|≤δ_i.

Write Δ=max_i δ_i/ε_i; every quotient is dimensionless only if residual and tolerance share units.

## Result name

Robust barrier inequality

## Result statement

|B(r̃)−B(r)|≤Δ. Hence B(r̃)+Δ≤1 certifies acceptance, while B(r̃)−Δ>1 certifies rejection. Values between those conditions require tighter information or an explicitly chosen uncertainty policy.

## Proof

For each i, r̃_i/ε_i≤r_i/ε_i+Δ≤B(r)+Δ. Taking the maximum gives B(r̃)≤B(r)+Δ; exchange r and r̃ for the reverse inequality. The acceptance and rejection conditions follow immediately. Geometrically B≤1 is the intersection of the coordinate inequalities r_i≤ε_i, so a single large residual cannot be hidden by averaging several small ones. The function is convex as a maximum of linear functions and is differentiable where one index uniquely attains the maximum; at a tie its subgradients are convex combinations of the corresponding normalized coordinate vectors.

## Worked example: setup

Use r=(.02,.03,.04), ε=(.01,.06,.1) and equal relative error budgets δ_i/ε_i=.05.

## Worked example: calculation

The normalized residuals are (2,.5,.4), so B=2 and the error margin .05 still certifies rejection. Replacing r₁ by .009 gives B=.9; B+Δ=.95≤1 certifies acceptance. A value .99 with the same error budget would not certify acceptance even though its central estimate lies below one.

## Worked example: conclusion

The decision, sensitivity and boundary ambiguity follow from one explicit inequality.

## Limits

A correct norm calculation does not validate the residual definitions or signed-release infrastructure. Correlated errors can improve a bound only after their joint constraint set is specified and proved.

## Extension

For a nonlinear pipeline, propagate certified componentwise errors into this barrier, derive a sharper support-function bound from correlated uncertainty, and specify a sound acceptance rule that includes all upstream approximation errors.
