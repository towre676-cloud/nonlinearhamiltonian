## Summary

The followup reports a wallpaper-related mirrorH¹=Z/2. That statement requires an action and coefficient module. We give an exact rank-one sign-action calculation and a contrasting trivial-action case, so the binary obstruction is a theorem with explicit inputs.

## Definitions

Let C₂={1, s} act onM=Z bys·m=−m. A1-cocycle is determined byx=f(s) withf(s²)=x+s·x=0. A1-coboundary hasf(s)=s·m−m.

For comparison, let the same group act trivially onZ. Cohomology uses the same definitions but a different action.

## Result name

A torus mirror cocycle can carry exactly one binary obstruction

## Result statement

For the sign action, H¹(C₂, Z_sign)≅Z/2Z. For the trivial action, H¹(C₂, Z)=0. Therefore an asserted mirror class cannot be read independently of its translation coefficient action.

## Proof

Under the sign action the cocycle condition isx−x=0, so every integerx is a cocycle. Coboundaries are−2m, exactly the even integers. Quotienting givesZ/2, with parity the complete invariant. Under the trivial action the condition is2x=0, forcingx=0 becauseZ has no2-torsion; the cohomology is therefore zero. In a geometric extension picture, changing a lift by an integral translation changes the sign-action cocycle by an even integer, so odd and even lifts cannot be identified by that adjustment. For torus-valued coefficientsM=R/Z the answer changes again: multiplication by two is surjective, making the corresponding quotient vanish. This reinforces that one must state whether translations are integral, real or torus-valued.
