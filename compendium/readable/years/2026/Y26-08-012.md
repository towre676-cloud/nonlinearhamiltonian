## Revised question retained in the annual source

Why do constant fibre dimension and a finite-order deformation computation not imply constant cohomology or unobstructedness?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The quiver project’s August16 source mentions Hochschild jumps and Kuranishi calculations through order twelve. A complete one-parameter algebra demonstrates the first phenomenon, and a simple formal equation isolates the logical gap in the second.

## Definitions

Assumek has characteristic zero andA_t=k[x]/(x²−t). It is a free rank-two k[t]-module with basis1,x. Hochschild cohomologyHH^n(A,A) is the cohomology of the Hochschild cochain complex; its degree-one elements are derivations modulo inner derivations.

## Result name

Flatness does not force cohomological constancy

## Result statement

For an algebraically closedk andt≠0, A_t≅k×k andHH^n(A_t,A_t)=0 forn>0. At t=0 the dual numbers have dimHH^n=1 for every n>0. A deformation verified through order12 need not extend to all formal orders.

## Proof

For t≠0 choosea witha²=t. The factorsx−a andx+a are coprime, so the Chinese remainder theorem identifiesA_t withk×k. Its separability idempotent gives a contracting homotopy for positive-degree Hochschild cochains. At t=0, the standard two-periodic bimodule resolution ofk[x]/x² has alternating mapsx⊗1−1⊗x andx⊗1+1⊗x. ApplyingHom_{A^e}(−,A) gives alternating cochain maps0 and multiplication by2x. For every positive degree, kernel or cokernel has dimension one: ker(2x)=kx andA/(2x)=k. MeanwhileHH⁰=A has dimension two. Thus the algebra is flat although its positive-degree cohomology jumps. For the finite-order issue, the equation s^{13}=0 is satisfied by the truncated formal parameter s=ε moduloε^{13}, but not bys=ε in k[[ε]] or moduloε^{14}. A valid order-twelve packet therefore cannot exclude an order-thirteen obstruction.

Flatness, cohomology-jump loci and all-order formal extension are separate mathematical claims.

## Limits
