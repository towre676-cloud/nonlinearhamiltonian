## Summary

The May discussion contrasted classical existence, constructibility and choice. A concrete finite-ring theorem gives positive witness-producing mathematics. It also prevents an unwarranted conclusion that every classical existence proof must already carry an efficient algorithm.

## Definitions

A finite commutative ring R has an explicitly enumerable underlying set and computable addition and multiplication tables. An ideal is a subset containing zero, closed under addition and multiplication by all ring elements.

A maximal proper ideal is a proper ideal contained in no strictly larger proper ideal. A finite search is constructive when it returns a witness with a terminating verification.

## Result name

A finite maximal-ideal algorithm separates constructive witnesses from unrestricted choice

## Result statement

Every finite nonzero commutative unital ring has a maximal proper ideal, and exhaustive search returns one. For Z/12Z the maximal ideals are (2) and (3), giving quotient fields F₂ and F₃.

## Proof

Enumerate all subsets of R and test the finite closure conditions for an ideal. At least the zero ideal is proper because 1≠0. Among the finitely many proper ideals choose one of maximal cardinality; no strictly larger proper ideal can exist, so it is maximal. Termination follows from the finite subset count 2^{|R|}. If I is maximal and x∉I, the ideal generated by I and x must be R. Thus 1=i+rx for some i∈I,r∈R, and [r][x]=1 in R/I. Every nonzero quotient element is invertible, proving the quotient is a field. In Z/12Z, ideals correspond to divisors d of 12, and maximality occurs when the quotient Z/dZ is a field, namely d=2 or 3. This is a completely finite witness procedure, although its exponential cost makes it unsuitable as a general efficiency claim.

## Worked example: setup

Enumerate ideals of Z/12Z through their divisor generators.

## Worked example: calculation

The proper ideals are (0),(2),(3),(4),(6); (2) contains six residues and (3) four. The quotient sizes are two and three, and their nonzero residue classes have inverses. The ideal (6) is not maximal because it lies properly inside (2).

## Worked example: conclusion

The example provides both an existence proof and an explicit checkable witness.

## Limits

For arbitrary infinite rings, the usual maximal-ideal theorem is proved with a choice principle such as Zorn’s lemma. That fact neither makes finite algebra nonconstructive nor makes every consequence of ZFC algorithmically decidable.

## Extension

Replace exhaustive subset enumeration by ideal arithmetic or Gröbner methods for a specified finitely presented ring, and state the computational model and termination assumptions separately from the classical maximal-ideal theorem.
