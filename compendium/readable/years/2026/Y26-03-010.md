## Revised question retained in the annual source

How can fixed-point and multiplier-minus-one conditions be eliminated without assuming a global uniqueness theorem?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered map Kα(x)=[x²+(log cos x)²]^(−α/2) is explicit enough to derive its flip equations directly. The historical numerical pair is reproducible, while the claimed uniqueness over the whole interval needs its own analytic proof.

## Definitions

For 0<x<π/2 set U(x)=x²+(log cos x)²>0 and Kα(x)=U(x)^(−α/2). Assume α>0 and U(x)≠1.

A fixed-point flip candidate satisfies Kα(x)=x and ∂xKα(x)=−1. It becomes a bifurcation theorem only after transversality and nonlinear nondegeneracy are established.

## Result name

Exact elimination

## Result statement

The two conditions are equivalent to F(x)=U(x)log U(x)+x log x U′(x)=0 and α=−2log x/log U(x)>0. Here U′=2x−2log cos x tan x.

## Proof

Take logarithms of the fixed-point condition to obtain log x=−α log U/2. Differentiate K with respect to x and substitute K=x, yielding K′=−αxU′/(2U). Setting this equal to −1 and eliminating α gives −x log x U′=U log U, exactly F=0. Every algebraic step is reversible when U≠1 and the resulting α is positive. At U=1 the fixed-point equation forces x=1, but U(1)>1, so no excluded fixed point is lost. A sign-changing interval therefore proves existence of a candidate by continuity, without proving that no second zero exists.

## Worked example: setup

Bracket the source candidate between x=0.94 and x=0.95.

## Worked example: calculation

Direct evaluation gives F(0.94)≈−0.01854546 and F(0.95)≈0.04773035. Bisection gives x≈0.9428576799823385, U≈1.1719159689972305 and α≈0.7418045150878935. Substitution makes Kα(x)−x and Kα′(x)+1 smaller than ordinary floating-point rounding at the reported digits.

## Worked example: conclusion

The candidate and scalar reduction are reproduced; the interval and residual checks are numerical, with no hidden global uniqueness conclusion.

## Limits

The source did not recover its promised uniqueness proof. Bisection alone is not a certificate of uniqueness or of a nondegenerate flip.

## Extension

Use interval arithmetic to bound F′ on a finite partition and endpoint asymptotics to control the two tails; then evaluate the classical flip coefficients with outward-rounded bounds.
