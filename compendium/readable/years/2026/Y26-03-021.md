## Revised question retained in the annual source

When do several scalar invariants determine an analytic object up to the intended equivalence?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The user asks whether four invariants are projections of one analytic singularity. It is possible for several measurements to share an origin without jointly determining that origin. A finite-dimensional example states the distinction cleanly and suggests an actual identifiability target.

## Definitions

Let a moduli space M carry an equivalence relation ∼, and let I:M→R^r be constant on equivalence classes. Its components are invariants.

A complete invariant means I(x)=I(y) implies x∼y. Merely defining several components from one object proves no completeness.

## Result name

Factorization versus reconstruction

## Result statement

Every invariant map factors through the set quotient M/∼. Reconstruction from invariant values requires injectivity of the induced quotient map. Differential rank can give local information but does not imply global injectivity.

## Proof

Define the induced map by [x]↦I(x); invariance makes this well-defined. It is injective exactly when equal outputs force the same equivalence class, proving the characterization. For a basic counterexample, take M=R with equality as equivalence and four components I(t)=(t²,t⁴,t⁶,t⁸). All are projections of the same parameter and the derivative has rank one at every t≠0, but t and −t have identical values. Restricting to t≥0 restores injectivity because the first component has unique nonnegative square root. Thus a chamber choice may repair ambiguity, but that chamber and its boundary need to be stated rather than inferred from a numerical fit.

## Worked example: setup

Use t=2 and t=−2.

## Worked example: calculation

Both yield (4,16,64,256), despite being different objects under equality. On the nonnegative chamber the value (4,16,64,256) reconstructs exactly t=2. At t=0 the derivative vanishes although the chamber map remains injective.

## Worked example: conclusion

Common origin, local rank and complete reconstruction are three separate claims.

## Limits

The four source quantities and equivalence group are not all fully defined in the retrieved passage, so their joint completeness cannot be asserted.

## Extension

Specify the analytic-germ equivalence group, prove invariant descent, find all fibers of the four-component map and give a branch or chamber theorem that resolves any remaining ambiguity.
