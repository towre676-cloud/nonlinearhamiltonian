## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which block structure justifies a sector rank sum3+2=5, and what happens to entropy?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The July4 source reports a sector–Schmidt split3+2. The exact algebra is a block singular-value decomposition when the sectors are orthogonal on both tensor factors. Orthogonality on just one side does not suffice to add ranks.

## Definitions

Represent a bipartite pure state by its coefficient matrix C. Assume orthogonal decompositions on both sides put C into block diagonal form diag(C₁,…,C_s). Normalize||C||_F²=1 and set p_j=||C_j||_F².

## Result name

Block Schmidt decomposition

## Result statement

rankC=ΣrankC_j. Its reduced-state spectrum is the union of the spectra C_jC_j*, and its entropy is H(p)+Σp_jS(C_jC_j*/p_j), omitting zero blocks.

## Proof

Block multiplication gives CC*=diag(C₁C₁*,…,C_sC_s*), so eigenvalues and ranks are unions. Write each nonzero block spectrum as p_jλ_{jk} withΣ_kλ_{jk}=1. Then−Σ_jk p_jλ_jk log(p_jλ_jk)=−Σ_jp_jlogp_j+Σ_jp_j[−Σ_kλ_jklogλ_jk], proving the entropy identity. If only one-side sectors are orthogonal, C may instead be a vertical stack with overlapping row spaces; its rank can be smaller than the sum. Thus a global charge label alone must be translated into the relevant local block structure before the formula is used. For normalized coefficients the entropy formula is obtained by listing every squared singular value of a block as p_j s_{j,k}, withΣ_k s_{j,k}=1. Expanding−Σ_{j,k}p_js_{j,k}log(p_js_{j,k}) separates the sector entropy−Σp_jlogp_j from the average within-sector entropy. This calculation shows exactly why probabilities, rather than unnormalized block ranks, enter the entropy identity.

## Worked example: setup

Take C=I₅/√5 split into a3×3 and2×2 block.

## Worked example: calculation

The Schmidt rank is five. Sector probabilities are3/5 and2/5 and the entropy is H(3/5,2/5)+(3/5)log3+(2/5)log2=log5. In contrast stacking two identical one-row matrices gives rank one, despite a sum of individual ranks two.
