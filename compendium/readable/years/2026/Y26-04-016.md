## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which information is preserved by the codimension determinant, and which is retained only by the full product polynomial?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The source proposes a hierarchy T(θ)=∏⟨m_k,θ⟩ and volume |det(m₁,…,m_d)|. Both are well-defined after the vectors and Euclidean metric are fixed. Equal volume does not determine the directional field or its spherical norm.

## Definitions

Put row vectors m₁,…,m_d into M. Define V=|det M| and T_M(θ)=∏_(i=1)^d(m_i·θ).

The Gram matrix is G=MMᵀ and V²=det G. The normalized spherical moment is Q(M)=E_(θ uniform on S^(d−1))[T_M(θ)²].

## Result name

Equal-volume directional distinction

## Result statement

V²=det G, but V does not determine Q(M). In dimension two, matrices of determinant one can have arbitrarily large Q.

## Proof

The determinant identity follows from det(MMᵀ)=det(M)². For the distinction choose rows m₁=(1,0),m₂=(t,1). Then det M=1 and T=θ₁(tθ₁+θ₂). On the unit circle, symmetry kills Eθ₁³θ₂, while Eθ₁⁴=3/8 and Eθ₁²θ₂²=1/8. Hence Q=(3t²+1)/8, which is unbounded as |t| grows although V stays one. The full directional product retains relative-angle and shear information discarded by the volume. A normalized torsion must therefore specify what scaling or row normalization it removes.

## Worked example: setup

Compare t=0 and t=1.

## Worked example: calculation

Both matrices have volume one. Their squared spherical product norms are 1/8 and 1/2, respectively, so their L² norms differ by a factor two. Their Gram matrices are I and [[1,1],[1,2]], both with determinant one.

## Worked example: conclusion

Volume and directional concentration are demonstrably independent statistics.

## Limits

The reported source torsion values at volume 24 require its precise normalization and vector inputs; they are not reproduced from the volume alone.

## Extension

Classify Q at fixed determinant under an explicit row-length constraint, and find extremizers or stability inequalities connecting volume loss to directional concentration.
