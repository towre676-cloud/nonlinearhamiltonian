## Revised question retained in the annual source

Reconstruction: Which characteristic functions are fixed by the stated rescaling map, and what exponent is compatible with finite nonzero variance?

## Status

Classical result or newly stipulated finite model; historical claims explicitly delimited

## Summary

The May artifact source includes a renormalization map on characteristic functions. This is mathematical data sufficient for a clean fixed-point calculation. The analysis below is newly developed and does not imply a universality theorem for an unspecified simulation.

## Definitions

For α>0 set R_αφ(t)=φ(t/2^{1/α})². If φ is the characteristic function of X, this is the characteristic function of 2^{−1/α}(X₁+X₂) for independent copies.

For 0<α≤2 the symmetric stable family has characteristic function φ_c(t)=exp(−c|t|^α), c≥0. Its positive-definiteness is a classical stable-law input, not inferred merely from the formula being bounded.

## Result name

The RG characteristic-function map has explicit stable fixed points and a finite-variance Gaussian obstruction

## Result statement

Every φ_c is fixed by R_α. If a fixed law has finite, nonzero variance, then α=2. For α=2, any centered finite-variance fixed law is Gaussian.

## Proof

Substitute: φ_c(t/2^{1/α})²=exp(−2c|t|^α/2)=φ_c(t). If X has variance σ²∈(0,∞), the variance of its rescaled two-copy sum is 2^{1−2/α}σ². Equality in law therefore forces 2^{1−2/α}=1 and α=2. In that case expectation equality first forces EX=0. A centered finite-variance characteristic function satisfies φ(t)=1−σ²t²/2+o(t²). Iterate the fixed equation to obtain φ(t)=φ(t/√(2^n))^{2^n}. The factors approach one, so use the continuous logarithm near one: 2^n logφ(t/√(2^n))→−σ²t²/2. Taking exponentials yields the Gaussian characteristic function at every real t. This proves classification within the stated moment class without classifying all semistable laws.

## Worked example: setup

Compare α=2 with the Cauchy exponent α=1.

## Worked example: calculation

For φ(t)=e^{−3t²}, R₂φ=e^{−3t²}, corresponding to variance six. For φ(t)=e^{−2|t|}, R₁φ=φ but variance is infinite. Applying R₁ to the Gaussian instead produces e^{−(3/2)t²}, so it fails the fixed-point equation except at zero.

## Worked example: conclusion

The exponent and moment assumptions now have a proved relationship, and the map has positive examples.

## Limits

The stable characteristic-function theorem is classical. There may be non-Gaussian fixed laws outside the finite-variance α=2 class or under discrete scaling variants; no broad classification is claimed.
