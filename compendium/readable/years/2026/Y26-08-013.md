## Revised question retained in the annual source

What are all solutions ofm=tanh(am), and which ones are stable under fixed-point iteration?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The August16 source gives the regulator equationm=tanh(gm/(2η)) and thresholdg_c=2η. A complete scalar analysis proves this threshold and its near-critical scaling. The equation is treated as a stipulated mathematical model rather than an empirical phase-transition claim.

## Definitions

Letη>0,g>0 anda=g/(2η). Defineh_a(m)=tanh(am)−m and the iterationm_{n+1}=tanh(am_n). A fixed point is locally attracting when the absolute derivative of this iteration is below one.

## Result name

Existence, uniqueness and critical scaling

## Result statement

For0<a≤1 the only fixed point is0. Fora>1 there are exactly three fixed points0,±m_*(a), with0<m_*<1. The nonzero points are locally attracting and0 is repelling. As a↓1, m_*²=3(a−1)/a³+O((a−1)²).

Strict concavity gives exactly one positive crossing, and oddness gives its negative. At the crossing the derivativeh_a′ is strictly negative: a positive concave function starting fromzero crosses the axis with negative slope. Hence0<a sech²(am_*)<1, proving attraction, while the derivative atzero isa>1.
