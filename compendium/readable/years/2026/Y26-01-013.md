## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which variational problem genuinely selects an Euler spiral, and how does it differ from bending-energy minimization?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

Linear curvature is forced by minimizing squared curvature rate with fixed endpoint curvatures. Integrating the tangent angle produces a clothoid; changing the functional to squared curvature instead selects a constant-curvature curve under an angle constraint.

## Definitions

Parametrize a regular plane curve by arc length s∈[0,L]. Write its unit tangent as (cos θ(s),sin θ(s)) and signed curvature κ=θ′.

Prescribe endpoint curvatures κ(0)=κ₀, κ(L)=κ₁ and minimize J=(1/2)∫₀ᴸ|κ′|²ds over absolutely continuous κ with square-integrable derivative.

This problem prescribes curvature endpoints, not arbitrary position and tangent endpoints; the integrated curve is defined up to its initial point and angle.

## Result name

Sharp variational selection of linear curvature

## Result statement

The unique minimizer is κ(s)=κ₀+(κ₁−κ₀)s/L, with J_min=(κ₁−κ₀)²/(2L). Its tangent angle is θ₀+κ₀s+(κ₁−κ₀)s²/(2L), so its coordinates are Fresnel-type integrals.

## Proof

The endpoint constraint gives ∫₀ᴸκ′ds=κ₁−κ₀. Cauchy–Schwarz implies (κ₁−κ₀)²≤L∫|κ′|², with equality exactly when κ′ is constant almost everywhere. This proves both minimality and uniqueness without solving an Euler–Lagrange boundary-value equation. Integrating κ gives θ and then x(s)=x₀+∫cos θ, y(s)=y₀+∫sin θ. The unit-speed property is automatic. In contrast, if one minimizes (1/2)∫κ² with only total turning ∫κ=Θ fixed, the same inequality selects κ=Θ/L. Thus the chosen cost, constraints and endpoint data determine whether one obtains a spiral or circular arc.

## Worked example: setup

Let L=2, κ₀=0, κ₁=1, x₀=y₀=θ₀=0.

## Worked example: calculation

Then κ=s/2, θ=s²/4 and J_min=1/4. Coordinates are x(s)=∫₀ˢcos(r²/4)dr and y(s)=∫₀ˢsin(r²/4)dr. Near zero, x=s−s⁵/160+O(s⁹) and y=s³/12−s⁷/2688+O(s¹¹). Differentiation verifies speed one and curvature s/2.

## Worked example: conclusion

The construction gives a full curve and a sharp certified cost, rather than selecting a shape by analogy.

## Limits

Adding endpoint positions, obstacle constraints, control bounds or a bending-energy term changes the minimizer. A priced-curvature learning model must specify its own admissible trajectories and dynamics.

## Extension

Add a fixed endpoint pose and curvature bounds, derive the Pontryagin or constrained Euler–Lagrange system, and determine when piecewise clothoids remain optimal. Include existence and active-constraint conditions rather than presume the same closed form survives.
