## Result statement

B-zeros occur atφ=kπ and A-zeros atφ=π/2+kπ, so they are simple and interlace. Each B-root continues analytically with λ′=−φ_α/φ_x. Its local Herglotz-normalized mass is A(λ)/B′(λ)=1/φ_x(λ)>0.

## Proof

The polar form gives A=r cosφ and B=r sinφ. Strict phase monotonicity makes each level have at most one root and gives the alternating order of the two level families. At a B-root, B′=rφ_x cosφ is nonzero, proving simplicity; the analytic implicit-function theorem then gives the parameter derivative. Dividing A by B′ cancels rcosφ and gives the mass. Near λ, m(z)=−[A(λ)/B′(λ)]/(z−λ)+an analytic term, matching a positive atomμ/(λ−z). None of this asserts global Herglotz positivity from local residues alone; an entire upper-half-plane condition is stronger. At aB-zero, B′=rφ_x cosφ because the termr′sinφ vanishes, whileA=r cosφ. Their ratio is therefore1/φ_x with no dependence on the amplitude r. This cancellation explains the positive mass formula. Real phase monotonicity on a finite interval alone does not establish a global upper-half-plane Herglotz representation.
