## Revised question retained in the annual source

How does a symbolic prefix metric differ from Euclidean real-number distance and the p-adic metric?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

A weighted symbolic boundary has a provable ultrametric and a computable Hausdorff dimension. Its ordinary radix-value map is continuous but identifies boundary expansions and has no uniform inverse modulus, while p-adic distance compares low-order digits under a different arithmetic convention.

## Definitions

Let Σ_b={0,…,b−1}^N, b≥2. For distinct sequences let ℓ(x,y) be their common-prefix length and define d(x,y)=a^{ℓ(x,y)}, with 0<a<1; d(x,x)=0.

A length-k cylinder fixes the first k digits and has diameter a^k. Use the uniform product measure, giving each cylinder mass b^{−k}.

The real radix map is π(x)=∑_{j≥1}x_jb^{−j}.

## Result name

Ultrametric, dimension and failure of an inverse Lipschitz estimate

## Result statement

d is an ultrametric and dim_HΣ_b=log b/log(1/a). Sequences agreeing through k digits have |π(x)−π(y)|≤b^{−k}. The map π need not be injective, and small real distance does not force a long common prefix.

## Proof

Two pairs with prefix lengths r and s force the third pair to agree through min(r,s), proving d(x,z)≤max(d(x,y),d(y,z)). Covering by all b^k cylinders gives Hausdorff s-content at most (ba^s)^k, hence the upper dimension bound. Conversely a ball of radius comparable to a^k has mass at most a constant times b^{−k}=a^{ks₀}, with s₀=log b/log(1/a). The mass-distribution inequality then bounds below the s₀-dimensional Hausdorff content, giving equality. For π, sum the largest possible tail: ∑_{j>k}(b−1)b^{−j}=b^{−k}. The familiar two expansions of a terminating radix value show noninjectivity, so a global inverse modulus is impossible.

## Worked example: setup

Use binary sequences and a=1/4.

## Worked example: calculation

The dimension is log2/log4=1/2. The sequences .1000… and .0111… both map to 1/2, despite disagreeing immediately. Replacing the latter by .011…11000… gives distinct real numbers arbitrarily close to 1/2 while their first digits still differ. By contrast, in Z₂ the numbers 1 and 1+2^k are 2^{−k} apart because their low-order digits agree.

## Worked example: conclusion

Representation geometry and arithmetic topology must be specified before prefix depth is used as a stability invariant.

## Limits

A real-valued scalar does not become p-adic merely by storing its leading binary digits. Signs, exponents, carries and nonunique expansions need explicit encoding conventions.

## Extension

For a general decreasing weight sequence w_k, establish dimension under precise regularity assumptions on log w_k, and classify when two weighted prefix metrics are bi-Lipschitz equivalent. Study arithmetic operations using their true moduli of continuity in each representation.
