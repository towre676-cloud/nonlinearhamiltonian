## Revised question retained in the annual source

When does a finite observation prefix determine an infinite series, and can the cutoff be computed?

## Status

Classical mathematics or a newly chosen finite model developed with proof; historical claims delimited

## Summary

The retrieved work separates a Noetherian existence claim from a finite-dimensional boundary-state closure. These are different kinds of certificate. One may prove that some finite prefix suffices without obtaining any effective numerical bound; a supplied finite transition system permits an explicit rank-based stopping test.

## Definitions

Let a_n(θ) be polynomials over a field k in a fixed finite parameter tuple θ. Compare two parameter tuples θ and θ′.

For a separate linear model, let W₀ be a row space in k^m and M₁,…,M_r fixed matrices; define W_(j+1)=W_j+Σ_i W_jM_i.

## Result name

Two precise finite-determination principles

## Result statement

There exists N such that equality a_n(θ)=a_n(θ′) for0≤n≤N implies equality for every n, with θ,θ′ in any field extension of k. In the linear model, the chain W_j stabilizes after at most m−dim W₀ strict increases; once stable, it contains every row wM_word with w∈W₀.

## Proof

In k[θ,θ′], let I_N be the ideal generated by a_n(θ)−a_n(θ′) for n≤N. Hilbert’s basis theorem makes this polynomial ring Noetherian, so the ascending ideal chain stabilizes at some I_N. Every later coefficient difference is then a polynomial combination of the first N+1 differences, and vanishes at any pair of parameter values making those differences zero. This proves existence but supplies no general effective N. For the linear claim, dimensions cannot strictly increase more than m−dim W₀ times. If one step has W_(j+1)=W_j, then W_j is invariant under all right multiplications M_i; induction over word length includes every future row. Thus exact row reduction and explicit spanning identities give a computable closure certificate.

## Worked example: setup

Use the rational family F_(a,b)(x)=a/(1−bx).

## Worked example: calculation

Its coefficients are a_n=abⁿ. Equality of a₀ and a₁ determines all coefficients: if a≠0 it determines b, while if a=0 the entire series vanishes independently of b. Therefore this chosen family has cutoff N=1, although its parameters are not identifiable on a=0.

## Worked example: conclusion

Finite series determination and parameter reconstruction are distinct conclusions.

## Limits

The theorem applies to a fixed finite polynomial parameterization; it gives no uniform cutoff across unbounded templates or arbitrary sequences. The historical22-dimensional closure ranks cannot be replayed without their matrices.

## Extension

For the actual graph-template family, supply an explicit generating ideal or boundary transition representation and derive an effective cutoff; separately prove when equality of the resulting invariant forces graph isomorphism in the stated graph class.
