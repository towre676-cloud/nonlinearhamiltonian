## Revised question retained in the annual source

## Summary

The source names the figure-eight expression twice the imaginary dilogarithm and a separate5₂ volume target. We develop the regular-shape special value exactly and bound its series tail. The geometric identification with an ideal tetrahedron is classical input; the triangulation of a particular knot must still be supplied.

## Definitions

Define D(z)=ImLi₂(z)+log|z|arg(1−z), with the usual Bloch–Wigner branch-independent continuation. On |z|=1, z≠1, D(e^{iθ})=Σ_{n≥1}sin(nθ)/n².

For ζ=e^{iπ/3}, |ζ|=1, so the logarithmic correction is zero. An oriented ideal tetrahedron of shape ζ is regular; its volume equals D(ζ), a classical hyperbolic-volume theorem.

## Result name

Bloch–Wigner values give a convergent regular-shape computation with an explicit tail

## Result statement

D(ζ)=Cl₂(π/3)>0, and the N-term series has absolute tail at most1/N. Therefore the two-regular-tetrahedra volume2D(ζ) is about2.0298832128, with a rigorously controlled series error. It is different from the reported5₂ target2.8281220883.

## Proof

The dilogarithm power series Σz^n/n² converges absolutely on the unit circle, so its imaginary part is exactly the sine series. The omitted absolute sum is at mostΣ_{n>N}1/n²≤∫_N∞x^{−2}dx=1/N. Positivity can be seen from the integral identity Cl₂(θ)=−∫₀^θlog(2sin(t/2))dt: on0<t<π/3,2sin(t/2)<1, making the integrand positive. The derivative identity follows by Abel damping the Fourier series of−log(2sin(t/2)), integrating, and letting the damping tend to one; the logarithmic endpoint is integrable. A complete two-tetrahedron triangulation with compatible positive orientations then adds the two volumes by the classical volume theorem. The numerical value alone cannot identify which manifold a shape belongs to.
