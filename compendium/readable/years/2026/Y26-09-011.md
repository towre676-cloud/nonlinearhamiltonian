## Revised question retained in the annual source

What does one call cover, and which combinatorial certificate proves that fewer calls cannot cover the required support?

## Status

Classical mathematics with newly authored derivation or explicitly defined worked model

## Summary

The degree-eight support result used complete bipartite incidence graphs and an81-call bound. Without its full call semantics, the count cannot be reconstructed from the number of words alone. A precise incidence model demonstrates how an exact lower bound and a matching construction should be certified.

## Definitions

In the newly declared vertex-call model on K_(a,b), one call chooses a vertex and covers every incident edge. The task is to cover all edges.

A matching is a collection of edges with disjoint endpoints. Every vertex cover must choose an endpoint of each matching edge.

## Result name

An incidence model needs a dual lower-bound witness before a call count is a theorem

## Result statement

The minimum number of vertex calls covering K_(a,b) is min(a,b). A matching of that size certifies the lower bound, and selecting the smaller vertex side certifies the upper bound.

## Proof

Assume a≤b and match each of the a left vertices to a different right vertex. The edges are disjoint, so one chosen vertex can cover at most one of these matching edges; at least a calls are necessary. Selecting all a left vertices covers every edge, giving equality. Equivalently, if at least one vertex on each side remains unselected, their connecting edge is uncovered. This exact primal/dual pair makes the cost independent of a heuristic enumeration of candidate covers.

## Worked example: setup

Take the new fixture K_(4,7).

## Worked example: calculation

There are28 required edges. Four left calls cover them all. Any three calls leave an unselected vertex on both sides and therefore an uncovered edge; the four-edge matching gives the same lower bound. If the meaning of a call were changed to one edge rather than one vertex, the minimum would instead be28. Thus even this small graph demonstrates that a support size does not determine a cost without a declared action model.

## Worked example: conclusion

The support theorem is represented by an exact covering problem and a short lower-bound certificate.

## Limits

The historical graphs Γ_(k,s)=K_(2^k,2^(8−k)−1), their fibers and allowed calls were not fully supplied. The new theorem does not claim to derive81 from488 or verify that missing-middle-fiber classification.
