## Definitions

For symmetric matrices use ⟨A,X⟩=tr(AX). Consider the primal minimum ⟨C,X⟩ subject to ⟨A_i,X⟩=b_i and X≽0.

A dual feasible y has slack S=C−Σ_i y_iA_i≽0. Its objective is bᵀy. Positive semidefinite matrices satisfy tr(SX)≥0.

## Result name

An SDP primal-dual pair gives an exact certificate and a traceable gap

## Result statement

Every primal/dual feasible pair has gap ⟨C,X⟩−bᵀy=tr(SX)≥0. A zero-gap pair proves global optimality without an appeal to strong duality or numerical convergence.

## Proof

Substitute the slack expression and use the primal equalities: tr(SX)=tr(CX)−Σ_i y_i tr(A_iX)=tr(CX)−bᵀy. To prove nonnegativity, write X=RRᵀ by the spectral theorem. Then tr(SX)=tr(RᵀSR), a sum of nonnegative diagonal quadratic forms. If the gap is zero, weak duality sandwiches every feasible primal value above the dual value and every feasible dual value below the primal one, so the pair is optimal. This argument requires only exact feasibility and positivity. Approximate residuals can also be handled, but then the result needs bounds on the unknown multipliers or variable size; a tiny objective gap alone cannot repair unbounded feasibility error.

## Worked example: setup

Minimize X₁₁+2X₂₂ subject to trX=1 and X≽0 in dimension two.

## Worked example: calculation

Take C=diag(1,2), A=I and b=1. The primal X=diag(1,0) has objective one. The dual y=1 gives slack diag(0,1), also feasible, and gap zero. For X=diag(3/4,1/4), the objective is 5/4 and its exact gap is 1/4. An off-diagonal perturbation of the optimal X is not automatically feasible: [[1,t],[t,0]] has determinant −t² and is positive semidefinite only for t=0.

Optimality follows from a finite algebraic witness, with positivity checked explicitly.
