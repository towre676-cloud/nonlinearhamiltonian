## Definitions

Let Φ(x; t)=x³/3−tx with real t>0. Its critical points are x_±=±√t. Consider a Laplace-type integrand e^{−Φ/h} on a contour for which it converges.

A thimble coefficient is an integer determined by a chosen contour’s intersections with dual steepest-ascent cycles. It is not determined solely by the list of critical points.

## Result name

A cubic fold produces the three-halves action law before any thimble claim

## Result statement

Φ(x_−; t)−Φ(x_+; t)=4t^{3/2}/3. Under x=h^{1/3}y the exponent becomes y³/3−(t/h^{2/3})y, so the fold transition scale is t≈h^{2/3}. The Hessians at the saddles have magnitude2√t and degenerate at t=0.

## Proof

The critical equation x²−t=0 gives the two roots. Substitution yields Φ(√t; t)=−2t^{3/2}/3 and Φ(−√t; t)=2t^{3/2}/3, proving the action difference. The second derivative is2x, giving opposite Hessian signs and magnitude2√t. Rescaling x by h^{1/3} removes h from the cubic term and leaves the single scaled parameter t/h^{2/3}; therefore a Gaussian approximation at two separated saddles becomes nonuniform as that parameter approaches zero. This is the precise mechanism behind an Airy uniform approximation. Yet choosing the real line is not automatically permissible for e^{−x³/(3h)} because one end diverges. A convergent contour must run into sectors where the cubic real part is positive, and different contour combinations can have different saddle coefficients while sharing the same local phase.
