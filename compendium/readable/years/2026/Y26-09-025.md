## Summary

The WIND atlas wrote horizontal velocity as a rotated gradient plus a gradient. On a periodic plane this yields exact Poisson equations and an explicit invisible harmonic component. The same source’s flow-map equations supply a separate kinematic conservation identity rather than a universal physical law.

## Definitions

On a flat two-torus let u=(−ψ_y+χ_x,ψ_x+χ_y)+h, with constant vector h. Require ψ andχ to have zero mean.

For a smooth flow map X(t,a), let F=∂X/∂a and J=detF. Along trajectories A=∇u, so F′=AF.

## Result name

Hodge potentials recover divergence and curl but leave a harmonic mean

## Result statement

divu=Δχ and curlu=Δψ. The nonzero Fourier modes of divergence and curl determineψ,χ uniquely, but do not determine h. Also J′=(divu)J. If a transported vector w obeys w′=Aw and a passive-scalar gradient g obeys g′=−Aᵀg, then w·g is conserved.

## Proof

Mixed partials cancel in the divergence and curl formulas. For a nonzero Fourier wavevector k, the Laplacian is multiplication by−|k|², so each potential coefficient is the corresponding negative data coefficient divided by|k|². The zero mode is unconstrained, exactly the constant harmonic field. Differentiating the flow ODE with respect to its initial label gives F′=AF. Jacobi’s determinant formula gives J′=JtrA while F remains invertible. Differentiating the scalar transport equation gives the gradient evolution; then(w·g)′=(Aw)·g−w·Aᵀg=0. Interpreting w as absolute-vorticity divided by density requires the additional fluid equations that imply its transport law.

## Limits

Boundary conditions change the harmonic space. Ertel potential-vorticity conservation additionally needs the appropriate inviscid and adiabatic hypotheses; the kinematic product alone does not prove a weather-model conservation law.
