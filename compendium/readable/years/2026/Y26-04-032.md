## Summary

The source proposes a new derivative with flux, scale and overlap terms. A discrete derivative generally cannot obey the classical product rule unchanged. An exact finite-difference identity and a conservative graph flux supply a constructive baseline for the claimed calculus.

## Definitions

For mesh step h≠0 define D_hf(x)=[f(x+h)−f(x)]/h. For a graph with incidence B, let m be cell masses and j oriented edge fluxes, with m′=−Bj.

The boundary of a closed graph has no external flux, so every incidence column sums to zero. A source term must be added explicitly if mass is not conserved.

## Result name

Exact discrete product correction and mass balance

## Result statement

D_h(fg)=fD_hg+gD_hf+h(D_hf)(D_hg). The graph flux equation conserves total mass because 1ᵀm′=0.

## Proof

Write f(x+h)=f+hD_hf and similarly for g. Multiplying and subtracting fg gives the product formula exactly. Thus an anomaly term is mathematically expected; omitting it is not a valid classical rule for a forward difference. The conservation law follows by left-multiplying m′=−Bj by 1ᵀ and using 1ᵀB=0. Moving cell boundaries require transport or remeshing terms, but any proposed compensation can be checked by whether its total contribution equals the actual boundary/source balance. Naming a scale or overlap operator does not by itself establish such a conservation theorem.

## Worked example: setup

Use f(x)=g(x)=x at x=0, and a two-cell graph with flux j=3.

## Worked example: calculation

D_h(x²)(0)=h, while fD_hg+gD_hf=0, so the correction h·1·1 is necessary. With B=(-1,1)ᵀ, m′=(3,−3) and total derivative is zero.

## Worked example: conclusion

Exact algebra identifies the correction and an independently checkable conservation requirement.
