## Revised question retained in the annual source

What does differentiating a weighted optimum prove, and which extra condition is needed for dimension collapse?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The source links nested behavioral signals with a low effective rank. The implicit derivative is valid under an invertible Hessian, but nested weighted residual forcings can span more than one dimension. A realizable stationary least-squares example isolates the missing proportionality assumption while respecting the necessary linear dependence among sensitivities.

## Definitions

Let E(x,γ)=1/2 Σ_kγ_k||Yx−b||²_(W_k), where γ_k>0, W_k are symmetric positive semidefinite and H=Σγ_kYᵀW_kY is positive definite. Let r=b−Yx*.

Define D_k=∂x*/∂γ_k. The stationarity equation is Σγ_kYᵀW_k(Yx*−b)=0.

## Result name

Exact sensitivity span and nesting limitation

## Result statement

H D_k=YᵀW_kr, hence span{D_k}=H⁻¹span{YᵀW_kr}. Stationarity forces Σγ_kD_k=0, so m positive weights give rank at most min(dim x,m−1). Nested supports alone do not force rank one.

## Proof

Differentiate stationarity with respect to γ_k. The derivative of the residual term contributes H D_k; the explicit derivative contributes −YᵀW_kr. Invert H to obtain the formula. Since H is invertible, it preserves the forcing-span dimension. The stationarity equation also gives Σγ_kYᵀW_kr=0, hence Σγ_kD_k=0. Thus three nonzero weights cannot have three independent stationary forcings. To disprove rank one without violating that identity, the exact example below realizes rank two with three strictly nested prefix supports. Its first two sensitivity columns have determinant 6/11, while all three sum to zero. Near collinearity, concentrated residual mass or a common low-dimensional image could create further effective collapse, but each is additional quantitative input.

## Worked example: setup

Take Y with rows (1,0),(0,1),(1,1), b=(2,3,−6), and γ=(1,1,1). Let W₁=diag(1,0,0), W₂=diag(1,1,0), W₃=I₃, giving strictly nested prefix supports.

## Worked example: calculation

Here ΣW_k=diag(3,2,1), H=[[4,1],[1,3]] has determinant11, and Yᵀ(ΣW_k)b=0, so x*=0 is the unique minimizer. The three forcings YᵀW_kb are (2,0),(2,3),(−4,−3), summing to zero. Multiplication by H⁻¹ gives D₁=(6,−2)/11, D₂=(3,10)/11 and D₃=(−9,−8)/11. Their span has rank two because det[D₁ D₂]=6/11≠0. This is a genuine least-squares sensitivity counterexample to rank one, with the obligatory scaling dependence intact.

## Worked example: conclusion

The span formula is a constructive route to a genuine rank theorem and also gives a decisive structural counterexample.

## Limits

The original KuaiRec observations may exhibit low rank; no dataset is reanalyzed here. Dense coverage and strict nesting alone do not establish its cause.

## Extension

Bound the forcing matrix’s tail singular values, propagate them through H⁻¹ using its condition number, and distinguish observed effective dimension from an exact rank conclusion.
