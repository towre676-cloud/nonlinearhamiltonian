## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which contact form encodes a thermodynamic potential, and how does a Legendre transform produce an integrability identity?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

A specified contact form distinguishes the ambient state space from an equilibrium surface. Pulling it back to a potential graph yields the first law; changing independent variables then derives a Maxwell relation by equality of mixed derivatives.

## Definitions

On coordinates (U,S,V,T,p), use θ=dU−T dS+p dV. It is a contact form because θ∧(dθ)² is nowhere zero.

A two-dimensional equilibrium surface is Legendrian if θ restricts to zero. A C² potential U=U(S,V) defines one by T=U_S and p=−U_V.

Assume U_SS≠0 locally, so T can replace S as an independent coordinate. Define the Helmholtz potential F(T,V)=U−TS.

## Result name

Legendre transform and Maxwell identity

## Result statement

On the equilibrium surface, dF=−S dT−p dV. Therefore ∂S/∂V|_T=∂p/∂T|_V. This is a local identity requiring a differentiable potential and an invertible change of variables.

## Proof

Differentiate θ: dθ=−dT∧dS+dp∧dV. Its square is a nonzero multiple of dT∧dS∧dp∧dV; wedging with θ leaves the dU component, proving the contact condition. On the graph of U, substitution gives dU−U_S dS−U_V dV=0. For F, expand d(U−TS)=dU−T dS−S dT and substitute dU=T dS−p dV, obtaining the differential. Its coefficients give S=−F_T and p=−F_V. Equality F_TV=F_VT proves the Maxwell relation. No equation of state is derived until a particular potential is supplied.

## Worked example: setup

Use the new dimensionless model U(S,V)=e^S/V with V>0.

## Worked example: calculation

Then T=e^S/V>0 and p=e^S/V²=T/V. Inverting gives S=log(TV), so F=T−T log(TV). Differentiate: −F_T=log(TV), −F_V=T/V. Both S_V|_T and p_T|_V equal 1/V. Also U_SS=T>0 makes the change of variables locally invertible.

## Worked example: conclusion

The contact form, equilibrium potential and transformed equation are all independently specified and checked.

## Limits

This geometric formalism does not by itself establish thermodynamic stability, extensivity or a physical equation of state. A contact Hamiltonian governor additionally needs a Hamiltonian, sign convention and dissipative interpretation.

## Extension

Add convexity and extensivity hypotheses, determine where the Legendre map loses invertibility, and analyze phase coexistence using convex duality rather than extending a smooth local Maxwell identity across a nonsmooth transition.
