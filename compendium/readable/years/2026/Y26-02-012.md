## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which distinct finite patterns can share every Fourier power value, and what does that imply for a spectral fingerprint?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

Two explicit binary patterns have identical cyclic autocorrelation and therefore identical power spectra. This finite certificate shows why spectral similarity alone does not identify a generator or certify a unique process, even when every frequency is observed.

## Definitions

For a real sequence x on Z/NZ, define X_k=∑_j x_j e^{−2πikj/N} and cyclic autocorrelation A_x(r)=∑_j x_jx_{j+r}.

Use binary indicator sequences of sets A and B. Then A_x(r) counts ordered differences of set elements.

The discrete Wiener–Khinchin identity is |X_k|²=∑_r A_x(r)e^{2πikr/N}, with the displayed sign convention.

## Result name

A complete finite spectral collision

## Result statement

In Z/12Z the sets A={0,1,4,6} and B={0,1,3,7} have identical autocorrelation: value 4 at r=0, value 2 at r=6, and value 1 at every other nonzero r. They are not related by cyclic translation or reflection.

## Proof

Expand |X_k|²=∑_{i,j}x_ix_j e^{−2πik(i−j)/N}, then group pairs by r=j−i to obtain the identity. Enumerating the twelve ordered nonzero differences of each four-element set gives one occurrence at each residue 1,2,3,4,5,7,8,9,10,11 and two at six. Hence all twelve spectral powers agree exactly. Translation preserves the cyclic gap multiset, as does reflection. The gaps of A are {1,2,3,6}, while those of B are {1,2,4,5}; the sets therefore cannot be related by either symmetry. This supplies a nontrivial collision beyond the familiar shift and reversal ambiguities.

## Worked example: setup

Compare the two indicator sequences and any statistic depending only on |X_k|².

## Worked example: calculation

Both have four ones, equal DC power 16 and the same energy four. Their full power vectors, spectral-flatness functions with identical regularization, and any common weighted power summaries coincide. Their ordered local patterns differ, and a time-domain test or suitable higher-order correlation can distinguish them.

## Worked example: conclusion

A spectral diagnostic is a declared feature map whose fibers can contain genuinely different signals.

## Limits

This does not refute every THSC legality constraint or learned classifier; those require the actual sequence language. It does refute unconditional uniqueness from a power-spectrum fingerprint alone.

## Extension

Add bispectral or phase-sensitive observations and determine their remaining equivalence classes. For a supplied dyadic language, prove a separating invariant or enumerate its finite collision classes before interpreting a benchmark score as generator identification.
