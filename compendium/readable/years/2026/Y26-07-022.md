## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which global sections and first cohomology survive a nontrivial rank-one transport around a cycle?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The late-July source connects cellular sheaves, cohomology and compositional consistency. A circle with scalar transports gives a complete exact example in which a topological cycle and a nonzero cohomology class are distinct questions: the coefficient system matters.

## Definitions

Use an oriented cycle with m vertices and m edges over a field k. Put k at every vertex and edge, choose nonzero r_j∈k, and define (δs)_j=s_{j+1}−r_js_j, with indices modulo m. Set H⁰=kerδ, H¹=cokerδ, and h=∏r_j.

## Result name

Holonomy criterion for a local system on the cycle

## Result statement

If h=1, both H⁰ and H¹ have dimension one. If h≠1, both vanish. The underlying graph still has an ordinary cycle in either case.

## Proof

A global section satisfies s_{j+1}=r_js_j. Propagating once around the cycle gives s₀=hs₀. If h≠1 this forces s₀=0 and hence all s_j=0. If h=1 every choice of s₀ propagates consistently, giving a one-dimensional kernel. Since δ is a square m×m matrix, its cokernel and kernel have equal dimensions by rank-nullity. This proves the complete statement without assuming the transports can be globally trivialized. Rescaling vertex coordinates changes individual r_j but leaves their product invariant. In a higher-dimensional cell complex, restriction maps must also satisfy the face-composition identities that make the cochain differential square to zero; arbitrary matrices called transports need not define a sheaf complex. Likewise a cup product needs compatible bilinear maps of coefficient systems, rather than following from linear stalks alone.

## Worked example: setup

Take a three-cycle over Q. First use r=(2,3,1/6), then r=(2,3,1).

## Worked example: calculation

For the first choice, s=(1,2,6) is a nonzero global section and spans H⁰; h=1, so dimH¹=1. For the second, h=6, and the equations s₁=2s₀,s₂=3s₁,s₀=s₂ imply5s₀=0, hence δ is invertible over Q and both groups vanish.

## Worked example: conclusion

Global compatibility is detected by the product around the loop, with a fully specified coefficient convention.

## Limits

This is a rank-one local system on a graph. It does not identify an unretrieved higher-dimensional Morphonic sheaf or justify arbitrary claimed multiplication laws.

## Extension
