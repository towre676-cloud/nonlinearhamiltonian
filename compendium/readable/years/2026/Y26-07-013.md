## Revised question retained in the annual source

What is a concrete singular model, and how does it differ from a complete inverse or a propagation theorem?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The July6 discussion emphasized singularities, Green functions and parametrix thinking. A one-dimensional model proves the central idea exactly. A wave example then distinguishes stationary elliptic singularities from hyperbolic propagation without importing an unspecified microlocal framework.

## Definitions

Let L=−d²/dx²+1 on R and G(x)=e^(−|x|)/2. Choose smooth compactly supported χ equal to one near zero. Distributional derivatives include the jump in G′ at zero.

## Result name

A local inverse with a smooth error

## Result statement

LG=δ₀ and L(χG)=δ₀+r, where r=−χ″G−2χ′G′ is smooth and supported away from zero. Thus convolution by χG is a local parametrix with smoothing remainder.

## Proof

For x≠0, G″=G. Its first derivative jumps from1/2 to−1/2, so distributionally G″=G−δ₀. This proves the fundamental-solution identity. The product rule gives the cutoff formula; since derivatives of χ vanish near zero, the potentially nonsmooth factors are only used where G is smooth, making r smooth. Convolution with the smooth compactly supported remainder smooths compactly supported distributions. This is the exact decomposition into a universal local singularity and a regular error. By contrast the1+1-dimensional wave equation transports initial singularities: with initial displacement f and zero velocity, u(t,x)=[f(x−t)+f(x+t)]/2. A step in f produces characteristic fronts x=±t rather than a singularity fixed at x=0. Distributionally the sign follows by integration by parts on the two half-lines: the smooth terms cancel againstG itself and the boundary contribution is−[G′]₀φ(0)=φ(0) for a test functionφ. This explains why a derivative jump, not a pointwise second derivative atzero, carries the Dirac source in a Green kernel.

## Worked example: setup

Use f=1_(x≥0) for the wave comparison.

## Worked example: calculation

The solution has jumps along two lightlike lines, while G is continuous with a derivative jump only at the source point. The different differential operators dictate the propagation law.

## Worked example: conclusion

A precise parametrix is an operator identity with a controlled remainder, not merely a metaphor for approximation.

## Limits

This elementary example does not construct a curved-space Hadamard parametrix, a wavefront-set calculus or an elliptic-cohomology invariant.

## Extension

For a specified variable-coefficient operator, construct the local singular kernel, prove remainder regularity and derive the correct propagation relation.
