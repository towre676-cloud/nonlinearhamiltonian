## Revised question retained in the annual source

How do nonuniform fixed-size sampling weights change face survival probabilities?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

A product-weight distribution on subsets has an elementary-symmetric normalizer. This yields an exact inclusion law and a dynamic program for expected Euler characteristic, while clarifying that different without-replacement sampling schemes need not share this law.

## Definitions

Choose positive vertex weights w₁,…,w_N and a size m. Give an m-element subset S probability ∏_{i∈S}w_i/e_m(w), where e_m(w)=∑_{|S|=m}∏_{i∈S}w_i.

For a fixed vertex set F of size b, let w_{Fᶜ} denote the remaining weights and w_F=∏_{i∈F}w_i.

This is a specified subset distribution, not an assertion about sequential probability-proportional-to-size draws.

## Result name

Exact weighted inclusion and an O(Nm) normalizer

## Result statement

P(F⊂S)=w_F e_{m−b}(w_{Fᶜ})/e_m(w), for b≤m. Consequently expected Euler characteristic is the alternating sum of these probabilities over the faces. The e_r can be computed by E_{j,r}=E_{j−1,r}+w_jE_{j−1,r−1}.

## Proof

Every subset containing F is uniquely F∪T with T⊂Fᶜ and |T|=m−b. Summing its product weight factors out w_F and leaves the elementary symmetric polynomial in the complement. Dividing by the original normalizer proves the formula. To derive the recurrence, split r-element subsets of the first j vertices into those omitting j and those containing it. Initialize E_{0,0}=1 and E_{0,r}=0 for r>0. Updating r in descending order uses O(m) storage and O(Nm) arithmetic operations. Equal weights give e_m=C(N,m)w^m and recover the uniform hypergeometric probability exactly.

## Worked example: setup

Take weights (1,2,3), m=2, and a graph with only the edge {1,2}.

## Worked example: calculation

The three subset weights are 2,3,6, so e₂=11. Vertex inclusion probabilities are 5/11,8/11,9/11 and sum to two, as required by fixed sample size. The edge survives with probability 2/11. Thus Eχ=2−2/11=20/11, also obtained by averaging χ=1,2,2 against probabilities 2/11,3/11,6/11.

## Worked example: conclusion

The weighted normalizer, all marginals and the topological statistic agree by independent enumeration.

## Limits

Large or highly unequal weights require stable arithmetic or exact rational computation. The formula alone does not choose scientifically justified weights, guarantee unbiased inference about a population or preserve homology.

## Extension

Compute pairwise face inclusion probabilities and exact variance using complement symmetric polynomials. Investigate weight design under a fixed budget to maximize detection of a declared family of cycle supports.
