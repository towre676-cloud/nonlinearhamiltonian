## Revised question retained in the annual source

Reconstruction: How can an exact positive matrix prove exponential stability for a nonnormal generator?

## Status

Classical result or newly stipulated finite model; historical claims explicitly delimited

## Summary

The Coercivity SDK source highlights a Jacobian with zero eigenvalues that cannot receive an unsupported strict certificate. A constructive positive example explains what a valid metric-based certificate looks like and why Euclidean eigenvalue checks alone are insufficient.

## Definitions

For x′=Ax with real A, take a symmetric positive-definite P and define ||x||_P²=xᵀPx. A certificate with rate α>0 is AᵀP+PA≼−2αP.

For a perturbation E define its P-operator norm ||E||_P=||P^{1/2}EP^{−1/2}||₂.

## Result name

A Lyapunov metric certifies coercivity even when the Euclidean symmetric part fails

## Result statement

The matrix inequality implies ||e^{tA}x||_P≤e^{−αt}||x||_P for t≥0. If ||E||_P≤δ<α, then A+E retains rate at least α−δ. Any zero eigenvalue of A prevents such a strict positive-definite certificate.

## Proof

Differentiate xᵀPx to obtain xᵀ(AᵀP+PA)x≤−2αxᵀPx. Multiplying by e^{2αt} and integrating proves the norm bound. For the perturbed generator, 2xᵀPEx is at most 2||x||_P||Ex||_P≤2δ||x||_P², giving the new rate. If Av=0 for a nonzero real or complex vector, the left quadratic form at v is zero while the strict right side is negative, a contradiction. More generally the same eigenvector calculation forces every eigenvalue to have real part at most −α. Conversely, a positive Euclidean symmetric part in some directions need not prevent stability, because a different metric may compensate for transient nonnormal growth.

## Worked example: setup

Let A=[[-1,3],[0,-1]] and P=[[1/2,3/4],[3/4,11/4]].

## Worked example: calculation

Direct rational multiplication gives AᵀP+PA=−I. The leading minor of P is1/2 and its determinant is13/16, so P is positive definite. Also3I−P has leading minor5/2 and determinant1/16, proving P≼3I. Hence−I≼−P/3 and α=1/6 is certified exactly. A perturbation with P-norm at most1/10 leaves rate at least1/15. The Euclidean symmetric part(A+Aᵀ)/2 has eigenvalues1/2 and−5/2: at x=(1,1), the derivative of ||x||² is two, so Euclidean growth really occurs while the P-energy decreases. Changing the second diagonal entry of A to zero would make every strict positive metric certificate impossible.

## Worked example: conclusion

The certificate is an exact matrix inequality with an explicit robustness budget.
