## Summary

The source lists chain complexes, maps, homotopies and contraction equations. We fix their signs and prove a complete reduction in a finite model. This supplies constructive homological content without treating artifact counts as homology calculations.

## Definitions

Use homological degree: d:C_k→C_{k−1}, d²=0. A reduction to H consists of chain maps i:H→C and p:C→H with pi=I and ip−I=dh+hd for a degree +1 map h.

Over Q let C₁=Qe and C₀=Qu⊕Qv, with d(e)=u, and let H₀=Qv, H_k=0 otherwise.

## Result name

A chain contraction proves homology equivalence and survives a controlled elementary perturbation

## Result statement

These contraction equations imply that i and p induce inverse homology isomorphisms. In the displayed complex, choose p(u)=0,p(v)=v, i(v)=v and h(u)=−e,h(v)=0. If d(e) changes to (1+ε)u with ε≠−1, replacing h(u) by −e/(1+ε) preserves every contraction identity.

## Proof

For any cycle z, the equation ipz−z=dhz+hdz=dhz says that ipz and z differ by a boundary. Thus H(i)H(p)=I; the identity pi=I gives the other composite. In the example, on e the map hd is −I, while on u the map dh is −I and on v both sides vanish. Therefore ip−I=dh+hd in every degree. Changing the nonzero coefficient simply rescales the inverse on the contractible summand. The equation at ε=−1 is exceptional: d becomes zero, so both e and u become new homology classes and no reduction to the old one-dimensional H can exist. Formally 1/(1+ε)=Σ_{j≥0}(−ε)^j when |ε|<1; in a nilpotent filtered coefficient ring the same series terminates and gives an exact algebraic inverse. This elementary calculation exhibits the invertibility hypothesis behind homological perturbation rather than asserting a full perturbation lemma without its side conditions.

## Worked example: setup

Set ε=1/2, so the new boundary matrix is the column (3/2,0).

## Worked example: calculation

Take h(u)=−(2/3)e. Then dh(u)=−u and hd(e)=−e exactly, while v remains fixed by ip. The homology is H₀≅Q and H₁=0. At ε=−1 the zero differential gives H₀≅Q² and H₁≅Q, showing the exact jump.

## Worked example: conclusion

The certificate is a list of rational matrix identities and a verified singular parameter, not merely a similarity of output summaries.

## Limits

This proves a particular perturbed contraction, not an unrestricted homological perturbation algorithm. Over integers the coefficient 3/2 is unavailable, and nonunit integer coefficients create torsion.

## Extension

For a general filtered contraction, specify the nilpotence or norm condition that makes the perturbation inverse exist, then derive the transferred differential and verify the full identities with the chosen sign convention.
