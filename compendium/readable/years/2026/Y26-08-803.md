## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which state equivalence preserves every remaining decision, and in which direction does partition refinement move?

## Status

Proved for the stated exact model; historical large-run claims remain unverified

## Summary

The source reports Bellman quotient sequences such as8→6→3→2→1. Counts alone do not show that a quotient is valid. A finite-horizon recursion gives an exact sufficient equivalence, identifies the needed state information and explains when partition counts can legitimately decrease.

## Definitions

At time t, a state s has a finite legal action set A_t(s), immediate costs c_t(s,a), deterministic successor T_t(s,a), and terminal cost h at time L. Infeasible continuations have cost+∞.

Define equivalence backward: terminal states are equivalent when h agrees; at time t, states are equivalent when they have identical action labels and, for each action, identical immediate cost and equivalent successors. Keep the minimum accumulated prefix cost for each class.

## Result name

Continuation-preserving quotient theorem

## Result statement

Equivalent states have equal cost for every legal labeled continuation and equal Bellman value. Replacing them by their class and retaining the cheapest prefix preserves the global optimum. On one fixed state set, a refinement splits classes and cannot lower their number.

## Proof

At the terminal stage, continuation cost is the agreed terminal value. Assuming the assertion at t+1, matching action labels, costs and successor classes gives matching cost for an action followed by any tail. Induction proves the full continuation statement and hence equality of its minimum. Two equivalent prefixes differ only in accumulated cost, so every future suffix adds the same amount; discarding the more expensive prefix cannot remove an optimum. For the partition direction, P refines Q precisely when every P-block lies within a Q-block; the induced map from P-blocks onto Q-blocks is surjective, so |P|≥|Q|. A decreasing numerical sequence may instead reflect backward horizons, elimination of unreachable states or deliberate validated merging; its direction is not evidence of a refinement theorem.

## Worked example: setup

At the final decision use states a,b,c with two actions L,R and cost vectors a=(0,10), b=(10,0), c=(0,10). All successors have zero terminal cost.

## Worked example: calculation

a and c are equivalent and can merge. State b also has minimum value zero but is not continuation-equivalent: restricting the future to actionL yields cost0 froma and10 fromb. Thus equal scalar Bellman values are insufficient for a quotient required to survive changes in the allowed continuation set.

## Worked example: conclusion

The quotient has a constructive correctness invariant rather than only an attractive reduction count.

## Limits

This is a sufficient recursive bisimulation, not a claim that all possible weighted equivalences require identical immediate costs; potential reweighting can yield other exact quotient notions. The source’s actual state graphs were not recovered.
