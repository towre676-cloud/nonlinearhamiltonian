## Revised question retained in the annual source

What are the exact per-cycle decay and input-memory formulas of a damped linear mode?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

Solving a damped oscillator produces an explicit multiplier whose magnitude controls memory depth. The sampled driven mode becomes a weighted convolution; the mathematics quantifies attenuation without establishing the performance of a proposed acoustic device.

## Definitions

Let u″+2γu′+ω₀²u=0 with 0<γ<ω₀, damped frequency ω_d=√(ω₀²−γ²) and Q=ω₀/(2γ).

The damped period is T=2π/ω_d. A complex mode sampled every τ has β=e^{(−γ+iω_d)τ}.

For input d_k and gain α, use z_{k+1}=βz_k+αd_k.

## Result name

Exact cycle attenuation and convolution solution

## Result statement

One damped cycle has |β_T|=exp[−π/(Q√(1−1/(4Q²)))]. The recurrence solves to z_n=β^nz₀+α∑_{j=0}^{n−1}β^{n−1−j}d_j. Attenuation to amplitude 10^{−3} requires n≥log1000/(−log|β|).

## Proof

The characteristic roots of the oscillator are −γ±iω_d, so amplitude decays as e^{−γt}. Substituting the damped period and γ=ω₀/(2Q) proves the exact expression. The high-Q approximation e^{−π/Q} drops only the explicitly displayed frequency correction. Induct on n to prove the driven recurrence: multiplication by β advances all previous powers and the new input appends the power-zero term. If |β|<1 and |d_k|≤D, then |z_n|≤|β|^n|z₀|+|α|D(1−|β|^n)/(1−|β|), giving a quantitative bounded-input estimate. Negative or near-π phase changes interference, but not the absolute decay factor.

## Worked example: setup

Use Q=400 and one-cycle sampling.

## Worked example: calculation

The high-Q factor is e^{−π/400}≈.992177; after five cycles it is about .961491. The 60 dB amplitude threshold, 20log₁₀|z_n/z₀|=−60, gives approximately 879.52 cycles before rounding up. Sampling once per five cycles instead gives about 175.90 sample steps for the same elapsed physical time.

## Worked example: conclusion

Sampling interval and amplitude-versus-power decibels account for otherwise confusing depth numbers. Integer depth is obtained by rounding the logarithmic threshold upward; changing the sampling interval changes that count but not the physical attenuation time.

## Limits

The formula assumes a single linear mode with constant damping and no noise floor. Mode coupling, nonlinearities, thermal fluctuations, transducer losses and fabrication tolerances require additional models and measurements.

## Extension

For a supplied coupled matrix M and sample interval τ, compute A=e^{Mτ}, bound its transient norm including nonnormality, and derive an input-to-output error budget before assigning a reliable memory depth.
