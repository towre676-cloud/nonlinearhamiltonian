## Revised question retained in the annual source

Why can B-roots approach critical points rather than zeros, and which renormalized mass has a finite limit?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The July18 frontier discussion proposes B=αBhat and finiteαμ_k. A real-analytic normal form proves this behavior under precise nondegeneracy assumptions. It also shows that the limiting mass sign is a substantive condition, not automatic from formal scaling.

## Definitions

Let F be real on a real interval and holomorphic nearby. Set E_α(x)=F(x+iα)=A_α(x)−iB_α(x). Let x₀ satisfy F′(x₀)=0,F″(x₀)≠0 and F(x₀)≠0.

## Result name

Critical-point continuation and renormalized mass

## Result statement

For smallα>0 there is a B-root λ(α)=x₀+[F‴(x₀)/(6F″(x₀))]α²+O(α⁴). With μ=A(λ)/B′(λ), αμ→−F(x₀)/F″(x₀).

## Proof

Taylor expansion gives A=F−α²F″/2+O(α⁴) and B=−αF′+α³F‴/6+O(α⁵). Divide B byα and useβ=α². Its derivative in x at(0,x₀) is−F″≠0, so the analytic implicit-function theorem supplies a root analytic inβ. Comparing theβ coefficient gives the shift. Also B′(λ)=−αF″(x₀)+O(α³), while A(λ)=F(x₀)+O(α²), proving the mass limit. It is positive precisely when F and F″ have opposite signs at the critical point. Multiple critical points or F(x₀)=0 require a different scaling. The finite-height qualification is mathematical, not merely computational: an enclosure on a compact x-interval cannot exclude a new critical point or sign change beyond that interval. To pass to all heights, one needs uniform lower bounds or an independently controlled tail that dominates every remainder. Shrinking α also amplifies μ like1/α, so transporting the unrenormalized masses with a fixed absolute error budget is intrinsically ill-conditioned. The finite limit of αμ suggests the correct unknown to enclose, but does not itself prove positivity of the full renormalized kernel.

## Worked example: setup

Take F(x)=1−x².

## Worked example: calculation

Then A=1−x²+α² and B=2αx. The root stays at zero,μ=(1+α²)/(2α), andαμ→1/2, exactly−F(0)/F″(0). The unscaled mass diverges although its renormalization is regular.

## Worked example: conclusion

A proved normal form explains the singular scale without converting a finite-height continuation into a global theorem.

## Limits

The example is newly chosen. Applying it to Ξ requires the stated analytic and nondegeneracy hypotheses at every relevant critical point; an unbounded-height uniform limit is not established.

## Extension

Certify the nondegenerate critical points and uniform Taylor remainders, then determine whether the renormalized family extends continuously or analytically to the full boundary.
