## Summary

The retrieved assistant passage reports an estimated cost per additional wave and also a recurring buffer residue. Those two observations motivate a useful exact model: a linear trend plus a periodic offset. Its slope is identifiable from matched phases even when adjacent differences alternate. The historical numerical slope is not adopted as verified data.

## Definitions

In a newly specified deterministic model let T(n)=a+bn+c_(n modp), where p≥1 is known and Σ_(j=0)^(p−1)c_j=0 fixes the intercept convention.

The phase-matched difference is T(n+p)−T(n). Identifiability means that different admissible parameter sets cannot produce the same specified observations.

## Result name

A period-matched difference separates a linear wave cost from a recurring offset

## Result statement

For every n, T(n+p)−T(n)=bp. Observations T(0),…,T(2p−1) uniquely determine b,a and all c_j. Observing only one full period T(0),…,T(p−1) generally does not identify b.

## Proof

Subtracting two values p apart cancels the same periodic offset and leaves bp. Once b is known, average the first p values: their mean is a+b(p−1)/2 because the c_j sum to zero. This determines a and then c_j=T(j)−a−bj. Conversely, with only the first p values, any proposed slope b′ gives a′ equal to their mean minus b′(p−1)/2 and residuals c′_j=T(j)−a′−b′j whose sum is zero. Thus infinitely many slopes fit one period exactly. The extra matched-phase observations remove that structural ambiguity.

## Worked example: setup

Use p=2 and the explicitly new model T(n)=10+3n+(−1)^n/2.

## Worked example: calculation

The values at n=0,1,2,3 are21/2,25/2,33/2,37/2. Adjacent differences are2,4,2, rather than the true slope three. Every two-step difference is six, yielding b=3; the mean of the first two observations is23/2, so a=23/2−3/2=10. The periodic offsets are1/2 and−1/2. A single adjacent difference would therefore conflate a phase transition with the linear cost.

## Worked example: conclusion

Period-aware contrasts provide an exact mathematical way to distinguish recurrent overhead from a trend.

## Limits

The historical18.953 microseconds-per-wave estimate has no raw timing table or uncertainty model in the recovered bullet. Noise, drift, nonlinear scaling and an incorrect period require additional assumptions; the exact identity does not validate that numerical measurement.

## Extension

Add bounded observation errors and derive interval bounds for b from all matched-phase contrasts; then test whether one common slope and periodic offset can explain every observation within those bounds.
