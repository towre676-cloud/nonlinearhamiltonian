## Definitions

One cycle acting on the P line isPQP. The commutator is[P, Q]=PQ−QP, measured in Euclidean operator norm.

## Result name

Alternating projections have an exact contraction factor and a distinct commutator norm

## Result statement

On imP, the cycle PQP contracts by ρ=c². Its n-th iterate is ρ^nP. The commutator norm is |cs|=|sin2θ|/2. To reach ρ^n≤ε with 0<ε<1 requires n≥ceil(logε/logρ).

## Proof

The matrices are P=[[1,0],[0,0]] and Q=[[c², cs],[cs, s²]]. Multiplication gives PQP=c²P, proving the iterate by induction. Their commutator is[[0, cs],[−cs,0]]. Its transpose times itself is c²s²I, so both singular values are |cs| and the norm formula follows. Since 0<ρ<1, taking logarithms of ρ^n≤ε and dividing by negative logρ reverses the inequality, giving the exact ceiling. As θ→0 the commutator tends to zero but ρ→1 and convergence becomes slow. As θ→π/2 the commutator again tends to zero but ρ→0 and one cycle annihilates the P line. Thus small commutator alone cannot distinguish near-coincident from near-orthogonal subspaces or predict a unique convergence rate.

## Worked example: setup

Take θ=π/3 and ε=1/100.

## Worked example: calculation

ρ=1/4 and||[P, Q]||=√3/4. Three cycles leave 1/64>1/100, while four leave 1/256<1/100, so the exact count is four. Atθ=π/6 the commutator norm is the same, but ρ=3/4 and the required count is seventeen, demonstrating nonidentifiability from the commutator norm alone. There is also a distinction between the norm of PQ and the eigenvalue of the cycle on the first line: ||PQ||=|cosθ|, while PQP restricted to that line is multiplication by cos²θ. A convergence table must specify which operation counts as one step. Squaring the wrong quantity changes its inferred exponent by a factor two.

## Limits

The theorem applies to these orthogonal linear projections. Arbitrary nonlinear closure maps need separate contraction and fixed-point assumptions, and floating residual floors affect practical stopping.
