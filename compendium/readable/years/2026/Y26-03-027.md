## Definitions

Let S_n=Σ_(k=0)^(floor(n/2))binomial(n−k,k). Let Fibonacci numbers satisfy F₀=0,F₁=1,F_(n+1)=F_n+F_(n−1).

Let φ=(1+√5)/2 and ψ=(1−√5)/2. A Pisot number is a real algebraic integer greater than one whose other conjugates have modulus below one.

## Result name

Binomial identity and an integer-nearness bound

## Result statement

S_n=F_(n+1). Moreover φ is Pisot, and φ^n+ψ^n is an integer, so dist(φ^n,Z)≤|ψ|^n for every positive integer n.

## Proof

Pascal’s identity gives binomial(n−k,k)=binomial(n−1−k,k)+binomial(n−1−k,k−1). Sum over k and shift the second index to obtain S_n=S_(n−1)+S_(n−2), with S₀=S₁=1. These are the initial values F₁,F₂, proving the identity by induction. Both φ and ψ solve t²−t−1=0, so L_n=φ^n+ψ^n obeys L_n=L_(n−1)+L_(n−2) with integers L₀=2,L₁=1. Hence every L_n is an integer. Since |ψ|=1/φ<1, subtracting ψ^n gives the stated exponentially small distance bound.
