## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which additional argument upgrades a nonzero Jacobian into a global uniqueness theorem?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The source describes three layers: a nonzero Jacobian, a global graph, and a contraction. These layers are mathematically different. An everywhere nonsingular map may still have many preimages, while a verified contraction on an invariant complete domain gives exactly one fixed point.

## Definitions

Let R:D→R² be continuously differentiable. A nonsingular Jacobian at a zero gives local uniqueness by the inverse function theorem.

Let G map a closed interval J into itself and satisfy |G(s)−G(t)|≤q|s−t| with q<1. Such a scalar reduction is an additional global structure, not a consequence of local invertibility.

## Result name

Local versus global certificates

## Result statement

The Jacobian test proves local uniqueness only. A self-map contraction has a unique fixed point, and its iterates satisfy |s_n−s*|≤q^n|s₁−s₀|/(1−q).

## Proof

The map R(x,y)=(e^x cos y−1,e^x sin y) has Jacobian determinant e^(2x)>0 everywhere, but R(0,2πk)=0 for every integer k. This disproves a general global implication from nonzero determinant alone. For the contraction claim, successive distances shrink by q; summing their geometric tail shows the iterates are Cauchy. Closedness gives a limit in J, continuity gives a fixed point, and two fixed points would have distance at most q times their distance, hence coincide. The tail sum also yields the displayed error bound. Every ingredient is checkable over the full domain rather than at one sampled point.

## Worked example: setup

Use G(s)=(1+s)/4 on J=[0,1].

## Worked example: calculation

G(J)=[1/4,1/2]⊂J and G′=1/4. The unique fixed point solves 4s=1+s, giving s*=1/3. Starting s₀=0 gives s₁=1/4 and the error bound at n is (1/3)4^(−n).

## Worked example: conclusion

A genuine global certificate consists of domain invariance and a uniform contraction bound.
