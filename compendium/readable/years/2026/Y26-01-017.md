## Revised question retained in the annual source

How does Newtonian motion approach a simple turning point, and why do physical time and Jacobi arc length scale differently?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

Energy conservation gives square-root momentum and time laws at a simple turning point. Integrating the Jacobi line element instead gives a three-halves power, exposing a metric degeneration rather than a contradiction in the physical trajectory.

## Definitions

Let m>0, H=p²/(2m)+V(x)=E, with V smooth. A simple turning point x_* satisfies V(x_*)=E and F=V′(x_*)>0.

Write δ=x_*−x>0 on the nearby allowed side. Hamilton’s equations give dx/dt=p/m.

The one-dimensional Jacobi metric is ds_J²=2m(E−V(x))dx², so ds_J=|p| |dx|. It degenerates at E=V.

## Result name

Leading turning-point asymptotics

## Result statement

|p|=√(2mF)δ^{1/2}(1+O(δ)); remaining physical time is √(2m/F)δ^{1/2}(1+O(δ)); remaining Jacobi distance is (2/3)√(2mF)δ^{3/2}(1+O(δ)).

## Proof

Taylor expansion gives E−V(x_*−δ)=Fδ+O(δ²). Taking the square root yields the momentum law. Since |dt|=m|dx|/|p|, integrate m/√(2mF r) from r=0 to δ; the error term is integrable and gives the displayed relative O(δ). For Jacobi distance, integrate √(2mFr) instead, yielding the factor 2/3 and exponent 3/2. Newton’s equation m x″=−V′ stays regular at the turning point. The singular factor appears only when energy is used to replace time by a spatial metric parameter, so it must not be treated as a physical blowup.

## Worked example: setup

Choose m=1, V(x)=x, E=0 and start at x=−a with positive momentum.

## Worked example: calculation

The exact motion near its turn at t=t_* is x(t)=−(t_*−t)²/2 and p(t)=t_*−t. At δ=a, the remaining time is √(2a) and Jacobi length is (2√2/3)a^{3/2}. A momentum tube |p|<ε corresponds exactly to δ<ε²/2 and a remaining time below ε.

## Worked example: conclusion

Different exponents arise from explicitly different quantities and can guide a time-step rule with declared accuracy. Zero instantaneous velocity therefore coexists with a finite remaining travel time.

## Limits

At a higher-order turning point E−V≈cδ^r, the time integral behaves as ∫δ^{−r/2}; for r≥2 it diverges. Multi-dimensional turning hypersurfaces require tangential dynamics and cannot be reduced to this formula without transversality assumptions.

## Extension

Classify simple and degenerate turning points by r, derive error-controlled coordinate charts across the turn, and compare symplectic time stepping with momentum-based adaptive scheduling while retaining a measurable energy error bound.
