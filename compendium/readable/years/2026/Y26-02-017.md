## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which curvature matrix does a gradient-outer-product routine estimate, and when does a spectral projection guarantee descent?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

A scalar squared-loss example separates three commonly conflated matrices exactly. An orthogonal projection has a precise first-order descent identity, while a finite Lanczos approximation needs an independently checked residual before its eigenpairs can be trusted.

## Definitions

For sample losses ℓ_i(θ), the empirical Fisher is F=n⁻¹∑g_ig_iᵀ, g_i=∇ℓ_i. It is positive semidefinite by construction.

For a scalar prediction f_θ and squared loss ℓ=(f_θ−y)²/2, the generalized Gauss–Newton matrix is G=JᵀJ, while the Hessian is G+(f_θ−y)∇²f_θ.

Let P be an orthogonal projector, so P=Pᵀ=P². Consider the update θ⁺=θ−ηP∇L(θ).

## Result name

Curvature distinction and projected descent

## Result statement

F,G and ∇²ℓ can differ even in one dimension. If L has M-Lipschitz gradient, then L(θ⁺)≤L(θ)−η(1−Mη/2)||P∇L||², so 0<η<2/M gives descent unless the projected gradient vanishes.

## Proof

Differentiating the squared loss gives g=(f−y)J and ∇²ℓ=JᵀJ+(f−y)∇²f; its outer product is (f−y)²JᵀJ, proving the distinction. For descent apply the smoothness inequality L(θ+v)≤L+gᵀv+(M/2)||v||² to v=−ηPg. Orthogonality gives gᵀPg=||Pg||² and ||v||²=η²||Pg||², yielding the result. If the projection discards the gradient entirely, the update is zero regardless of large retained eigenvalues. For a unit approximate eigenvector v of a symmetric matrix, residual ||Av−λv|| bounds the distance of λ to the true spectrum; expand v in an orthonormal eigenbasis to prove this immediately.

## Worked example: setup

Take f_θ=θ², y=1 and θ=0.

## Worked example: calculation

Then J=0 and g=0, so empirical Fisher and GGN are both zero. But the Hessian is 2(3θ²−1)=−2 at zero, revealing negative curvature invisible to either PSD matrix. At θ=1, the exact-fit gradient and empirical Fisher vanish, whereas GGN and Hessian both equal four.

## Worked example: conclusion

A metrics harness must label its operator and report what its approximation actually measures.

## Limits

The projected-descent theorem is local smooth optimization, not a guarantee of lower perplexity, faster training or generalization. LoRA parametrization also changes which parameter-space matrix is being studied.

## Extension

Implement matrix-vector products for each declared curvature operator, verify symmetry and residuals, and compare projected updates at matched compute and learning-rate conditions. State the approximation subspace and retained gradient energy.
