## Definitions

Let Q be a gas volume flow in m³/hour, x its carbon-dioxide volume fraction, η∈[0,1] a captured fraction and t elapsed hours. At fixed reference conditions let ρ be carbon-dioxide density in kg/m³.

For time-varying data, captured mass is m=∫Q(t)x(t)η(t)ρ(t)dt. The constant-input formula is m=Qxηρt. Parts per million means x=ppm×10^{−6}.

## Result name

Breath-stream capture arithmetic is a transparent volume-to-mass balance

## Result statement

The model predicts11.664 kg for Q=150, x=0.003,η=0.6,ρ=1.8, t=24. For Q=120, x=0.006 andt=2 it predicts1.5552 kg. Multiplying separate averages of Q, x andη is not generally equal to averaging their product.

## Proof

The flow of carbon-dioxide volume isQx, measured in m³/hour. Multiplying by density gives kg/hour, multiplying by recoveryη gives the captured rate and integrating over hours gives kilograms. The ppm conversion is essential:3000ppm is0.003, not0.3. For varying inputs the integral follows by summing small time intervals and taking the Riemann limit under ordinary bounded regularity. The product-of-averages issue is already visible with two time intervals: if high flow always coincides with low concentration, the covariance is negative and EQx<EQ Ex. A concentration average must be flow-weighted to preserve the total mass, namely x_weighted=∫Qx/∫Q when the denominator is positive. Efficiency and density variations require their own weighting as well.
