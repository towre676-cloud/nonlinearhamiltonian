## Revised question retained in the annual source

Reconstruction: How can a small quotient field, its Frobenius map and a primitive-element claim be certified exactly?

## Status

## Summary

The frontier followup lists finite-field witnesses and warns about generator maps and primitive tests. A complete field of eight elements gives constructive arithmetic and explains the important distinction between irreducible, primitive and embedded.

## Definitions

Let f(T)=T³+T+1 over F₂ and F=F₂[T]/(f), with α the residue of T. Represent every element uniquely by a binary polynomial of degree at most two.

Frobenius is x↦x². An embedding from a quotient F₂[U]/(g) must specify an element β satisfying g(β)=0, and then sends U to β.

## Result name

Finite-field construction needs an irreducible polynomial and an explicit generator image

## Result statement

f is irreducible, F has eight elements, α has multiplicative order seven, and Frobenius cubed is the identity. The three conjugates of α are α,α²,α⁴, and all satisfy f.

## Proof

A cubic over a field is reducible if and only if it has a linear factor. Here f(0)=f(1)=1, proving irreducibility and hence that the quotient is a field. Its nonzero multiplicative group has seven elements; α is neither zero nor one, and Lagrange implies its order is seven because seven is prime. Direct reduction with α³=α+1 also verifies α⁷=1. In any field of characteristic two, squaring respects sums and products, so Frobenius is a homomorphism. It is injective and hence surjective on a finite field. Every nonzero element has seventh power one, so x⁸=x for every element; thus the third iterate is identity. Applying Frobenius to f(α)=0 proves the conjugate equations. The three conjugates are distinct because α has order seven and the exponents1,2,4 differ modulo seven. An embedding is therefore a concrete substitution map, not merely an equality of field sizes.

Use binary representations to enumerate α powers.

## Worked example: calculation

They are1,α,α²,α+1,α²+α,α²+α+1,α²+1,then1. None of the first seven nonzero powers repeats. The map α↦α² is an automorphism; the map α↦1 is not a field homomorphism from this presentation because f(1)=1≠0. A primitive-root test must actually exclude the identity element, especially when the group order is prime.

## Worked example: conclusion

The example provides a full arithmetic table and explicit automorphism certificate.

## Limits

An irreducible polynomial need not be primitive in larger multiplicative groups. Sampled Frobenius checks are not a proof for every element unless a basis-preserving algebra argument supplies the universal step.

## Extension

For a proposed field tower, give each generator image, prove its minimal polynomial and compatibility with the lower field, then verify a basis map and all defining relations exactly.
