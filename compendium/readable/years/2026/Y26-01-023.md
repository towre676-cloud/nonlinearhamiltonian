## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which quantity provably decreases under a stated loss of information, and why does a proposed capacity formula need its own theorem?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

Relative entropy has a precise data-processing inequality under a stochastic coarse-graining. Its proof identifies the exact map and comparison state, providing a rigorous finite replacement for an asserted universal computational c-theorem.

## Definitions

Let p,q be probability vectors on a finite set, with q_i>0 whenever p_i>0. Set D(p||q)=∑p_i log(p_i/q_i), interpreting 0 log0 as zero.

Let T be a column-stochastic matrix: T_ai≥0 and ∑_aT_ai=1. It maps fine distributions to coarse ones.

If Q is a Markov generator with stationary π, then e^{tQ} is such a matrix and e^{tQ}π=π.

## Result name

Data-processing monotonicity

## Result statement

D(Tp||Tq)≤D(p||q). Consequently D(e^{tQ}p||π) is nonincreasing in t. Equality under a coarse-graining requires the likelihood ratio p_i/q_i to be constant within every mixed output fiber with positive weight.

## Proof

For each output a, apply the log-sum inequality to x_i=T_ai p_i and y_i=T_ai q_i: (∑x_i)log((∑x_i)/(∑y_i))≤∑x_i log(x_i/y_i). Sum over a and cancel T_ai in the logarithm wherever it is positive. Column sums one leave D(p||q). The log-sum inequality itself follows from Jensen’s inequality for r log r after normalizing by ∑y_i. The strict-convexity equality condition gives the likelihood-ratio criterion. For a semigroup, apply the result from time s to time t using e^{(t−s)Q}, which fixes π. This proves monotonicity without assuming a rate or identifying it with computational usefulness.

## Worked example: setup

Take p=(1/2,1/4,1/4), q=(1/4,1/4,1/2), and merge the first two states.

## Worked example: calculation

The original entropy is (1/4)log2≈.173287. The coarse vectors are (3/4,1/4) and (1/2,1/2), giving (3/4)log(3/2)+(1/4)log(1/2)≈.130812. Strict loss occurs because the first two fine likelihood ratios are 2 and 1.

## Worked example: conclusion

A concrete coarse-graining and an exact inequality substantiate the monotone.

## Limits

The historical expression log N+log log(1/ε)+log τ_coh−log τ_op is not proved monotone by data processing. It needs a domain, dimensionless ratios, transformation law and a demonstrable sign of its derivative.

## Extension

Specify the proposed RG map on states and resources. Prove either an order-preserving comparison with relative entropy or a direct Lyapunov inequality, and identify equality cases that characterize genuine fixed points rather than arbitrary plateaus.
