## Revised question retained in the annual source

What quotient is defined by the two quadrics, and what changes when all cubic monomials are added?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The July3 target specifies two quadrics and a Hilbert function(1,2,1,0). The quadrics alone do not have that finite Hilbert function. An invertible coordinate change makes both the obstruction and the intended cubic closure completely explicit over every field.

## Definitions

Let k be any field and λ∈k. In k[a,b] set Q=(a²−λ²b²,ab−λb²). Let m=(a,b),u=a−λb and v=b. The coordinate change is invertible with a=u+λv.

## Result name

Exact jet normal form

## Result statement

Q=(u²,uv). Its quotient has Hilbert function1,2,1,1,1,… and is not Artinian. Adding m³ gives I=(u²,uv,v³), with basis1,u,v,v² and Hilbert function(1,2,1,0,…).

## Proof

The second quadric becomes uv. The first becomes u²+2λuv, so subtracting2λ times the second gives u²; this works even in characteristic two. Conversely these formulas recover the original generators. Every quotient monomial is then a power of v or the single monomial u; powers v^d survive in every degree. Adding all cubics kills v³, while u³,u²v,uv² were already killed. The four remaining monomials are independent because the ideal is monomial and none is divisible by u²,uv or v³. Thus the desired finite jet depends essentially on the cubic-closure hypothesis, not on a two-prime experimental agreement. The finite quotient has maximal ideal generated byu,v, with square spanned byv² and cube zero. Its socle containsu andv² and has dimension two. Consequently its symmetric Hilbert function does not make it a Gorenstein algebra: the multiplication pairing from degree one to the top degree is degenerate becauseu annihilates both generators.

## Worked example: calculation

With u=a−2b,v=b, the original quadrics are u²+4uv and uv. Both fields give the same four standard monomials after cubic closure. Without closure, v⁴ survives, contradicting the claimed terminal zero Hilbert coefficient.
