## Summary

The summarized source gives h−D≤χ≤h and a separate necessary inequality D≥h+2V/3−ρ. Their algebra can be checked independently of the unprovided combinatorial theorem. The resulting threshold is useful as a consistency test, not as a proof of the original complexity goal.

## Definitions

Let h,D,χ,V,ρ be real numbers, with D≥0. Here h is a candidate projection exponent, D a concentration loss, χ a retained exponent and ρ a target budget.

Assume, rather than prove, the two displayed source inequalities. The symbols have no universal interpretation until the underlying support and limiting sequence are specified.

## Result name

Feasibility region of the stated bounds

## Result statement

The inequalities force D≥max(0,h−χ,h+2V/3−ρ) and χ≤h. If h→2/3, V→1 and ρ→1, then liminf D≥1/3, provided the assumed asymptotic inequalities have errors tending to zero.

## Proof

Rearrange h−D≤χ into D≥h−χ; combine this with nonnegativity and the second bound. To pass to a limit, write D_n≥h_n+2V_n/3−ρ_n−ε_n with ε_n→0. Taking liminf preserves the inequality because the right side converges. Substituting the declared limits gives 2/3+2/3−1=1/3. This reasoning does not show that such a sequence exists, nor that D is bounded above by 1/3. It also reveals that χ is not fixed by h: a whole interval remains unless another identity or construction is supplied.

## Worked example: setup

Choose h=2/3, V=1 and ρ=1, then test D=1/4 and D=1/3.

## Worked example: calculation

The first choice violates D≥1/3 by 1/12. The second meets the necessary threshold, and χ may still take any value from h−D=1/3 through h=2/3. For example χ=1/2 satisfies the interval.

## Worked example: conclusion

The calculation separates a necessary obstruction from an attainable optimum.
