## Revised question retained in the annual source

What matching estimate follows from regular support, and why does it not by itself establish a matrix-multiplication exponent?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The source links projection entropy, regular hypergraphs and an ambitious exponent-two target. Ordinary matching has an elementary certificate; induced matching and tensor restriction impose extra exclusion conditions. Keeping those objects separate prevents a combinatorial lower bound from becoming an unsupported complexity theorem.

## Definitions

A three-partite three-uniform hypergraph has vertex classes X,Y,Z and edges containing one vertex from each class. A matching has pairwise vertex-disjoint edges.

Let m be the number of edges and Δ the maximum vertex degree. An induced matching additionally forbids every other edge among the chosen vertex sets.

## Result name

Greedy matching certificate and induced obstruction

## Result statement

Every such hypergraph has an ordinary matching of size at least m/(3Δ−2), rounded upward. The largest possible size is at most min(|X|,|Y|,|Z|). Neither bound alone controls induced matching size.

## Proof

Choose an edge and delete every edge sharing one of its vertices. At most 1+3(Δ−1)=3Δ−2 edges disappear in a step, because counting overlaps multiple times only enlarges this upper bound. Repeating until no edge remains gives m≤(3Δ−2)|M|. The upper bound follows because each matching edge consumes one vertex from each class. For the induced limitation, take the complete three-partite hypergraph with each class of size n≥2. It has an ordinary perfect matching of size n. Any two selected edges already create cross edges using vertices from their six endpoints, so no induced matching has size two. Regularity and perfectly balanced projection sizes therefore do not remove the obstruction.

## Worked example: setup

Use n=2 in the complete three-partite hypergraph.

## Worked example: calculation

There are m=8 edges and Δ=4. The greedy bound is ceil(8/10)=1, the ordinary optimum is 2, and the induced optimum is 1. All three vertex projections have size 2, yet their logarithmic sizes do not determine the induced optimum.
