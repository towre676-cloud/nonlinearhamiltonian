## Revised question retained in the annual source

What can a seven-sector symmetry prove about modal coupling, and why can small symmetry breaking change exact rank?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The August17 source reports Fourier–Bessel modal models, cyclic-sector structure and rank changes under residual spread. A finite cyclic matrix example proves the relevant algebraic mechanism. It does not validate the reported65-state or21-port device model.

## Definitions

Letω=e^{2πi/7} andF_{jk}=7^{-1/2}ω^{jk}, j,k=0,…,6. A circulant matrixT has entriesT_{jk}=c_{(k−j) mod7}; its Fourier eigenvalues areΣ_{r=0}^6c_rω^{kr}, with a sign change if the opposite shift convention is used.

## Result name

Fourier diagonalization and rank-opening perturbation

## Result statement

F is unitary, and every circulantT is diagonal in its Fourier basis. Rank equals the number of nonzero Fourier eigenvalues. A matrix can have low exact rank at a symmetric point yet full rank after an arbitrarily small perturbation, while remaining poorly conditioned.

## Proof

The inner product of Fourier columnsa,b is(1/7)Σ_jω^{j(b−a)}. It is one fora=b and zero otherwise by the geometric-series identity, proving unitarity. Substituting a Fourier vector into the cyclic convolution gives its eigenvalue formula. For a rank-opening example letJ be the7×7 all-ones matrix. Its eigenvalues are7 on the constant mode andzero on six orthogonal modes. ThusJ+εI has eigenvalues7+ε andε repeated six times: it is full rank forε≠0, but its2-norm condition number is(7+ε)/ε whenε>0. Full rank therefore does not mean effective high-precision control or reliable inversion. For three independent radial blocks, direct sums add ranks, but an actual coupled radial/angular model needs its full matrix.

## Limits

The reported rank10 and rank21 claims, gains and measured modal spread were not recovered as matrices. They are not established by this illustrative perturbation.

## Extension
