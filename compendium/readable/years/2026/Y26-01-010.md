## Revised question retained in the annual source

What is the correct pointwise equation for log|ω|, and which terms help or hinder growth?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

Decomposing vorticity into magnitude and direction yields an exact identity wherever vorticity is nonzero. The directional-gradient term enters with a negative sign, while the magnitude-log gradient enters positively; retaining both prevents an unsupported coercivity claim.

## Definitions

Let u be a smooth incompressible solution in three dimensions, ω=curl u, q=|ω|>0 and ξ=ω/q on the region under consideration.

Set D_t=∂_t+u·∇, S=(∇u+(∇u)ᵀ)/2 and σ=ξᵀSξ. The vorticity equation is D_tω=(ω·∇)u+νΔω.

All identities here are classical pointwise identities on q>0; division by q is not asserted at a vorticity zero.

## Result name

Exact magnitude and logarithmic equations

## Result statement

D_tq=σq+νΔq−νq|∇ξ|², and D_t log q=σ+νΔlog q+ν|∇log q|²−ν|∇ξ|². The recovered formula with a positive directional term and no magnitude-gradient term is not this identity.

## Proof

Differentiate ξ·ξ=1 to obtain ξ·∂_jξ=0 and ξ·Δξ=−|∇ξ|². Expanding Δ(qξ), then taking its scalar product with ξ, gives ξ·Δω=Δq−q|∇ξ|². Taking ξ· of the vorticity equation yields the magnitude equation because ξᵀ∇u ξ=ξᵀSξ. Divide by q and use Δq/q=Δlog q+|∇log q|² to obtain the logarithmic equation. Separately, multiply the original vorticity equation by ω and integrate on a periodic box or with sufficient decay. Incompressibility and integration by parts give (1/2)d_t∫q²=∫ωᵀSω−ν∫|∇ω|². Neither identity estimates the stretching term without additional information.

## Worked example: setup

Take the periodic shear u=(e^{−νt}sin y,0,0), on the open strip 0<y<π.

## Worked example: calculation

Its vorticity is (0,0,−e^{−νt}cos y); away from cos y=0, ξ is locally constant and σ=0. There q=e^{−νt}|cos y|, so D_t log q=−ν. The right side is ν(−sec²y)+νtan²y=−ν, exactly. Dropping |∇log q|² would give the wrong spatially varying value.

## Worked example: conclusion

A genuine exact solution independently tests the sign and the otherwise easy-to-miss gradient term.

## Limits

Splitting σ into near and far integrals is an algebraic decomposition, not a proof of control. Directional regularity criteria require their full hypotheses; no global three-dimensional regularity theorem is claimed here.

## Extension

Regularize q by q_ε=(|ω|²+ε²)^{1/2}, derive the corresponding identities without division by zero, and determine which proposed directional estimates remain uniform as ε→0.
