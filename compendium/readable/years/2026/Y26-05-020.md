## Revised question retained in the annual source

Reconstruction: Which lattice and generating-polynomial invariants are determined solely by an exponent species?

## Status

Classical result or newly stipulated finite model; historical claims explicitly delimited

## Summary

The source gives D(360) as C₄×C₃×C₂ and links rank coefficients to τ and lattice height to Ω. These statements admit a direct complete proof, with a useful rank symmetry that goes beyond merely counting divisors.

## Definitions

If n=∏p_i^{a_i}, order its positive divisors by divisibility. Represent d=∏p_i^{b_i} by the vector (b_i), 0≤b_i≤a_i. Give it rank ρ(d)=Σb_i.

Let R_n(t)=Σ_{d|n}t^{ρ(d)} and Ω(n)=Σa_i. A chain C_{a_i+1} is the totally ordered set {0,…,a_i}.

## Result name

The divisor lattice is a product of chains with a symmetric rank polynomial

## Result statement

D(n)≅∏C_{a_i+1} as lattices, with meet and join given by coordinate minima and maxima. R_n(t)=∏(1+t+⋯+t^{a_i}); R_n(1)=τ(n), its degree is Ω(n), and t^{Ω(n)}R_n(t^{−1})=R_n(t).

## Proof

Unique factorization makes the divisor-vector correspondence bijective. Divisibility is coordinatewise inequality, so greatest lower and least upper bounds are coordinatewise minimum and maximum, proving the lattice isomorphism. Multiplying the finite geometric polynomials records every independent choice of b_i once, proving the rank formula. Substitution t=1 counts all choices and hence all divisors. The top vector has rank Σa_i, and every saturated chain from bottom to top increments one exponent at a time, giving that height in edges. The involution d↦n/d replaces each b_i by a_i−b_i and reverses rank, proving coefficient symmetry and the reciprocal polynomial identity. Thus not only τ and Ω, but the full rank distribution, is determined by the species.

## Worked example: setup

For n=360 use exponents3,2,1.

## Worked example: calculation

R(t)=(1+t+t²+t³)(1+t+t²)(1+t)=1+3t+5t²+6t³+5t⁴+3t⁵+t⁶. The coefficients sum to24, and the longest chain has six edges. Complementary ranks zero/six, one/five and two/four have matching counts. The rank-three divisors are the six choices of exponent sum three inside the box.

## Worked example: conclusion

The product-of-chains description yields exact combinatorics and a canonical duality.

## Limits

Lattice isomorphism forgets actual prime values. Different integers of the same species have the same divisor lattice but can differ radically in size and arithmetic congruences.

## Extension

Construct a symmetric chain decomposition of this product lattice and derive unimodality of the rank coefficients; formalize the decomposition as a finite combinatorial witness rather than relying on a plotted histogram.
