## Revised question retained in the annual source

Why do Gramian positivity and the Kalman rank conditions agree, even on a finite horizon?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The source lists controllability and observability alongside a reported dynamic benchmark. A theorem for declared matrices proves their relationship without adopting benchmark margins whose underlying model was not recovered.

## Definitions

For realẋ=Ax+Bu andy=Cx, defineW_c(T)=∫₀ᵀe^{At}BBᵀe^{Aᵀt}dt andW_o(T)=∫₀ᵀe^{Aᵀt}CᵀCe^{At}dt forT>0. The controllability matrix is[B,AB,…,A^{n−1}B].

## Result name

Gramian kernel and rank equivalence

## Result statement

W_c(T)>0 exactly when the controllability matrix has rankn. LikewiseW_o(T)>0 exactly when the stacked matrix[C;CA;…;CA^{n−1}] has rankn. These statements do not requireA to be stable.

## Proof

For anyv, vᵀW_cv=∫₀ᵀ||Bᵀe^{Aᵀt}v||²dt. The continuous nonnegative integrand has zero integral exactly when it is identically zero. Differentiating the analytic function at zero then givesBᵀ(Aᵀ)^kv=0 for allk≥0. Cayley–Hamilton reduces these equations tok=0,…,n−1. ThuskerW_c is the orthogonal complement of the Kalman span, proving the criterion. The observability proof repeats withCe^{At}v. Positivity is a qualitative condition; a small smallest eigenvalue indicates costly control or poorly conditioned reconstruction even when the rank is full. For a target statez from zero, the minimum L² input energy iszᵀW_c^{-1}z whenW_c>0, obtained by Hilbert-space least-norm inversion of the state-transfer map. A useful finite-horizon energy identity follows from the state-transfer operatorK:u↦∫₀ᵀe^{A(T−t)}Bu(t)dt. Its adjoint isK*z(t)=Bᵀe^{Aᵀ(T−t)}z, andKK*=W_c(T). WhenW_c(T)>0, u*=K*W_c^{-1}z reachesz and is orthogonal to every input inkerK, proving the least-energy formula rather than only quoting it.

## Worked example: setup

TakeA=diag(−1,−2), B=(1,1)ᵀ and compareC₁=(1,0) withC₂=(1,1).

## Worked example: calculation

[B,AB]=[[1,−1],[1,−2]] has determinant−1, henceW_c(T)>0 for everyT>0. ForC₁ the second state is invisible andW_o is singular. ForC₂, [C₂;C₂A]=[[1,1],[−1,−2]] also has determinant−1, soW_o>0. Infinite-horizonW_c, when desired, is[[1/2,1/3],[1/3,1/4]], with determinant1/72.

## Worked example: conclusion

Rank, conditioning and energy are related but distinct outputs of the same exact framework.
