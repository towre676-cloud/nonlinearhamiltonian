## Revised question retained in the annual source

How can a small integer certificate verify a finite indicator group without pretending to have recovered a crystallographic classification table?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

An exact compatibility kernel and induction image produce a quotient with Smith invariants two and four. The example checks the integer maps, basis choice and unimodular transformation explicitly; external symmetry-indicator data remain a separate provenance requirement.

## Definitions

Let B=[1,−1,0]:Z³→Z enforce compatibility. Its kernel has basis e=(1,1,0), f=(0,0,1).

Let A:Z²→Z³ have columns (2,2,0) and (2,2,4). Then BA=0, so im A⊂ker B.

In the basis (e,f), the presentation of im A is S=[[2,2],[0,4]]. A Smith certificate requires integer unimodular U,V with USV diagonal and each diagonal entry dividing the next.

## Result name

Exact quotient and certificate

## Result statement

ker B/im A≅Z/2⊕Z/4. The certificate U=I, V=[[1,−1],[0,1]] gives USV=diag(2,4), with det U=det V=1.

## Proof

Solving x₁−x₂=0 shows every compatible vector uniquely equals x₁e+x₃f, so the displayed basis is a genuine integer basis, not merely a rational spanning set. The two image columns have coordinates (2,0) and (2,4), giving S. Subtracting the first column from the second is multiplication by V and yields diag(2,4). Since V is invertible over Z, its image lattice is unchanged; U is the identity basis change. The diagonal relations independently identify the quotient. Its order is eight, agreeing with |det S|. A matrix with the same rational rank but different Smith factors would produce different torsion.

## Worked example: setup

Classify the compatible vector (3,3,7).

## Worked example: calculation

Its coordinates are (3,7), so its quotient label is (1 mod2,3 mod4), of order four. Doubling gives (0,2), nonzero; multiplying by four gives zero. Every quotient class has a representative (a,a,b) with a∈{0,1} and b∈{0,1,2,3}, producing eight distinct classes.

## Worked example: conclusion

The certificate supports an exact finite algebraic conclusion with all integer lattices exposed.

## Limits

This newly specified lattice is not an asserted SG2 AII indicator table. Representation bundles alone do not determine compatibility, induced atomic bands, anomaly maps or a physical topological phase; the source itself separates those stages.

## Extension

For a cited representation dataset, construct the integer restriction and induction maps with explicit bases, prove BA=0, compute a kernel basis and Smith witnesses, then compare the resulting quotient with independently sourced classifications.
