## Revised question retained in the annual source

What can a graph’s cycle charges and a probability master equation actually certify about one another?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

Incidence matrices give exact cycle and cohomology dimensions, while a Markov generator governs mass and positivity. An oriented triangle has a persistent topological cycle even when its stochastic dynamics mixes to one stationary distribution, separating topology from dynamical multiplicity.

## Definitions

For a finite connected graph with n vertices and m oriented edges, let B:R^m→R^n be its signed incidence matrix, with −1 at an edge’s tail and +1 at its head.

The cycle space is ker B. A vertex potential f gives an exact edge cochain Bᵀf; a cycle charge is a pairing cᵀω with c∈ker B.

For column probability vectors use p′=Qp, where off-diagonal Q_ij≥0 and each column sums to zero.

## Result name

Cycle dimension, gauge invariance and Markov conservation

## Result statement

dim ker B=m−n+1. Charges are invariant under ω↦ω+Bᵀf. The master equation preserves total probability and nonnegativity; these statements do not require, or imply, nonzero cycle charges.

## Proof

For a connected graph, Bᵀf=0 means f agrees across every edge and is therefore constant. Hence rank B=n−1, and rank-nullity gives the cycle dimension. For c in ker B, cᵀBᵀf=(Bc)ᵀf=0, proving gauge invariance. For probability conservation, 1ᵀp′=1ᵀQp=0. Choose a≥max_j(−Q_jj); then Q+aI is entrywise nonnegative, so e^{tQ}=e^{−at}∑t^k(Q+aI)^k/k! is entrywise nonnegative. Together with column sums one this proves stochasticity. Thus a generator must satisfy concrete matrix conditions before a symbolic drift–diffusion–jump decomposition becomes a valid probability model.

## Worked example: setup

Take a triangle with consistent edge orientation and Q having diagonal −2 and every off-diagonal entry 1.

## Worked example: calculation

The cycle vector c=(1,1,1) spans ker B. For ω=(a,b,c₃) its loop charge is a+b+c₃, unchanged by adding a potential gradient. Meanwhile Q=J−3I has stationary distribution (1/3,1/3,1/3), and p_t=π+e^{−3t}(p₀−π). Every initial probability converges to that same state even though the graph’s first cohomology is one-dimensional.

## Worked example: conclusion

A topological cycle is neither a second stationary distribution nor a proof of a proposed cognitive fixed point.

## Limits
