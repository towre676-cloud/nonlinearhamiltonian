## Revised question retained in the annual source

Is the claim local to one tangent vector, or uniform over the entire finite-amplitude observation fiber?

## Status

Classical mathematics with newly authored derivation or explicitly defined worked model

## Summary

The WIND work moved from tangent nullspaces to finite-amplitude quotients. This is mathematically important: a hidden direction can be invisible to the target derivative at one point while changing the target at second order. A sharp Taylor bound gives both a counterexample and a quantitative certificate.

## Definitions

Let H be linear and let N have orthonormal columns spanning kerH. The radius-r hidden fiber through x is{x+Nz:||z||≤r}.

Let T be twice continuously differentiable on that fiber’s containing segment domain, with Hessian operator norm at most M.

## Result name

A zero directional derivative does not make a nonlinear target constant on a hidden fiber

## Result statement

|T(x+Nz)−T(x)|≤||DT(x)N||·||z||+(M/2)||z||². If DT(x)N=0, only the quadratic bound remains. Exact constancy on a connected affine fiber follows if DT(y)N=0 at every point y of that fiber.

## Proof

Set f(t)=T(x+tNz). The fundamental theorem of calculus gives f(1)−f(0)=∫₀¹DT(x+tNz)Nz dt. Applying it again to the derivative gives the second-order remainder∫₀¹(1−t)(Nz)ᵀD²T(x+tNz)(Nz)dt. Its magnitude is at most M||z||²/2 because N is an isometry. If the derivative vanishes everywhere along every fiber segment, the first integral is zero, proving constancy; vanishing only at its initial point does not do so.

## Worked example: setup

Use the recovered target form T(x)=x₁+x₃², observation Hx=(x₁,x₂), and base point x=0.

## Worked example: calculation

The hidden unit direction is e₃ and DT(0)e₃=0. Yet on the radius-one hidden fiber T(ze₃)=z² ranges from zero to one. The Hessian has norm two, so the bound|ΔT|≤r² is exact and attained. This is a complete finite-amplitude calculation, independent of any atmospheric dataset. At a point with x₃≠0, the same target already has a nonzero first-order hidden response2x₃.

## Worked example: conclusion

The quotient test distinguishes tangent blindness, controlled finite error and genuine target constancy.

## Limits

A local Jacobian test cannot certify a global nonlinear quotient. Conversely a nonzero target variation need not imply that every practical estimator has the same error; that requires a probability model or a minimax loss.

## Extension

Compute target extrema on the actual constrained fiber using interval Hessian bounds or certified optimization, and report their width as the finite-amplitude ambiguity rather than only a tangent rank.
