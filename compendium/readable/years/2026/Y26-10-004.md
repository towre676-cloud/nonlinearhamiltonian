## Revised question retained in the annual source

Can the nonlinear integer image be parametrized in both directions, including signs and zero?

## Status

Classical mathematics or a newly chosen finite model developed with proof; historical claims delimited

## Summary

The recovered power-chart examples admit global elementary proofs. Prime valuations establish surjectivity of the parametrization; substitution alone would show only that a proposed family gives some solutions. Keeping the reverse direction is essential for exact enumeration or inverse lookup.

## Definitions

For a nonzero integer a and a prime p, v_p(a) is the exponent of p in |a|.

All variables in the following statements are integers; powers and signs are interpreted in Z, not merely over positive reals.

## Result name

Complete cusp parametrizations

## Result statement

x²=y³ if and only if (x,y)=(t³,t²) for a unique t∈Z. Also 2x²=3y³ if and only if (x,y)=(18t³,6t²) for a unique t∈Z.

## Proof

The zero case forces both coordinates to vanish. Otherwise y>0. In x²=y³, the equality 2v_p(x)=3v_p(y) implies v_p(x)=3a_p and v_p(y)=2a_p for nonnegative integers a_p. Thus y=t² and |x|=|t|³; choose the sign of t to match x. This t is unique because integer cubing is injective. For 2x²=3y³, at p=2 we have 1+2a=3b, hence b=2r+1 and a=3r+1 with r≥0. At p=3, 2a=1+3b gives b=2s+1 and a=3s+2. Every other prime again has valuations (3a_p,2a_p). Extracting these factors yields |x|=18u³ and y=6u², where u≥1 is an integer. The sign of x determines t=±u. Conversely, direct substitution gives 2·18²t⁶=3·6³t⁶, so the parametrization is exact.

## Worked example: setup

Invert a concrete point and impose an arithmetic restriction.

## Worked example: calculation

For (x,y)=(−144,24), x/18=−8 gives t=−2, and 6t²=24 checks the other coordinate. Restricting y≤150 gives |t|≤5, hence exactly eleven solutions including zero. A further congruence can be tested on the single parameter; for example x≡0 mod 5 is equivalent to t≡0 mod 5 because 18 is invertible modulo five.

## Worked example: conclusion

The nonlinear image becomes an explicitly invertible one-parameter integer population.

## Limits

Adding a nonzero constant generally destroys this cusp parametrization: y²=x³+k is an elliptic curve when k≠0 and its discriminant is nonzero. No global Mordell completeness theorem follows from the singular k=0 case.

## Extension

For a x^m=b y^n, separate primes dividing ab and use gcd(m,n) to solve each valuation congruence; assemble finitely many coefficient classes and prove their images disjoint before supplying rank/unrank algorithms.
