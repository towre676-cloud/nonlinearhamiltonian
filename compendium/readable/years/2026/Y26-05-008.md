## Summary

The source reports a finite gauge/parton preflight and explicitly limits its physical interpretation. We construct a graph theorem that can actually be proved: the gauge quotient is a binary cycle space, and even a three-cycle has a flux-dependent adjacency spectrum.

## Definitions

On a connected finite graph with v vertices and e edges, assign s_e∈{±1}. A vertex gauge g_v∈{±1} changes s_{uv} to g_us_{uv}g_v.

The flux around a cycle is the product of its edge signs. A signed adjacency matrix has entries s_{uv} on edges and zero otherwise; gauge change is conjugation by diag(g).

## Result name

Z₂ gauge classes are counted by cycle rank and retain a flux detectable in the spectrum

## Result statement

There are 2^{e−v+1} gauge classes, classified by the signs on the e−v+1 non-tree edges after fixing every edge of a spanning tree to +1. Flux and adjacency spectrum are gauge invariant.

## Proof

Choose a root and spanning tree. Set its gauge to +1 and propagate the remaining vertex gauges so that each tree edge becomes positive. This is possible uniquely once the root gauge is chosen, because a tree has a unique path to every vertex. A simultaneous sign flip of every gauge acts trivially, so the effective gauge group has 2^{v−1} elements and acts freely on edge assignments of a connected graph. The residual non-tree signs are independent, giving 2^{e−v+1} orbits. Each is the product around the corresponding fundamental cycle, because every tree sign is now positive. Every other cycle flux is a product of fundamental-cycle fluxes. Matrix conjugation preserves eigenvalues, and each gauge factor in a cycle product appears twice, proving both invariances.

## Worked example: setup

Use the triangle graph with all three edge signs initially positive, then flip one edge.

## Worked example: calculation

The all-positive adjacency has characteristic polynomial t³−3t−2=(t−2)(t+1)². Flux −1 gives t³−3t+2=(t+2)(t−1)². Their spectra are (2,−1,−1) and (−2,1,1). Both configurations have the same absolute edge weights and unsigned graph, but their flux and signed spectra differ. There are exactly two gauge classes because e−v+1=1.

## Limits

The theorem is combinatorial. It does not prove a quantum phase, a numerical gap claimed by a parton approximation, or a thermodynamic-limit distinction.
