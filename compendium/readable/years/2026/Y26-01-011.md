## Revised question retained in the annual source

What does the polynomial M(a,b) detect, and can pairwise alignment force a bound on spatial gradients?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

The Gram determinant gives an exact, division-free angular defect and a sharp transverse-projection identity. A smooth parallel vector field then disproves any unconditional inference from zero misalignment to zero gradient energy.

## Definitions

For vectors a,b in a real Euclidean space set M(a,b)=|a|²|b|²−(a·b)².

For a≠0 define P_{a⊥}b=b−(a·b)a/|a|². When both vectors are nonzero, M/(|a|²|b|²)=sin²θ.

For a smooth vector field ω=qξ with |ξ|=1, distinguish amplitude variation ∇q from directional variation ∇ξ.

## Result name

Projection identity and a coercivity obstruction

## Result statement

M≥0 and M(a,b)=|a|²|P_{a⊥}b|². Moreover |∇ω|²=|∇q|²+q²|∇ξ|² where q>0. Consequently pairwise M(ω(x),ω(y)) can vanish identically while ∫|∇ω|² is strictly positive.

## Proof

Expand the squared orthogonal projection: |b−ca|²=|b|²−(a·b)²/|a|² at c=(a·b)/|a|². This proves nonnegativity and the identity; equivalently M is the determinant of the two-vector Gram matrix. For the field identity differentiate ω=qξ. Each ∂_jω=(∂_jq)ξ+q∂_jξ, and the cross term vanishes because ξ·∂_jξ=0. Sum over j. If all field vectors are scalar multiples of one fixed vector, every two-vector Gram determinant is zero. Their scalar coefficients may nonetheless oscillate arbitrarily quickly, so the amplitude-gradient energy is unconstrained by M alone.

## Worked example: setup

On the three-torus [0,2π]³ take ω(x,y,z)=(0,0,sin y).

## Worked example: calculation

All pairs of its values are collinear, including values of opposite sign, hence M is zero for every pair. But |∇ω|²=cos²y, so ∫|∇ω|²=(2π)²∫₀^{2π}cos²y dy=4π³. Replacing sin y by sin(ny) keeps M identically zero while multiplying the gradient energy by n².

## Worked example: conclusion

The counterexample isolates exactly the missing amplitude information in a proposed misalignment-to-dissipation implication. Unlike a normalized angular ratio, the polynomial M remains defined at zero vectors without any convention.

## Limits

Angular control can still be powerful when combined with amplitude, nonlocal-kernel and regularity hypotheses. The counterexample rejects an unrestricted coercive inequality, rather than every geometric vorticity criterion.

## Extension

Prove a conditional estimate that separately controls ∇q and q∇ξ. For nonlocal direction energies, specify the integration kernel, the treatment of zeros and the amplitude weights, then test the inequality against high-frequency parallel fields.
