## Definitions

On the disk |z|<1 use ds=2|dz|/(1−|z|²). For real t≥0, distance from zero to r∈[0,1) is 2artanh r.

For M∈SL₂(R), its projective action on the upper half-plane is hyperbolic when |trM|>2, parabolic when |trM|=2 with nontrivial projective action, and elliptic when |trM|<2.

## Result name

The Poincaré disk distance and hyperbolic generator gate have exact formulas

## Result statement

The disk distance is d(z,w)=2artanh|(z−w)/(1−conj(w)z)|. A hyperbolic M has translation length ℓ=2arcosh(|trM|/2); parabolic or elliptic generators do not have this positive hyperbolic translation length.

## Proof

The disk automorphism φ_w(z)=(z−w)/(1−conj(w)z) sends w to zero. Direct differentiation gives |φ_w′(z)|/(1−|φ_w(z)|²)=1/(1−|z|²), so it preserves the line element. Integrating the radial line gives ∫₀^r2du/(1−u²)=2artanh r. A radial line is shortest: for any path, radial variation gives the same integral as a lower bound on its length, with equality for the radial path. For the matrix formula, hyperbolicity gives real eigenvalues of absolute values λ,λ^{−1} with λ>1, so |trM|=λ+λ^{−1}=2cosh(logλ). The conjugated dilation calculation yields length 2logλ and hence the formula. At trace magnitude two the length degenerates to zero and a cusp orbit has different growth geometry.

## Worked example: setup

Take M=[[2,1],[1,1]], and compare a disk radial displacement r=3/5.

## Worked example: calculation

M has trace three, so ℓ=2arcosh(3/2)=4logφ. The radial disk distance is log((1+3/5)/(1−3/5))=log4. Using the half-sized line element would instead give log2 and change every inferred distance-based exponent by a factor two.

## Limits

The trace criterion classifies individual generators. It does not prove discreteness, free generation, a critical-exponent limit or equality between that exponent and a separately computed fractal dimension.
