## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which normal subgroup defines the action quotient, and which class of quotient groups determines the residual?

## Status

Classical mathematics with newly authored derivation or explicitly defined worked model

## Summary

The functional-subgroup calculus separated internal commutators from external action and proposed a residual pullback formula. Its finite-group mechanism can be proved cleanly when the actor quotient and the admissible quotient class are specified.

## Definitions

Let L be normal in a finite group G, so C=C_G(L) is normal and G/C is the effective conjugation actor. Let F be a formation: a class closed under quotients and finite subdirect products.

Define G^F as the intersection of all normal N with G/N∈F. Let B_F(L) be the inverse image of(G/C)^F in G.

## Result name

An action barrier is a residual pullback only under stated quotient hypotheses

## Result statement

B_F(L)=C_G(L)G^F. For abelian quotients this becomes C_G(L)G′. Since abelian groups are nilpotent and nilpotent groups solvable, the corresponding barriers satisfy B_solv≤B_nil≤B_ab.

## Proof

Finiteness and subdirect closure ensure G/G^F∈F: it embeds as a subdirect product of the finitely many defining quotients. The quotient G/(CG^F) is a quotient of that group and belongs to F, so the actor residual is contained in CG^F/C. Conversely, if R/C is the actor residual, then G/R∈F; hence G^F≤R and CG^F≤R. These containments prove equality. For the abelian formation, the smallest normal subgroup killing all commutators is G′. Enlarging the allowable quotient class can only decrease the intersection residual, proving the inclusion chain.

## Worked example: setup

Use G=S₃ and first L=G, then L=A₃.

## Worked example: calculation

For L=G the centralizer is its trivial center, so the abelian barrier is A₃. For L=A₃, its centralizer is A₃ and the effective actor is C₂, already abelian; the barrier is again A₃, now because it is precisely the action kernel. The same resulting subgroup has two different structural explanations. Internal L′ is trivial in the second case although the external actor is nontrivial.

## Worked example: conclusion

The barrier formula becomes a theorem about a defined actor quotient rather than a label assigned to a test battery.

## Limits

Normality of L is needed for this quotient formulation. An arbitrary centralizer need not be normal. A list of observed behaviors does not prove a formation property or a universal residual identity.

## Extension

For a declared family of normal sections, compute their effective actors and residual pullbacks functorially, verifying which inclusions remain valid under restriction and quotient.
