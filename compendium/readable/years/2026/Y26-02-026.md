## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which minimization problem yields Δx_i=−ηg_i/(1+ηλ_i), and when is the denominator a genuine stability certificate?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

A proximal quadratic model gives the source’s update exactly in a Hessian eigenbasis. Positive curvature damps stiff directions, while a vanishing or negative model denominator invalidates the convex minimization interpretation.

## Definitions

Let η>0 and H be a real symmetric matrix with eigenvalues λ_i. Consider q(δ)=gᵀδ+(1/2)δᵀHδ+||δ||²/(2η).

The model is strictly convex precisely when H+η⁻¹I is positive definite, equivalently 1+ηλ_i>0 for every i.

In an orthonormal eigenbasis write g_i for the gradient components and δ_i for the update components.

## Result name

Exact resolvent update and spectral damping

## Result statement

Under strict convexity, q has unique minimizer δ=−η(I+ηH)⁻¹g, hence δ_i=−ηg_i/(1+ηλ_i). If H≥0, ||δ||≤η||g||, and the minimum value is −(η/2)gᵀ(I+ηH)⁻¹g.

## Proof

Differentiate q to obtain g+(H+η⁻¹I)δ=0. Positive definiteness makes this system invertible and its stationary point the unique global minimum. Diagonalize H orthogonally to obtain each scalar formula. Completing the square with A=H+η⁻¹I gives q(δ)=.5(δ+A⁻¹g)ᵀA(δ+A⁻¹g)−.5gᵀA⁻¹g, yielding the minimum. For λ_i≥0, every damping factor 1/(1+ηλ_i) lies in (0,1], proving the norm bound. These statements concern the quadratic model; actual objective decrease requires an upper-model or remainder condition.

## Worked example: setup

Take H=diag(0,9), g=(1,1) and η=1.

## Worked example: calculation

The minimizer is δ=(−1,−.1), compared with the explicit gradient step (−1,−1). Its model minimum is −.55. The stiff second direction is reduced by a factor of ten. If instead λ₁=−1 at η=1, the first quadratic coefficient vanishes and a nonzero g₁ makes the model unbounded below, so the apparent infinite update signals a failed assumption.

## Worked example: conclusion

The formula is a checked optimization theorem rather than an automatic mode-elimination principle.

## Limits

A projected feasible-set flow requires a declared tangent cone, projection and existence theory. The local Hessian model does not establish a global conical normal form or universal learning behavior.

## Extension

Add a closed convex constraint set and derive the proximal variational inequality. Prove contraction for a strongly monotone operator, then quantify how approximate eigenspaces and inexact solves affect the objective model and its stability margin.
