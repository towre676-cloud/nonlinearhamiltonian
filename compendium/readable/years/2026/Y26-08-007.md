## Revised question retained in the annual source

When is the worst ratio vᵀPv/vᵀIv finite, and why can determinant-based information design choose a different panel?

## Status

## Summary

The August19 source emphasizes disagreement between dynamical dangerP and informationI. Its useful mathematics is a classical generalized Rayleigh quotient with a crucial singular case. An exact example shows that maximizing information volume and controlling worst danger are different objectives.

## Definitions

LetP,I be symmetric positive semidefinite matrices withI≠0. Defineχ*=sup{vᵀPv:vᵀIv=1}. Information-null directions arekerI. The quotient space bykerI can be identified with its Euclidean orthogonal complement.

## Result name

Finite quotient criterion and minimax contrast

## Result statement

χ* is finite exactly whenkerI⊆kerP. Under this condition, restrict to(kerI)⊥ andχ* is the largest eigenvalue ofI^{-1/2}PI^{-1/2} there. Without it,χ*=∞.

## Proof

Ifn∈kerI butn∉kerP, positive semidefiniteness givesnᵀPn>0. Choosew withwᵀIw=1. Then(w+tn)ᵀI(w+tn)=1, while the P-value is a quadratic int with positive leading coefficient, hence unbounded. Conversely ifkerI⊆kerP, all P cross terms involving that kernel vanish. On its orthogonal complementI is positive definite, so the change of variablez=I^{1/2}v converts the constraint to||z||=1. The ordinary spectral theorem proves the maximum formula and attainment. This is a generalized eigenvalue result, not evidence of a novel physical law. Eigenvector transport or curvature claims need a specified parameter-dependent bundle and connection; a ratio alone does not supply them. The singular criterion also explains why adding ε times the identity to the information matrix can conceal an essential obstruction: a direction inkerI withpositive P becomes finite but its ratio scales as1/ε. Its apparent generalized eigenvalue is then a regularization artifact rather than evidence that the original worst ratio was bounded.
