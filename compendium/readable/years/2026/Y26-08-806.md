## Revised question retained in the annual source

Why can weighted partitions of an eight-vertex boundary admit128 representatives, and what completion criterion is preserved?

## Status

Proved for the stated exact model; historical large-run claims remain unverified

## Summary

The source reports connectivity compression from4,140 to128. These are not arbitrary empirical counts:4,140 is the eighth Bell number and128 is the exact rank of a standard connectivity matrix over F₂. A greedy weighted row basis preserves the best cost for every connected completion.

## Definitions

Let Π_k be all set partitions of a k-element boundary. Write p∨q for the least common coarsening and define M_pq=1 when p∨q has one block, zero otherwise, over F₂.

A cut is a two-coloring with vertex one fixed to colorzero; there are2^(k−1) cuts. Define C_p,c=1 when every block ofp is monochromatic under cutc. A weighted family assigns a finite costw(p) to each available partition.

## Result name

Exact connectivity rank and weighted representatives

## Result statement

M=CCᵀ over F₂ and rank M=2^(k−1). Processing available C-rows in nondecreasing cost and retaining only linearly independent rows yields at most2^(k−1) representatives, preserving min{w(p):p∨q is one block} for every completionq.

## Proof

A cut constant on blocks of bothp andq is constant on each block ofp∨q. If that join hasb blocks, exactly2^(b−1) cuts qualify, odd exactly when b=1. Thus CCᵀ=M over F₂. The one-block partition has C-row equal to the trivial-cut unit vector. Each nontrivial bipartition has row equal to that unit vector plus its own cut unit vector. These rows span all2^(k−1) cut columns, so C has full column rank. Consequently Cᵀ is onto and C is injective, giving rank(CCᵀ)=2^(k−1). For weighted preservation, a discarded row is an F₂-sum of retained rows of no greater weight. If it is compatible withq, pairing that sum withC_q gives one; at least one retained row is also compatible and no more expensive. The reverse inequality follows because representatives are a subfamily. This proves equality of optimal completion costs.

## Limits
