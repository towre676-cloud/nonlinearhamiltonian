Reconstruction: How do a Puiseux parametrization, intersection multiplicity and strict transform describe the same cusp?

## Summary

The source supplies y²=x³, x=t²,y=t³, intersection multiplicity three with y=0 and a blowup chart. We prove these finite algebraic facts and state exactly what they resolve. This is stronger than attaching a geometric label to a numerical singular point.

## Definitions

Work over Q or any characteristic-zero field k. The plane cusp is C:y²−x³=0. Its completed local intersection with the line y=0 is the k-vector-space length of k[[x,y]]/(y²−x³,y).

In the blowup chart y=tx, factor the total transform and remove the exceptional factor x² to define the strict transform.

## Result name

The cusp parametrization, local intersection and blowup are compatible exact calculations

## Result statement

The parametrization x=s²,y=s³ lies on the cusp and normalizes its function field. The intersection multiplicity with y=0 at the origin is three. In the chart y=tx the strict transform is t²−x=0, which is smooth and parametrized by x=t²,y=t³.

## Proof

Substitution makes y²−x³=s⁶−s⁶=0. Away from x=0 the rational inverse is s=y/x, so the parametrization is birational; s is integral over the cusp ring because s²=x. The ring k[s] is integrally closed, giving the normalization in this affine example. Setting y=0 in the completed local quotient leaves k[[x]]/(x³), with basis1,x,x², proving length three. Under y=tx the defining equation becomes t²x²−x³=x²(t²−x). The strict transform therefore has gradient(−1,2t), whose x-component is always−1, proving smoothness including over the exceptional point x=t=0. Its intersection with the exceptional divisor x=0 is t²=0, so the strict transform is smooth but tangent to that divisor; resolving the curve singularity and obtaining a normal-crossings total transform are different goals.

## Worked example: calculation

The lowest homogeneous term is y², a double tangent line y=0. Nevertheless the branch is not two distinct lines: the parametrization has one parameter and the local intersection length with that tangent is three. The blowup replaces the singular cusp by a smooth parabola x=t² in its chart.

## Limits

The finite chart computation is not a general Puiseux theorem, a global resolution algorithm or a claim that every Newton-polygon edge determines a unique branch without coefficient conditions.
