## Revised question retained in the annual source

What does the proposed log-alignment slope estimate actually compute, and when is it undefined?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered analysis proposes a covariance-over-variance slope, but its data-processing summary later reports no analyzed records. The mathematical estimator can be proved and checked without inventing a dataset or treating reported cohort correlations as established results.

## Definitions

For paired observations a_i,E_i≥0 choose ε>0, set x_i=log(a_i+ε), y_i=log(E_i+ε), and fit y=b+sx by unweighted least squares.

Let Sxx=Σ(x_i−x̄)² and Sxy=Σ(x_i−x̄)(y_i−ȳ). The fit requires at least one nonconstant x value, so Sxx>0.

## Result name

Least-squares slope and a degeneracy certificate

## Result statement

When Sxx>0, the unique fitted slope is s=Sxy/Sxx and intercept b=ȳ−sx̄. If Sxx=0, the slope is unidentifiable. The formula estimates an association in the transformed sample, not a causal gain law.

## Proof

Differentiate Σ(y_i−b−sx_i)² in b to obtain b=ȳ−sx̄. Substituting leaves Σ[(y_i−ȳ)−s(x_i−x̄)]², a quadratic with leading coefficient Sxx. Its unique minimum is Sxy/Sxx when that coefficient is positive. If Sxx=0 then every x_i=x̄ and changing s can be compensated by b, so the objective cannot identify a slope. The positive offset ε changes the model: log(a+ε) is not log a plus a constant, and comparisons across offsets need a sensitivity analysis.

## Worked example: setup

Use x=(0,1,2) and y=(1,3,5), corresponding to positive transformed inputs.

## Worked example: calculation

x̄=1, ȳ=3, Sxx=2 and Sxy=4, so s=2,b=1 and the residual sum is zero. With x=(1,1,1), Sxx=0 and no slope is identified, regardless of variation in y.

## Worked example: conclusion

The estimator and its failure condition are exact; no historical numerical result is fabricated.
