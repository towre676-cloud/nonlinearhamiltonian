## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which exact physical-assignment model supports P(16,4)=43,680→4⁴=256 and a341-node route diagram bound?

## Status

Proved for the stated exact model; historical large-run claims remain unverified

## Summary

The source reports a dramatic channel-erasure quotient. A simple capacity model reproduces the counts and, more importantly, states the condition that makes erasure exact. The count itself does not validate a cost model or a route implementation.

## Definitions

Partition sixteen distinct channels into four banks of four lanes. Assign four labeled active objects injectively to physical channels. Erase the lane identity and retain only each object’s bank label.

Assume costs, all legal future choices and all required constraints depend only on bank labels and occupancy, and that no forgotten channel identity is pinned or externally observed. Each bank has capacity four.

## Result name

Exact quotient and diagram size

## Result statement

There are16·15·14·13=43,680 physical assignments, and every one of4⁴=256 bank tuples is feasible. A tuple with bank occupancies n₁,…,n₄ has fiber size∏_bP(4,n_b). Under the stated invariance, minimizing cost over physical assignments is equivalent to minimizing over bank tuples. The full four-ary depth-four prefix tree has341 nodes.

## Proof

Injection counting gives the physical total. Any four-object bank tuple uses at most four objects in a bank, so its occupants can be assigned distinct lanes; this proves surjectivity of erasure. In each bank, its n_b labeled objects have P(4,n_b) injections into four lanes, and independent banks multiply the choices. If feasibility and cost are constant on each fiber, every physical feasible assignment projects to a quotient state of the same cost and every quotient state has a physical lift; the two infima therefore coincide. If future constraints distinguish two lanes, these hypotheses fail and the quotient must retain that information. The prefix count is1+4+4²+4³+4⁴=(4⁵−1)/3=341. Merging identical suffixes can reduce this diagram, but the tree bound already holds without claiming such merging.
