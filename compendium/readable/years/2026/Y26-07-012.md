## Definitions

An ideal hyperbolic tetrahedron is determined by a shape z in the upper half-plane. Its volume is the Bloch–Wigner function D(z)=ImLi₂(z)+arg(1−z)log|z|. A triangulated complete manifold requires edge gluing and cusp-completeness equations, not just a root of one polynomial.

## Result name

Finite-cover volume ratios and certified-shape scope

## Result statement

If two finite-volume hyperbolic manifolds have a common finite cover with degrees m,n, then Vol(M)/Vol(N)=n/m. A collection of certified positively oriented complete shapes permits summing their tetrahedral volumes; an approximate shape list alone does not prove the topology or completeness.

## Proof

A covering is a local isometry, so the volume form pulls back and integration over the cover multiplies volume by its degree. Thus mVol(M)=nVol(N), proving the rational ratio. Restricting the ratio further to powers of two and three would require corresponding index or arithmetic information. For shapes, gluing equations identify the tetrahedral metrics along faces and ensure the angle/product condition around edges; cusp completeness is the additional condition preventing incomplete peripheral geometry. These hypotheses, plus a validated solution and the correct triangulated manifold, are what permit the volume sum. Numerical agreement at38 digits does not establish them or an equality involving a Mahler-measure integral.
