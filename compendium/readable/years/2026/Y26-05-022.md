## Summary

The May source proposes a specific logarithmic asymptotic. We give its leading-scale derivation conditional on explicitly stated Laplace asymptotics obtainable from the prime number theorem. The proof does not certify the more delicate finite-saddle prefactor.

## Definitions

Use the weighted species partition function F(s) from the preceding entry and t=logN. Assume as s↓0 that F(s)~C/[slog(1/s)], −F′(s)~C/[s²log(1/s)] and F″(s)~2C/[s³log(1/s)], with C=π²/6.

Under the tilted law, the independent d_j are geometric with P_s(d_j=r)=(1−e^{−sw_j})e^{−srw_j}. Their total weight W has mean −F′(s) and variance F″(s).

## Result name

The species exponential scale follows from a tilted-measure argument under explicit prime asymptotics

## Result statement

Under these hypotheses, logS(t)~2√(2Ct/logt), hence logS(logN)~(2π/√3)√(logN/loglogN). The assumptions follow from the counting asymptotic #{j:w_j≤x}~x/logx plus standard positive-kernel Laplace estimates; those estimates are identified as an analytic input here.

## Proof

The Euler-product upper bound is F(s)+st. Choose s asymptotic to √(2C/[tlogt]); then log(1/s)~(1/2)logt and each term F(s),st is asymptotic to √(2Ct/logt), proving the limsup. For the lower bound fix ε∈(0,1/2) and choose s so that E_sW=(1−ε)t. Such a value exists for large t by continuity and the assumed mean asymptotic. Then Var_sW/t² is asymptotic to 2(1−ε)/(st), which tends to zero. Chebyshev implies P_s((1−2ε)t≤W≤t)→1. Since S(t)=e^{F(s)}E_s[e^{sW}1_{W≤t}], it is at least e^{F(s)+s(1−2ε)t} times that probability. Taking logarithms, dividing by √(2Ct/logt), and using the corresponding s asymptotic gives a lower coefficient tending to two as ε↓0. Together with the upper bound this proves the leading constant. It deliberately uses a broad concentration window rather than an unproved density approximation at one exact weight.

## Limits

The verifier checks the finite partition model and symbolic scale constants; it does not prove the prime number theorem or a local central limit theorem. No finite-N accuracy or prefactor is inferred.
