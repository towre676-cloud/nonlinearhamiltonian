## Result statement

## Proof

First x must be odd: if x were even, y²+2≡0 mod4 would require a square congruent to2, impossible. Then y is odd. The ring R is norm-Euclidean: for any element of its fraction field, round the real coefficients in a+b√−2 to nearest integers; the remainder has norm at most1/4+2/4=3/4<1. Thus R is a UFD. Factor x³=(y+√−2)(y−√−2). A common divisor has norm dividing both the odd number x³ and N(2√−2)=8, so it is a unit. Unique factorization forces each coprime factor to be a cube, with a possible unit absorbed because both units are cubes. Write y+√−2=(a+b√−2)³. Comparing the √−2 coefficient gives1=b(3a²−2b²). Hence b=1 or−1. For b=1, a²=1; for b=−1,3a²=1 has no integer solution. The real coefficient is y=a³−6ab²=−5a, so y=±5. Norms give x=N(a+b√−2)=3. Direct substitution proves both points occur.
