## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which dimensionless selector can consistently combine a plate’s rigidity, pressure, thickness and cost ratio?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

Checking units exposes a missing length factor in the recovered selector. A newly specified dimensionless variant then yields an exact thickness exponent, demonstrating how dimensional analysis changes a proposed threshold before any engineering interpretation.

## Definitions

Let E have pressure units, t and L length units, ν and R dimensionless, and D=Et³/[12(1−ν²)] the plate rigidity, with units force times length.

Let pressure p have units force/length². Let ΔC/S be a dimensionless cost ratio, with ΔC=2πcρtL², density ρ and cost per mass c.

Assume E,p,t,ρ,c,S,R>0 and |ν|<1. These are algebraic model parameters; no structural safety claim is made.

## Result name

A dimensionally valid selector and its threshold

## Result statement

The historical (ΔC/S)D/(RpL⁴) has units inverse length. A possible newly defined dimensionless selector is Φ=(ΔC/S)Dt/(RpL⁴). Under the stated mass model, Φ=A t⁵/L², where A=2πcρE/[12(1−ν²)SRp]. The threshold Φ=1 is L_crit=√A·t^{5/2}.

## Proof

Rigidity has units (force/length²)·length³=force·length. Pressure times L⁴ has units force·length², leaving inverse length in the old ratio. Multiplying by t removes that defect. Substitute ΔC=2πcρtL² and D=Et³/[12(1−ν²)]; the numerator contributes t·t³·t=t⁵, while L²/L⁴=L^{−2}. Taking the positive square root solves Φ=1. Since cρ/S has units length^{−3}, A has units length^{−3} and √A t^{5/2} indeed has length units. The correction is not unique: a different scientifically motivated selector could use another length, and must be derived from its governing equations.

## Worked example: setup

Use the numerical parameters recovered in the source: E=9.57×10⁹ Pa, ρ=2000, ν=.25, p=10⁵, c=2.173×10⁴, S=7.22×10⁷, R=.8, t=.30 m.

## Worked example: calculation

The stated algebra gives √A≈200.54 in compatible units. Its uncorrected t² expression is about 18.05, whereas the new dimensionless selector gives L_crit≈9.886 m. Doubling thickness multiplies this new threshold by 2^{5/2}≈5.657, rather than four.

## Worked example: conclusion

Every exponent and unit follows from a declared formula; changing the model is recorded explicitly.

## Limits

The new selector is a mathematical construction, not a validated buckling, deflection or economic criterion. Shell geometry, boundary conditions and material behavior can change the governing scaling entirely.

## Extension

Start from a stated plate or shell boundary-value problem, derive its nondimensional parameters by rescaling the PDE, and only then couple a cost functional. Determine whether the resulting stability threshold has a rigorously bounded asymptotic error.
