## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which positivity statement follows from positive Clark-type masses, and which residual positivity does not?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The July Clark campaign reports a finite symmetric atomic measure and a residual positivity atlas. The atomic transform has a complete elementary proof. It must be separated from a difference of transforms and from the unbounded-height theorem required by the broader RH programme.

## Definitions

Let λ_k>0 be distinct and μ₀,μ_k>0. For Imz>0 define M(z)=−μ₀/z+Σ_{k=1}^N2μ_kz/(λ_k²−z²). A Herglotz function is analytic in the upper half-plane with positive imaginary part.

## Result name

Finite atomic positivity and real interlacing

## Result statement

M is Herglotz. Its real poles are0,±λ_k, with negative residues of magnitudesμ₀,μ_k. Between successive poles M is strictly increasing and has exactly one zero.

## Proof

Rewrite each symmetric summand asμ_k/(λ_k−z)+μ_k/(−λ_k−z). Every atom μ/(t−z) has imaginary partμ Imz/|t−z|²>0. Their finite sum proves analyticity and positivity. On the real line away from poles, M′(x)=Σ_t μ_t/(t−x)²>0. Immediately right of a pole the function tends−∞ and immediately left of the next it tends+∞, so monotonicity gives exactly one zero. But the class of Herglotz functions is not closed under subtraction: M₁=−1/z and M₂=−2/z are both Herglotz, while M₁−M₂=1/z has negative imaginary part. Therefore positivity of a reported residual requires its exact definition and a separate certificate. Near a real poleλ with massμ>0, the summandμ/(λ−z) tends to plus infinity from the left and minus infinity from the right. Between adjacent poles the derivative is a sum of positive terms, so continuity, strict monotonicity and these endpoint limits give exactly one zero. This is a finite rational-function theorem independent of the number137.
