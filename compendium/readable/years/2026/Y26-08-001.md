## Revised question retained in the annual source

When is singular E legitimate, and what goes wrong if it is repaired by adding εI?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The August2 source correctly distinguishes differential–algebraic structure from malformed data. Singular E does not imply a singular matrix pencil. A two-dimensional exact example shows how regularization can create a large unstable eigenvalue absent from the original constrained dynamics.

## Definitions

Consider Eẋ=Ax. A square pencil A−λE is regular when det(A−λE) is not identically zero. Its finite generalized eigenvalues are the roots of that determinant; an infinite eigenvalue describes the missing polynomial degree relative to the matrix size.

## Result name

Constraint preservation versus a spurious fast mode

## Result statement

For E=diag(1,0), A=diag(−1,1), the pencil is regular, the only finite eigenvalue is−1, and consistent trajectories have x₂=0. Replacing E by E+εI, ε>0, introduces an unstable eigenvalue1/ε.

## Proof

The original equations are ẋ₁=−x₁ and0=x₂. Thus all consistent solutions are x(t)=(ae^{−t},0). The determinant det(A−λE)=−1−λ is not the zero polynomial; its degree one in a two-dimensional pencil accounts for one infinite eigenvalue. The algebraic component is a constraint, not a hidden differential state that may be evolved arbitrarily. After regularization, the equations become(1+ε)ẋ₁=−x₁ andεẋ₂=x₂, with eigenvalues−1/(1+ε) and1/ε. Any nonzero error in the constrained coordinate grows as e^{t/ε}. A pseudoinverse replacement also changes the equations unless its constraint consistency is separately enforced. Descriptor-native reduction instead retains the finite deflating subspace and algebraic constraints; an index or minimal-index assertion additionally requires the appropriate canonical data.

## Worked example: setup

Use ε=1/100 and consistent initial value(2,0), then perturb the second coordinate by10⁻⁶.

## Worked example: calculation

The original constrained solution is(2e^{−t},0). The regularized second component becomes10⁻⁶e^{100t}; at t=1/10 it is approximately0.0220. Its rapid growth is an artifact of the modified problem, even though E+εI is invertible.

## Worked example: conclusion

Regularity, constraints and numerical conditioning are separate mathematical properties.

## Limits
