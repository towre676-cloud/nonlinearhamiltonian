## Revised question retained in the annual source

What can a six-jet prove about polynomial relations P(h,F,F′)=0 of a fixed total degree?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The user explicitly requested a bounded algebraic-differential theorem class rather than vague closure. Such a search can be formulated rigorously as a coefficient matrix. It has a strong limitation: a finite jet can exclude a finite ansatz but cannot certify an infinite identity merely because a null vector exists.

## Definitions

Fix a degree bound d and list monomials h^iF^j(F′)^k with i+j+k≤d. For a known jet F through h^N, expand each monomial to every order reliably determined by that jet.

A candidate polynomial P is a linear combination of these monomials with unknown constant coefficients. A relation through order h^r gives a homogeneous coefficient system Mc=0.

## Result name

Finite-jet exclusion and noncertification

## Result statement

If M has full column rank, no nonzero P in the ansatz can vanish to the tested order. If M has a nontrivial kernel, its vectors give only candidate truncated relations. Every polynomial jet automatically satisfies some higher-degree algebraic relation through its truncation order.

## Proof

Coefficient extraction is linear in the unknown coefficients of P, so a true identity must produce a vector in ker M. Full column rank therefore gives a logically valid exclusion for exact input. Conversely, let p_N(h) be any truncated polynomial and let F(h)=p_N(h)+ch^(N+1)+… . The degree-one-in-F polynomial F−p_N(h) vanishes through order N for every value of c, yet it is not identically zero when c≠0. Thus finite agreement alone cannot establish a relation. Differentiation also lowers available order: an unknown a_(N+1) enters F′ at h^N, so rows through h^N cannot generally be assembled from an N-jet.

## Worked example: setup

For the exact germ F(h)=h+h² test P=F−h−h²; compare G(h)=h+h²+h⁷.

## Worked example: calculation

Both give P=O(h⁷), so their six-jets are indistinguishable. For F, P is identically zero; for G, P=h⁷. At degree d=2 there are ten monomials in three variables, while reliable coefficients through h⁵ give at most six rows, so full column rank is impossible from those rows alone.

## Worked example: conclusion

The dimension count prevents claiming a degree-two exclusion from insufficient jet information.

## Limits

The full source ADE matrix was not recovered and the coefficients are rounded. No reported uncomputed rank is silently promoted to an exact result.

## Extension

Recover additional certified derivatives, choose a relation normalization, and prove either exact full rank over the coefficient field or an independent ODE-based identity for a surviving candidate.
