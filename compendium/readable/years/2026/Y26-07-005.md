## Definitions

Let p,q be distinct primes and M an integer matrix. A polynomial membership witness for f∈(g₁,…,g_s) over Q is an exact identity Df=Σh_ig_i with D a nonzero integer and h_i integral polynomials. Minimal nilpotence exponent also requires a valid nonmembership witness for the preceding power.

## Result name

One-sided rank lifting and a two-prime counterexample

## Result statement

rank_Fp(M modp)≤rank_QM. A nonzero modular minor proves the corresponding rational minor is nonzero. Vanishing modulo p and q alone does not prove rational vanishing.

## Proof

A modular nonzero determinant is the reduction of the same integer determinant, so that integer is nonzero; this proves the rank inequality. For the converse failure take the1×1 matrix[pq]. It is zero over both finite fields and nonzero over Q. Polynomial membership has an additional denominator issue: I=(px) in Z[x] becomes(x) over Q but becomes the zero ideal modulo p. Thus a failed modular membership test may also be a bad-prime phenomenon unless a monic Gröbner basis or a flat integral model controls specialization. An exact identity Df=Σh_ig_i avoids ambiguity and lifts directly after dividing by D. To claim the smallest exponent e, also prove f^(e−1) has nonzero normal form under a certified rational Gröbner basis.
