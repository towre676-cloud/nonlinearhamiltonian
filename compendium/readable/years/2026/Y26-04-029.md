## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which frequency is actually determined by the recovered silver-mean substitution matrix?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The source writes M=[[2,1],[1,0]], λ=1+√2 and λ⁻¹=√2−1 as a frequency. The reciprocal is a useful slope parameter, but the normalized two-letter frequencies follow from a normalized eigenvector and are different numbers.

## Definitions

Let the substitution be a↦aab and b↦a, with columns of M counting letters in the images. Then M=[[2,1],[1,0]].

The substitution is primitive because M² has all positive entries. Let v be its positive right eigenvector, normalized so its entries sum to one.

## Result name

Exact substitution frequencies

## Result statement

The eigenvalues are 1±√2. A positive right eigenvector is (λ,1), so f_a=λ/(λ+1)=1/√2 and f_b=1/(λ+1)=1−1/√2. Their ratio f_b/f_a is λ⁻¹=√2−1.

## Proof

The characteristic polynomial is t²−2t−1. For λ=1+√2, M(λ,1)=(2λ+1,λ)=(λ²,λ)=λ(λ,1). Normalize by λ+1 to obtain frequencies. To justify the limit, decompose the count vector M^ne_a along the two eigenvectors. The second contribution is proportional to (1−√2)^n, whose magnitude relative to λ^n tends to zero because |1−√2|<λ. Dividing by total word length therefore yields the positive normalized eigenvector. The reciprocal λ⁻¹ equals the ratio of frequencies, not either individual frequency.
