## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which local arithmetic pattern can be proved directly for an elliptic curve with an explicit complex multiplication symmetry?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

For y²=x³−x, pairing x and −x proves vanishing Frobenius trace at every good prime congruent to three modulo four. The same curve has a visible order-four automorphism, providing a concrete link between symmetry and point-count behavior without assuming a global distribution theorem.

## Definitions

Let E:y²=x³−x over Q, with good reduction at every odd prime p. Write N_p=#E(F_p) and a_p=p+1−N_p.

For the quadratic character χ_p with χ_p(0)=0, N_p=p+1+∑_{x∈F_p}χ_p(x³−x).

The local L-factor is (1−a_pT+pT²)⁻¹; the bound |a_p|≤2√p is Hasse’s theorem, used as cited background.

## Result name

Trace cancellation and an explicit CM endomorphism

## Result statement

If p≡3 mod4, then a_p=0. Over Q(i), the automorphism ι(x,y)=(−x,iy) preserves E and satisfies ι²=[−1], so its endomorphism ring contains a copy of Z[i].

## Proof

Since f(−x)=−f(x), χ_p(f(−x))=χ_p(−1)χ_p(f(x)). For p≡3 mod4 the multiplier is −1. Pairing x and −x cancels the entire sum, with x=0 contributing zero, proving N_p=p+1. For the automorphism, (iy)²=−(x³−x)=(−x)³−(−x), so it preserves the curve equation. Applying it twice gives (x,−y), the group inverse. Thus ι satisfies the polynomial X²+1, defining the stated Gaussian-integer action. This establishes a specific algebraic symmetry, not a classification of all endomorphisms of an arbitrary Jacobian.

## Worked example: setup

Count at p=5 and p=7.

## Worked example: calculation

At p=5 the affine counts over x=0,1,2,3,4 are 1,1,2,2,1, so N₅=8 and a₅=−2. At p=7 cancellation gives N₇=8 and a₇=0, also verifiable by enumeration. The local polynomials are 1+2T+5T² and 1+7T². Both satisfy the Hasse trace bound.

## Worked example: conclusion

An exact symmetry, finite count and local factor are mutually consistent and reproducible.

## Limits

A handful of primes does not establish Sato–Tate equidistribution or a full endomorphism-ring computation. CM and non-CM curves have different distribution laws; local traces alone do not prove a universal semicircle model or BSD.

## Extension

For a specified curve, certify its endomorphism algebra and compare point-count distributions with the correct CM or non-CM theorem. Analyze functorial transformations only after defining their local factors and global hypotheses.
