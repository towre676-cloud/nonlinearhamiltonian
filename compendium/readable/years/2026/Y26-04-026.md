## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which mathematical properties survive rearranging critical-line zeta samples into an integer spiral?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The user’s construction places Z_k=ζ(1/2+ikT) in an Ulam-style spiral. Rearranging samples is a visualization map, not an arithmetic correspondence between spiral coordinates and primes. A precise symmetry and index-preservation statement remain available.

## Definitions

Fix real T>0 and define s_k=1/2+ikT for integer k. Let p be any bijection from a finite index set to grid cells, such as a square spiral.

The displayed field is Z(p(k))=ζ(s_k). Modulus and phase are separate observables; phase is undefined at an exact zero unless a convention is supplied.

## Result name

Conjugation and layout invariance

## Result statement

ζ(1/2−ikT)=conj ζ(1/2+ikT). Any bijective layout preserves the multiset of sampled values, but it does not preserve adjacency-based statistics under arbitrary reordering.

## Proof

For Re s>1, conjugate the absolutely convergent Dirichlet series Σn^(−s) term by term, obtaining ζ(conj s)=conjζ(s). Both sides extend meromorphically, so the identity holds away from the pole at one by uniqueness of analytic continuation. The multiset statement follows because a bijection merely permutes indices. For adjacency, a sequence (0,0,1,1) has one signaled jump in its linear ordering, while the permutation (0,1,0,1) has three, even though the values and histogram agree. Thus apparent spatial structures may reflect the chosen embedding and should be tested against index-preserving alternatives or permutation baselines.

## Worked example: setup

Compare k=1 and k=−1, and compare two four-cell layouts of the same samples.

## Worked example: calculation

The pair has equal modulus and opposite phase modulo 2π whenever neither value is zero. A grid permutation leaves their individual values unchanged but can move them from adjacent cells to opposite corners.

## Worked example: conclusion

The sampling construction is precisely defined without treating its visual pattern as a theorem about integer arithmetic.

## Limits

No values or zero locations are fabricated, and no claim is made that a finite spiral detects or proves the Riemann hypothesis.

## Extension
