## Definitions

## Result name

The recovered Habiro matrix has a free direction that must be removed before Gaussian integration

## Result statement

A is positive semidefinite with eigenvalues0,1,3 and kernelK. The unrestricted Gaussian withε=0 diverges. The integral restricted toV is2π/√3, and√(ε/(2π))I_ε tends to that value asε↓0.

## Proof

Expand Q=(x₁+x₂)²+(x₁+x₃)². It vanishes exactly when x₂=x₃=−x₁, proving the kernel and positivity. The vectors(0,1,−1) and(2,1,1) are orthogonal eigenvectors with eigenvalues one and three. Together with the kernel vector they give an orthogonal eigenbasis. In orthonormal coordinates(u₀, u₁, u₃), Q=u₁²+3u₃² and the u₀ integral is ∫_R1du₀=∞. Removing that direction gives two independent Gaussian integrals,√(2π)√(2π/3)=2π/√3. Regularization shifts the eigenvalues to ε,1+ε,3+ε, so I_ε=(2π)^{3/2}/√[ε(1+ε)(3+ε)]. Multiplying by√(ε/(2π)) isolates the finite transverse factor and proves the limit. A determinant-zero matrix cannot be used in the nondegenerate three-dimensional stationary-phase formula without this reduction.
