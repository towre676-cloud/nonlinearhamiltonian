## Revised question retained in the annual source

What does a spectral-flatness score prove, and what empirical evidence would support a tiny false-acceptance probability?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

The score has an exact range and equality condition but does not identify a probability law. A zero-failure binomial confidence calculation quantifies the enormous gap between a claimed security exponent and a modest benchmark.

## Definitions

For positive powers P₁,…,P_N define SF=(∏P_i)^{1/N}/[(1/N)∑P_i]. If some powers vanish, use the zero-product convention or explicitly specify regularization.

A false-acceptance probability p is defined only after a distribution of invalid inputs and a fixed acceptance rule are supplied.

For M independent trials under that distribution, the probability of observing zero false accepts is (1−p)^M.

## Result name

Flatness bound and an exact zero-failure upper limit

## Result statement

0<SF≤1, with equality exactly when all powers agree. After zero false accepts in M independent trials, the one-sided confidence upper limit at confidence 1−α is p_U=1−α^{1/M}; for small p_U it is approximately −logα/M.

## Proof

Apply the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality to the positive powers for the score bound; strict concavity of log gives equality only at equal powers. It says nothing about the frequency of adversarial inputs satisfying a chosen band. To obtain the statistical bound, solve (1−p_U)^M=α. For any p>p_U, observing zero events is less likely than α under the binomial experiment. Expanding exp(logα/M) yields the approximation. A worst-case adversarial security theorem is stronger still and cannot be inferred from independent random trials alone.

## Worked example: calculation

P=(1,1,1,1) gives SF=1, while P=(1,1,1,16) gives SF=2/(19/4)=8/19≈.421053. With M=10⁶ and α=.05, zero false accepts gives p_U≈2.99573×10⁻⁶. To make this statistical upper limit below 2^{−128} would require roughly (−log .05)2^{128} independent trials, before considering any adversarial mismatch.

## Worked example: conclusion

A score range and an observed failure rate are different mathematical objects with different supporting evidence.

## Limits

The historical PQ-128/192/256 labels and flatness bands do not prove their stated FAR exponents. A Hann window changes the statistic’s distribution and correlations; sample independence and the null model need verification.

## Extension

Specify the invalid-input family, derive a calibrated null distribution for the exact windowed statistic, and prove a worst-case reduction if a cryptographic security claim is intended. Report finite-sample confidence separately from that theorem.
