## Revised question retained in the annual source

How can a locally solvable problem have one global obstruction, and which exact complex computes it?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

Two overlapping arcs covering a circle give a four-dimensional finite Čech calculation. The resulting one-dimensional obstruction explains why exactness and gluing require the actual restriction maps rather than broad categorical labels.

## Definitions

Cover S¹ by two contractible open arcs U,V whose intersection has two contractible components A,B. Use locally constant real-valued sections.

The Čech groups are C⁰=R² and C¹=R². With fixed component order, d(u,v)=(v−u,v−u). There are no nonzero higher Čech groups for this cover.

Each component and each arc is acyclic for the constant sheaf, so this Leray-cover calculation computes its sheaf cohomology.

## Result name

One global constant and one gluing obstruction

## Result statement

H⁰(S¹;R)=ker d≅R and H¹(S¹;R)=coker d≅R. A pair of overlap discrepancies (a,b) is the restriction difference of two local constants exactly when a=b; its obstruction is b−a.

## Proof

The differential matrix is [[−1,1],[−1,1]], with rank one. Its kernel is the diagonal line u=v and its image is the diagonal line in the overlap data. Rank-nullity gives both cohomology dimensions. The linear map (a,b)↦b−a has precisely that image as kernel, producing an explicit quotient isomorphism. Thus local constants can adjust both overlaps only by the same amount; their difference is globally invariant. This is the simplest finite model of a connecting obstruction in a long exact sequence. Exactness means image equals kernel at a particular map, not that all cohomology vanishes.

## Worked example: setup

Take overlap discrepancies (2,5).

## Worked example: calculation

No constants u,v solve v−u=2 and v−u=5 simultaneously. The obstruction is three. Changing both discrepancies by the same gauge shift leaves that difference unchanged. By contrast (2,2) is solved by u=0,v=2. A directed acyclic diamond graph similarly has an undirected cycle and H¹ dimension one: absence of directed loops does not force ordinary graph cohomology to vanish.

## Worked example: conclusion

Both the positive gluing case and the obstruction have complete matrix certificates.

## Limits

The coefficient sheaf, cover and restriction maps are essential. Neither a DAG label nor reversibility determines ordinary cohomology, and this example does not prove microlocal or derived exactness claims for unprovided categories.

## Extension

For a specified sheaf or local system, build its actual restriction matrices, compute cocycles and coboundaries and identify connecting homomorphisms. Compare persistent cohomology only after declaring how those complexes vary with scale.
