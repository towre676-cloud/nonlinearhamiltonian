## Revised question retained in the annual source

What additional theorem would turn an encoding of discrete logarithms into a provably efficient algorithm?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

Baby-step giant-step supplies a complete finite benchmark for the distinction between representation and inversion. Its correctness is elementary, whereas a low-dimensional encoding alone gives neither a short-vector promise nor an efficient way to identify the encoded answer.

## Definitions

Let G=⟨g⟩ be cyclic of known order n and h=g^x, with 0≤x<n. The task is to recover x.

An encoding must specify a computable map, representation lengths, construction cost and a decoder. An injective map only establishes uniqueness of the represented answer.

Set m=⌈√n⌉. Write x=im+j with 0≤j<m and 0≤i<m.

## Result name

A complete collision algorithm and its cost

## Result statement

Store g^j for 0≤j<m. Among h(g^{−m})^i for 0≤i<m, one matches a stored value, yielding x≡im+j mod n. The algorithm uses O(√n) group operations and storage, with logarithmic-size exponent arithmetic.

## Proof

Euclidean division gives the required i,j because x<n≤m². At that pair, h(g^{−m})^i=g^{x−im}=g^j. Conversely, any matching pair implies g^{x−im−j}=1, so x≡im+j modulo the known group order. Producing consecutive baby and giant steps uses one multiplication per step once g^{−m} is known. Binary exponentiation computes that initial element in O(log n) operations. This separates a verified complexity statement from fitting a power law on small primes. Shoup’s generic-group lower bound is relevant background: in the prime-order generic model, constant success requires order √n operations. It restricts algorithms using only generic group operations, not algorithms exploiting a particular group’s arithmetic or encodings.

## Worked example: setup

Solve 5^x≡8 mod 23, where 5 has order 22.

## Worked example: calculation

With m=5 the baby steps are 1,5,2,10,4. Since 5⁵=20 and 20⁻¹=15 mod 23, the first giant step after h is 8·15≡5. This matches j=1 at i=1, giving x=6. Direct multiplication gives 5⁶≡8 mod 23.

## Worked example: conclusion

Every representation, collision and final answer can be certified exactly; the running-time claim follows from a counted procedure.

## Limits

The recovered scaling exponent 0.3–0.4, proposed log-log dimension and hoped-for polynomial algorithm are historical claims or conjectures, not consequences of the lattice embedding. Generic lower bounds do not prove impossibility for every structured group.

## Extension

Specify the proposed lattice basis and its bit length, prove a gap separating the target vector from competitors, and bound construction plus decoding uniformly in log n. A successful result must identify exactly which nongeneric arithmetic feature evades generic collision bounds.
