## Revised question retained in the annual source

How can the proposed quartic and quintic spherical norms be computed exactly and checked independently of Monte Carlo?

## Summary

The pair counts in the source are classical Wick counts. A Gaussian-to-sphere decomposition converts them into exact spherical moments, supplying both a formula and a small rational calibration.

## Definitions

Let θ be uniform on S^(n−1), and let a₁,…,a_(2d)∈R^n. A pairing partitions 2d labels into d unordered pairs.

The number of pairings is (2d−1)!!, obtained by pairing label one with any of the other 2d−1 labels and recurring.

## Result name

Exact spherical Wick formula

## Result statement

E∏_(j=1)^(2d)(a_j·θ) equals the sum over pairings of ∏_(i,j paired)(a_i·a_j), divided by n(n+2)…(n+2d−2). For T=∏_(i=1)^d(m_i·θ), repeat every m_i twice to compute E T².

## Proof

Let Z be a standard n-dimensional Gaussian. Write Z=Rθ, with R independent of θ. Gaussian moment expansion pairs the linear forms and gives the numerator. The radial moment is E R^(2d)=2^dΓ(n/2+d)/Γ(n/2)=n(n+2)…(n+2d−2), so dividing yields the formula. The Gaussian pairing identity itself follows by differentiating the moment generating function exp(||t||²/2): each degree-two exponential factor contributes a pair. This proof identifies all normalization factors rather than only counting pairings.

## Worked example: setup

Take n=d and m_i=e_i, so T=θ₁…θ_d.

## Worked example: calculation

Only pairings joining each repeated coordinate with itself survive, making the numerator one. For d=4, E T²=1/(4·6·8·10)=1/1920 and the full pairing sum has 7!!=105 terms. For d=5, E T²=1/(5·7·9·11·13)=1/45045 and there are 9!!=945 pairings.

## Worked example: conclusion

The source pairing counts and a complete spherical normalization are exactly certified.

## Limits

A Monte Carlo relative error near 0.003 is not an exact certificate of a more complicated source input; the exact Gram entries must be supplied.
