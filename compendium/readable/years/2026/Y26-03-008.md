## Revised question retained in the annual source

For which exponents does the proposed integral converge, and how can its evaluation be justified without an invalid interchange?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The source integral admits a classical analytic proof. The endpoint at zero is essential: an unrestricted Laplace–Fubini argument fails above one half even though the complex integral itself remains integrable up to exponent one.

## Definitions

For 0<x<π/2 set L(x)=−log cos x, W(x)=L(x)+ix, and use the principal complex power W^(−α). Let I(α)=∫₀^(π/2)Re W(x)^(−α)dx for real α.

Near zero, W=ix+O(x²); near π/2, Re W tends to infinity. The holomorphic complex integral J(α)=∫W^(−α)dx exists for 0<Re α<1.

## Result name

Exact integral identity

## Result statement

For real 0<α<1, I(α)=π/[2(log 2)^α]. The proof first uses absolute Fubini on 0<Re α<1/2 and then analytic continuation inside the convergence strip.

## Proof

For t>0, set z=e^(2ix) on the upper unit semicircle. The integrand (cos x)^t e^(−itx) equals ((1+z⁻¹)/2)^t. Equivalently conjugating uses ((1+z)/2)^t; integrate dz/(2iz), and close by the real diameter with a small indentation at zero. The diameter contribution has real part zero except the indentation, whose contribution is π/2 times the value at zero, 2^(−t). Thus ∫cos^t x cos(tx)dx=π/2^(t+1). The Laplace formula W^(−α)=Γ(α)⁻¹∫₀^∞t^(α−1)e^(−tW)dt is absolutely integrable jointly when Re α<1/2 because L∼x²/2. Taking the symmetric combination W^(−α)+conj(W)^(−α) gives the cosine identity and π/[2(log2)^α]. Both sides are holomorphic for 0<Re α<1, using local domination x^(−Re α), so the identity theorem extends the result throughout that strip.

## Worked example: setup

Take α=1/2.

## Limits

The proof concerns the integral of the real part. At α=1 the leading real singular coefficient vanishes and the limiting integral requires a separate endpoint analysis; one cannot simply substitute beyond the strip.

## Extension

Derive log-weighted integrals by differentiating in α on compact substrips and certify quadrature after subtracting the explicit x^(−α) endpoint term.
