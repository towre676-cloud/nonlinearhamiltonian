## Summary

The exact phase set makes this one of the strongest independently checkable October identities. Fourier analysis reduces the integral to a finite Bernoulli-polynomial sum. Logarithmic endpoint and interior singularities are square-integrable and are part of the proof, not exclusions from it.

## Definitions

Let S={0,1,2,4,11}, θ_s=2πs/17 and P(x)=∏_(s∈S)(2x−2cos θ_s).

Define I=∫_(−1)^1 log²|P(x)|/√(1−x²)dx, equivalently ∫₀^πlog²|P(cos t)|dt. Let B₂(u)=u²−u+1/6 and {u} denote fractional part.

## Result name

Exact five-phase integral

## Result statement

I=(955/867)π³. More generally I/π³=Σ_(s,r∈S)[B₂({(s−r)/17})+B₂({(s+r)/17})] for this convention of product factors.

## Proof

The factorization 2cos t−2cos θ=e^(−it)(e^(it)−e^(iθ))(e^(it)−e^(−iθ)) shows that log|2cos t−2cos θ|=−2Σ_(n≥1)cos(nt)cos(nθ)/n in L²(0,π). One can justify the boundary Fourier series by radial regularization of log|1−re^(iu)| and L² convergence of its square-summable coefficients as r↑1. Local logarithmic singularities, including the doubled endpoint singularity when θ=0, are L², so the factorization agrees with this limit. Summing over S gives −2Σa_ncos(nt)/n, where a_n=Σ_scos(nθ_s). Parseval yields I=2πΣa_n²/n². The identity2cos u cos v=cos(u−v)+cos(u+v), followed by Σ_(n≥1)cos(2πnu)/n²=π²B₂({u}), gives the finite formula. Evaluating its fifty rational B₂ terms with denominator17² gives exactly955/867. Individual B₂ terms may be negative, but the full quadratic expression is nonnegative for every phase multiset by its Parseval representation. This also checks the factor of two and shows why estimating each pair contribution independently can obscure the positivity of the original integral.

## Limits

A different normalization of each product factor would add constants inside the logarithm and change the integral. No KZ or group-closure conclusion follows merely from this phase integral.
