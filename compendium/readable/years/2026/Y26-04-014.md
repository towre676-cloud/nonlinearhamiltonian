## Revised question retained in the annual source

What does the proposed effective dimension measure, and how does a power-law spectrum determine its growth?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered Gram geometry separates rank, gap and participation ratio. These quantities answer different questions. A power-law calculation shows why a full-rank matrix can have bounded effective dimension and why rank alone does not certify a stable reduced model.

## Definitions

For a nonzero positive semidefinite Gram matrix G with eigenvalues λ_i≥0, define d_eff=(Σλ_i)²/Σλ_i². Rank is the number of positive eigenvalues.

For λ_i=i^(−a), i=1,…,n, write H_(n,a)=Σi^(−a). Then d_eff=H_(n,a)²/H_(n,2a).

## Result name

Bounds and power-law regimes

## Result statement

1≤d_eff≤rank G, with equality at the upper bound precisely when all positive eigenvalues are equal. For a>1, d_eff tends to ζ(a)²/ζ(2a), whereas rank remains n. For a=1 it grows like (log n)²/ζ(2).

## Proof

The lower bound follows from Σλ_i²≤(Σλ_i)². Cauchy–Schwarz gives (Σλ_i)²≤rΣλ_i², where r is rank; equality requires equal positive eigenvalues. The power-law formula is immediate substitution. For a>1 both sums converge to their zeta limits. For a=1, the harmonic sum is log n+O(1), while Σi⁻² tends to ζ(2), yielding the asymptotic. A gap δ_m=λ_m−λ_(m+1) instead measures separation at a chosen truncation boundary; small d_eff does not guarantee that this particular gap is large or robust under perturbation.

## Worked example: setup

Use G=diag(9,1,0).

## Worked example: calculation

rank G=2, d_eff=10²/(9²+1)=50/41≈1.2195122, and the first gap is eight. For G=I₂, rank and d_eff both equal two, but the first gap is zero.

Effective dimension, rank and eigengap carry complementary mathematical information.

## Limits
