## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which convention yields K(1−z)+½log z→log4, and can its constant be derived without fitting?

## Status

Classical mathematics or a newly chosen finite model developed with proof; historical claims delimited

## Summary

The recovered limit is correct for the parameter convention of the complete elliptic integral. A symmetric change of variables reduces its asymptotic to an elementary inverse hyperbolic sine, yielding both the logarithmic divergence and its finite constant.

## Definitions

Use the parameter convention K(m)=∫₀^(π/2)(1−m sin²θ)^(−1/2)dθ for0<m<1. In the DLMF modulus convention this is K_DLMF(√m).

Let z∈(0,1), s=√z and L=s^(−1/2).

## Result name

Parameter-normalized logarithmic asymptotic

## Result statement

K(1−z)+½log z→log4 as z↓0. More explicitly, |K(1−z)−2asinh(z^(−1/4))|≤√z/2, which already determines the limit.

## Proof

The substitution t=tanθ gives K(1−z)=∫₀^∞dt/[√(1+t²)√(1+s²t²)]. The inversion t=1/(su) interchanges the intervals (0,L) and (L,∞) and preserves the integrand times its differential. Hence the integral is twice its restriction to(0,L). Its difference from2∫₀^Ldt/√(1+t²)=2asinh L is nonpositive. Using0≤1−(1+u)^(−1/2)≤u/2 gives absolute difference at most s²∫₀^L t²/√(1+t²)dt. Since t²/√(1+t²)≤t, this is at most s²L²/2=s/2. Finally2asinh L=2log(L+√(1+L²))=2log(2L)+o(1)=log4−log s+o(1). Because log s=½log z, the limit follows with its exact constant.

## Worked example: setup

Take z=10⁻⁸ and keep a certified remainder.

## Worked example: calculation

Then L=100 and the difference between K(1−10⁻⁸) and2asinh100 has magnitude at most5·10⁻⁵. No numerical integration is required for this enclosure. The elementary expression also gives the leading approximation log4−½log(10⁻⁸). Indeed0≤2asinh100−2log200≤1/20000, using √(1+u)≤1+u/2 and log(1+u)≤u. Combining this nonnegative correction with the nonpositive split error puts K minus its leading logarithm in[−1/20000,1/20000]. The constant is therefore fixed together with an explicit numerical enclosure. The inequality is also nonasymptotic: at z=1/16 it gives2asinh2−1/8≤K(15/16)≤2asinh2. Thus the same proof yields usable enclosures away from the limiting endpoint, without assuming z is infinitesimal.

## Worked example: conclusion

The convention, divergence and finite constant are all fixed by a direct proof.

## Limits

Writing K for a modulus instead of a parameter changes the argument and the apparent logarithmic constant unless translated correctly. This local asymptotic is not a global period-comparison or arithmetic-motive identification.
