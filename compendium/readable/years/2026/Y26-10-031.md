## Revised question retained in the annual source

How do all264 roots above q relate to the240 primitive conjugates, and which polylogarithm identity is actually valid?

## Status

## Summary

The source records the integers384,264 and240. They measure different objects: base-field degree, the number of complex roots of a power equation, and extension degree. The root-of-unity filter makes this distinction explicit and supplies a convergent exact identity.

## Definitions

Set q=ζ₁₆₈₀, Q=ζ₄₄₃₅₂₀ and N=264, so Qᴺ=q. Let ξ=ζ_N.

For Re(s)>1 and |z|≤1, define Li_s(z)=Σ_(n≥1)zⁿ/nˢ by its absolutely convergent series.

## Result name

Full-root polylogarithm filter and primitive count

## Result statement

Σ_(r=0)^(N−1)Li_s(Qξʳ)=N^(1−s)Li_s(q). There are264 distinct roots of X²⁶⁴=q, but exactly240 of them are primitive order443520 roots, and [Q(Q):Q(q)]=240.

## Proof

Absolute convergence permits the finite root sum to be interchanged with the series. The geometric sum Σ_rξ^(rn) is N when N divides n and zero otherwise. Writing n=Nℓ leaves NΣ_ℓQ^(Nℓ)/(Nℓ)ˢ=N^(1−s)Li_s(q), proving the filter with no branch continuation. All roots are Qξʳ=ζ₄₄₃₅₂₀^(1+1680r),0≤r<264. Their exponents are automatically coprime to2,3,5,7. The only possible extra common factor is11; exactly one residue class of r modulo11 makes1+1680r divisible by11, removing24 roots. Thus240 are primitive. Independently, φ(443520)=92160 and φ(1680)=384, giving the same extension degree240 by the tower law. The excluded r satisfy r≡4 mod11. The remaining240 exponents are exactly the cyclotomic automorphisms fixing q, so their sum is a relative field trace, whereas the264-term expression is a complete root filter. Filtering over only the240 primitive roots would therefore be a different sum and does not satisfy the displayed264-term identity unchanged.

## Worked example: calculation
