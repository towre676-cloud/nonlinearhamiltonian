## Revised question retained in the annual source

What does the integral Gramian prove, and how does it differ from an eigenvalue-only stability test?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The March resolvent program contains a valid finite-dimensional core. A positive Gramian turns asymptotic eigenvalue stability into an energy identity, while a triangular example shows why stable eigenvalues alone do not control transient gain.

## Definitions

Let A be a real Hurwitz matrix, meaning every eigenvalue has negative real part, and let Q be symmetric positive definite.

Define P=∫₀^∞e^(Aᵀt)Qe^(At)dt. Finite dimension ensures exponential integrability, even for Jordan blocks.

## Result name

Continuous Lyapunov construction

## Result statement

The integral is positive definite, satisfies AᵀP+PA=−Q, and is the unique symmetric solution. Conversely, P>0 and Q>0 in this equation imply A is Hurwitz.

## Proof

Differentiate e^(Aᵀt)Qe^(At) and integrate from zero to infinity; the boundary values are zero and Q. Positivity follows because the integrand at t=0 is positive on each nonzero vector and is continuous. If AᵀX+XA=0, the function e^(Aᵀt)Xe^(At) is constant and tends to zero, so X=0. For the converse, if Av=λv for a complex nonzero eigenvector, then 2 Re(λ)v*Pv=−v*Qv<0, forcing Re λ<0. These arguments also distinguish solving the equation from merely observing a small numerical residual: coercivity must be checked.

## Worked example: setup

Take A=[[-1,2],[0,−1]] and Q=I.

## Worked example: calculation

e^(At)=e^(−t)[[1,2t],[0,1]]. Integrating its Gram product gives P=[[1/2,1/2],[1/2,3/2]], with determinant 1/2>0. Direct multiplication gives AᵀP+PA=−I. Starting from (0,1), the first coordinate is 2te^(−t), which initially grows despite both eigenvalues being −1.

## Worked example: conclusion

The Gramian supplies a rigorous metric; it does not remove amplification in the original Euclidean metric.

## Limits

An infinite-dimensional integral Gramian may be bounded and injective without being coercive. The converse then needs additional observability or energy hypotheses.

## Extension

For a family of matrices, certify lower and upper Gramian eigenvalue bounds uniformly and compare the resulting energy bound with direct resolvent estimates.
