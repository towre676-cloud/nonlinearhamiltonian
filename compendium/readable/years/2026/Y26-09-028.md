## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which d-free coefficient controls the first hit and the counting constant?

## Status

Classical mathematics with newly authored derivation or explicitly defined worked model

## Summary

The classifier audit found an infinite branch misclassified after a floating coefficient underflowed. Exact prime valuations remove that problem and also clarify a normalization subtlety: for squares, n=cw² describes all hits only when the coefficient c is squarefree, or when a restricted subfamily is intended.

## Definitions

Fix integers c≥1 and d≥2. Write c=∏p p^(e_p), and define a=∏_(p|c)p^(−e_p mod d), using residues in{0,…,d−1}.

Let A_(c,d)(N) count positive n≤N for which cn is an integer d-th power; it counts n once, regardless of the possible sign choices for its root.

## Result name

The linear perfect-power branch has an exact valuation formula and never becomes finite through underflow

## Result statement

The hits are exactly n=at^d with t≥1. Therefore A_(c,d)(N)=floor((N/a)^(1/d)), interpreted by an exact integer d-th root. In particular this branch is infinite for every positive c. For d=2, a is the squarefree part of c.

## Proof

For each prime p, the condition that cn be a d-th power is e_p+v_p(n)≡0 mod d, with e_p=0 for primes not dividing c. Thus v_p(n) is the prescribed least residue plus a nonnegative multiple of d. Collecting all prime powers gives n=at^d. Conversely ca has every valuation divisible by d, so cn=(u t)^d with integer u=(ca)^(1/d). The bijection t↦at^d proves the count and infinitude. No approximate coefficient or limiting classification is required.

## Worked example: setup

Take c=12,d=2.

## Worked example: calculation

Here12=2²·3, so a=3 and ca=36. All hits are n=3t² with square root m=6t. Up to N=100 there are exactly five:3,12,27,48,75. Writing only n=12w² would omit3,27,75 and therefore requires an extra convention. For c=72 the squarefree part is two and the roots are m=12t. Huge c can delay the first hit beyond a finite search range but cannot make the infinite family bounded.

## Worked example: conclusion

The classifier obtains an exact structural branch and a reproducible population count.

## Limits

This theorem is for a positive linear coefficient with zero constant term. A general polynomial or shifted linear core needs additional congruence and integrality conditions. A finite prefix containing no hits is not a finiteness certificate.

## Extension

Normalize every polynomial square or d-th-power factor exactly, propagate all denominator and residue restrictions, and classify the remaining core with an explicit proof of completeness.
