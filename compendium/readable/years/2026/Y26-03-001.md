## Revised question retained in the annual source

Under which domain and coercivity hypotheses is quadratic semigroup decay equivalent to a generator inequality?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered contraction equivalence is correct after specifying its metric and the generator domain. It is a theorem about one chosen quadratic energy; existence of some equivalent contractive metric is a separate question. The proof works without assuming a bounded generator.

## Definitions

Let T(t) be a strongly continuous semigroup on a real or complex Hilbert space with generator A and dense domain D(A). Let P be bounded self-adjoint with mI≤P≤MI for constants 0<m≤M.

Write V(x)=⟨Px,x⟩. The expression A*P+PA is interpreted as the quadratic form 2 Re⟨PAx,x⟩ on D(A), without assuming P maps D(A) into D(A*).

## Result name

Generator–energy equivalence

## Result statement

For α real, V(T(t)x)≤e^(−2αt)V(x) for all x and t≥0 if and only if 2 Re⟨PAx,x⟩≤−2αV(x) for all x∈D(A). Consequently ||T(t)||≤√(M/m)e^(−αt).

## Proof

For x∈D(A), semigroup invariance of D(A) gives a differentiable trajectory and dV(T(t)x)/dt=2 Re⟨PAT(t)x,T(t)x⟩. Multiplying by e^(2αt) makes the derivative nonpositive. Integrating proves decay on D(A), and density plus boundedness of T(t) and P extends it to every x. Conversely, divide the energy inequality by t after subtracting V(x), and take t↓0 for x∈D(A). The two norm bounds m||x||²≤V(x)≤M||x||² yield the asserted ordinary-norm estimate. This establishes both directions in exactly the declared metric, including α=0.

## Worked example: setup

Use A=diag(−1,−3), P=diag(2,1), α=1.

## Worked example: calculation

AᵀP+PA=diag(−4,−6)≤−2P=diag(−4,−2). The trajectory has V(T(t)x)=2e^(−2t)x₁²+e^(−6t)x₂²≤e^(−2t)V(x). The metric condition ratio is M/m=2.

## Worked example: conclusion

The rate and the norm-equivalence prefactor are explicit and independently checkable.

## Limits

If P is merely positive and not coercive in infinite dimension, this energy may fail to control the original norm. The form statement avoids an unjustified operator-domain identity.

## Extension

For a PDE, specify D(A), prove the integration-by-parts boundary terms have the required sign, and establish uniform coercivity under discretization.
