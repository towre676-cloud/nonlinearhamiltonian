## Definitions

On R^n with cyclic indices define (Bx)_j=x_{j+1}−x_j and E(x)=||Bx||²/2. Constants form kerB. Work on the quotient by constants, identified with the mean-zero subspace.

The Hessian is L=BᵀB, the cycle Laplacian. A unit Fourier mode has coordinates n^{−1/2}exp(2πikj/n), k=0,…,n−1.

## Result name

A quotient obstruction energy has a computable Hessian and an exact Fourier spectrum

## Result statement

L has eigenvalues λ_k=4sin²(πk/n). For mean-zero x, E(x)≥2sin²(π/n)||x||²; the constant is sharp. Adding μ||x||²/2 makes the Hessian L+μI positive definite exactly when μ>0.

## Proof

Apply B to a Fourier mode: it multiplies by exp(2πik/n)−1. Applying Bᵀ multiplies by its conjugate, giving squared modulus 2−2cos(2πk/n)=4sin²(πk/n). The discrete Fourier vectors form an orthonormal basis by finite geometric-sum orthogonality. The k=0 eigenvalue is zero, and all others are positive. Their minimum is λ₁=λ_{n−1}, so expansion in that basis proves the quotient inequality and exhibits equality on the first sine/cosine modes. Adding a scalar diagonal shifts each eigenvalue by μ; since the original minimum is zero, positive definiteness is equivalent to μ>0. On the quotient the condition number is λ_max/λ₁ and grows proportionally to n², so a finite small Hessian value may reflect increasing domain size rather than a newly identified arithmetic effect.
