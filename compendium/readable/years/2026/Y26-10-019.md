## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which requested outputs are identifiable from a given observation, even when the entire hidden state is not?

## Status

Classical mathematics or a newly chosen finite model developed with proof; historical claims delimited

## Summary

The recovered kernel criterion is a complete finite-dimensional identifiability theorem. It separates state reconstruction from recovering a particular target. The distinction prevents unnecessary sensing and makes a missing-information claim testable by an explicit nullspace vector.

## Definitions

Let V,W,Z be finite-dimensional vector spaces over Q or R, with observation H:V→W and target G:V→Z.

The target is identifiable when Hx=Hy implies Gx=Gy for every x,y∈V.

## Result name

Kernel factorization criterion

## Result statement

The following are equivalent: the target is identifiable; ker H⊆ker G; and there is a linear L:W→Z such that G=LH. L is uniquely determined on im H but need not be unique on all W.

## Proof

If the target is identifiable, set y=0 and x∈ker H to obtain Gx=0. Conversely, Hx=Hy means x−y∈ker H, so kernel inclusion gives Gx=Gy. To construct L on im H, define L(Hx)=Gx. If Hx=Hy the kernel condition makes this independent of the representative, and linearity follows from the linearity of H and G. Extend a basis of im H to a basis of W and define L arbitrarily on the complement. Then G=LH. The converse factorization immediately implies kernel inclusion. A violating vector v with Hv=0 but Gv≠0 is therefore a complete impossibility certificate: x and x+v have identical data and different targets.

## Worked example: setup

Observe Hx=(x₁+x₂,x₂+x₃) and ask for Gx=x₁+2x₂+x₃.

## Worked example: calculation

Here ker H is span((1,−1,1)), and G annihilates this vector. The reconstruction is Gx=(1,1)Hx. Asking instead for x₁ fails, since its value changes along the kernel. In four dimensions, observing only the first two coordinates leaves a two-dimensional fibre; two further independent scalar linear measurements are necessary and sufficient to reconstruct the whole state, although fewer may suffice for a particular G.

## Worked example: conclusion

The theorem identifies precisely which uncertainty matters for a requested mathematical output.

## Limits

Nonlinear, noisy or restricted-state observations require revised conditions. Dimension counting is a lower bound for full linear reconstruction, not a universal lower bound for arbitrary discontinuous encodings or finite-state problems.

## Extension

Under bounded noise, minimize an explicit norm of L subject to G=LH; the resulting operator norm bounds target error and turns exact identifiability into a conditioned sensor-design problem.
