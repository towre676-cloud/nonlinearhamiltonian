## Revised question retained in the annual source

Assuming the reported maximal genus-three curve exists, which code parameters and decoding radius follow rigorously?

## Status

## Summary

The July31 assistant source gives a genus-three curve over F₄₉,92 rational points and a code[91,37,52]. The curve equation, point list and decoder were not recovered. A conditional theorem proves the guaranteed dimension and distance bound without promoting an asserted exact distance into a demonstrated one.

## Definitions

Let X/F_q be a smooth projective geometrically integral curve of genus g. Choose distinct rational points P₁,…,P_n and a divisor G disjoint from them. The evaluation code is C_L(D,G)={ (f(P₁),…,f(P_n)):f∈L(G)}, with D=ΣP_i.

## Result name

One-point Goppa parameter certificate

## Result statement

If2g−2<degG<n, then dimC_L=degG+1−g and its minimum distance is at least n−degG. Thus a genus-three curve with92 rational points yields a one-point code[91,37,d≥52] usingG=39P_∞ and the other91 points.

## Proof

The kernel of evaluation is L(G−D). Its divisor degree is negative, so only zero belongs to that space: a nonzero function has principal divisor degree zero and cannot satisfy a negative-degree effectivity condition. Evaluation is therefore injective. Riemann–Roch and degG>2g−2 giveℓ(G)=degG+1−g. A nonzero f∈L(G) has at mostdegG zeros among the evaluation points, since its zero divisor and pole divisor have the same degree and its poles are bounded byG. Its evaluation has weight at least n−degG. These arguments prove a lower bound; equality additionally needs an f attaining exactlydegG evaluation zeros. The Hasse–Weil upper bound atq49,g3 is49+1+2·3·7=92, so the reported count would indeed be maximal, provided the actual curve and count are verified.

## Worked example: setup

Work conditionally with q=49,g=3,N=92,n=91 and degG=39.

## Worked example: calculation

The dimension is39+1−3=37 and the designed distance is91−39=52. A nearest-codeword decoder is uniquely defined for every received word within⌊(52−1)/2⌋=25 errors of a codeword. An algorithm reported only through24 errors may have a weaker guarantee; that does not contradict the distance bound. It does not establish an efficient25-error algorithm either.

## Worked example: conclusion

The construction separates existence, parameter bounds, exact distance and algorithmic decoding into independent mathematical obligations.
