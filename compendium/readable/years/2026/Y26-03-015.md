## Revised question retained in the annual source

When is the proposed Lambert-W fixed-point form equivalent to e^v=R(v), and does it solve the equation?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered master equation is rewritten using Lambert W. That rewriting is legitimate on a carefully selected branch, but its right side still contains v. It is therefore an implicit fixed-point formulation, not an evaluation by a constant special-function argument.

## Definitions

Let R(v)>0 and v>0. Lambert W solves w e^w=z. On real arguments z∈[−1/e,0), the two branches are W₀(z)∈[−1,0) and W_(−1)(z)≤−1.

From e^v=R(v), one obtains (−v/2)e^(−v/2)=−v/[2√R(v)].

## Result name

Branch-resolved equivalence

## Result statement

At a positive solution, v=−2W₀(−v/[2√R(v)]) when 0<v≤2, and v=−2W_(−1)(−v/[2√R(v)]) when v≥2. Conversely, either identity with its stated branch implies e^v=R(v).

## Proof

The substitution w=−v/2 places w in the principal branch range precisely for v≤2 and on the lower branch for v≥2. Applying the inverse on the appropriate range gives the identity. Conversely, w=W(z) implies w e^w=−v/(2√R); dividing by −v/2 and squaring yields e^(−v)=1/R. Positivity removes sign ambiguity. Since R still depends on v, this equivalence supplies no independent existence, uniqueness or arithmetic information. Iteration would require a self-map interval and a derivative bound less than one, neither supplied merely by writing W.

## Worked example: setup

Use the constant function R(v)=e⁴ and the solution v=4.

## Worked example: calculation

The argument is −2e^(−2), about −0.270671. W_(−1)(−2e^(−2))=−2, yielding v=4. The principal branch has value between −1 and 0 and therefore returns a number below 2, not 4. Thus an unconditional W₀ formulation loses a valid solution.

## Worked example: conclusion

The branch restriction is substantive even when R is as simple as a positive constant.

## Limits

The source candidate v≈1.06383 lies below two and is compatible with W₀, but this observation does not establish convergence of a proposed iteration.
