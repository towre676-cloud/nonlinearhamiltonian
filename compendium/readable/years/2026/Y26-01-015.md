## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which state and time evolution actually satisfy a KMS relation, and what extra definition would a q-deformation require?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

Finite matrix algebras make the analytic continuation in the KMS boundary condition explicit. A two-level Gibbs state verifies the relation exactly and shows that changing temperature is different from introducing an undefined q-deformed algebra.

## Definitions

Let H be a self-adjoint d×d matrix, β>0, ρ=e^{−βH}/Z and Z=tr(e^{−βH}). Define φ(A)=tr(ρA).

The dynamics is α_t(A)=e^{itH}Ae^{−itH}. In finite dimension it extends to every complex t.

Use the KMS convention φ(Aα_{iβ}(B))=φ(BA). The sign depends on the declared dynamics convention.

## Result name

Finite-dimensional KMS boundary identity

## Result statement

The Gibbs state obeys the stated identity for all matrices A,B, and φ is invariant under α_t. For a nondegenerate two-level system with gap E, the population ratio is e^{−βE}.

## Proof

At imaginary time α_{iβ}(B)=e^{−βH}Be^{βH}. Therefore Zφ(Aα_{iβ}(B))=tr(e^{−βH}Ae^{−βH}Be^{βH}). Cyclically move the final exponential to the front to obtain tr(Ae^{−βH}B)=tr(e^{−βH}BA), proving the boundary identity. For invariance, ρ commutes with e^{itH}, so cyclicity gives φ(α_t A)=φ(A). Positivity follows from tr(ρA* A)≥0 and normalization from trρ=1. These elementary properties supply the algebra, state, positivity and dynamics needed before discussing any analytic equilibrium condition.

## Worked example: setup

Take H=diag(0,E), βE=log 3, and A=|0⟩⟨1|, B=|1⟩⟨0|.

## Worked example: calculation

Then ρ=diag(3/4,1/4), α_{iβ}(B)=B/3 and AB=|0⟩⟨0|. The left side is (1/3)(3/4)=1/4; the right side is φ(BA)=φ(|1⟩⟨1|)=1/4. Meanwhile β→0 gives the normalized trace, and β→∞ concentrates on the ground state.

## Worked example: conclusion

A concrete matrix identity verifies equilibrium without any appeal to a fitted spectral intercept. For degenerate energy levels the Gibbs state is uniform within each eigenspace, so its low-temperature limit is the normalized projection onto the full ground eigenspace.

## Limits

The source names q-KMS but does not recover a complete deformed algebra, coproduct, state or evolution. Merely writing q=e^{−βE} reparametrizes a Gibbs ratio; it does not establish a quantum-group or deformed KMS theorem.

## Extension

Supply the proposed q-algebra and positive state, prove that its evolution is a one-parameter automorphism group, and verify the analytic strip condition. Compare its q→1 limit with this finite Gibbs benchmark.
