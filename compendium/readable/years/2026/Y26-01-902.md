## Result name

The semicircle law has exact Catalan moments before any random-matrix limit

## Result statement

m_{2k}=(1/(k+1))binom(2k,k), m_{2k+1}=0, and G(z)=(z−√(z²−4))/2. Consequently G²−zG+1=0 and G(z)∼1/z.

## Proof

Symmetry gives the odd moments. Substitute x=2√t on the positive half of the even-moment integral. One gets m_{2k}=2^(2k+1)B(k+1/2,3/2)/π. Using Γ(3/2)=√π/2 and Γ(k+1/2)=(2k)!√π/(4^k k!) reduces this to the Catalan number. In particular m_0=1, so normalization is proved rather than assumed. For |z|>2, expand1/(z−x) geometrically and interchange with the integral by uniform absolute convergence. Thus G(z)=Σ_{k≥0}C_k z^(−2k−1). The Catalan generating function C(t)=1+tC(t)², obtained either from the binomial formula or the quadratic binomial expansion, gives the asserted expression and quadratic equation. Analytic continuation determines G off the spectral interval with the chosen branch. These calculations establish properties of the measure; convergence of GUE empirical spectra to this measure is a further probabilistic theorem with an ensemble normalization and limiting argument.
