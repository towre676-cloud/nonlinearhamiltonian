## Revised question retained in the annual source

Can the reported incidence rank and four-dimensional null space be established exactly rather than by floating residuals?

## Status

## Summary

The recovered K₃,₃ calculation has a clean combinatorial proof. Choosing an orientation and a spanning tree constructs the entire circulation space over the integers. This also gives an exact starting point for any later probe calculation.

## Definitions

Label left vertices u₁,u₂,u₃ and right vertices v₁,v₂,v₃. Orient edge e_ij from u_i to v_j. Let B be the six-by-nine incidence matrix with −1 at its tail and +1 at its head.

For i,j∈{2,3}, define z_ij=e_ij−e_i1−e_1j+e_11 as an edge-coordinate vector.

## Result name

Exact rank and integral cycle basis

## Result statement

rank B=5, dim ker B=4, and the four vectors z₂₂,z₂₃,z₃₂,z₃₃ form a basis of all real circulations and an integral basis of all integer circulations.

## Proof

Every column sums to zero, so rank is at most five. The five edges e_11,e_12,e_13,e_21,e_31 form a spanning tree. If a linear combination of their incidence columns vanishes, a leaf equation kills its adjacent coefficient; repeatedly stripping leaves kills all five, so rank is at least five. Each displayed four-cycle has zero incidence by cancellation at its four vertices. They are independent because z_ij is the only one with coefficient one on its corresponding off-tree edge e_ij. For any circulation z, subtract its four off-tree coefficients times these basis vectors. The remainder is supported on the tree and is therefore zero by the same leaf argument. Integer coefficients are preserved throughout.

## Worked example: setup

Use z₂₂=e₂₂−e₂₁−e₁₂+e₁₁.

## Worked example: calculation

Its four nonzero entries are (1,−1,−1,1), its Euclidean norm is 2, and Bz₂₂=0 exactly. The pair z₂₂,z₂₃ has dot product 2 because it shares e₂₁ and e₁₁ with equal signs, giving cosine 1/2.

## Worked example: conclusion

Exact topology and vector geometry are available without mistaking a 10⁻¹⁶ residual for a proof.

## Limits
