## Revised question retained in the annual source

What recurrence counts every feasible selection once, including skipped variables, and what makes an optimum certificate complete?

## Status

Classical mathematics or a newly chosen finite model developed with proof; historical claims delimited

## Summary

The Petersen records give capacities and historical totals, but not the fifty coefficient and profit rows needed to replay them. The rigorous core is a threshold dynamic program with explicit terminal multiplicities. A small instance below independently verifies counting and uniqueness while preserving each original project coordinate.

## Definitions

For n distinct projects, let w_i∈Z_≥0^d and capacity b∈Z^d. A binary selection x is feasible if Σw_i x_i≤b coordinatewise.

Let C(i,b) count feasible suffix assignments from coordinate i. A true terminal before the last variable represents every assignment of all remaining coordinates.

## Result name

Counting recurrence and optimization certificate

## Result statement

C(i,b)=0 if any coordinate of b is negative, C(n,b)=1 for b≥0, and C(i,b)=C(i+1,b)+C(i+1,b−w_i). If b dominates the entire suffix weight, C(i,b)=2^(n−i). For integer profits, uniqueness at value v follows from one feasible witness of value v and exactly one feasible assignment with value at least v.

## Proof

Partition the feasible suffixes by x_i=0 or x_i=1. These disjoint sets are respectively the two smaller capacity problems, proving the recurrence inductively from its terminal cases. If every suffix assignment fits, there are n−i freely chosen bits, so its contribution is a power of two, not one. A reduced decision diagram may omit coordinates on which the residual predicate does not depend; each such free bit still doubles the represented assignments. Equivalently, an edge skipping g variables carries multiplicity 2ᵍ. For the optimization statement, the threshold count excludes every assignment of higher profit and every second assignment of equal profit. Conversely a unique maximizer makes that threshold population a singleton. The coefficient-generating product ∏_i(1+z^(w_i)t^(p_i)) gives the same count by selecting either monomial from each distinct project factor; equal weight vectors do not identify the underlying selections.

## Worked example: setup

Use weights (2,3,4), capacity five and profits (3,4,5).

## Worked example: calculation

The feasible bit strings are 000,001,010,100,110, so the population is five. Their profits are 0,5,4,3,7; the unique optimum is 110 at seven, and the profit-at-least-seven population is one. A separate predicate x₀∧x₂ on four coordinates has four solutions because x₁ and x₃ are free, illustrating skipped-variable multiplicity.

## Worked example: conclusion

The recurrence and a complete small optimum certificate are replayed independently.
