## Revised question retained in the annual source

What mathematical hypothesis makes a joint residual minimum unique across coupled sectors?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered program organizes several residuals on a product moduli space and proposes at most one joint minimizer. A precise finite model shows how coupling enters the Hessian and gives a strong global criterion. It also separates zero residuals from the existence of a minimum.

## Definitions

For R:D⊂R^n→R^m define I(z)=||R(z)||²/2. Assume D is convex and R is twice differentiable.

The exact Hessian is ∇²I=DRᵀDR+Σ_iR_i∇²R_i. The second term may have either sign away from R=0.

## Result name

Uniform convexity certificate

## Result statement

If ∇²I(z)≥μI_n for every z∈D with μ>0, then I has at most one minimizer on D. If D is all of R^n and I is continuous with this bound, a unique minimizer exists. A minimizer is a common residual zero only if its attained value is zero.

## Proof

Along the segment x+t(y−x), integrate the second derivative lower bound to obtain I(y)≥I(x)+∇I(x)·(y−x)+μ||y−x||²/2. Two distinct minimizers would violate strict convexity at their midpoint. On all of R^n, the inequality with x=0 implies a coercive quadratic lower bound; compact sublevel sets and continuity yield existence. The Hessian formula follows by differentiating ΣR_i²/2 twice. A nonsingular residual Jacobian at one zero ensures local regularity, but cannot replace the uniform Hessian argument over a whole convex domain.

## Worked example: setup

Take R(x,y)=(x+y−1,x−y).

## Worked example: calculation

DR=[[1,1],[1,−1]], so DRᵀDR=2I and the residual Hessians vanish. Solving gives x=y=1/2, the unique zero and global minimizer. Adding the incompatible residual R₃=1 preserves strict convexity but changes the minimum energy to 1/2, demonstrating that uniqueness and compatibility are distinct.

## Worked example: conclusion

Joint coupling can be certified by an exact matrix inequality without interpreting a numerical minimum as zero.

## Limits

This supplies no proof of a particular particle-physics residual model. Its domain, scales, smoothness and numerical residual implementation remain missing.

## Extension
