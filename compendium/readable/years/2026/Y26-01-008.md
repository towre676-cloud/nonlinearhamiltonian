## Revised question retained in the annual source

When is a fourth-trace variance formula correct, and how do Gaussian and sign probes differ?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

Independent Gaussian and Rademacher probes give equally unbiased trace estimates but different exact variances. Applying the calculation to a squared nonnormality commutator shows why a variance spike cannot be inferred without specifying the probe law.

## Definitions

Let B be a real symmetric n×n matrix and estimate tr B by T_m=m⁻¹∑_{r=1}^m z_rᵀBz_r, using independent probes with E[zzᵀ]=I.

A Gaussian probe has independent N(0,1) entries. A Rademacher probe has independent equiprobable entries ±1.

For a real matrix A let C=AAᵀ−AᵀA, B=C², and ζ=tr(C²)/n.

## Result name

Two exact variance identities

## Result statement

E T_m=tr B. Gaussian probes give Var(T_m)=2tr(B²)/m; Rademacher probes give Var(T_m)=2∑_{i≠j}B_{ij}²/m. Thus the stated Gaussian variance for ζ is 2tr(C⁴)/(mn²), whereas sign probes omit the squared diagonal of C².

## Proof

Expand zᵀBz=∑_iB_{ii}z_i²+2∑_{i<j}B_{ij}z_iz_j. Independence and zero means make distinct off-diagonal pairs uncorrelated. Each pair contributes 4B_{ij}² because E z_i²z_j²=1. For Gaussian entries Var(z_i²)=2, adding 2∑_iB_{ii}² gives 2tr B². For sign entries z_i²=1 exactly, so the entire diagonal part has zero variance. Averaging m independent samples divides each variance by m. The mean follows from E z_iz_j=δ_ij. This fourth-moment derivation also identifies what changes for complex probes or correlated sampling. Unbiasedness uses only second moments; variance requires the fourth moments and independence explicitly used above.

## Worked example: setup

Use A=[[-1,4],[0,−1]] from the Jordan example.

## Worked example: calculation

Here C=diag(16,−16), so B=256I₂ and ζ=256. Every sign probe returns zᵀBz/2=256 exactly: its variance is zero. Gaussian probes return 128(z₁²+z₂²), with variance 65536/m after m-sample averaging. The historical trace expression gives 2·131072/(4m)=65536/m, agreeing only with the Gaussian specification.

## Worked example: conclusion

An identical matrix can exhibit noisy or noiseless estimates solely because the estimator changed.

## Limits

An unbiased estimator does not prove that ζ is a causal training diagnostic. Threshold selection, repeated testing and model-dependent normalization require separate statistical analysis.

## Extension

Derive nonasymptotic confidence bounds under a supplied spectral norm or PSD assumption; then compare Gaussian, sign and orthogonalized probes at equal matvec budgets. Test any proposed temporal cascade on held-out runs with uncertainty bands.
