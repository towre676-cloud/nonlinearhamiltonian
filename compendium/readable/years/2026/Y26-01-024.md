## Revised question retained in the annual source

How do parameter-dependent indicial roots produce logarithmic solutions without mistaking a removable basis pole for a singular solution?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

An Euler equation gives an exact model of colliding exponents. A divided-difference basis remains finite as the parameter vanishes and produces logarithmic coefficients satisfying a triangular differential hierarchy.

## Definitions

For t>0 let θ=t d/dt and L_ε=θ(θ+ε). Its indicial roots are 0 and −ε.

For ε≠0 use f₁=1 and f₂=t^{−ε}, and define g_ε=(f₂−f₁)/ε.

The branch log t is real on t>0; complex continuation requires a chosen branch on a simply connected punctured domain.

## Result name

Regularized basis and coefficient hierarchy

## Result statement

g_ε extends analytically to ε=0 with g₀=−log t. Its expansion is ∑_{r≥0}(−1)^{r+1}ε^r(log t)^{r+1}/(r+1)!. Writing g_ε=∑ε^r g_r gives θ²g₀=0 and θ²g_r=−θg_{r−1} for r≥1.

## Proof

Since t^{−ε}=exp(−ε log t), its Taylor series proves the extension and formula. Both f₁ and f₂ solve L_εf=0, so the divided difference does too for ε≠0, and continuity gives the limiting equation. Alternatively substitute the power series into (θ²+εθ)g=0 and equate coefficients, obtaining the hierarchy. Direct differentiation of powers of log t checks each equation. The Wronskian of 1 and g_ε is g_ε′=−t^{−ε−1}, which tends to −1/t, so the regularized pair stays independent as ε→0 even though 1 and t^{−ε} become identical. An explicit 1/ε in a poorly chosen basis therefore need not be a pole of the underlying solution.

## Limits

A general Fuchsian family can have resonant compatibility obstructions and genuine parameter poles. This elementary model does not supply the missing Picard–Fuchs operator of the historical elliptic integral.
