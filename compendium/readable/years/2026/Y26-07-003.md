## Revised question retained in the annual source

What exact resolution and socle distinguish this quotient from a complete intersection?

## Status

Developed classical mathematics with explicit hypotheses and scoped evidence

## Summary

The July3 source correctly isolates I=(x²,xy,y³), Betti numbers(1,3,2) and socle dimension two. These can be proved directly, exposing why a short symmetric Hilbert function does not imply Gorenstein duality or a Pfaffian structure.

## Definitions

Let R=k[x,y],A=R/(x²,xy,y³). Its socle is{x∈A:(x,y)x=0}. Use φ=[[y,0],[−x,y²],[0,−x]], whose columns are syzygies among the ordered generators x²,xy,y³.

## Result name

Exact minimal graded resolution

## Result statement

There is a minimal resolution0→R(−3)⊕R(−4)→R(−2)²⊕R(−3)→R→A→0. A has basis1,x,y,y² and socle span{x,y²}, so its type is two.

## Proof

The two column relations are yx²−x(xy)=0 and y²(xy)−x(y³)=0. To prove they generate every syzygy, let ax²+bxy+cy³=0. Setting x=0 implies x divides c; write c=xc₁. Divide by x to get ax+by+c₁y³=0. Setting y=0 implies y divides a; write a=ya₁. Then b=−xa₁−y²c₁. This is a₁ times the first column plus−c₁ times the second. Injectivity of φ follows from ya₁=0 and−xc₂=0 in the domain R. All entries lie in the homogeneous maximal ideal, proving minimality. Multiplication by x,y on the four standard monomials gives the stated socle. The alternating graded resolution gives Hilbert numerator1−2t²−t³+t³+t⁴=1−2t²+t⁴. Dividing by(1−t)² yields1+2t+t². The cancellation of the two degree-three terms in this numerical series conceals their distinct homological origins, which is why the Hilbert series alone cannot decide whether the displayed resolution is a complete intersection.

## Worked example: setup

Compare with B=R/(x²,y²).

## Worked example: calculation

Both A and B have Hilbert function(1,2,1). But B has socle span{xy} and total Betti numbers(1,2,1), whereas A has a degree-one socle class x and type two. The Hilbert numerator of A has a cancellation at degree three that does not cancel a minimal free module.

## Worked example: conclusion

Resolution and socle data distinguish algebras that their length and Hilbert function cannot.

## Limits

This elementary proof concerns the declared ideal, not every reported finite-field target. Gorenstein and Pfaffian classifications need the appropriate codimension and type assumptions.
