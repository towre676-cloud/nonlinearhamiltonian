## Revised question retained in the annual source

Reconstruction: Which constraint makes two bosonic modes a finite-dimensional spin representation?

## Status

Classical result or newly stipulated finite model; historical claims explicitly delimited

## Summary

The May source names Schwinger bosons and slave-fermion partons without providing a complete operator construction. The two-boson realization below is exact algebra: a number constraint selects one spin representation. It does not treat an unconstrained enlarged Hilbert space as the original spin system.

## Definitions

Let a,b satisfy [a,a†]=[b,b†]=1 and all cross commutators zero on the algebraic Fock space. Define J₊=a†b, J₋=b†a, J_z=(N_a−N_b)/2 and N=N_a+N_b.

For an integer n≥0, the N=n sector has orthonormal basis |r,n−r⟩, r=0,…,n.

## Result name

Schwinger bosons realize the spin algebra on each fixed-particle sector

## Result statement

[J_z,J₊]=J₊, [J_z,J₋]=−J₋ and [J₊,J₋]=2J_z. The N=n sector is invariant, has dimension n+1 and Casimir J_z²+(J₊J₋+J₋J₊)/2=(n/2)(n/2+1)I.

## Proof

Use the canonical commutators to obtain [N_a,a†]=a† and [N_b,b]=−b. These prove the first two identities, and all J operators commute with N because every raising in one mode lowers the other. Next J₊J₋=N_a(N_b+1) and J₋J₊=N_b(N_a+1); their difference is N_a−N_b. Their symmetric average is N_aN_b+N/2. Adding J_z²=(N_a²−2N_aN_b+N_b²)/4 gives N²/4+N/2. On N=n this is the stated scalar. The ladder action J₊|r,n−r⟩=√((r+1)(n−r))|r+1,n−r−1⟩ connects every basis vector, so there is no proper invariant ladder subspace; this is spin j=n/2. The proof uses only finite particle sectors, avoiding unbounded-operator domain claims.

## Worked example: setup

Take n=2, corresponding to spin one.

## Worked example: calculation

The J_z eigenvalues are −1,0,1. J₊ connects the first to the second and the second to the third with coefficient √2; it annihilates the top state. The Casimir is 2I. For n=1 the dimension is two and the Casimir is 3I/4. These matrices can be verified symbolically by storing squared ladder coefficients.

## Worked example: conclusion

The construction has a precise constraint, matrix representation and complete algebraic certificate.

## Limits

The bosonic Fock space without N=n is a direct sum of many spins. A mean-field parton ansatz requires projection and an independent argument for observables; this algebra alone does not validate it.

## Extension

Derive a specified spin Hamiltonian in the constrained bosonic representation and compare exact small-system spectra before introducing any mean-field decoupling or thermodynamic-limit interpretation.
