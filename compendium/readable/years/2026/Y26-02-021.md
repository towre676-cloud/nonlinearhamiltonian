## Revised question retained in the annual source

What is the correctly signed second-order discriminant expansion for y″+(a−2q cos2t)y=0?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

Fourier perturbation derives the nonresonant characteristic-value correction, which then determines the trace of the π-period monodromy. Its analytic limit at a=0 has a negative q² term, correcting the sign in the retrieved assistant formula.

## Definitions

Use the Mathieu equation y″+(a−2q cos2t)y=0 and a Bloch expansion y=e^{iνt}∑_{n∈Z}c_n e^{2int}, normalized by c₀=1.

For fixed noninteger ν away from resonances, solve the coefficients perturbatively in small q.

The π-period monodromy has determinant one and eigenvalues e^{±iπν}; its discriminant is Δ=2cosπν.

## Result name

Second-order expansion with its removable limits

## Result statement

a=ν²+q²/[2(ν²−1)]+O(q⁴). Consequently, away from the initial resonant denominators, Δ=2cos(π√a)+πq²sin(π√a)/[2√a(a−1)]+O(q⁴). At a=0 this gives Δ=2−(π²/2)q²+O(q⁴).

## Proof

Substitution gives [a−(ν+2n)²]c_n=q(c_{n−1}+c_{n+1}). To first order, c₁=q/[ν²−(ν+2)²] and c_{−1}=q/[ν²−(ν−2)²]. Their sum is q/[2(ν²−1)]. The n=0 equation then gives the a expansion. Invert it as ν=√a−q²/[4√a(a−1)]+O(q⁴), and expand 2cosπν to obtain the trace correction. Although this inversion is unsuitable directly at ν=0, the monodromy trace is analytic in (a,q), and the displayed coefficient has a removable limit there: sin(π√a)/√a→π and a−1→−1. The sign is therefore negative. Translation t↦t+π/2 changes q to −q and conjugates monodromy, explaining evenness in q.

## Worked example: setup

Set a=0 and q=.02.

## Worked example: calculation

The second-order prediction is Δ≈2−π²(.02)²/2≈1.998026079. Independently integrating the two fundamental initial conditions over [0,π] checks the trace and determinant numerically. The lowest periodic characteristic value a₀(q)=−q²/2+O(q⁴) is consistent: substituting into Δ≈2−π²a−π²q²/2 restores Δ≈2.

## Worked example: conclusion

Two independent perturbative consistency checks identify the historical sign error. The coefficient’s analytic continuation is a property of the monodromy trace; choosing a single branch of the characteristic exponent is a separate local operation.

## Limits

A remainder uniform near every resonance requires a separate analytic argument; individual characteristic exponents have branch behavior even though the trace is analytic. Higher-order coefficient values are not certified by the second-order derivation.

## Extension
