## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which equivalence relation makes a finite observation system minimal, and what can a finite log fail to reveal?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

Future-trace equivalence gives a transition-compatible quotient and a minimal observable realization. A concrete parity machine shows why repeated state labels can be merged while finite traces only certify bounded-depth indistinguishability.

## Definitions

A deterministic observation machine consists of a finite state set S, alphabet Σ, transitions δ_a:S→S and output o:S→O.

Write δ_w for the transition along a word w, including δ_empty=id. Define s∼t when o(δ_w s)=o(δ_w t) for every finite word w.

The depth-r relation ∼_r checks only words of length at most r.

## Result name

Observable quotient and finite refinement

## Result statement

The relation ∼ is an equivalence compatible with every transition. Its quotient is observable, and any transition-respecting realization reproducing all outputs must distinguish its classes. For a finite machine, iterative output-partition refinement reaches ∼ after finitely many strict refinements.

## Proof

Reflexivity, symmetry and transitivity hold pointwise for each word. If s∼t, then for any word w the outputs after a followed by w agree, so δ_a s∼δ_a t. This makes quotient transitions well-defined. Distinct quotient classes have some distinguishing word by the definition of ∼, giving observability. A map that merged two such classes would preserve every future output and thereby contradict that word. For the algorithm, start by grouping equal present outputs; replace a block by the tuple consisting of its output and the current blocks of its a-successors. The kth partition equals ∼_k by induction. Any strict refinement increases the number of nonempty blocks, so there are at most |S|−1 strict refinements. Once a refinement is stable, induction on word length proves that it already tests every future word.

## Worked example: setup

Take states 0,1,2,3, a single input a with δ_a(s)=s+1 mod 4, and output o(s)=s mod 2.

## Worked example: calculation

States 0 and 2 give the same alternating trace 0,1,0,1,…; states 1 and 3 give 1,0,1,0,…. The quotient therefore has two states and δ_a swaps them. If the output were instead 1 only at state 3, all four states would become distinguishable by words of lengths at most three.

## Worked example: conclusion

The quotient depends on declared observations and inputs, rather than on names attached to internal states.

## Limits

Finite logged traces do not establish equality of all untested futures without a model or completeness bound. The recovered M29/F7 and dynamical-invariant labels do not supply a finite transition table or an equivalence theorem between unrelated theories.

## Extension

For a supplied system, compute the stable partition and shortest distinguishing word for every pair of quotient states. For probabilistic machines, replace point outputs by distributions and prove when bisimulation agrees with the chosen trace semantics.
