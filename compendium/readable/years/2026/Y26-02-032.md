## Revised question retained in the annual source

What contribution stream would reach a specified nominal target under a constant-return model?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

An exact linear recurrence separates contributions, assumed returns and payouts. It turns the historical numerical target into a transparent mathematical scenario, without treating a proposed allocation or future return as established fact.

## Definitions

Let W_n be a fund balance after year n, W₀=0, r a constant net annual return and c a constant contribution paid at year end. Use W_{n+1}=(1+r)W_n+c.

Assume r>−1 and specify that all values are nominal. Fees, inflation and uncertain returns are absent unless introduced explicitly.

For a monthly payout D to P recipients, the annual payout is 12DP, a flow with different units from the asset stock.

## Result name

Exact target and payout arithmetic

## Result statement

For r≠0, W_n=c[(1+r)^n−1]/r; at r=0 it equals nc. A target T at year n requires c=Tr/[(1+r)^n−1]. A balance by itself does not determine a sustainable payout.

## Proof

Iterate the recurrence to get c∑_{j=0}^{n−1}(1+r)^j and sum the geometric series. The r→0 limit is n by the derivative of (1+r)^n. If a fixed annual payout b is also made, replace c by c−b in the same recurrence; a nonnegative nominal equilibrium under this simplified accounting needs inflow plus modeled return to cover b. For variable returns, the exact balance is a weighted sum of contributions multiplied by subsequent return products, so order and volatility matter. Neither an expected return nor a portfolio percentage makes the constant-r path certain.

## Worked example: setup

Take T=10 trillion units and n=20.

## Worked example: calculation

At r=0 the required yearly contribution is .5 trillion. At the explicitly hypothetical r=.05, it is about .302426 trillion because 1.05²⁰≈2.653298. For P=100 million, monthly D=200 costs .24 trillion annually, while D=800 costs .96 trillion. These are arithmetic flows, not forecasts of affordable distributions.

## Worked example: conclusion

The target, horizon, contribution timing and payout denominator are all visible in the calculation.

## Limits

This entry supplies no investment recommendation or prediction. Inflation, taxes, risk, demographics and changes in contributions can materially alter the model; historical policy targets remain proposals.

## Extension

Replace constant r by a stated stochastic return model, derive the distribution of terminal wealth and shortfall probability, and compare real rather than nominal targets with an explicit inflation process.
