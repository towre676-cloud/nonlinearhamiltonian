## Definitions

Represent A=C² on H=C² by diagonal matrices diag(a_1,a_2). Let D=[[0,m],[conj(m),0]] with m≠0.

For the two evaluation states δ_i, define d(δ_1,δ_2)=sup{|a_1−a_2|:a=a*,||[D,a]||≤1}. This is the distance formula in this explicitly specified finite model.

## Result name

A two-point spectral distance can be computed from the commutator norm

## Result statement

d(δ_1,δ_2)=1/|m|. If m=0, the two states have infinite distance under the same supremum.

## Proof

For self-adjoint a, the commutator is [[0,m(a_2−a_1)],[conj(m)(a_1−a_2),0]]. Its adjoint times itself is |m|²|a_1−a_2|²I, so its operator norm is |m||a_1−a_2|. The Lipschitz constraint is therefore precisely |a_1−a_2|≤1/|m|. This upper bound is attained by a_1=0 and a_2=1/|m|, proving the distance. If m=0 the constraint imposes no restriction on the difference, so the supremum diverges. The calculation shows exactly where a geometry enters: in the specified operator D and its interaction with the representation. An algebra alone does not determine a metric; changing |m| continuously changes the distance while leaving the algebra and its two states unchanged.
