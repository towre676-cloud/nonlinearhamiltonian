## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which observable genuinely factors through a projection class, and which gap values are only allowed rather than guaranteed?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The source’s restricted factorization through K₀ is correct for a spectral projection separated by a gap. It does not imply that every numerical invariant, every energy or every allowed trace label is realized as an open gap.

## Definitions

Let A be a unital C*-algebra with trace τ and let H=H*∈A. If E lies in a spectral gap, the function equal to one below E and zero above E is continuous on σ(H).

Functional calculus therefore defines P_E=1_((−∞,E])(H)∈A, and N(E)=τ(P_E).

## Result name

Projection-class factorization and gap stability

## Result statement

N(E)=τ*([P_E]). Along a norm-continuous path of self-adjoint H_t for which a common gap around E stays open, [P_E(t)] and N_t(E) are constant.

## Proof

Because the indicator is continuous on the spectrum separated by the gap, continuous functional calculus places P_E in A and gives P_E²=P_E=P_E*. The definition of τ* on projection classes yields the factorization. With a uniform gap, one can choose a single continuous cutoff function agreeing with the indicators near every spectrum; functional calculus then makes t↦P_E(t) norm-continuous. A norm-continuous projection path represents one K₀ class. For nearby projections this follows from their unitary equivalence when their norm difference is less than one; compactness divides the path into such neighborhoods. Thus the trace is locally and globally constant along the path.

## Worked example: setup

Use A=M₃(C), normalized trace, and H=diag(−2,1,4).

## Worked example: calculation

For E=0, P_E=diag(1,0,0) and N(E)=1/3. Any self-adjoint perturbation of norm below 1/2 leaves the eigenvalue separation around zero open, so the same normalized count remains 1/3. The algebra allows label 2/3, but this particular gap at zero does not have that label.

## Worked example: conclusion

Gap labeling supplies an exact obstruction and stability statement for a named observable.

## Limits

Inside the spectrum, the indicator need not lie in A. A trace range lists possible labels and is not a theorem that every label opens a gap.

## Extension

For the original operator family, prove the relevant gap is open, identify its K₀ class and bound the perturbation size that preserves it.
