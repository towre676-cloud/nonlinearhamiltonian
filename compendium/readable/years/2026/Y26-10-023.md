## Revised question retained in the annual source

How can a decision be certified when separate interval bounds overlap but the competing scores share the same uncertainty?

## Status

Classical mathematics or a newly chosen finite model developed with proof; historical claims delimited

## Summary

The recovered final October target is to certify a decision using common uncertainty and the cheapest sufficient measurement. Joint modeling preserves cancellation that is destroyed by independent interval bounds. The following theorem is deterministic and concerns abstract scores, with no empirical or financial forecast assumed.

## Definitions

Let X be a nonempty uncertainty set and let J_A,J_B:X→R be two scores, with larger preferred. Set δ(x)=J_A(x)−J_B(x).

A strict robust certificate is a positive lower bound m≤inf_(x∈X)δ(x). Attainment of the infimum is unnecessary if a bound is proved directly.

## Result name

Joint dominance and measurement certificates

## Result statement

If inf_X(J_A−J_B)>0, A wins for every compatible state. In the shared model J_A=c+δ, J_B=c with δ∈[7/4,9/4], the margin is at least 7/4 regardless of the range of c. Separate interval subtraction can fail to certify the same fact.

## Proof

The first assertion is immediate from the defining inequality for every x. Its content is the order of operations: subtraction must take place at the same uncertainty state before minimization. In general inf(A−B)≥inf A−sup B, but the inequality can be strict because the separate extrema need not occur at compatible states. For c∈[0,100], separate score ranges are A∈[7/4,409/4] and B∈[0,100]; their interval difference includes −393/4, even though the exact shared difference is always positive. A new measurement with result z and error bound ε restricts X to X_z={x:|h(x)−z|≤ε}; computing the margin on X_z can only improve its lower bound because X_z⊆X. A decision-sufficient experiment is one whose every nonempty outcome fibre gives a positive margin for one of the two directions.

## Worked example: setup

Suppose δ itself is measured with absolute error at most 1/10.

## Worked example: calculation

A result z=1/4 implies δ≥z−1/10=3/20>0, certifying A. A result z=1/20 gives δ∈[−1/20,3/20], so it does not certify either direction. With several possible tests, their costs and outcome fibres can be inserted into the finite Bellman recurrence to identify the cheapest guaranteed decision procedure.

## Worked example: conclusion

The certificate exploits correlation exactly and states which remaining uncertainty matters.

## Limits

Overlapping separate intervals do not imply an undecidable joint model, while a positive estimate without an error bound is not a robust certificate. This theorem supplies no calibration of an empirical uncertainty set.

## Extension

Use polynomial or rational score models on a semialgebraic X and produce an exact positivity certificate for J_A−J_B; then optimize measurement cost subject to such certificates on every admissible outcome fibre.
