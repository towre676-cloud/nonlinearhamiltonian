## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which sensitivity follows from a resolvent bound, and what extra data are needed for a Hessian condition number?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; recovered claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The source asserts a proportionality involving a Hessian condition number, directional resolvent gain and metric eigenvalues. Such a universal identity is unsupported without defining the objective and its parameterization. A precise derivative formula gives a useful replacement.

## Definitions

For an invertible matrix M(θ), define R(θ)=M(θ)⁻¹ and use the Euclidean operator norm. Assume M is twice differentiable in θ.

A stable system can use M=iωI−A(θ). The Hessian of a separate scalar objective additionally depends on its output map and second derivatives.

## Result name

Inverse derivative and a sharp limitation

## Result statement

DR[H]=−RHR when H is the perturbation of M; hence ||DR[H]||≤||R||²||H||. No Hessian-condition identity depending on R alone holds for arbitrary scalar objectives.

## Proof

Differentiate MR=I to obtain H R+M DR[H]=0, and multiply by R on the left. Differentiating again along affine M gives D²R[H,K]=RHRKR+RKRHR, bounded by 2||R||³||H||||K||. For the limitation, hold A=−I fixed, so the resolvent at ω=0 is I. Choose a parameter objective f_c(θ)=θ₁²/2+cθ₂²/2 for any c≥1. Its Hessian has condition number c, while every system resolvent remains unchanged. Even for a fixed objective, changing parameter units rescales its Hessian by a congruence. An invariant comparison therefore requires an explicitly transported parameter metric.

## Worked example: setup

Perturb the scalar matrix M=2 by h.

## Worked example: calculation

R(h)=1/(2+h), R′(0)=−1/4 and R″(0)=1/4. The derivative bound ||R||²=1/4 is attained for H=1. The independently chosen f_100 has Hessian condition 100 although the unperturbed resolvent is 1/2.

## Worked example: conclusion

A valid gain-to-sensitivity estimate emerges, with a transparent counterexample to universal curvature identification.
