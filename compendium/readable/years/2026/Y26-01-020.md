## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which assumptions turn a Fokker–Planck equation into a provable entropy-dissipating dynamics?

## Status

Developed mathematical treatment with explicit hypotheses, derivation and checked example

## Summary

Writing the drift–diffusion equation relative to its invariant density reveals a nonnegative Fisher-information dissipation. A Gaussian Ornstein–Uhlenbeck solution gives a complete entropy curve, while exponential decay for general data requires an additional functional inequality.

## Definitions

Let π=Z⁻¹e^{−V} be a positive probability density on R^d, and ρ solve ∂_tρ=div(ρ∇V)+Δρ with enough smoothness and decay for integration by parts.

The relative entropy is D(ρ||π)=∫ρ log(ρ/π). Its relative Fisher information is I(ρ||π)=∫ρ|∇log(ρ/π)|².

Equivalently the PDE is ∂_tρ=div(ρ∇log(ρ/π)), conserving total mass under zero flux at infinity.

## Result name

Entropy dissipation identity and Gaussian benchmark

## Result statement

d_tD(ρ_t||π)=−I(ρ_t||π)≤0. If π satisfies D≤I/(2λ) with λ>0, then D(ρ_t||π)≤e^{−2λt}D(ρ₀||π).

## Proof

Differentiate the entropy. The derivative of ρ log(ρ/π) gives (1+log(ρ/π))∂_tρ. The term with one integrates to zero by mass conservation. Substitute the divergence form and integrate by parts to obtain minus the stated squared gradient integral. If the log-Sobolev inequality is assumed, this becomes D′≤−2λD; multiply by e^{2λt} and integrate. Dissipation alone gives monotonicity, not a uniform exponential rate, so the functional inequality is a genuine hypothesis. For V=|x|²/2 the invariant density is the standard Gaussian and the Gaussian log-Sobolev constant is λ=1.

## Worked example: setup

In one dimension take ρ₀=N(m₀,v₀), V=x²/2.

## Worked example: calculation

The solution remains Gaussian with m_t=m₀e^{−t} and v_t=1+(v₀−1)e^{−2t}. Its entropy is D=.5[m_t²+v_t−1−log v_t]. For m₀=2,v₀=1, this is exactly 2e^{−2t}, and I=m_t²=4e^{−2t}; therefore D′=−I and the exponential bound is attained. The stationary mean-zero variance-one state has both D and I zero.

## Worked example: conclusion

An actual density evolution verifies the dissipation coefficient and the sharp translation-mode rate.

## Limits

A generic diffusion may have no normalizable invariant density or no positive log-Sobolev constant. The formula is not automatically valid for arbitrary non-gradient drifts, changing reference measures or jump terms.

## Extension

Add a reversible jump generator and derive its entropy production separately. Establish a combined functional inequality with a computable constant, then compare entropy contraction with geometric convergence in Wasserstein distance under explicit convexity assumptions.
