## Revised question retained in the annual source

What can elementary arithmetic actually prove about the rational rank of the recovered curve?

## Status

Classical result; recovered proposal repaired

## Summary

A numerical deformation of Frobenius traces is not a rank theorem. For the same curve, a short exact argument proves a genuine rank lower bound by identifying a non-torsion rational point. This separates an established statement from the much stronger BSD claim.

## Definitions

The rational points of an elliptic curve form an abelian group with identity O, the point at infinity.

At a good prime p, reduction is injective on rational torsion whose order is prime to p. This classical reduction theorem is the only structural input used here.

## Result name

A certified non-torsion point

## Result statement

For E:y²+y=x³−x, the torsion subgroup E(Q)_tors is trivial. Consequently P=(0,0) has infinite order and rank E(Q)≥1.

## Proof

The preceding exact counts give #E(F5)=8 and #E(F7)=9. Suppose E(Q) contains torsion of prime order ℓ. If ℓ≠5, injectivity of ℓ-torsion under reduction at5 forces ℓ|8, so ℓ=2. But reduction at7, where ℓ≠7, then forces2|9, impossible. If ℓ=5, reduction at7 forces5|9, also impossible. Every nontrivial finite abelian group has an element of prime order, so rational torsion is trivial. The point(0,0) lies on the curve and differs from O; it is therefore non-torsion. A non-torsion element supplies a copy of Z inside E(Q), proving the rank lower bound. This does not require knowing the analytic order of vanishing of L(E,s). The two reduction primes are used asymmetrically only to respect the prime-to-p restriction. In general, gcd(#E(Fp),#E(Fq))=1 at distinct good primes does not justify simply quoting an unrestricted injection twice; the proof must separately exclude possible p-primary and q-primary torsion. Those exclusions are explicit here.

## Worked example: setup

Check several multiples of P by the generalized Weierstrass group law.

## Worked example: calculation

Negation is(x,y)↦(x,−y−1). The tangent at P has slope−1, giving2P=(1,0). Adding P gives3P=(−1,−1). Further exact rational additions yield7P=(−5/9,8/27); substitution gives y²+y=280/729 and x³−x=280/729. The non-torsion conclusion itself comes from the reduction argument, not from merely observing several nonzero multiples.

## Worked example: conclusion

An exact arithmetic certificate proves rank at least1 while leaving rank upper bounds and BSD outside its scope.

## Limits

The injectivity theorem is stated with its prime-to-p restriction; ignoring that restriction would invalidate many reduction arguments. This proof does not establish rank exactly1, finiteness of the Tate–Shafarevich group or the BSD leading coefficient.

## Extension

For a full rank computation, add a rigorously documented descent giving an upper bound1. Treat an analytic L-value calculation as a separate theorem with explicit error control.
