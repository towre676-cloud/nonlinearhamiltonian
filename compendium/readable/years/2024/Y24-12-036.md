## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which factor of two belongs in the recovered one-loop coupling formula?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered formula wrote a coupling with log(μ²/Λ²) while also giving a derivative with respect to logμ. These logarithm conventions differ by a factor of two. Direct integration identifies the consistent prefactor and avoids importing a numerical physics claim.

## Definitions

Assume dα/dlogμ=−(β₀/(2π))α², withβ₀constant.

The reference scaleΛ is defined by the integration constant; the formula is used only where its denominator is nonzero.

## Result name

Consistent one-loop model formula

## Result statement

The stated differential equation integrates toα(μ)=2π/[β₀log(μ/Λ)]=4π/[β₀log(μ²/Λ²)]. A numerator2π overlog(μ²/Λ²) instead satisfies a beta function twice as large.

## Proof

Differentiate1/α with respect tologμ to getβ₀/(2π). Integration gives1/α=(β₀/(2π))log(μ/Λ), proving the first expression. Since log(μ²/Λ²)=2log(μ/Λ), the second expression follows. To audit the inconsistent variantα=2π/(β₀L) withL=log(μ²/Λ²), use dL/dlogμ=2. Its derivative is−4π/(β₀L²), whileα²=4π²/(β₀²L²), so dα/dlogμ=−(β₀/π)α². The distinction is entirely a chain-rule calculation. Coupling definitions may introduce other factors, but they must be changed consistently in both the ODE and its solution.

## Worked example: setup

Takeβ₀=9 andμ/Λ=e.

## Worked example: calculation

The stated ODE givesα=2π/9. Expressing the same value throughL=2 requires4π/(9·2)=2π/9. Using2π/(9·2)=π/9 halves the coupling and fails the specified differential equation. The reference scale ratio is dimensionless, so no logarithm of a dimensional quantity appears.

## Worked example: conclusion

The exact integration resolves the historical normalization ambiguity and provides a reusable consistency check.

## Limits

The equation is an assumed truncated model. It does not establish a continuum quantum field theory or validate a measured strong coupling at any scale. The number of flavors and matching thresholds would require further physical input.

## Extension

For higher-order beta functions, choose one coupling convention and integrate with an explicit remainder estimate on a fixed perturbative interval.
