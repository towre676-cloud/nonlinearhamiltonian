## Revised question retained in the annual source

## Summary

The recovered source states a288-bit claim and benchmark score but gives no parameters or reduction. An exact coordinate-change theorem is a meaningful mathematical replacement: it states when a modified noisy linear equation is merely the same sample distribution in different coordinates.

## Definitions

OverZ/qZ, an LWE-form sample is(a,b), with a∈(Z/qZ)^d andb=aᵀs+e, for a specified secret and error distribution.

LetT be an invertibled×d matrix overZ/qZ and set a′=Tᵀa,s′=T⁻¹s.

## Result name

Exact invariance under an invertible change of basis

## Result statement

The equation is unchanged: a′ᵀs′=aᵀs. Uniforma remains uniforma′. The secret distribution becomes its pushforward underT⁻¹, while the scalar error distribution is unchanged.

## Proof

Transpose multiplication gives a′ᵀs′=aᵀTT⁻¹s=aᵀs, so the sameb ande satisfy the transformed equation. The map a↦Tᵀa is a bijection of the finite module and therefore preserves the uniform distribution exactly. The map on secrets is likewise a bijection, but it preserves a particular prior only if that prior is invariant under the map; a small-coordinate distribution usually is not. Thus exact sample equivalence includes the transformed secret law, not merely the equation. Over a composite modulus, invertibility requires detT to be a unit moduloq, equivalently gcd(detT,q)=1. A noninvertible map can collapse samples and secrets, so the inverse reduction is unavailable.

## Worked example: setup

Letq=7,T=[[1,1],[0,1]],s=(2,3),a=(4,5) ande=1.

## Worked example: calculation

T⁻¹=[[1,−1],[0,1]], sos′=(6,3) mod7. Alsoa′=(4,2) andb=4·2+5·3+1=24≡3. The transformed product is4·6+2·3+1=31≡3, confirming the identical sample equation. The determinant is1, so the inverse exists over every modulus.

The verified algebra says exactly what this transformation preserves. It supplies no numerical security level.
