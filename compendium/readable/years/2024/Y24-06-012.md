Which metric determines the Christoffel symbols, curvature and geodesics, and how are the disk and half-plane models related?

## Summary

The recovered passage discussed Riemannian geometry, geodesics and hyperbolic models. Specifying the metric makes these claims testable. The following calculations use the upper half-plane, give its curvature, and check an actual geodesic rather than inferring geometry from a picture.

## Definitions

Let H={x+iy:y>0} with metric ds²=(dx²+dy²)/y². Use the Levi-Civita connection of this metric.

For a conformal metric e^(2u)(dx²+dy²), Gaussian curvature is K=−e^(−2u)(u_xx+u_yy). A geodesic is affinely parametrized when its covariant acceleration vanishes.

## Result name

Hyperbolic curvature and geodesics in a declared metric

## Result statement

For the stated metric, the only nonzero Christoffel symbols are Γˣ_xy=Γˣ_yx=−1/y, Γʸ_xx=1/y and Γʸ_yy=−1/y. Its curvature is −1. Vertical lines x=x₀, y=y₀e^t are unit-speed geodesics. The Cayley map w=(z−i)/(z+i) identifies this metric with 4|dw|²/(1−|w|²)² on the unit disk.

## Proof

The Christoffel formula Γᵏ_ij=(1/2)g^(kℓ)(∂ig_jℓ+∂jg_iℓ−∂ℓg_ij) uses g_xx=g_yy=y⁻² and inverse y²I. Differentiating yields the displayed coefficients. Put u=−log y: u_xx=0 and u_yy=y⁻², so K=−y²y⁻²=−1. For the vertical curve, ẋ=ẍ=0 and ẏ=ÿ=y, so its second geodesic equation ÿ+(ẋ²−ẏ²)/y=0 holds; its speed squared is ẏ²/y²=1. Finally |dw|²=4|dz|²/|z+i|⁴ and 1−|w|²=4y/|z+i|². Substitution proves equality of the two metrics, including their scale.

## Worked example: setup

Find the hyperbolic distance along a vertical segment from iy₁ to iy₂.

## Worked example: calculation

Its length is the integral of |dy|/y, giving |log(y₂/y₁)|. A general curve joining these endpoints has length at least ∫|dy|/y≥|log(y₂/y₁)|, so the vertical segment attains the minimum. In particular i and ie² are distance2 apart although their Euclidean separation is e²−1. The exponential parametrization is therefore a consequence of the metric rather than an arbitrary time redefinition. The same vertical-distance proof works for arbitrary positive endpoints without a small-distance approximation. The logarithm is dimensionless because it depends on a ratio, and exchanging the endpoints changes its sign before the absolute value. This also verifies symmetry of that particular distance formula.

## Worked example: conclusion

Metric, connection, curvature and distance agree in one explicit example. This fixes the normalization that an isolated claim of negative curvature leaves unspecified.

## Limits

Curvature sign conventions for the Riemann tensor can differ, but Gaussian curvature of this declared metric is −1 in the standard sectional-curvature convention. No application to neural or physical spacetime is inferred.

## Extension

Use a real Möbius transformation to obtain the remaining geodesics as semicircles perpendicular to the boundary and transport the length calculation by isometry.
