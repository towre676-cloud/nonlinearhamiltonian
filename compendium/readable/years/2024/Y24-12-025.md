## Revised question retained in the annual source

What is the precise lattice associated with rational points, and what does it have to do with a crystal lattice?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered discussion identified Mordell–Weil rank with generators and torsion with repetition, then extended this to mineral symmetries. The actual arithmetic object is an abstract finitely generated group whose free part carries a canonical height pairing; it is not inherently a three-dimensional atomic lattice.

## Definitions

For an elliptic curve E/Q, E(Q)≅Z^r⊕T with T finite.

Use a canonical quadratic height q(P)=ĥ(P), with q(nP)=n²q(P), q(P)≥0 and q(P)=0 exactly for torsion over Q.

## Result name

Two-torsion and a well-defined positive height form

## Result statement

On E:y²=x³−x, the rational points of order dividing2 are O,(−1,0),(0,0),(1,0), forming C₂×C₂. More generally the pairing b(P,Q)=[q(P+Q)−q(P)−q(Q)]/2 descends to a positive definite form on the real span of the free quotient.

## Proof

In a short Weierstrass equation inverse is(x,y)↦(x,−y). A nonidentity point has order two precisely when it equals its inverse, hence y=0. The cubic x(x−1)(x+1) has exactly the displayed three rational roots, giving all rational2-torsion. The line y=0 intersects in those three points, so the chord-and-tangent law makes their sum O and the group is C₂². For the height statement, adding a torsion point does not change the canonical height; thus the polarization descends to the quotient. The standard canonical-height theorem makes it a positive quadratic form on the free part. Choosing independent generators gives a Gram matrix, and its determinant is the regulator in the selected pairing convention.

## Worked example: calculation

The Gram matrix is[[2,1],[1,2]], with eigenvalues1,3 and determinant3. The norm of P−Q has square2+2−2=2. A torsion point T has q(T)=0 and b(T,P)=0, so it disappears from this real metric space; it is not a nonzero periodic translation vector of positive Euclidean length.

## Worked example: conclusion

Rank, torsion, and the height lattice are mathematically precise but do not identify an atomic space group or E₈ representation without additional data.

## Limits

The two-torsion calculation does not prove the full rank or torsion group of the example. No unsupported mineral polynomial is retained. The cited height theorem is background, not reproved from first principles.

## Extension

For documented independent generators, compute certified height-pairing intervals and bound the regulator away from zero before claiming independence.
