## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which frame equations are intrinsic geometric identities, and how do they change on a curved manifold?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The historical Beta-frame equations reproduce the Frenet–Serret system under an arc-length and positive-curvature hypothesis. They describe local rotation of an orthonormal moving frame; their coefficients are not automatically new physical tensor components.

## Definitions

Let γ(s) be a C³ unit-speed curve in oriented Euclidean three-space with curvature κ=||γ″||>0.

Set T=γ′, N=T′/κ, B=T×N, and τ=−B′·N.

## Result name

Frenet–Serret equations and the Darboux vector

## Result statement

T′=κN, N′=−κT+τB, B′=−τN. The vector Ω=τT+κB satisfies E′=Ω×E for E=T,N,B.

## Proof

Because the frame is orthonormal, differentiating its pairwise dot products makes its derivative matrix skew-symmetric. The definition gives T′=κN and therefore N′·T=−κ. Since T′·B=0 and B′·T=−B·T′=0, B′ lies along N; its coefficient is −τ. Orthogonality then gives N′·B=τ and no N-component, proving all three equations. Cross products T×N=B, B×T=N and T×B=−N verify the Darboux expression. On an oriented Riemannian three-manifold, replace ordinary derivatives by ∇_T and use the metric-compatible cross product; covariant frame equations still describe motion along the curve. Ambient curvature enters comparisons around loops and commutators of covariant derivatives, not an arbitrary extra term in each Euclidean formula.

## Worked example: setup

Take γ(s)=(a cos(s/c),a sin(s/c),bs/c) with c=√(a²+b²) and a>0.

## Worked example: calculation

Its speed is one, κ=a/(a²+b²), and τ=b/(a²+b²). For a=b=1 these are both 1/2. A planar circle has b=0, hence τ=0 and a fixed binormal; a straight line has κ=0 and the Frenet normal is undefined, requiring a different frame such as a parallel-transport frame.

## Worked example: conclusion

The equations have exact hypotheses, a geometric derivation, and a worked nonplanar example.

## Limits

At zero curvature, the Frenet construction fails even for a smooth curve. A frame equation by itself supplies no field equation, stress tensor, or unification with quantum groups.

## Extension

Use a Bishop frame to pass smoothly through curvature zeros, and compute holonomy separately when the ambient connection has nonzero curvature.
