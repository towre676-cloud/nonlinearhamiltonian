## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which residual representation and coefficient category define the deformation functor, and what do tangent directions and obstructions actually compute?

## Status

Revised classical development with scoped worked result

## Summary

A finite-group character gives a complete toy deformation problem. It makes the difference between a nonzero tangent space and a smooth deformation space explicit, without pretending to reconstruct an absent Galois representation or Eisenstein ideal. The group is deliberately small so that the full relation can be checked by polynomial arithmetic.

## Definitions

Let G be cyclic of order two, generated by g. Over a field k, take the trivial one-dimensional residual character. A deformation over a local Artinian k-algebra sends g to a unit u≡1 modulo its maximal ideal and must satisfy u²=1. Conjugation is trivial in dimension one.

## Result name

Characteristic two supplies a nontrivial obstructed tangent direction

## Result statement

Over k[ε]/(ε²), write u=1+aε. The character relation is2a=0. For characteristic different from two, the tangent space is zero. In characteristic two it is one-dimensional, represented by arbitrary a. The tangent u=1+ε does not lift to u=1+η+bη² over k[η]/(η³) for any b.

## Proof

Expand(1+aε)²=1+2aε because ε²=0. In characteristic two the linear relation disappears. For a prospective second-order lift, square1+η+bη² moduloη³: all doubled terms vanish and η² survives, yielding1+η² rather than1. Thus no choice of b solves the relation. The complete fixed-characteristic local relation can be represented by k[[t]]/(t²), since u=1+t and u²=1 is exactly t²=0 in characteristic two.

## Worked example: setup

Compare the number of first-order characters over F₂ and F₃.

## Worked example: calculation

Over F₂ there are two, with a=0 or1; over F₃ only a=0 works. The nontrivial F₂ tangent direction is obstructed at the next order. A one-dimensional tangent space therefore does not imply a one-dimensional smooth formal family.

## Worked example: conclusion

The deformation functor, its coefficient category and its relation determine what the tangent calculation means.

## Limits

This is a finite-group character example, not a proof about G_Q, a modularity theorem or an Eisenstein deformation ring. Mixed-characteristic deformation rings need their own base ring; the relation would be2t+t² rather than simply t².

## Extension

Specify a residual matrix representation and local conditions, compute its H¹ tangent data and an H² obstruction, then test whether the resulting functor has a representing ring.
