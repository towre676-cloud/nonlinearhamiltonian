## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which covariance condition makes the recovered Gaussian cylinder data an actual Hilbert-space-valued random variable?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

Consistent finite-dimensional Gaussian distributions need not live in the proposed infinite-dimensional space. On a separable Hilbert space, a positive trace-class covariance gives a direct construction, while identity covariance supplies the decisive counterexample.

## Definitions

Take H=ℓ² with orthonormal basis e_n and eigenvalues q_n≥0.

Let Z_n be independent standard real normal random variables.

## Result name

Trace-class diagonal Gaussian construction

## Result statement

If Σq_n<∞, then X=Σ√q_n Z_ne_n converges in H almost surely and in mean square, with characteristic function E exp(i⟨h,X⟩)=exp(−Σq_n h_n²/2). Identity covariance does not define an ℓ²-valued Gaussian.

## Proof

The nonnegative random variable S=Σq_nZ_n² has expectation Σq_n by monotone convergence. Finite expectation implies S<∞ almost surely, proving that the coordinate series is square-summable almost surely. The tail mean square is Σ_{n>N}q_n→0, proving L² convergence. Finite-dimensional normal characteristic functions multiply, and dominated convergence passes to the limit, giving the displayed formula. Since ℓ² is a separable complete metric space, its Borel probability law is Radon. In contrast, for q_n=1, the strong law gives N^(−1)Σ_{n≤N}Z_n²→1 almost surely, so the squared norm diverges. The consistent cylinder marginals still exist as a product measure on R^N, but that measure gives ℓ² probability zero.

## Worked example: setup

Choose q_n=2^(−n), n≥1, and compare with q_n=1.

## Worked example: calculation

The trace is Σ2^(−n)=1, so E||X||²=1, and truncating after N coordinates has squared mean error 2^(−N). The identity-covariance truncation instead has expected squared norm N and no tight ℓ² limit. For any fixed radius R, the strong law shows its probability of lying in that radius tends to zero.

## Worked example: conclusion

The missing issue is support and tightness, not merely consistency of finite-dimensional marginals.

## Limits

This proves the diagonal Hilbert-space Gaussian case; general Sazonov extension theorems use an appropriate topology on the dual and require their own hypotheses. A pushforward also requires a specified measurable map and target space.

## Extension

For a general positive self-adjoint trace-class covariance, diagonalize it by the compact spectral theorem and repeat the construction; compare the Cameron–Martin range with the sample space.
