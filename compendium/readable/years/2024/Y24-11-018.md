## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which of the recovered Harper spectral statements follow directly from the operator, and how should rational bands be counted?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The Harper discussion contains genuine exact symmetries and a useful rational-flux reduction. “Q bands” should mean Q continuous band branches counted with multiplicity; adjacent branches may meet, so it does not imply Q disjoint intervals or Q−1 open gaps.

## Definitions

On ℓ²(Z), define (H_{α,θ}u)_n=u_{n+1}+u_{n−1}+2cos(2παn+θ)u_n.

At α=P/Q in lowest terms, the coefficients are Q-periodic and a Bloch fiber satisfies u_{n+Q}=e^(ik)u_n.

## Result name

Spectral bound and flux symmetry

## Result statement

H is bounded self-adjoint with ||H||≤4, so its spectrum lies in [−4,4]. H_{α+1,θ}=H_{α,θ}; H_{−α,θ}=H_{α,−θ}. The union over θ is symmetric under E↦−E.

## Proof

The forward and backward shifts each have norm one and are adjoints; the real diagonal cosine operator has norm at most two. The triangle inequality gives the norm bound and self-adjointness. Periodicity in α follows because 2πn is an integer multiple of 2π. The reflection identity follows from cosine’s evenness. Let (Ju)_n=(−1)^nu_n; conjugating the shifts changes their signs, whereas the diagonal potential is unchanged. Therefore JH_{α,θ}J=−H_{α,θ+π}, proving energy reflection for the phase-union spectrum. For rational flux, Bloch decomposition reduces to a Q-dimensional Hermitian fiber depending continuously on k and θ; its Q ordered eigenvalues are continuous branches, whose ranges may touch.

## Worked example: setup

At α=1/2, write even and odd coordinates and use the two-site Bloch phase.

## Worked example: calculation

The fiber is [[2cosθ,1+e^(−ik)],[1+e^(ik),−2cosθ]]. Its eigenvalues are ±√(4cos²θ+|1+e^(ik)|²). At θ=π/2 and k=π both vanish, so the two branches meet. The phase-union range is [−2√2,2√2], consistent with the universal [−4,4] bound.

## Worked example: conclusion

The elementary facts are exact, while the rational band count is stated without asserting nonexistent open gaps.

## Limits

For irrational α, fine Cantor-spectrum and spectral-type results are deep and depend on coupling, arithmetic, and phase hypotheses. A positive Lyapunov exponent alone does not mean a finite sample has GUE statistics.

## Extension

Compute rational approximants with error control, distinguish the spectrum at fixed θ from the phase union, and track actual open gaps rather than merely sorting eigenvalues.
