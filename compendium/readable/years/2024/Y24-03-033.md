## Revised question retained in the annual source

Reconstruction: How must phase be chosen to make frequency sweep linearly, how many cycles occur in a fixed interval, and what frequency ambiguity remains after sampling?

## Status

## Summary

A time-dependent number placed directly into the argument of a cosine is not automatically its instantaneous frequency. Frequency is the derivative of phase divided by 2π. Starting from that definition fixes a common factor-of-two error and exposes an exact aliasing identity for sampled signals.

## Definitions

For the declared complex unit-amplitude signal z(t)=exp(iφ(t)) with differentiable unwrapped phase, define instantaneous frequency f(t)=φ′(t)/(2π), in cycles per unit time.

A linear chirp with starting frequency f₀ and rate κ is z(t)=exp(i[φ₀+2πf₀t+πκt²]). Its real part is the corresponding cosine.

Uniform samples have times tₙ=n/F_s. The phase must be interpreted as an unwrapped model; an isolated real cosine sample does not itself determine a unique phase.

## Result name

Integrated frequency and an exact aliasing identity

## Result statement

The stated chirp has f(t)=f₀+κt and net phase cycles N(T)=f₀T+κT²/2. Signals with f₀ replaced by f₀+ℓF_s, for any integer ℓ, have exactly the same complex samples. The discrete phase increment in cycles is [f₀+κ(n+1/2)/F_s]/F_s.

## Proof

Differentiate φ₀+2πf₀t+πκt² and divide by 2π to obtain the frequency. Integrating that frequency from zero to T gives the cycle count. In contrast, the tempting phase 2π(f₀+κt)t differentiates to frequency f₀+2κt, so it sweeps twice as fast. For sampled ambiguity, the extra starting frequency ℓF_s contributes phase 2πℓF_stₙ=2πℓn, whose complex exponential is one for every integer n. Thus no method using those samples alone can distinguish these models without a frequency prior. Finally subtract the quadratic phase at tₙ and tₙ₊₁; the difference of squares yields the displayed midpoint-frequency increment. If the modeled increment stays strictly between zero and half a cycle, the model’s successive phase steps are in the principal positive half-turn range. This condition describes the selected phase progression; it is not a proof that a finite-duration chirp is band limited.

## Worked example: setup

Take f₀=100Hz, κ=50Hz/s, T=2s and F_s=1000Hz.

## Worked example: calculation

The ending instantaneous frequency is 200Hz and the total net phase advance is 300 cycles. The naive unsafely substituted phase would end at 300Hz and accumulate 400 cycles. For n=0,…,1999 the correct sample increment ranges from 0.100025 to 0.199975 cycles. Increasing f₀ to 1100Hz gives the same 2001 complex samples at tₙ=n/1000 for n=0,…,2000, although its continuous frequencies differ by 1000Hz. The verifier proves the increment and cycle identities with rational arithmetic and checks a few exponential sample coincidences numerically.

## Worked example: conclusion

Integrating frequency gives the intended signal, and the exact sampling identity states a real nonidentifiability rather than an implementation defect.

## Limits

The instantaneous frequency of a real signal requires a chosen phase representation; zeros and multi-component mixtures can complicate that choice. A time-truncated chirp is not strictly band limited, so a Nyquist claim based solely on its endpoint instantaneous frequencies would overstate what was proved.

## Extension

For an arbitrary specified frequency law f(t), define φ(t)=φ₀+2π∫₀ᵗf(s)ds before sampling. An exponential frequency sweep then has exponential-integral phase rather than the quadratic phase of this linear model.
