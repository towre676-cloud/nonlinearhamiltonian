## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which prime ideal defines the circle, and how does a geometric scaling act on its coordinate ring?

## Status

Classical mathematics with newly authored derivation or finite example and explicit scope

## Summary

The historical answer invoked circle coordinate rings, prime ideals and scaling. A point prime is not the same thing as an irreducible component, and a map of spaces acts in the opposite direction on coordinate functions. Both distinctions can be checked in this small example.

## Definitions

Let A=R[x,y]/(x²+y²−1) and B=R[u,v]/(u²+v²−4).

A geometric scaling φ:(x,y)↦(2x,2y) induces φ*:B→A by substituting u↦2x and v↦2y.

## Result name

The circle is irreducible and its radius scaling has a contravariant map

## Result statement

The defining polynomial is irreducible over R and generates a prime ideal, so A is a domain. The scaling φ is an algebraic isomorphism with inverse division by two, and its pullback is well defined. The point ideal (x−1,y) in A is maximal but does not define an extra irreducible component.

## Proof

If x²+y²−1 factored nontrivially over R, its degree two would force two affine-linear factors. Their highest homogeneous parts would multiply to x²+y², which has no real linear factor: a nonzero real direction cannot make a sum of two squares vanish identically along a line. Thus the polynomial is irreducible. R[x,y] is a unique factorization domain, where irreducibles are prime, proving the domain assertion. Substituting into B’s relation gives 4x²+4y²−4=0 in A; substituting x↦u/2,y↦v/2 gives the inverse homomorphism. The quotient of A by (x−1,y) is R, proving maximality.

## Worked example: setup

Evaluate a coordinate function f(u,v)=u+v on the radius-two point φ(3/5,4/5).

## Worked example: calculation

The geometric point is (6/5,8/5) and f takes value14/5. The pulled-back function φ*f=2x+2y takes the same value14/5 at (3/5,4/5). This illustrates the reversed arrow B→A. Meanwhile the zero ideal of the domain A describes its single generic irreducible component; individual point ideals provide locations within that component. Evaluation at a real circle point is a surjective algebra map to R whose kernel is the ideal of coordinate functions vanishing there. The quotient is R, hence that ideal is maximal. This is why a point ideal and the zero ideal describing the irreducible component carry different information.

## Worked example: conclusion

Coordinate rings make maps and component claims exact when the relevant ideal and arrow direction are stated.

## Limits

Over another field, irreducibility and point interpretation must be reconsidered. Even over R, not every maximal ideal of a finitely generated algebra need have real residue field, so this example is not a classification of every prime.

## Extension

Use the rational parametrization on a suitable open subset to describe the localized coordinate-ring map and its missing point.
