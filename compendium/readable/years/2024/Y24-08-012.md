## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which algebra and sign convention realize the BV identity, and what bracket does the operator actually generate?

## Status

Revised classical development with scoped worked result

## Summary

A one-even-variable, one-odd-variable algebra gives an explicit model in which square-zero and bracket calculations can be completed. It turns the recovered BV definition into an actual example, while keeping the full Gerstenhaber identity a consequence of a correctly specified second-order operator rather than an arbitrary nilpotent linear map.

## Definitions

Let A=k[x]⊗Λ(θ), characteristic zero, with|x|=0, |θ|=1 andθ²=0. LetΔ=∂_x∂_θ, degree−1. For homogeneous a,b define{a,b}=(−1)^|a|[Δ(ab)−(Δa)b−(−1)^|a|aΔb].

## Result name

A square-zero second-order differential operator and its generated bracket

## Result statement

Δ²=0 and Δ has differential order at most two. For polynomials f,g, {f,g}=0, {f,gθ}=f′g, {fθ,g}=−fg′ and{fθ,gθ}=(f′g−fg′)θ. In particular{x,θ}=1 and{θ,x}=−1.

## Proof

Every element is f(x)+g(x)θ, andΔ sends it to g′(x), whose odd derivative vanishes on a second application. The operator is a composition of two derivations, giving order at most two, and it kills1. Substitute each of the four parity cases into the defining formula to obtain the bracket expressions. These expressions verify the degree−1 graded antisymmetry; differentiation of products gives the graded derivation law. The standard BV theorem, or a direct check of the finitely many parity cases using commuting ordinary derivatives, gives the odd Jacobi identity. Nilpotence without the second-order condition would not justify this conclusion.

## Worked example: setup

Take a=x² and b=xθ.

## Worked example: calculation

Δa=0, Δb=1 andΔ(ab)=Δ(x³θ)=3x². Thus{a,b}=3x²−x²=2x², agreeing with f′g=(2x)x. For two odd elements xθ and x²θ, the bracket is(x²−2x²)θ=−x²θ. The verifier represents even and odd polynomials separately and checks these formulas and sample Jacobi identities.

## Worked example: conclusion

The algebra, grading, operator order and signs now determine a checkable bracket.

## Limits

The example is a classical BV algebra. It does not supply a quantum field theory, a geometric BV operator on an unspecified space or a proof that every Gerstenhaber algebra admits such a generator.

## Extension

For a declared graded algebra, compute whether a proposed square-zero operator has differential order at most two and whether its induced bracket matches the intended geometry.
