## Revised question retained in the annual source

Can the thermal and Laplace-transform parts of the historical operator proposal be made exact?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

Thermal analyticity and resolvent transforms are genuine operator identities with different domains. A finite model establishes both and makes clear why they do not automatically produce an arithmetic L-function or a thermal state for an unconfined Coulomb system.

## Definitions

For a Hermitian matrix H and β>0, let ρ=e^(−βH)/Tr(e^(−βH)) and α_t(B)=e^(itH)Be^(−itH).

Set ω(B)=Tr(ρB), using units ℏ=1.

## Result name

Finite-dimensional KMS and Laplace identities

## Result statement

F_AB(z)=ω(Aα_z(B)) is entire and satisfies F_AB(t+iβ)=ω(α_t(B)A). For Re(s)>0, ∫₀∞e^(−st)e^(−itH)dt=(sI+iH)^(−1).

## Proof

In an eigenbasis H e_j=E_je_j, F_AB(z) is a finite sum of terms ρ_j A_jk B_kj exp(iz(E_k−E_j)); hence it is entire. Replacing z by t+iβ multiplies the summand by exp(−β(E_k−E_j)), changing ρ_j to ρ_k. Interchanging j,k gives precisely the reversed-order trace. This proves the KMS boundary relation with the stated sign convention. For the Laplace transform, the spectral theorem reduces each matrix component to ∫₀∞exp(−(s+iE_j)t)dt=1/(s+iE_j), valid because Re(s)>0. Reassembling the eigenbasis proves the resolvent identity; no formal exchange with an infinite sum is needed.

## Worked example: setup

Take H=diag(0,Δ), A=|0⟩⟨1| and B=|1⟩⟨0|.

## Worked example: calculation

F_AB(t)=ρ_0e^(itΔ). At t+iβ it becomes ρ_0e^(−βΔ)e^(itΔ)=ρ_1e^(itΔ), exactly ω(α_t(B)A). The Laplace transform is diag(1/s,1/(s+iΔ)); its poles encode the two chosen energies, not zeros of an independently specified zeta function.

## Worked example: conclusion

The thermal strip boundary and the transform have complete proofs, explicit conventions, and a directly testable two-level example.

## Limits

An infinite-volume hydrogen Hamiltonian does not have a finite Gibbs trace merely because it is self-adjoint; its continuum and accumulating bound states obstruct this naive partition function. Infinite-system KMS states require an algebraic framework.

## Extension

For a confined Schrödinger operator, prove heat-trace finiteness and appropriate trace bounds before replacing the finite sums by infinite spectral expansions.
