## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which parts of a proposed prime-and-zero moment identity can be justified without inventing convergence?

## Status

Classical result; recovered proposal repaired

## Summary

The recovered moment formula is nonstandard and its quantities, factors, branches and error term were not defined adequately. A rigorous replacement starts with a classical Euler product in its absolute-convergence domain and derives an explicit truncation error. A Hadamard product over zeros is a separate analytic construction.

## Definitions

For real σ>1 let Z_P(σ)=∏_{p≤P}(1−p^(−σ))⁻¹.

Absolute convergence permits expanding geometric series and reordering positive terms; for complex s one needs the corresponding absolute-value argument.

## Result name

A reproducible Euler-product tail bound

## Result statement

For σ>1 and integer P≥2, 0≤logζ(σ)−logZ_P(σ)≤P^(1−σ)/[(σ−1)(1−2^(−σ))].

## Proof

Unique factorization shows a finite product expands as the sum of n^(−σ) over integers whose prime factors are at mostP. Increasing P exhausts all integers, and monotone convergence yields ζ(σ). Next, −log(1−u)=Σ_{m≥1}u^m/m≤u/(1−u) for0≤u<1. Therefore the missing logarithm is at mostΣ_{p>P}p^(−σ)/(1−p^(−σ))≤(1−2^(−σ))⁻¹Σ_{n>P}n^(−σ). Since the summand decreases, the last tail is bounded by∫_P∞x^(−σ)dx=P^(1−σ)/(σ−1). This proves both convergence and the displayed certified inequality. It does not continue the product into the critical strip. The logarithmic enclosure can be exponentiated because all factors in the realσ>1 model are positive. For complexs, a chosen analytic logarithm is available through the absolutely convergent series, but an argument based only on positive inequalities no longer applies directly. The absolute-value majorant remains the appropriate starting point.

## Worked example: setup

Take σ=2 and P=100.

## Worked example: calculation

The logarithmic tail is at most1/[100(1−1/4)]=1/75. Thus Z_100(2)≤ζ(2)≤e^(1/75)Z_100(2), an explicit relative enclosure obtainable from finitely many primes. The bound is deliberately elementary and can be sharpened with arithmetic information.

## Worked example: conclusion

A product formula needs definitions, a region and a truncation theorem. These are missing from the historical mixed moment/zero expression.

## Limits

The proposed exponent involving λ(1−λ) has ambiguous grouping in the retrieved text and may require complex branch choices. No claimed O(T^(−1/2+ε)) estimate is accepted. The proof above concerns only the classical zeta Euler product for σ>1.

## Extension

For a specific L-function, first state its local factors and completed functional equation. Construct its canonical zero product with specified genus, and derive any moment relation from independently convergent expressions.
