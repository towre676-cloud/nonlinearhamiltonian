## Revised question retained in the annual source

What does sphere eversion allow, and what does the Willmore energy of a spherical immersion actually bound?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The historical geometry discussion combined sphere eversion, Boy and Morin surfaces, and bending energy. Regular homotopy permits self-intersections but keeps the differential injective. This distinction is essential: an eversion is not a deformation through embedded spheres.

## Definitions

An immersion f:S²→R³ has rank-two differential everywhere; an embedding is additionally injective with the subspace topology.

Use H=(κ₁+κ₂)/2 and W(f)=∫H²dA, where κ₁,κ₂ are principal curvatures.

## Result name

Spherical Willmore inequality

## Result statement

Every smooth immersion of S² into R³ satisfies W≥4π. A round sphere has W=4π independently of its radius.

## Proof

Pointwise H²−K=((κ₁−κ₂)²)/4≥0, where K=κ₁κ₂ is Gaussian curvature. Integrating and applying Gauss–Bonnet to the intrinsic metric on the parameter sphere yields ∫K dA=2πχ(S²)=4π, even if the image self-intersects. Therefore W=4π+(1/4)∫(κ₁−κ₂)²dA≥4π. Equality forces the surface to be umbilic everywhere; the bound itself needs only the displayed identity. For a round radius-R sphere, H=±1/R and area=4πR², so W=4π. Scaling all lengths by c changes H² by c^(−2) and area by c², proving scale invariance. The existence of sphere eversion is a separate regular-homotopy theorem and is not proved by this energy inequality.

## Worked example: setup

Compare a round sphere at radii one and three, and distinguish the parameter sphere from an immersed projective plane such as Boy’s surface.

## Worked example: calculation

The energies are 1·4π=4π and (1/9)·36π=4π. In a regular homotopy the oriented normal may reverse relative to the original parametrization while the differential remains rank two; intermediate self-intersections are allowed. A projective plane has different topology from S², so its Gauss–Bonnet integral is 2π rather than 4π and the spherical proof must not be copied without changing the Euler characteristic.

## Worked example: conclusion

The energy convention, topology, and admissible class of deformations are explicit, preventing a factor-of-four normalization error or an embedding claim.

## Limits

The pointwise estimate alone does not describe an optimal eversion path or its maximal bending energy. Singular pinches are not immersions and are excluded.

## Extension

Track the differential rank and self-intersection set along an explicitly specified immersion family, then study its maximum Willmore energy as a separate variational problem.
