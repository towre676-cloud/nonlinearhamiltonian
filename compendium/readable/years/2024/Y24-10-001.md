## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which explicit hypotheses make a prime-indexed perturbation of a Laplacian self-adjoint with compact resolvent?

## Status

Classical result; recovered proposal repaired

## Summary

The recovered operator formula is useful as a template but does not define a domain, geometry or convergent prime sum. A rigorous starting point is a Dirichlet Laplacian on a bounded interval together with a norm-convergent bounded perturbation. This proves an actual spectral theorem in a stated model.

## Definitions

Let A=−d²/dx² on L²(0,π), with domain H²(0,π)∩H₀¹(0,π).

Assume W is a bounded real multiplication operator, T_p are bounded self-adjoint operators on this same Hilbert space, and Σ_p|R_p| ||T_p||<∞.

## Result name

A bounded self-adjoint perturbation theorem

## Result statement

For real λ_R,R_p, the operator L=A+λ_R W+Σ_pR_pT_p is self-adjoint on D(A), bounded below and has compact resolvent.

## Proof

The coefficient condition makes the prime sum Cauchy in operator norm; its limit B is bounded and self-adjoint because taking adjoints is norm-continuous. Including λ_RW preserves these properties. The adjoint identity (A+B)*=A*+B* on D(A*) holds for bounded B, so L is self-adjoint on D(A). The form inequality 〈u,Lu〉≥(1−||B||)||u||² follows from the first Dirichlet eigenvalue1. For a sufficiently large negative real z, ||B(A−z)⁻¹||<1; a Neumann series makes I+B(A−z)⁻¹ invertible. The resolvent factorization (L−z)⁻¹=(A−z)⁻¹[I+B(A−z)⁻¹]⁻¹ is compact because the Laplacian resolvent is compact. Compactness then holds at all resolvent points by the resolvent identity.

## Worked example: setup

Take W=0, T_p=I and R_p=p⁻².

## Worked example: calculation

The series is bounded byΣ_(n≥2)n⁻²<∞. Hence B=cI with c=Σ_p p⁻², and eigenvalues are exactly n²+c with the ordinary sine eigenfunctions. The construction yields a valid prime-indexed operator but visibly does not supply a zeta-zero correspondence.

## Worked example: conclusion

Self-adjointness is a domain theorem, not a consequence of real coefficients alone. Compact resolvent gives discrete eigenvalues in this concrete model.
