## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which exact residual is minimized, and what mathematical condition would turn a returned optimizer into a certified solution?

## Status

Classical result or corrected formulation with a worked example

## Summary

The function R(a)=a²+1 has E(a)=(a²+1)² with unique global minimum one at a=0, yet the real equation R(a)=0 has no solution. For the toy polynomial x²+t on [0,1]², the double integral is exactly 5/6. A computational demonstration should have a mathematical contract before it has a success flag.

## Definitions

For an equation R(a)=0, minimizing E(a)=R(a)² is an auxiliary optimization problem. Optimizer convergence and residual vanishing are different claims.

For an integral, the integration variables and bounds define the problem; nested integration routines must respect the chosen argument order.

A certificate must report a residual bound, an existence or uniqueness argument where needed, and the numerical error sources.

## Result name

A numerical optimizer does not certify an equation

## Result statement

The function R(a)=a²+1 has E(a)=(a²+1)² with unique global minimum one at a=0, yet the real equation R(a)=0 has no solution. For the toy polynomial x²+t on [0,1]², the double integral is exactly 5/6.

## Proof

Since a²≥0, R(a)≥1 and E(a)≥1, with equality exactly at zero. No successful minimization can change that impossibility. For the integral, integrate x first to obtain 1/3+t and then integrate t to obtain 1/3+1/2. Composite Simpson quadrature is exact on polynomials up to degree three in each variable, so the tensor-product method reproduces this result with exact rational arithmetic. Entropy integrands involving logρ need a separate positivity and endpoint analysis; x²+t vanishes at the origin when t starts at zero and cannot simply be passed to a rule that samples log0.

## Worked example: setup

Use the polynomial integral as a quadrature regression example and the impossible residual equation as an optimization counterexample.

## Worked example: calculation

The verifier evaluates tensor Simpson weights with Fraction arithmetic and obtains 5/6. It also evaluates E(a)−1=a²(a²+2), a nonnegative polynomial, giving a symbolic global certificate of the nonzero minimum.

## Worked example: conclusion

A computational demonstration should have a mathematical contract before it has a success flag.

## Limits

These examples do not certify a general nonlinear equation solver or an arbitrary singular integral. Small residual alone may correspond to a large state error when the inverse problem is poorly conditioned.

## Extension

For a scalar smooth equation with a known derivative lower bound |R′|≥m>0 near a unique root, combine a residual bound with the mean-value theorem to obtain a state-error bound |a−a*|≤|R(a)|/m. Verify the neighborhood and existence assumptions separately.
