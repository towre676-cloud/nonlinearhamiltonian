## Revised question retained in the annual source

What mathematical identity underlies a trace anomaly, and what does the cited lattice paper actually provide?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered source correctly points to a 2024 lattice-QCD calculation of glue trace-anomaly form factors. Its numerical results do not supply a general construction of quantum field theory. The useful mathematical content is the scale dependence of a renormalized effective quantity.

## Definitions

Use a dimensionless running coupling g(μ) with β(g)=μ dg/dμ.

For a renormalized dimensionless observable F(g,μL), scale independence means (μ∂_μ+β(g)∂_g)F=0 when the assumed renormalization equation holds.

## Result name

Integrated one-coupling scale identity

## Result statement

If F is differentiable and obeys the displayed renormalization equation, then at fixed g its explicit scale derivative is μ∂_μF=−β(g)∂_gF. Thus β≠0 can obstruct classical scale invariance even with no explicit mass parameter.

## Proof

The equation is simply the chain rule along a trajectory μ↦(g(μ),μL). Holding the physical observable fixed makes its total derivative zero, and rearrangement gives the claimed identity. This proves the formal implication within the assumed renormalized model, not existence of the model. In a gauge theory one often writes a trace term proportional to β(g) times a renormalized F² operator; the coefficient depends on whether g is absorbed into the connection and on the normalization of the action. Therefore an unspecified coefficient should not be copied between conventions. Matrix elements of the resulting trace operator between hadron states are form factors and may be computed approximately on a lattice with stated systematic errors.

## Worked example: setup

Take the solvable formal flow β(g)=−bg² and F(g,μL)=1/g−b log(μL).

## Worked example: calculation

μ∂_μF=−b, while β∂_gF=(−bg²)(−1/g²)=b. Their sum vanishes exactly. A nonzero explicit logarithm is required to compensate the running coupling. For a one-loop coupling defined as α rather than g, β and the prefactor change by the chain rule.

## Worked example: conclusion

The scale identity is a rigorously checked calculus statement; the QCD application is attributed to its actual finite-lattice source.

## Limits

The cited paper reports form factors and extrapolation/systematic limitations, not a proof of the Yang–Mills mass gap. No exact continuum QCD construction or particle-mass formula is claimed here.

## Extension

Fix an action normalization and derive the local Ward identity from the response of its renormalized effective action to a Weyl rescaling, retaining operator mixing and mass terms.
