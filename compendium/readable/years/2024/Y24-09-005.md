## Revised question retained in the annual source

Under what hypotheses does an angular series descend from R to the circle and describe a closed planar curve?

## Status

Classical result; recovered proposal repaired

## Summary

The recovered “irrational monogon” series must first pass a periodicity test. A formula on the real angular line is not automatically a function on the quotient circle R/(2πZ). Without that descent, closing the curve and assigning its homology are extra operations.

## Definitions

A function r:R→R descends to S¹ precisely when r(θ+2π)=r(θ) for all θ.

For positive continuous periodic r, the map θ↦r(θ)(cosθ,sinθ) is a polar graph.

## Result name

A one-mode obstruction

## Result statement

For a≠0 and ξ>0, r(θ)=1+a cos(ξθ) is 2π-periodic if and only if ξ is an integer. For irrational ξ, the endpoints of its restriction to [0,2π] have different radial distances.

## Proof

If r is periodic, evaluate at θ=0 to obtain cos(2πξ)=1, hence ξ is an integer. Conversely an integer frequency is periodic by the cosine addition formula. For irrational ξ, cos(2πξ)≠1, so r(2π)−r(0)=a[cos(2πξ)−1]≠0. The same polar direction at the two endpoints therefore has different radii. This proof does not automatically classify infinite sums: different frequencies can cancel, and their coefficients must be examined. It does show that irrationality by itself is not a mechanism producing a closed “one-sided” polygon. One may instead study an almost-periodic function on R, or add a closing segment, but those choices define different spaces. If a closing segment is explicitly added, the resulting loop can be studied, but its geometry depends on that segment. An endpoint identification in the parameter interval is a quotient construction instead and may fail to give a continuous planar map when the two endpoints have different images. These are distinct repairs.

## Worked example: setup

Take r(θ)=1+(1/4)cos(√2θ). It stays between3/4 and5/4, so the issue is not passage through the origin.

## Worked example: calculation

r(0)=5/4, while r(2π)=1+(1/4)cos(2π√2)≠5/4. The polar arc therefore has a nonzero endpoint gap of magnitude (1/4)|1−cos(2π√2)|. Replacing √2 by3 makes the endpoint gap zero and produces a threefold periodic radial graph.

## Worked example: conclusion

A continuous nonclosed arc and a simple closed curve have different topological models. The model must be chosen before computing homology.

## Limits

This is a necessary-definition correction, not a proof that every irrational-frequency infinite series is nonperiodic. Nor does it preclude studying nonperiodic angular curves as legitimate objects.

## Extension

For a finite trigonometric sum with distinct positive frequencies, prove linear independence of exponentials to determine exactly which coefficients can survive a prescribed period. Then specify whether the domain is R, an interval or S¹.
