## Revised question retained in the annual source

Can the proposed geometric complexity hierarchy be replaced by a well-defined decision problem with a verifiable algebraic certificate?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered labels P_AG, NP_AG, and SEACC lack an encoded input, cost model, and quantifiers. A precise invariant-subspace problem shows how Grassmannian dimension and polynomial equations enter without mistaking them for computational complexity classes.

## Definitions

Input consists of rational n×n matrices A₁,…,A_m and an integer k, with rational entries encoded by numerator and denominator bits.

A k-plane may be represented by a full-column-rank n×k matrix X, modulo right multiplication by GL_k.

## Result name

Invariant-subspace certificate on a Grassmannian chart

## Result statement

The columns of X span a common invariant k-plane exactly when, for every i, there exists a k×k matrix B_i with A_iX=XB_i. On a chart where selected k rows of X form I_k, these are explicit polynomial equations of degree at most two.

## Proof

If the span is invariant, each column of A_iX is a linear combination of the independent columns of X, and the coefficient vectors form B_i. Conversely the equation places every A_i image in the span. On the chosen chart the identity minor already proves rank k, while the remaining k(n−k) entries are free coordinates on the Grassmannian chart. The equations have rational coefficients inherited from the input and can be checked exactly for a rational certificate. However an existing invariant subspace may require algebraic or real coordinates with large description size; therefore this formulation alone does not place the unrestricted problem in classical NP. A degree-times-dimension score ignores coefficient bit length and cannot establish a polynomial-time algorithm.

## Worked example: setup

Take A=[[0,−1],[1,0]], a rational planar rotation, and ask for a one-dimensional invariant subspace.

## Worked example: calculation

Over R there is no invariant real line: a real eigenvalue would satisfy λ²+1=0. Over C there are two lines, represented by X=(1,−i)^T and X=(1,i)^T with eigenvalues i and −i. Thus the coefficient field is part of the problem, not an implementation detail. A diagonal matrix diag(1,2) instead has two rational invariant lines.

## Worked example: conclusion

The revised question has explicit inputs, equations, rank conditions, and field dependence. It does not rely on an undefined hierarchy.

## Limits

Geometric dimension is not bit complexity. Borel subgroups, profinite groups, and inverse Galois terminology do not resolve this missing computational specification.

## Extension

Fix a rational-certificate variant with bounded coordinate bit length and analyze exact verification time; separately study algebraic certificates through minimal polynomials and isolating data.
