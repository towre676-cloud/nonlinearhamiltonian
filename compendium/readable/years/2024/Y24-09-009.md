## Revised question retained in the annual source

How can one compute an actual sheaf-cohomology example associated with a closed one-dimensional object?

## Status

Classical result; recovered proposal repaired

## Summary

The recovered passage names sheaf spectral sequences without specifying a sheaf or cover. A two-set calculation gives the missing data and shows exactly where first cohomology comes from. The example uses the constant real sheaf on a circle.

## Definitions

Cover S¹ by two connected proper open arcs U,V whose intersection has two connected components.

Locally constant real sections on a space with m connected components form R^m.

## Result name

The circle Čech calculation

## Result statement

For this cover and the constant real sheaf, the Čech complex is R²→R², (a,b)↦(b−a,b−a), in degrees0 and1. Its cohomology is R in each of degrees0 and1, and zero above1.

## Proof

A section on each connected arc is a single number, so degree0 is R⊕R. A section on U∩V can take independent values on its two components, so degree1 is R⊕R. Restriction subtracts the U-value from the V-value on both components, producing the displayed differential. Its kernel is the diagonal line a=b, and its image is the diagonal line in degree1. Thus H0 has dimension1 and H1 is R² modulo a one-dimensional diagonal, also dimension1. There are no triple intersections of distinct cover members, so higher Čech groups vanish. Because the arcs and their intersection components are contractible and acyclic for this sheaf, this cover computes the sheaf cohomology. The acyclicity is part of why this particular cover is useful.

## Worked example: setup

Take the degree1 cochain c=(1,0). Determine whether it is a coboundary.

## Worked example: calculation

Every coboundary has equal coordinates, so c cannot equal(b−a,b−a). Its class is nonzero. The quotient can be identified with R by the difference map (u,v)↦u−v; c maps to1. A class represented by(5,4) is the same class because their difference(4,4) is a coboundary.

## Worked example: conclusion

The nontrivial class records incompatibility between the two overlap components. There is no need to infer it from an undefined “deformed” spectral sequence.

## Limits

A general sheaf, singular space or non-acyclic cover can have different cohomology. Čech and derived sheaf cohomology need not agree without suitable hypotheses. No intersection pairing has been introduced in this example.

## Extension

Choose an explicit local system with monodromy q. The circle cellular complex then has differential q−1; when q≠1 both H0 and H1 vanish, illustrating that the sheaf matters as much as the space.
