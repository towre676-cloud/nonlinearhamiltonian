## Revised question retained in the annual source

What information is actually supplied by six zeta ordinates and a matrix with chosen eigenvalues?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered numerical framework combined six low zeros, a six-point correlation, and a five-by-five mass matrix. Both claims require definitions and independent data. An empirical configuration is not an ensemble correlation, and conjugating chosen eigenvalues does not predict them.

## Definitions

For a point process, a k-point correlation is defined through expectations of sums over ordered distinct k-tuples against test functions.

For a Hermitian matrix, unitary conjugation preserves the multiset of eigenvalues.

## Result name

Isospectral fitting identity and an empirical-count limitation

## Result statement

For arbitrary real numbers m₁,…,m₅ and any unitary U, M=U diag(m₁,…,m₅)U* has exactly those eigenvalues. A sample of exactly six points contains only one unordered six-element subset, so it cannot by itself estimate a universal six-point distribution.

## Proof

If e_j is the j-th coordinate vector, M(Ue_j)=m_jUe_j. The vectors Ue_j form an orthonormal basis, proving the spectrum without any model-specific information. Changing U changes eigenvectors but not fitted masses, and choosing U=I makes the fitting tautology immediate. For a point process, the correlation definition requires an expectation or an explicit averaging procedure. A six-point sample produces 6! ordered permutations of the same six distinct points, not 6! independent realizations. Zeta-spacing comparisons additionally require an unfolding convention because the mean density changes with height; the leading density near T is log(T/(2π))/(2π), which is not constant over arbitrary intervals.

## Worked example: calculation

Their diagonal matrix has the quoted masses exactly without using any zero data. Any claimed connection must therefore specify the entries independently of the desired spectrum and quantify fit error or out-of-sample predictions. The six ordinates determine their fifteen pairwise differences, but those differences are deterministic data and do not establish an asymptotic GUE law.

## Worked example: conclusion

The calculation separates a reproducible spectral fit from a mathematical or physical prediction and a finite sample from a limiting statistical theorem.

## Limits

No mass values are endorsed as physical measurements, and no Riemann-hypothesis or GUE proof follows. An ensemble, averaging window, and uncertainty model were not recovered.
