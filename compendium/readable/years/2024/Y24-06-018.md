## Definitions

Take vertices(i,j)∈Z/3×Z/4. Triangulate each periodic square with edges in directions(1,0),(0,1),(1,1). This is a simplicial triangulation of a torus.

A Hamiltonian cycle visits each graph vertex once. Betti numbers of the filled simplicial surface use both edges and faces. On the smooth torus set f(u,v)=cos u+2cos v.

## Result name

A toroidal toy Tonnetz separates graph cycles from surface homology

## Result statement

The complex has12 vertices,36 edges and24 triangular faces, with Betti numbers(1,2,1) over F₂. Its one-skeleton alone has first Betti number25. The graph contains a Hamiltonian cycle. The smooth function f has one minimum, two saddles and one maximum, satisfying1−2+1=0.

## Proof

The periodic rectangular quotient is a torus: gluing opposite sides of a rectangle gives the standard surface, and the chosen diagonal triangulates it. Each of12 vertices contributes one edge in each of three distinct directions, yielding36 edges, and each of12 squares has two triangles, yielding24 faces. Connectedness gives b₀=1. A connected closed triangulated surface has one mod-two fundamental two-cycle, so b₂=1; Euler characteristic12−36+24=0 then gives b₁=2. Removing all faces leaves a connected graph with cycle rank E−V+1=25. For f, derivatives vanish when sin u=sin v=0. The four points have Hessian diag(−cos u,−2cos v), which is nondegenerate, with the stated indices. Thus the Morse alternating sum matches the surface Euler characteristic.

## Worked example: calculation

Visit(0,0),(1,0),(2,0),(2,1),(1,1),(0,1),(0,2),(1,2),(2,2),(2,3),(1,3),(0,3), then return to(0,0). Every consecutive pair is an allowed horizontal or vertical edge, and all12 vertices occur once. This graph cycle is not a claim that a gradient trajectory of f is periodic. Indeed the negative gradient equation gives df/dt=−|∇f|², so a nonstationary periodic gradient orbit is impossible. The checker constructs both boundary matrices and obtains ranks11 and23, separately confirming b₁=2.
