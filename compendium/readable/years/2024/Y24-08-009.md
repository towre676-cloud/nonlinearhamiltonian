## Revised question retained in the annual source

What arithmetic object is counted, and what exact generating function records its extension-field counts?

## Status

Revised classical development with scoped worked result

## Summary

The projective line over a finite field supplies a complete classical example of arithmetic counting and a rational zeta function. It illustrates an actual bridge between algebraic geometry and an analytic-looking generating series. It does not identify this finite-field theorem with a number-field Riemann hypothesis or with an unspecified physical operator.

## Definitions

For a finite-type variety X over F_q define its formal zeta series Z(X,t)=exp(Σ_{n≥1}#X(F_{q^n})t^n/n). The exponential and logarithm are formal power series over Q, so every finite coefficient calculation is well defined.

## Result name

Exact zeta function of the projective line

## Result statement

For X=P¹ over F_q, #X(F_{q^n})=q^n+1 and Z(X,t)=1/[(1−t)(1−qt)]. Its two elementary factors record the constant and degree-two cohomological contributions in the standard Frobenius interpretation.

## Proof

Every F_{q^n}-point of P¹ is either an affine coordinate in F_{q^n} or the single point at infinity, giving the count. Substitute it into the logarithm of Z. The formal identity−log(1−at)=Σ_{n≥1}a^nt^n/n gives−log(1−qt)−log(1−t); exponentiating yields the rational expression. Coefficients can be checked independently by multiplying the two geometric series: the coefficient of t^m is1+q+⋯+q^m. This establishes the complete rational formula without a numerical approximation.

## Worked example: setup

Choose q=2 and calculate the first three extension-field counts and series coefficients.

## Worked example: calculation

The counts for n=1,2,3 are3,5,9. The series coefficients through degree three are1,3,7,15. The count at n=2 is five, not the series coefficient seven: logarithmic point-count data and ordinary generating coefficients are different sequences. The verifier checks both and their formal logarithmic relation.

## Worked example: conclusion

The example provides an explicit arithmetic object, a count and a theorem connecting them. It also exposes which sequence an algorithm is actually computing.

## Limits

The general Weil conjectures and arbitrary motivic L-functions are far beyond this elementary example. The formula does not establish a number-field GRH, a cryptographic hardness claim or a QFT interpretation.

## Extension

For a specified curve over F_q, compute enough counts to determine its zeta numerator under a stated degree bound and verify the resulting recurrence with independently counted extensions.
