## Definitions

For good primes set t_p=a_p/(2√p)∈[−1,1] and θ_p=arccos t_p∈[0,π].

## Result name

Normalization and elementary moments

## Result statement

The Sato–Tate probability law has E[t]=0 and E[t²]=1/4; equivalently for u=2t=a_p/√p it has E[u²]=1. If g is measurable, a transformed variable g(t) follows the pushforward law, not automatically the original law.

## Proof

The substitution t=cosθ changes sin²θdθ into√(1−t²)dt, including the reversed integration limits. Symmetry makes E[t]=0. For the second moment, write t=sinφ and integrate: ∫_(−1)^1t²√(1−t²)dt=π/8. Multiplication by2/π gives1/4. More directly the beta integral gives the same value. The density integrates to1 because the upper semicircle has areaπ/2. For a transformed variable, probabilities satisfy P(g(t)∈B)=μ(g⁻¹(B)); unless g preserves μ, the law changes. For example g(t)=ct with |c|≠1 changes the second moment to c²/4 and its support to[−|c|,|c|]. Hasse’s bound is what keeps the classical t_p inside[−1,1]. If a correction sends it outside that interval, arccos no longer gives a real Frobenius angle at all. Even corrections remaining inside must be treated as a pushforward and can create atoms or changed endpoint behavior if the map is not injective.

## Limits
