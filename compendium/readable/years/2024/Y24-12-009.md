## Revised question retained in the annual source

How can the recovered energy-rate arithmetic be corrected, and what does a tunneling exponent actually require?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The historical arithmetic confused milli-watts with kilo-watts by a factor of one million. This can be corrected as an abstract conversion of event rate and energy per event. A separate one-dimensional barrier model explains the dimensionless exponent without suggesting an operational device.

## Definitions

One electronvolt is exactly 1.602176634×10^(−19) joule.

For a rectangular barrier V₀>E, set κ=√(2m(V₀−E))/ℏ, which has units inverse length.

## Result name

Power conversion and exponential barrier scale

## Result statement

An event rate R and energy ε per event give power P=Rε. In a constant forbidden region, a stationary Schrödinger solution is a combination of exp(κx) and exp(−κx); the leading thick-barrier transmission scale is exp(−2κa), with a separate matching prefactor.

## Proof

Energy accumulated in time dt is Rdt times ε, so dividing by dt gives power. In the barrier, −ℏ²ψ″/(2m)+(V₀−E)ψ=0 implies ψ″=κ²ψ, whose characteristic equation has roots ±κ. The amplitude attenuation across width a is exp(−κa); a probability or flux factor squares it, giving exp(−2κa). The exponent is dimensionless because κa has no units. For a varying barrier the WKB exponent replaces κa by ∫√(2m(V(x)−E))dx/ℏ over the forbidden region, but turning-point matching and smoothness assumptions are required. It is not a universal exact formula and should not be copied with an extra ℏ in the square root.

## Worked example: setup

Use the historically recovered abstract rate R=5×10⁸ s^(−1) and energy ε=14.1×10⁶ eV.

## Worked example: calculation

ε=2.25906905394×10^(−12) J. Multiplication gives P=0.00112953452697 W=1.12953452697 mW. A purely assumed efficiency factor 0.35 gives 0.3953370844395 mW. The historical 1.13 kW is larger by approximately 10⁶. This calculation concerns only units and multiplication; it supplies no source realization.

## Worked example: conclusion

The numerical correction is exact at the stated decimal inputs, and the barrier exponent has a derived sign and dimension.

## Limits

No reaction engineering, source construction, yield, shielding, or clinical application is developed. The barrier derivation is a mathematical one-dimensional model, not a performance prediction.

## Extension

For an exact rectangular barrier, impose wavefunction and derivative matching at both interfaces and compare the full transmission formula to its thick-barrier asymptotic.
