## Revised question retained in the annual source

What can be proved from a defined partition function, without converting statistical resemblance into an arithmetic theorem?

## Status

Classical result; recovered proposal repaired

## Summary

The recovered spectral proposal introduces a partition function. In a finite-dimensional model this gives an exact probability distribution and useful derivative identities. Infinite-dimensional heat traces require summability, which is not implied merely by self-adjointness.

## Definitions

For real eigenvalues λ_1,…,λ_d and β>0, set Z(β)=Σ_je^(−βλ_j) and p_j=e^(−βλ_j)/Z.

The mean energy is E_β[λ]=Σ_jp_jλ_j and its variance isΣ_jp_j(λ_j−E_β[λ])².

## Result name

Convexity of the log partition function

## Result statement

d(logZ)/dβ=−E_β[λ] and d²(logZ)/dβ²=Var_β(λ)≥0. For a nonnegative infinite spectrum, the same identities hold wherever the needed weighted sums converge locally.

## Proof

Finite differentiation gives Z′=−Σλ_je^(−βλ_j) and Z″=Σλ_j²e^(−βλ_j). Therefore(logZ)′=Z′/Z=−E_β[λ], and(logZ)″=Z″/Z−(Z′/Z)²=E_β[λ²]−E_β[λ]². The latter equals a sum of nonnegative squared deviations. Local domination justifies the identical calculation for an infinite series if, for example, Σ(1+λ_j²)e^(−β₀λ_j)<∞ for some β₀ below the evaluation interval. A bounded operator on an infinite-dimensional space generally fails this trace condition: infinitely many bounded eigenvalues makeΣe^(−βλ_j) diverge.

## Worked example: setup

Take λ_1=0 and λ_2=1.

## Worked example: calculation

Z=1+e^(−β), E_β[λ]=1/(e^β+1), and Var=e^β/(e^β+1)². At β=log3 the probabilities are3/4 and1/4, the mean is1/4 and the variance is3/16. For a graph Laplacian, a zero mode similarly contributes a constant1 to the heat trace on each connected component.

## Worked example: conclusion

The derivatives measure moments of a specified spectrum. They do not establish that this spectrum is the zeta spectrum.

## Limits

A time-derivative operator with eigenvalues unbounded below does not have a finite positive-temperature heat trace without a different construction. A formal sum Z is not enough to justify differentiation or a path-integral interpretation.

## Extension

For a concrete compact-resolvent operator, prove heat-trace convergence from eigenvalue growth, then derive its small-time asymptotics and compare them with the proposed arithmetic counting law.
