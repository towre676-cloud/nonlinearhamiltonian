## Summary

The recovered list names entropy without specifying a probability space or convention. On a finite alphabet, Shannon entropy is a concrete concave function and its maximum has a short complete proof. This supplies mathematical content without importing thermodynamic interpretations.

## Definitions

For probabilitiesp_i≥0 withΣp_i=1, defineH(p)=−Σp_i logp_i, with0log0=0 and natural logarithms.

For positive reference probabilitiesq_i, the relative entropy isD(p||q)=Σ_(p_i>0)p_i log(p_i/q_i).

## Result name

Exact upper bound and equality case

## Result statement

For a distribution onm outcomes,0≤H(p)≤logm. The maximum occurs exactly at the uniform distribution; the minimum0 occurs exactly at point masses.

## Proof

The elementary inequalitylogu≤u−1 gives−log(q_i/p_i)≥1−q_i/p_i forp_i>0. Multiplying byp_i and summing provesD(p||q)≥1−Σ_(p_i>0)q_i≥0. Withuniformq_i=1/m, D(p||q)=logm−H(p), soH≤logm. Equality inlogu≤u−1 requiresu=1 on the support and no missing positiveq mass, forcingp uniform. Nonnegativity follows termwise from−p_i logp_i≥0. A term vanishes only atp_i=0 or1, soH=0 forces a point mass. On the interior, the Hessian ofH isdiag(−1/p_i); restricting to the probability simplex shows strict concavity, consistent with uniqueness of the maximum.

## Worked example: conclusion

Entropy is a defined functional whose inequality and equality cases can be proved, rather than a universal synonym for disorder.

## Limits

Continuous differential entropy has different transformation properties and can be negative. No thermodynamic entropy, residual calibration or arithmetic spectral invariant is identified with this finite functional without an explicit map.
