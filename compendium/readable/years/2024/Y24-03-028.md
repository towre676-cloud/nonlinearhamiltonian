## Revised question retained in the annual source

Reconstruction: Why does a uniformly weighted flexible chain satisfy a hyperbolic-cosine equation, and when do span and length determine its scale uniquely?

## Status

Classical mathematics newly developed from a recovered assistant-only catalog name

## Summary

A catenary has a parameter with a physical and geometric meaning: horizontal tension divided by weight per unit arc length. Stating that meaning avoids confusing a chain under its own weight with a cable under a load uniform per horizontal distance, which instead gives a parabola.

## Definitions

Use a planar, perfectly flexible, inextensible chain in static equilibrium. Let w>0 be weight per unit arc length, H>0 the constant horizontal tension, and a=H/w.

Measure x horizontally and y upwards, with the lowest point at x=0. Let s(x) be signed arc length from that point, so s′=√(1+y′²).

For equal-height supports at x=±b, b>0, write the lowest height as y(0)=0. The total chain length is L>2b.

## Result name

The catenary law and uniqueness of the length parameter

## Result statement

Force balance gives y″=(1/a)√(1+y′²). With a horizontal tangent at the lowest point, y(x)=a(cosh(x/a)−1). Its length between ±b is L(a)=2a sinh(b/a). For each L>2b there is exactly one a>0 satisfying this equation.

## Proof

The vertical tension on the segment between the lowest point and x balances its weight ws. The tangent slope is vertical tension divided by horizontal tension, hence Hy′=ws. Differentiating and substituting s′ gives the stated ODE. Therefore d(arcsinh y′)/dx=1/a. With y′(0)=0, integration gives y′=sinh(x/a) and then the catenary expression. Its arc-length density is cosh(x/a), so integrating from −b to b gives L(a). To prove uniqueness, put z=b/a>0 and differentiate: L′(a)=2(sinh z−z cosh z). The expression in parentheses is zero at z=0 and has derivative −z sinh z<0 for z>0. Thus L′<0. As a→∞ the limit is 2b, while a→0⁺ sends L to infinity. Continuity and strict monotonicity give existence and uniqueness for every L>2b. The argument determines the one-parameter equilibrium graph in this model; it is not a claim about elastic or self-contacting chains.

## Worked example: setup

Take a=2 length units and b=2, so the supports are four units apart.

## Worked example: calculation

The endpoint sag above the lowest point is 2(cosh 1−1)≈1.08616126963. The length is 4sinh 1≈4.70080477458. At x=2 the slope is sinh 1 and the arc-length density is cosh 1. The ODE residual cancels because y″=(1/2)cosh(x/2) and √(1+y′²)=cosh(x/2). The verifier checks these identities numerically and brackets the unique inverse length parameter near a=2 using the proven monotonicity.

## Worked example: conclusion

The length condition fixes the scale and exposes exactly why a bare cosh formula has an undetermined parameter.

## Limits

Uniform weight here means uniform per actual arc length. A distributed horizontal load, bending stiffness, stretch, friction or unequal support heights changes the model or its boundary conditions. Finite numerical residuals confirm the chosen example; the differentiations and monotonicity argument provide the proof.

## Extension

For unequal support heights use y=a cosh((x−x₀)/a)+c and solve the endpoint and length constraints for a,x₀,c. Keeping the signed arc-length relation is often a cleaner route than squaring the equations and introducing spurious signs.
