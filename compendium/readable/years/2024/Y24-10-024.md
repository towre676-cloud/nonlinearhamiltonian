## Summary

The recovered comparison is not a theorem until its two sites, coefficient sheaves and comparison map are defined. The standard crystalline prism(Z_p,(p)) supplies a complete elementary verification of the underlying algebraic axioms.

## Definitions

Aδ-ring has a Frobenius liftφ(x)=x^p+pδ(x); on a p-torsion-free ring such a lift determinesδ.

A prism(A,I) hasI defining a Cartier divisor,A derived(p,I)-complete andp∈I+φ(I)A. It is bounded whenA/I has boundedp-power torsion.

## Result name

The crystalline base prism

## Result statement

OnA=Z_p takeφ=id andδ(x)=(x−x^p)/p. Then(A,(p)) is a bounded prism with quotientF_p.

## Proof

Fermat’s congruence and continuity showx−x^p is divisible byp inZ_p, soδ is well-defined. The identity map is a ring homomorphism whose reduction modulo p equals Frobenius onF_p, becausex^p=x there. Thereforeδ has exactly theδ-ring identities induced by this lift. The ideal(p) is generated by a non-zero-divisor inZ_p and so defines a Cartier divisor. The ring is p-adically complete; because it is p-torsion-free this also supplies the relevant derived completeness in this example. Finallyφ(p)=p, sop lies in(p)+φ((p))A=(p). The quotient isF_p, whosep-torsion is killed byp, establishing boundedness. These checks exhibit all the named data, rather than merely appending a subscript to a cohomology symbol.

## Worked example: setup

Takep=3 and evaluateδ on a few ordinary integers embedded inZ_3.

## Worked example: calculation

δ(2)=(2−8)/3=−2, δ(3)=(3−27)/3=−8 andδ(1)=0. The mapδ is not an ordinary derivation:δ(2·2)=δ(4)=−20, whereas2δ(2)+2δ(2)=−8. Its product rule includes the Frobenius andp terms encoded byφ.

## Worked example: conclusion

The simplest valid prism already requires a precise algebraic structure. A height-weighted version must establish analogous axioms and a genuine site.

## Limits

This proves a base example, not a general comparison theorem. Bhatt–Scholze comparisons involve particular coefficient rings and base changes; they do not assert that any two expressions labeled perfectoid and prismatic are isomorphic.

## Extension

Define the proposed modifiedδ-ring and ideal, verify the prism axioms, and give a map of ringed sites or complexes. Only then ask whether that map is a quasi-isomorphism.
