## Revised question retained in the annual source

What theorem follows from the two recovered eigenvalues, and which missing identification would be needed for an arithmetic conclusion?

## Summary

The recovered passage takes a two-number spectrum as evidence for prime-counting conclusions. An exact Markov operator shows what these eigenvalues really control: relaxation of functions or probability distributions in the specified finite dynamical system.

## Definitions

Let P=[[3/4,1/4],[1/4,3/4]] act on functions on two states, and letΠ be projection onto constants by the uniform average.

The total variation distance between probabilities(p,1−p) and(q,1−q) is|p−q|.

## Result name

An exact mixing identity

## Result statement

P has eigenvalues1 and1/2 and satisfies P^n=Π+2^(−n)(I−Π). Every initial probability distribution approaches(1/2,1/2) in total variation by the exact factor2^(−n).

## Proof

The vectors(1,1) and(1,−1) are an orthogonal basis. Applying P gives respectively the same vector and half the vector. Hence P=Π+(1/2)(I−Π), and the two orthogonal projections annihilate one another. Raising to the nth power yields the formula. An initial probability(p,1−p) equals(1/2,1/2)+(p−1/2)(1,−1). Multiplication by P^n scales its second component by2^(−n), giving total variation2^(−n)|p−1/2|. This exhausts what the two-state spectrum establishes. There are no primes, integer cutoffx or zeta function in this model, even though its eigenvalues are exactly the recovered pair.

## Worked example: conclusion

The spectral gap1/2 quantifies a defined mixing problem. An arithmetic conclusion requires a separate theorem connecting that problem to prime-counting observables.

## Limits

This finite countermodel does not rule out genuine transfer-operator methods in number theory. Such methods specify spaces, nuclearity or trace properties, a determinant identity and a precise link to an arithmetic L-function.

## Extension

If a transfer operator is intended to encode primes, prove an exact trace or Fredholm determinant relation and show how the relevant arithmetic cutoff is represented. Then derive, rather than assume, the error term.
