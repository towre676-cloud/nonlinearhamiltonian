## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which equivalence relation on line bundles is intended, and which part is discrete enough to have a finite rank?

## Definitions

For a smooth projective complex variety, Pic(X) is the abelian group of line bundles under tensor product. Pic⁰(X) denotes the subgroup algebraically equivalent to zero.

The Néron–Severi theorem makes NS(X) a finitely generated abelian group. Its real vector space has finite dimension ρ(X). This background theorem is not proved by a two-dimensional matrix check.

On X=P¹×P¹, write O(a,b) for the external tensor product of the two degree-a and degree-b bundles.

## Result name

Picard classes and the Néron–Severi quotient

## Result statement

On P¹×P¹, NS(X)=Pic(X)≅Z² with fiber-class intersection matrix [[0,1],[1,0]]. The class (a,b) has self-intersection 2ab. For a,b≥0, its space of sections has dimension (a+1)(b+1).

## Proof

The projective-bundle description of the product gives the two independent fiber classes and no Pic⁰ component. Two fibers of the same projection are disjoint and equivalent, so each fiber class squares to zero. A fiber of one projection meets a fiber of the other in one point; bilinearity gives (aF₁+bF₂)²=2ab. Sections of O(a,b) are bihomogeneous polynomials of bidegree (a,b). Their monomial basis consists of the (a+1) choices of first-factor exponent and (b+1) choices of second-factor exponent, proving the dimension formula directly. These explicit calculations use the product structure; they do not imply that Picard groups of arbitrary varieties are free of rank two.

## Worked example: setup

Take O(2,3) on the product and compare it with O(1,0).

## Worked example: calculation

The first class has twelve independent sections and self-intersection twelve. The second has two sections and self-intersection zero. Both are discrete divisor classes, whereas on an elliptic curve the degree-zero line bundles form a positive-dimensional Picard variety and cannot be replaced by a single rank count.

## Worked example: conclusion

The finite-rank divisor quotient is a well-defined geometric object. It is different from an unspecified real vector space obtained by tensoring every line bundle with R.

## Limits

Picard number does not classify a variety or its automorphism group. Torsion in NS and continuous Pic⁰ information can be lost after tensoring with R. Statements about singular or nonprojective spaces require different hypotheses.
