Which discrete operator and preconditioner make conjugate gradients valid, and what improvement can actually be certified?

## Summary

## Definitions

Let A be the3×3 tridiagonal matrix with diagonal2 and adjacent off-diagonal−1. A symmetric positive-definite matrix M defines the symmetric preconditioned operator M^(−1/2)AM^(−1/2).

For an SPD operator with condition number κ, the classical CG energy-error bound after k steps is at most2[(√κ−1)/(√κ+1)]^k times the initial energy error.

## Result name

The SPD conditions behind conjugate gradients and multigrid

## Result statement

A is SPD with eigenvalues2−√2,2,2+√2, condition number3+2√2, and A⁻¹(1,1,1)ᵀ=(3/2,2,3/2)ᵀ. The Jacobi preconditioner M=2I leaves the condition number unchanged. An AMG cycle used inside ordinary PCG must supply a fixed symmetric positive-definite preconditioning action to retain the standard theory.

## Proof

For x=(x₁,x₂,x₃), xᵀAx=x₁²+(x₂−x₁)²+(x₃−x₂)²+x₃². This is positive for every nonzero vector, proving SPD without numerical eigenvalue tolerances. The sine vectors x_j=sin(jkπ/4), k=1,2,3 satisfy the difference equation with eigenvalue2−2cos(kπ/4). Their ratio gives κ=(2+√2)/(2−√2)=3+2√2. Multiplying the proposed solution by A gives(1,1,1). For M=2I the preconditioned eigenvalues are exactly half the original values, so their ratio is unchanged. Standard CG orthogonalizes residuals in the appropriate Krylov space; in exact arithmetic it terminates within the number of distinct eigenvalues, but a nonsymmetric or varying preconditioner invalidates this particular argument.

## Worked example: setup

Run unpreconditioned CG from zero with b=(1,1,1).

## Worked example: calculation

The first step has α₀=(bᵀb)/(bᵀAb)=3/2, x₁=(3/2,3/2,3/2) and residual r₁=(−1/2,1,−1/2). Its squared norm is3/2, so β₀=1/2 and p₁=r₁+(1/2)b=(0,3/2,0). Then α₁=(3/2)/(9/2)=1/3, giving x₂=(3/2,2,3/2) and zero residual. Symmetry of this right-hand side reduced the active spectral support to two modes, so convergence occurs in two steps rather than the general three-step bound. Positive diagonal scaling alone changes the units of eigenvalues, while the condition number measures their ratio. The example therefore separates a valid SPD preconditioner from an effective one: validity permits PCG, whereas effectiveness requires an improved spectral distribution or a cheaper total solve.

## Worked example: conclusion

Both the operator hypotheses and the actual finite iteration are explicit. The preconditioner claim is assessable through its spectrum and symmetry.

## Limits

Finite precision, nonlinear or changing AMG cycles and indefinite PDE discretizations need separate methods and stopping analysis. The ideal M=A gives condition number one but solving with M may cost as much as the original problem.

## Extension

Specify one concrete symmetric multigrid cycle and prove positive definiteness of its action before comparing measured iterations on larger mesh families.
