## Revised question retained in the annual source

What arithmetic function is actually defined by the recovered pointwise product?

## Status

Classical result; recovered proposal repaired

## Summary

The recovered equation contains an exact sign and support error. With the standard ideal Möbius and von Mangoldt functions, their pointwise product is negative on prime ideals and zero on all higher prime powers. It is not the von Mangoldt function whose summatory function appears in the usual explicit formula.

## Definitions

For nonzero integral ideals, μ_K(𝔞)=0 if a prime square divides𝔞; otherwise it is(−1)^r for a product of r distinct primes.

Λ_K(𝔞)=logN𝔭 when𝔞=𝔭^m,m≥1, and is zero otherwise.

## Result name

Exact support and sign

## Result statement

J(𝔞)=μ_K(𝔞)Λ_K(𝔞) equals−logN𝔭 at𝔞=𝔭 and0 otherwise. ThusΣ_(N𝔞≤x)J(𝔞)=−θ_K(x), while ψ_K(x)=Σ_(N𝔭^m≤x)logN𝔭 includes positive prime-power contributions.

## Proof

On a prime ideal, μ_K(𝔭)=−1 andΛ_K(𝔭)=logN𝔭. On𝔭^m withm≥2, μ_K is zero. On an ideal containing at least two different prime factors, Λ_K is zero. These cases exhaust the possibilities and prove the formula. The recovered equality to a positive prime-only sum therefore fails already at the first prime ideal. It also conflates θ_K andψ_K, which differ byΣ_(m≥2,N𝔭^m≤x)logN𝔭. The standard logarithmic derivative of an Euler product producesψ_K, because its geometric expansion retains all powers. Multiplying byμ_K pointwise removes precisely those higher-power terms rather than recovering them.

## Worked example: calculation

J(2)=−log2; J(4)=J(6)=J(8)=J(9)=0. The prime-only sum isθ(10)=log2+log3+log5+log7. The von Mangoldt sum isψ(10)=θ(10)+2log2+log3, because4,8 and9 contribute. The J-sum is−θ(10). These identities can be checked symbolically by keeping coefficients of each log prime as integers.
