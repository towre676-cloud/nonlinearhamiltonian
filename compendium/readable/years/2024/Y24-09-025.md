## Result name

An exact level-one basis

## Result statement

M_38 has basis E4^8E6, E4^5E6³, E4²E6⁵ and dimension3. Its cusp subspace has dimension2 and equals ΔM_26, with basis ΔE4^5E6 and ΔE4²E6³.

## Proof

The classical level-one structure theorem says the graded ring is C[E4,E6], with weights4 and6, and its cusp ideal is generated by Δ of weight12. These structural theorems are the stated external input; the remaining computation is elementary. Solving4a+6b=38 means2a+3b=19. Nonnegative solutions are(a,b)=(8,1),(5,3),(2,5). Algebraic independence supplies the displayed basis. Similarly4a+6b=26 gives(a,b)=(5,1),(2,3), proving the cusp dimension and basis after multiplication by Δ. Each weight38 monomial has constant term1, so the cusp condition on a linear combination is exactly that its three coefficients sum to zero; this independently identifies a two-dimensional kernel. For example E4^8E6−E4^5E6³=1728ΔE4^5E6 by the defining relation for Δ. Multiplication byΔ is injective because a holomorphic form whose product with the nonzero holomorphic functionΔ vanishes must itself vanish on a nonempty open set and hence everywhere. This justifies the dimension transfer S38=ΔM26 once the classical cusp-ideal theorem is supplied. A Fourier truncation is a check, not a replacement for the structure theorem.
