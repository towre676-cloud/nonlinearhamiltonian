## Revised question retained in the annual source

How do prime-ideal counts differ from rational-prime counts in a concrete quadratic field?

## Status

Classical result; recovered proposal repaired

## Summary

The recovered ideal-distribution proposal becomes arithmetically transparent over the Gaussian rationals. The local factor at a rational prime depends on its splitting type. This is information that a generic scalar J-value cannot replace without an explicit relation to ideal factorization.

## Definitions

Let K=Q(i), with ring of integers Z[i]. The norm of(a+bi) is a²+b².

A prime ideal𝔭 has absolute norm |Z[i]/𝔭|. For an unramified rational prime p, residue degreef means N𝔭=p^f.

## Result name

Exact splitting and Euler factors

## Result statement

The prime2 ramifies, primes p≡1 mod4 split into two primes of normp, and primes p≡3 mod4 remain prime with normp². Hence ζ_K(s)=ζ(s)L(s,χ_−4) in Re(s)>1.

## Proof

For odd p, reduce x²+1 modulo p. It has a root exactly when−1 is a square, equivalently p≡1 mod4 by the cyclic group F_p*. A split polynomial gives two quotient fields F_p, while an irreducible polynomial gives F_(p²). At2, 2 is a unit times(1+i)², giving a single prime of norm2. The ideal Euler factors are therefore(1−2^(−s))⁻¹ at2,(1−p^(−s))⁻² for split primes, and(1−p^(−2s))⁻¹ for inert primes. Multiplying the rational zeta factor by the quadratic-character factor gives these three cases: χ_−4(p)=1,−1,0 respectively. Equality of the absolutely convergent Euler products proves the asserted identity in its initial domain.

There is one above2 of norm2; two above5 of norm5; one above3 of norm9; and none above7 within the bound because its prime ideal has norm49. Thusπ_K(10)=4. The corresponding prime-only log sum is log2+2log5+log9. It differs from summing logp over rational primes≤10 and from including all ideal prime powers.

## Worked example: conclusion

Even a simple field changes the meaning of the sampling variable. Norm cutoffs and splitting multiplicities must be stated.

## Limits

This local calculation is not a proof of a prime-ideal theorem or Chebotarev density. Ramification and character conductors require care in more general fields. Elements differing by units represent the same principal ideal and must not be overcounted.

## Extension
