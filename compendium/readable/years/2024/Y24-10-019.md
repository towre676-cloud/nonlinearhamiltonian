## Revised question retained in the annual source

What information does the recovered polynomial feature map preserve, and how stable is its inverse?

## Status

Classical result; recovered proposal repaired

## Summary

The recovered two-component feature has a precise geometric interpretation independent of any unspecified spectral application. It parameterizes a cusp, retains the sign through its cubic coordinate and loses first-order sensitivity at zero.

## Definitions

LetΦ:R→R² beΦ(x)=(x²,x³). Its image satisfiesv²=u³ andu≥0.

The inverse isx=v/u whenu>0, withx=0 at(u,v)=(0,0).

## Result name

Injectivity and inverse instability

## Result statement

Φ is injective. It is a homeomorphism onto the real cusp, but its inverse is not locally Lipschitz at the origin. In polynomial coordinate rings,C[t²,t³] is strictly smaller than its normalizationC[t].

## Proof

Ifx²=y² theny=x ory=−x. In the latter case equality of cubic coordinates forcesx³=−x³, hencex=0; thereforex=y. The displayed inverse is continuous away from zero; near zero,|x|=√u ensures continuity there as well. But||Φ(x)−Φ(0)||=x²√(1+x²), so|x|/||Φ(x)||=1/(|x|√(1+x²))→∞. No finite inverse Lipschitz constant exists near zero. Algebraically,t is integral overR0=C[t²,t³] because it satisfiesX²−t²=0 witht²∈R0, but t∉R0: every nonconstant monomial in the generators has degree at least2. Moreover t=t³/t² lies in its fraction field. The ringC[t] is integrally closed and integral overR0, yielding the standard cusp normalization.

## Limits

This establishes properties of the specified feature map, not a spectral-learning advantage or an arithmetic correspondence. Inverse behavior under noisy measurements depends on a declared noise model and domain.
