## Revised question retained in the annual source

How should the recovered frequency sequence be compared with pitch names without assigning incompatible notes?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The historical mapping assigned named notes to a sequence starting240,360,456,600,816,1056Hz. A pitch name requires a tuning and reference. Ratios and logarithmic cents provide exact comparisons even when those conventions are not fixed.

## Definitions

Relative cents are c(f;f₀)=1200 log₂(f/f₀).

In twelve-tone equal temperament, the semitone displacement is s=12 log₂(f/f₀); an octave multiplies frequency by two.

## Result name

Octave-invariant interval comparison

## Result statement

Multiplying a frequency ratio by2 adds1200cents; reducing modulo1200 compares octave classes. A ratio3/2 is approximately701.955cents, close to a perfect fifth, not a major third.

## Proof

The logarithm identity log₂(2r)=1+log₂r proves the octave rule. Equal temperament divides the octave into twelve equal logarithmic parts, so its frequency ratios are 2^(k/12). Comparing a given ratio r with an equal-tempered interval k gives error1200log₂r−100k. These equations define a reproducible assignment only after a reference note and octave are selected. A list of arbitrary frequencies therefore cannot be labeled by intuition alone, and a harmonic coefficient from a modular series does not acquire a particular musical spelling unless an explicit map is chosen.

## Worked example: setup

Take240Hz as the reference and compare360Hz and456Hz.

## Worked example: calculation

360/240=3/2, giving about701.955cents or7.01955semitones. The equal-tempered major third is four semitones and ratio2^(1/3)≈1.259921, far from1.5. The456Hz ratio is1.9, giving about1111.199cents, near an octave-minus-semitone rather than an ordinary fifth. The600Hz ratio is2.5, equivalent modulo octaves to1.25, a just major third at386.314cents. Each statement follows from the chosen reference and ratios, without claiming a unique note name.

## Worked example: conclusion

The recovered sequence can be discussed accurately as interval data, while the old note labels require correction.

## Limits

The calculation makes no claim about musical quality, psychoacoustics, or an arithmetic law selecting the sequence. Equal-tempered and just-intonation intervals intentionally differ.

## Extension

Define a map from integer coefficients to frequency ratios in advance, state whether octave reduction is allowed, and assess its musical structure under one consistent tuning.
