## Revised question retained in the annual source

What exact mathematical inequality underlies the recovered uncertainty flashcard?

## Status

Classical result; recovered proposal repaired

## Summary

The recovered list mentions uncertainty without a proof or operator domains. The finite-dimensional version gives a clean theorem, and a Gaussian computation explains the familiar position–momentum case when the required differentiability and decay are available.

## Definitions

For a unit vectorψ and HermitianA,B, seta=〈A〉,b=〈B〉 andΔA=||(A−aI)ψ||.

The commutator is[A,B]=AB−BA. In an unbounded settingψ must belong to domains needed for the products or an appropriate form version.

## Result name

A commutator lower bound

## Result statement

ΔAΔB≥(1/2)|〈ψ,[A,B]ψ〉|. For positionX and momentumP=−iℏd/dx on a suitable Schwartz state, this givesΔXΔP≥ℏ/2.

## Proof

Letu=(A−aI)ψ andv=(B−bI)ψ. Cauchy–Schwarz gives||u||||v||≥|〈u,v〉|≥|Im〈u,v〉|. Hermiticity implies〈ψ,[A,B]ψ〉=2iIm〈u,v〉, proving the bound. For Schwartz functions, integration by parts has no boundary terms and direct differentiation gives[X,P]ψ=iℏψ, yieldingℏ/2. The finite-dimensional commutator cannot equaliℏI because its trace is zero; thus the position–momentum relation is not a finite matrix identity and its domains cannot be ignored.

## Worked example: setup

Takeψ(x)=(2πσ²)^(−1/4)exp(−x²/(4σ²)), withσ>0.

## Worked example: calculation

The squared magnitude is a centered Gaussian of varianceσ², soΔX=σ. Differentiation givesPψ=iℏxψ/(2σ²), with mean momentum0 and varianceℏ²/(4σ²). ThereforeΔP=ℏ/(2σ) and the product is exactlyℏ/2, attaining the lower bound.

## Worked example: conclusion

The inequality is a precise norm-and-commutator statement. Equality is realized by an explicit family rather than asserted by analogy.

## Limits

For unbounded observables, formal commutator algebra can be invalid outside a common domain. The proof does not interpret measurement disturbance or establish a spectral–arithmetic connection.

## Extension

Use the full complex covariance in Cauchy–Schwarz to derive the stronger Schrödinger–Robertson bound, and characterize equality by linear dependence of the centered vectors.
