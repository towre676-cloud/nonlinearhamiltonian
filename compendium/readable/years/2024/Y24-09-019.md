## Summary

The historical formula becomes rigorous when local outcomes are specified as signs on a common probability space. The same expression can then be evaluated for a singlet state and explicit spin observables. The two calculations use different mathematical models.

## Definitions

Let A,A′,B,B′ be random variables taking values±1 on one probability space.

For the two-qubit singlet, spin measurements σ_a=a·σ and σ_b=b·σ have correlation E(a,b)=−a·b for unit vectors.

## Result name

Local bound and exact quantum example

## Result statement

The displayed sum is at most2 in the common-probability-space model. The singlet with a=z,a′=x,b=(x+z)/√2,b′=(x−z)/√2 gives2√2.

## Proof

Write E(AB)−E(AB′)=E[A(B−B′)] and E(A′B)+E(A′B′)=E[A′(B+B′)]. By the triangle inequality their absolute values sum to at most E[|B−B′|+|B+B′|]. For signs B,B′, exactly one of the two inner quantities is2 and the other is0, so the expectation is2. The singlet correlation identity follows from its density matrix or by evaluating the Pauli tensor products: equal-axis correlations are−1 and cross-axis correlations vanish. For the chosen unit directions, E(a,b)=−1/√2, E(a,b′)=+1/√2, and both E(a′,b),E(a′,b′)=−1/√2. Substitution gives√2+√2. The same local bound applies to convex mixtures of deterministic assignments: integrate the pointwise2 bound against the hidden-variable distribution. This is why checking deterministic signs suffices for the common-probability model. The quantum example violates that model’s correlation constraint; it does not violate an algebraic inequality proved under the model’s own assumptions.

## Limits

The local proof assumes all four outcome variables coexist on one probability space with the required correlations. The calculation does not justify unrelated claims about spacetime, arithmetic spectra or automatic temporal orthogonality.
