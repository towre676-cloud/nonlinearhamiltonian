## Revised question retained in the annual source

Can the proposed width be defined so that its choices and invariances are explicit?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The historical proposal called for a complexity or spread based on heights and pairings. A useful definition can be made on a finite subset of the Mordell–Weil free quotient, but it depends on the chosen set and metric. It is not automatically a canonical new invariant of the curve.

## Definitions

Let V=(E(Q)/torsion)⊗R with canonical-height norm ||P||=√q(P).

For a finite nonempty S⊂V and a unit vector u, define w_u(S)=max_{P∈S}b(P,u)−min_{P∈S}b(P,u).

## Result name

Width bounded by height diameter

## Result statement

With diam(S)=max_{P,Q∈S}||P−Q||, one has0≤w_u(S)≤diam(S), and sup_{||u||=1}w_u(S)=diam(S) when V has positive dimension. The induced distance on E(Q) collapses torsion and becomes a true metric only on the quotient.

## Proof

For a fixed u, the width equals max_{P,Q∈S}b(P−Q,u). Cauchy–Schwarz bounds each term by||P−Q||||u||≤diam(S), proving the upper bound. Choose a diameter-achieving pair P,Q; if its distance is positive, take u=(P−Q)/||P−Q||, giving equality for that pair. If all points coincide, every width is zero. Translation of S does not change pair differences and hence leaves widths and diameter invariant. Replacing the height normalization by cq rescales lengths by√c, so even a numerical width must state its convention. A torsion translate has zero height displacement, explaining the quotient requirement.

## Worked example: setup

Use a two-dimensional height space with Gram matrix[[2,1],[1,2]] and S={0,P,Q}.

## Worked example: calculation

The three pairwise squared distances areq(P)=2,q(Q)=2,q(P−Q)=2, so diam(S)=√2. Takingu=P/√2 gives projected values0,√2,1/√2 and width√2, attaining the diameter. Choosingu=(P+Q)/√6 gives projections0,3/√6,3/√6 and a smaller width√(3/2). The direction is substantive data.

## Worked example: conclusion

The proposal becomes a reproducible finite-set statistic with proved invariances and a sharp bound.

## Limits

This definition is newly chosen for clarity and is not attributed to an established historical theorem. Modular-polynomial relations alone do not select S, a direction, or a height normalization. A number field and its height convention must also be fixed.

## Extension

Compare widths of points of bounded canonical height after specifying a height cutoff and a basis-independent rule for choosing the finite set.
