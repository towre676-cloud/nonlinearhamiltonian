## Revised question retained in the annual source

What distinguishes a high-dimensional clique from a genuine homological cavity?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered topology discussion named directed cliques and five-dimensional cavities without a specified complex. The underlying mathematics can be developed independently of the unsupported physical narrative: dimension measures available simplices, while homology detects cycles modulo boundaries.

## Definitions

A k-simplex has k+1 vertices and all its faces; its geometric realization is contractible.

A flag complex contains a simplex for every clique of its undirected1-skeleton.

## Result name

A twelve-vertex flag triangulation of S⁵

## Result statement

The join of six copies of S⁰ is the boundary of the six-dimensional cross-polytope. It is a flag5-dimensional complex with reduced homology Z in degree5 and zero in other degrees. By contrast a filled5-simplex has zero reduced homology in every degree.

## Proof

A simplex contracts linearly to any vertex, so its reduced homology vanishes regardless of its dimension. For the cross-polytope, take vertices ±e₁,…,±e₆. A face chooses at most one vertex from each opposite pair, making it exactly the join S⁰*⋯*S⁰. Its1-skeleton connects every pair except opposites; a clique therefore also chooses at most one from each pair, proving the complex is flag. The boundary of the convex cross-polytope is homeomorphic to S⁵ by radial projection from its interior origin. Hence its reduced homology has a single rank-one group in degree5. This is a genuine cavity in the homological sense, unlike an individual filled clique.

## Worked example: setup

Compute its face numbers and Euler characteristic, and compare to a clique on six vertices.

## Worked example: calculation

The number of k-faces is2^(k+1)·C(6,k+1), for k=0,…,5:12,60,160,240,192,64. Their alternating sum is0, matchingχ(S⁵)=0; reduced Euler characteristic is−1. The six-vertex clique’s flag complex is one5-simplex with all faces and Euler characteristic1, not a5-cycle.

An explicit complex demonstrates the difference between simplex dimension and homological dimension, including exact counts.

## Limits

The example is undirected and abstract. A directed flag complex needs a separately stated orientation convention; observed network topology would require actual data and a chosen filtration. No brain-scale quantum or Galois inference is retained.
