## Revised question retained in the annual source

Reconstruction: What are the linear tidal equations on a sphere, what assumptions give their local f-plane form, and what energy law and plane-wave dispersion relation follow?

## Status

Classical mathematics newly developed from a recovered assistant-only catalog name

## Summary

The tidal equations are a system for horizontal velocity and free-surface displacement. Their spherical geometry should not be silently replaced by a Cartesian formula. This treatment states the global coordinate model and then develops a precisely delimited local constant-depth calculation whose signs can be checked directly.

## Definitions

On a sphere of radius a use longitude λ, latitude φ, eastward velocity u, northward velocity v, constant mean depth H>0 and displacement η. Set f=2Ωsinφ and take g>0.

For a specified potential Φ, the spherical linear equations are u_t−fv=−(gη+Φ)_λ/(a cosφ), v_t+fu=−(gη+Φ)_φ/a, and η_t+H[ u_λ+(v cosφ)_φ ]/(a cosφ)=0, away from coordinate poles.

A small local patch freezes f and replaces metric derivatives by ∂x,∂y. With Φ=0 this gives u_t−fv=−gη_x, v_t+fu=−gη_y, η_t+H(u_x+v_y)=0. The calculation below is for this f-plane system.

## Result name

Local energy balance and Poincaré-wave dispersion

## Result statement

The local unforced system obeys ∂t[H(u²+v²)/2+gη²/2]+∇·[gHη(u,v)]=0. For a nonzero horizontal wavenumber k, a wave traveling along x has ω²=f²+gHk² and can be written η=Acosθ, u=ωAcosθ/(Hk), v=fAsinθ/(Hk), θ=kx−ωt.

## Proof

Multiply the first momentum equation by Hu and the second by Hv. Their Coriolis terms cancel exactly because −fuv+fvu=0. Multiply continuity by gη and add. The gradient products combine into the displayed divergence by the product rule. Thus integration over a periodic region or a region with zero normal velocity conserves the total positive quadratic energy. For the wave, continuity requires u to have amplitude ωA/(Hk). The second momentum equation then fixes the quadrature component v=fAsinθ/(Hk). Substitution in the first gives (ω²−f²)A/(Hk)=gkA, proving the dispersion relation for A≠0. This direct substitution also fixes the sign of the sine component. A stationary geostrophic component is possible in the full system and is not represented by this propagating branch.

## Worked example: setup

Use the constructed nondimensional coefficients H=1, g=16, f=3, k=1 and A=1/10.

## Worked example: calculation

Then ω=5, η=(1/10)cos(x−5t), u=(1/2)cos(x−5t), and v=(3/10)sin(x−5t). The momentum coefficient identities are 5·(1/2)−3·(3/10)=16/10 and −5·(3/10)+3·(1/2)=0. Continuity gives 5/10−1/2=0. The time period is 2π/5. These residuals and the dispersion equation 25=9+16 are checked exactly with rational coefficients.

## Worked example: conclusion

The example is a real solution certificate of the declared local system, while the global equations retain the geometry needed for an actual spherical tide model.

## Limits

The f-plane approximation is local, and the source’s catalog name alone supplies no coastline, depth map, forcing or dissipation data. The sinusoidal example is not an ocean prediction. Nonlinear advection, changing depth and friction require additional terms and may change the energy budget.

## Extension

Add Rayleigh friction −ru,−rv with r≥0. The energy law then has the nonpositive sink −rH(u²+v²). Including a specified potential contributes work −H(u,v)·∇Φ, which must be retained when comparing forcing and dissipation.
