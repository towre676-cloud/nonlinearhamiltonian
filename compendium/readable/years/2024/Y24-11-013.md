## Revised question retained in the annual source

Why are finite-support binary sequences homoclinic to zero, and what is still required to infer a physical horseshoe?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered symbolic example is correct once the two-sided shift topology is specified. It supplies a complete model calculation; applications to a flow require a separate conjugacy or hyperbolic return-map argument.

## Definitions

Let Σ={0,1}^Z with d(x,y)=Σ_{k∈Z}2^(−|k|)|x_k−y_k|.

The left shift is (σx)_k=x_{k+1}; zero is a fixed point.

## Result name

Finite-support homoclinic characterization

## Result statement

A binary sequence is homoclinic to zero, meaning d(σ^n x,0)→0 as n→±∞, if and only if it has only finitely many nonzero coordinates. The full two-shift has topological entropy log 2.

## Proof

If x is supported in [−M,M], then σ^n x is supported in [−M−n,M−n]. As |n| grows this interval leaves every fixed coordinate window. The metric tail outside a large window is arbitrarily small, proving convergence in both directions. Conversely d(σ^n x,0)≥|(σ^n x)_0|=x_n. Since this is zero or one, convergence as n→+∞ forces x_n=0 for every sufficiently large positive n; convergence as n→−∞ forces the same in the negative tail. Only a finite window remains. For entropy, every length-N binary word occurs and two differing words can be distinguished at some one of the first N iterates, yielding 2^N separated orbit patterns. Conversely there are only 2^N such central words up to fixed finite-window boundary factors, giving entropy log 2.

## Worked example: setup

Let x have ones at −2,0,3 and zero elsewhere. Track the support under σ^n.

## Worked example: calculation

The support becomes {−2−n,−n,3−n}. For n≥4, all nonzero coordinates are negative and the largest metric weight is at most 2^(−(n−3)); summing three contributions bounds d(σ^n x,0)≤3·2^(−(n−3)). A similar bound applies as n→−∞. The convergence is thus explicitly exponential for this finite pattern.

## Worked example: conclusion

The symbolic homoclinic statement is fully proved and gives a precise recurrent-orbit model.

## Limits

A transverse homoclinic intersection for a smooth hyperbolic periodic orbit can lead to a horseshoe, but no concrete historical vector field, return map, or transversality certificate was recovered. Listing application areas is not such a certificate.

## Extension

For an actual map, locate a hyperbolic periodic point and rigorously bound stable/unstable manifold intersections or exhibit two mapped strips with verified cone conditions.
