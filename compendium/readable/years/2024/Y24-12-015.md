## Revised question retained in the annual source

When do odd generators define a cohomological differential, and what extra checks are needed for a proposed superalgebra deformation?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered formula assigned nonzero anticommutators to symbols θ while also calling them Grassmann coefficients. Ordinary Grassmann coordinates anticommute to zero; nonzero anticommutators describe operators in a different algebra. A minimal Lie superalgebra makes the distinction precise.

## Definitions

For homogeneous elements, [x,y]=xy−(−1)^{|x||y|}yx is the graded commutator.

Take an odd Q and an even central H with [Q,Q]=2H and [H,Q]=0.

## Result name

Adjoint differential and its square

## Result statement

For d(x)=[Q,x], the graded Jacobi identity gives d²(x)=[H,x]. Hence d²=0 when H is central, or after restricting to elements commuting with H.

## Proof

Apply graded Jacobi to Q,Q,x. Since Q is odd, the two terms with nested Q brackets agree and yield 2[Q,[Q,x]]=[[Q,Q],x]. Substituting [Q,Q]=2H gives d²=[H,·]. If H is central the right side vanishes, defining cohomology ker d/im d because im d⊆ker d. The one-even, one-odd bracket table itself satisfies graded Jacobi: the only possibly nontrivial all-Q triple reduces to [Q,H]=0. For a proposed deformation [x,y]_ε=[x,y]+εφ(x,y)+O(ε²), the first-order Jacobi coefficient must vanish; this is the 2-cocycle condition. At the next order, the quadratic obstruction is a 3-cocycle, so an H² class is an infinitesimal deformation and need not extend to an exact one.

## Worked example: setup

In a 1|1 graded matrix space take Q=[[0,1],[1,0]] and H=I.

## Worked example: calculation

Q²=I, so [Q,Q]=2I and H is central. For an even diagonal X=diag(a,b), dX=[[0,b−a],[a−b,0]], an odd matrix; applying d again gives Q(dX)+(dX)Q=0. This directly checks d²=0. By contrast two exterior-algebra generators θ₁,θ₂ satisfy θ₁θ₂+θ₂θ₁=0, not an arbitrary scalar C₁₂.

## Worked example: conclusion

The bracket parity, Jacobi condition, and square-zero property are explicitly proved instead of inferred from notation.

## Limits

An x,t-dependent bracket table must satisfy graded Jacobi and suitable regularity; not every coefficient tensor defines a Lie superalgebra. No field theory or biological interpretation is supplied.

## Extension

Write all independent structure constants for a chosen finite graded space, expand graded Jacobi symbolically, and solve its polynomial constraints before computing cohomology.
