## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which arithmetic operation actually produces von Mangoldt weights from Möbius inversion?

## Status

Classical result; recovered proposal repaired

## Summary

The pointwise product in the historical J-function can be contrasted with a genuine Möbius-inversion identity. Dirichlet convolution, rather than pointwise multiplication, converts logarithmic size into the von Mangoldt function. This explains exactly why the logarithmic derivative retains prime powers.

## Definitions

For functions on positive integers, (f*g)(n)=Σ_(d|n)f(d)g(n/d). Let log(n) denote the ordinary logarithm.

The same definitions apply to nonzero integral ideals, replacing divisors by ideal divisors and logn by logN𝔞.

## Result name

An exact Möbius inversion identity

## Result statement

μ*log=Λ. Consequently, for Re(s)>1, −ζ′(s)/ζ(s)=Σ_(n≥1)Λ(n)n^(−s)=Σ_pΣ_(m≥1)(logp)p^(−ms).

## Proof

For n=∏p_i^e_i, the sum of Λ(d) over divisors d isΣ_ie_i logp_i=logn, because precisely the divisors p_i^m contribute. Thus1*Λ=log. Also μ*1 is the convolution identity ε, sinceΣ_(d|n)μ(d)=0 for n>1 and1 for n=1; expanding over subsets of distinct prime divisors gives(1−1)^r. Associativity of the finite divisor sums yields μ*log=μ*(1*Λ)=Λ. To obtain the Dirichlet series, differentiate the absolutely convergent logarithmic Euler expansion logζ(s)=Σ_pΣ_(m≥1)p^(−ms)/m in any half-plane Re(s)≥1+δ. Differentiation cancels m in the denominator and yields the displayed prime-power sum. Absolute convergence on these smaller half-planes justifies the operation.

## Worked example: setup

Compute the convolution at n=12 and n=8.

## Worked example: calculation

At12, the squarefree divisors1,2,3,6 give log12−log6−log4+log2=0=Λ(12). At8 they give log8−log4=log2=Λ(8). The pointwise product μ(8)Λ(8) is instead0. This single example distinguishes the two operations conclusively.
