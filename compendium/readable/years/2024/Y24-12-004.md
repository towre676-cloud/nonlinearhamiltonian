## Revised question retained in the annual source

Do the recovered sequences come from polygon triangulations, and how does a transfer matrix encode the correct recurrence?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The historical sequences were used to motivate knot and particle identifications. Before such interpretations, they must pass elementary triangulation invariants. The recovered examples fail a necessary incidence sum; a genuine quadrilateral example shows the correct algebra.

## Definitions

For a triangulated n-gon, a_i counts incident triangles at vertex i.

Set M(a)=[[a,−1],[1,0]], so det M(a)=1, and define continuants K₀=1,K₁=a₁,K_n=a_nK_{n−1}−K_{n−2}.

## Result name

Incidence sum and transfer recurrence

## Result statement

A polygon triangulation quiddity satisfies Σa_i=3(n−2). The tridiagonal determinant with diagonal a₁,…,a_n and adjacent entries one is K_n. The quadrilateral quiddity (2,1,2,1) satisfies M(2)M(1)M(2)M(1)=−I.

## Proof

A triangulation has n−2 triangles, and each triangle contributes one incidence at three vertices, giving the sum by double counting. Expand the tridiagonal determinant along its last row: the last diagonal term contributes a_nK_{n−1}, and the adjacent pair contributes −K_{n−2}, proving the continuant recurrence. The transfer equation (K_n,K_{n−1})^T=M(a_n)(K_{n−1},K_{n−2})^T follows immediately. For the quadrilateral, choosing a diagonal makes its two endpoints incident to two triangles and the other vertices to one, giving (2,1,2,1). Direct matrix multiplication then proves the displayed −I relation with the chosen order. This is a concrete valid example, not a claim that the incidence sum alone characterizes all quiddities.

## Worked example: setup

Test (2,2,1,3), (2,2,1,3,2,1), and (2,2,1,3,2,1,2,2).

## Worked example: calculation

The sums are 8,11,15. Their lengths are 4,6,8, requiring sums 6,12,18 respectively. Thus none is a polygon-triangulation quiddity under this definition. For a constant diagonal a, the recurrence K_n=aK_{n−1}−K_{n−2} has generating function ΣK_nz^n=1/(1−az+z²), as multiplying and comparing coefficients verifies.

## Worked example: conclusion

The historical input sequences fail a necessary combinatorial test, while the corrected example has both a diagrammatic meaning and an exact matrix certificate.

## Limits

Other generalized quiddity conventions exist, but none was specified. No map from these sequences to particle masses or a particular knot was recovered, and no such map is invented.

## Extension

For a q-continuant choose an explicit recurrence and noncommutative ordering if needed; prove its determinant or transfer representation before assigning applications.
