## Revised question retained in the annual source

What can be deduced from ∇T=0 and a proposed factorization of det T, and what cannot?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered wave-tensor formula combines a bundle section, curvature, knot polynomials, and mock modular factors. Its components must first have compatible mathematical types. A parallel section imposes an integrability condition; a chosen determinant formula can otherwise be satisfied tautologically.

## Definitions

For a connection ∇=d+Γ on a vector bundle, curvature is R=dΓ+Γ∧Γ.

An endomorphism-valued section T has a determinant fiberwise only after its source and target fibers have the same finite rank.

## Result name

Integrability and the insufficiency of prescribing a determinant

## Result statement

If a vector-bundle section v obeys ∇v=0, then Rv=0. For a matrix-valued parallel endomorphism T, the induced connection gives [R,T]=0. Conversely an arbitrary scalar function f can be realized as det diag(f,1,…,1), so prescribing det T=f alone proves no independently specified correspondence.

## Proof

Apply ∇ again to a parallel vector: ∇²v=Rv and the left side vanishes, proving the necessary condition. On End(E), the induced covariant derivative is ∇T=dT+ΓT−TΓ; squaring gives [R,T]. This distinction matters: writing dT+ΓT treats columns as vectors and is not the induced endomorphism connection. For the determinant assertion, a diagonal matrix’s determinant is the product of its diagonal entries, immediately yielding f. Thus any desired scalar combination of a Jones polynomial, a modular function, and a phase can be encoded by construction. A substantive theorem would start with T defined independently from geometric or algebraic data and then derive the factorization with its domains and normalization.

## Worked example: setup

On a trivial rank-two bundle over a plane choose Γ=[[iA,0],[0,0]] with dA nonzero.

## Worked example: calculation

The curvature is R=diag(i dA,0). A parallel vector with nonzero first component would violate Rv=0 wherever dA≠0; only the second flat direction is immediately available. The endomorphism T=diag(f,1) has determinant f, but it is parallel only if df=0 in this diagonal example. The determinant condition therefore does not imply the parallel-section equation.

## Worked example: conclusion

The geometry imposes differential constraints that cannot be inferred from a symbolic product formula.
