## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which quantity has units of energy per volume, and what additional directional-transport assumption converts it to a flux?

## Status

Revised classical development with scoped worked result

## Summary

The important mathematical correction is dimensional, not a revised high-power threshold. Number density multiplied by energy per particle has units of energy density. Flux additionally involves transport speed and direction. The historical extrapolations therefore cannot be repaired by a new numerical substitution alone, and no operational optical calculation is developed here.

## Definitions

Let n have units L⁻³, wavelength λ units L, Planck’s constant h units ML²T⁻¹, and speed c units LT⁻¹. For a monochromatic idealization, photon energy is E=hc/λ and energy density is u=nE. Energy flux has units energy/(area×time).

## Result name

Energy density and directed flux have different dimensions

## Result statement

The product nhc/λ has units ML⁻¹T⁻², not the MT⁻³ units of intensity. For a perfectly collimated stream normal to a plane, flux is cu. An isotropic radiation field has zero net vector flux despite positive energy density.

## Proof

Add unit exponents in nhc/λ to obtain L⁻³·ML²T⁻¹·LT⁻¹/L=ML⁻¹T⁻². To form a flux one multiplies directional energy density by velocity and integrates its normal component. For a collimated normal stream that velocity factor is c. For an isotropic directional distribution, opposite directions have equal energy and opposite velocity, so their vector contributions cancel exactly. The one-sided incident flux through a plane in an isotropic field is instead cu/4, obtained by averaging cosθ over one hemisphere, not cu.

## Worked example: setup

Use a normalized isotropic angular energy distribution u/(4π).

## Worked example: calculation

The incident hemispherical normal flux is ∫₀^{2π}∫₀^{π/2}c[u/(4π)]cosθ sinθ dθdφ=cu/4. Integrating over the full sphere gives zero net flux. The same scalar energy density can therefore accompany distinct transport configurations.

## Worked example: conclusion

A unit audit exposes the missing transport assumption before any threshold numbers are considered.

## Limits

The historical material parameter and proposed photon-density limits were not verified, and no vacuum or material threshold follows from this derivation. It provides no beam-design or exposure instructions.

## Extension
