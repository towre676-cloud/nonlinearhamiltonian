## Definitions

For X/F_q put N_r=#X(F_(q^r)) and Z(X,t)=exp(Σr≥1 N_r t^r/r), interpreted as a formal series.

For a smooth elliptic curve E/F_q, write a_q=q+1−#E(F_q). Its standard degree-two numerator is P_q(t)=1−a_q t+q t²; the full zeta function is P_q(t)/[(1−t)(1−qt)].

## Result name

Point-counting zeta functions and one elliptic local factor

## Result statement

For the affine and projective lines, Z(A¹,t)=1/(1−qt) and Z(P¹,t)=1/[(1−t)(1−qt)]. For E:y²=x³+x+1 over F₅, direct enumeration gives N₁=9 and N₂=27, consistent with a₅=−3 and P₅(t)=1+3t+5t².

## Proof

For the line, N_r=q^r; substitute into the exponential and use −log(1−u)=Σu^r/r. The projective line has one additional point, adding the factor 1/(1−t). Also Symⁿ(A¹) is affine n-space because an unordered multiset of n roots is encoded by the coefficients of its monic polynomial. This explains the symmetric-power coefficient qⁿ in that example. For the elliptic curve, count each right-hand-side value and its square roots, then add the point at infinity. The standard elliptic zeta theorem supplies the degree-two form; comparing the first coefficients gives N₁=q+1−a and N₂=q²+1−(a²−2q). The finite counts verify this instance but are not a proof of that theorem for every curve.

## Worked example: setup

Count the specified elliptic curve first over F₅, then over F₂₅=F₅[u]/(u²−2).

## Worked example: calculation

Over F₅ the numbers of affine y solutions for x=0,1,2,3,4 are 2,0,2,2,2, so N₁=9. Exact finite-field enumeration over F₂₅ gives 26 affine points plus infinity. Since a²−2q=9−10=−1, the predicted extension count is 25+1−(−1)=27. The inverse local elliptic L-factor is 1/(1+3·5^(−s)+5·5^(−2s)); it is not the full point-counting zeta function with its extra degree-zero and degree-two factors. The recovered symmetric-power series has a precise point-counting interpretation: an effective zero-cycle is a nonnegative sum of closed points. A closed point of degree d contributes the geometric series(1−t^d)⁻¹. Multiplying over closed points gives the generating series of their multisets; taking its logarithm yieldsΣ_r N_r t^r/r, because a degree-d point contributes d rational points exactly over extension degrees divisible by d. This connects the symmetric-power generating function, Euler product and exponential point counts under the declared finite-field interpretation.
