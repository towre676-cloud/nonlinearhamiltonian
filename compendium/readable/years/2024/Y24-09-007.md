## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which concrete spaces realize the claimed homology ranks, and what does a positive periodic radial deformation preserve?

## Status

Classical result; recovered proposal repaired

## Summary

The historical rank formula needs a definition of κ and a construction of the underlying space. Two elementary models expose the issue: a finite graph has computable cycle rank, whereas every positive continuous periodic radial graph is a single topological circle.

## Definitions

A finite graph has V vertices, E edges and C connected components; homology here uses integer coefficients.

A positive radial graph is Γ={r(θ)(cosθ,sinθ):θ∈S¹} with continuous r>0.

## Result name

Two exact homology models

## Result statement

For a finite graph, H0≅Z^C, H1≅Z^(E−V+C), and higher homology vanishes. A positive continuous radial graph has H0≅Z and H1≅Z.

## Proof

Orient the graph edges. Its boundary map ∂1:Z^E→Z^V sends an oriented edge to head minus tail. On each connected component, a spanning tree supplies V_component−1 independent boundary columns; all columns lie in the subgroup whose coordinate sum on that component is zero. Thus rank ∂1=V−C. There are no 2-cells, so H1=ker∂1, a free group of rank E−V+C; H0 is the component group. For Γ, the polar parameterization is a continuous bijection from compact S¹ to a Hausdorff space: positivity makes its inverse the angular projection. It is therefore a homeomorphism, and the usual circle homology applies. Subdividing a graph edge increases both E and V by one and leaves E−V+C unchanged. This is an elementary consistency check for the claimed invariant: changing the mesh of a polygonal approximation should not create a new cycle merely by inserting vertices. Adding an actual closing edge can change the rank.

## Worked example: setup

Compare a circle with a connected graph consisting of two circles meeting at one vertex.

## Worked example: calculation

The one-vertex, one-loop circle has V=1,E=1 and β1=1. The wedge of two circles has V=1,E=2 and β1=2. Inserting a continuous positive factor r*(θ)/r(θ) in a radial graph changes neither its one-to-one angular parameterization nor β1.

## Worked example: conclusion

To obtain rank κ−1, one can specify a connected graph with E−V+1=κ−1. The formula is then a graph theorem rather than a consequence of an irrational exponent.

## Limits

Self-intersections, quotient identifications, added closing arcs or singularities can change the space. They require an explicit cell complex; the simple radial-graph proof does not apply to them.

## Extension

Build the proposed construction as a finite cellular approximation, list its incidence matrices, and compute their integer Smith normal forms. This separates actual topology from pictures of an oscillatory parametrization.
