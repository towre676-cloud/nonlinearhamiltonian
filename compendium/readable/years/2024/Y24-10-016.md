## Revised question retained in the annual source

What algebraic condition distinguishes a homomorphic map from a nonlinear feature map?

## Status

Classical result; recovered proposal repaired

## Summary

The recovered application list uses the word homomorphic but gives no map identity. The mathematical core can be developed without making an applied cryptographic recommendation: a homomorphism must preserve a specified operation, and its kernel controls the information lost.

## Definitions

For additive groups, f is a homomorphism when f(x+y)=f(x)+f(y) andf(0)=0.

For a ring map, multiplication and the unit must also be handled under the chosen convention.

## Result name

A quadratic correction is generally not additive

## Result statement

Over a commutative ringR, letf(x)=x+cx². Then f(x+y)−f(x)−f(y)=2cxy. OverZ/qZ, additivity for allx,y is equivalent to2c=0 modq.

## Proof

Expand(x+y)²=x²+2xy+y² and subtract. Necessity follows by settingx=y=1, and sufficiency follows immediately if2c=0. Thus over an odd prime field the only additive member of this family is c=0. In characteristic2, squaring is additive, so the conclusion changes: x↦x² is the Frobenius homomorphism. Additivity alone still does not ensure multiplicativity or invertibility. This small calculation illustrates why the base ring and operation cannot be omitted from a claimed “homomorphic” deformation. If an encoding transports the operation by a bijection as in the previous entry, it is homomorphic by construction, but that is a different operation from ordinary addition on the output labels.

## Worked example: setup

TakeR=Z/7Z andc=1.

## Worked example: calculation

f(1)=2 andf(2)=6, whereasf(1)+f(1)=4, so additivity fails. Alsof(1)=f(5)=2 mod7, showing this map is not injective. A collision matters algebraically: it prevents recovery of a unique input through an inverse, regardless of how the map is named. For comparison, x↦3x mod7 is an additive bijection with inversex↦5x, but it is not a unital ring automorphism because it maps1 to3.

## Worked example: conclusion

One exact identity can validate or refute a proposed algebraic property. Nonlinearity by itself is neither homomorphism nor secrecy.
