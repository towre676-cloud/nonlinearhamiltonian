## Definitions

Take states σ=(σ₁,σ₂)∈{−1,1}² and energy E(σ)=−Jσ₁σ₂. At inverse temperature β set π(σ)=exp(−βE(σ))/Z.

A Metropolis step chooses one spin uniformly, proposes its flip and accepts with probability min(1,exp(−βΔE)); rejection leaves the state unchanged.

## Result name

A complete two-spin Gibbs and Metropolis model

## Result statement

Z=4cosh(βJ), E_π[σ₁σ₂]=tanh(βJ), and the one-spin Metropolis chain is reversible with stationary law π. For βJ=log2, each aligned state has probability2/5 and each anti-aligned state probability1/10.

## Proof

There are two aligned states of energy−J and two anti-aligned states of energy J, so Z=2e^(βJ)+2e^(−βJ)=4cosh(βJ). Inserting the product σ₁σ₂=±1 into the expectation gives [2e^(βJ)−2e^(−βJ)]/Z=tanh(βJ). For neighboring states x,y differing by one spin, the symmetric proposal has probability1/2. Thus π(x)P(x,y)=(1/(2Z))min(e^(−βE(x)),e^(−βE(y))), visibly symmetric in x,y. Summing detailed balance over x proves stationarity. At βJ=log2, the aligned Boltzmann weight is2, the anti-aligned weight1/2, and Z=5, giving the stated probabilities.

## Worked example: calculation

From an aligned state, a proposed flip raises energy by2J and is accepted with probability e^(−2βJ)=1/4. Therefore each of its two anti-aligned neighbors has transition probability1/8 and the self-transition is3/4. From an anti-aligned state, both single-spin flips lower energy and each aligned neighbor is reached with probability1/2. Every aligned-to-anti edge has stationary flow(2/5)(1/8)=1/20, matching its reverse flow(1/10)(1/2). The correlation is(4/5)−(1/5)=3/5, agreeing with tanh(log2). The finite chain is irreducible, and its positive aligned self-transition makes it aperiodic. At β=0 all four states have equal probability and the spin correlation is zero. Increasing positive βJ raises the aligned probability continuously, approaching one in total as βJ tends to infinity. These are finite-state limiting statements, not a nonanalytic phase transition.
