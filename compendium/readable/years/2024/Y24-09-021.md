## Revised question retained in the annual source

How can the recovered Toeplitz limit be proved in a concrete nontrivial family?

## Status

Classical result; recovered proposal repaired

## Summary

The recovered spectral-limit expression needs a symbol, matrix convention and test-function class. The tridiagonal Toeplitz family gives a complete exact calculation and makes the limiting measure visible without relying on a general theorem whose hypotheses were omitted.

## Definitions

Let T_n have diagonal entries a and adjacent off-diagonal entries b, with real a,b. Its symbol is f(θ)=a+2b cosθ.

The empirical spectral average is n⁻¹Σ_(k=1)^nF(λ_k(T_n)).

## Result name

An exact Toeplitz spectral limit

## Result statement

The eigenvalues are λ_k=a+2b cos(kπ/(n+1)). For every continuous F on[a−2|b|,a+2|b|], the empirical average tends toπ⁻¹∫₀πF(a+2b cosθ)dθ, equivalently the normalized circle integral.

## Proof

For k=1,…,n set v_j=sin(jkπ/(n+1)), with boundary values v_0=v_(n+1)=0. The identity v_(j−1)+v_(j+1)=2cos(kπ/(n+1))v_j shows T_nv=λ_kv. The sine vectors are an orthogonal basis, so these are all eigenvalues, including multiplicities. The average of F at the equally spaced interior angles is a Riemann sum for the continuous integrand. Replacing n by n+1 in the normalization changes the limit by a factor tending to1, and the endpoints contribute vanishing weight. Reflection of cosine extends the half-circle integral to the full-circle expression. When b=0 the measure is the point mass at a, covered by the same formula. For b≠0, changing variablesx=a+2b cosθ gives limiting density1/[π√(4b²−(x−a)²)] on the interior of the spectral interval. Its integrable endpoint singularities explain why a uniform angular mesh does not produce a uniform eigenvalue density. This density is derived from the same cosine map, not separately fitted.

## Worked example: setup

Choose a=3,b=1 and F(t)=t².

## Worked example: calculation

Tr(T_n²)=n a²+2(n−1)b²=11n−2, so the empirical second moment is11−2/n. The integral gives the same limit because the cosine mean is0 and cosine-square mean is1/2: a²+4b²(1/2)=11. At n=3, the eigenvalues are3+√2,3,3−√2, with squares totaling31=11·3−2.

## Worked example: conclusion

The recovered limit is valid here with explicit finite-size error for the quadratic test function.

## Limits

This proof is for a scalar real tridiagonal symbol and continuous F. General symbols, discontinuous tests, nonnormal Toeplitz matrices or logarithms near zero require additional theorems. A spectral average alone does not establish a proposed decay-rate formula.

## Extension

For polynomial symbols, compare normalized traces of powers and control boundary terms. Then extend from polynomial to continuous test functions by uniform approximation.
