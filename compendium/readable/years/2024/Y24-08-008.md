## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which L-function and exact operator correspondence are intended, and what hypothesis covers every nontrivial zero rather than a finite sample?

## Status

Revised classical development with scoped worked result

## Summary

A rigorous revision identifies the specific analytic function and the missing all-zero theorem. Functional symmetry, entire continuation and a finite verified zero list are valuable information, but do not force every future zero onto a line in a broad class of analytic functions. A polynomial modification makes that limitation exact without constructing a counterexample to an actual L-function conjecture.

## Definitions

For the Riemann zeta function define ξ(s)=s(s−1)π^(−s/2)Γ(s/2)ζ(s)/2, with its analytic continuation. It is entire and satisfiesξ(s)=ξ(1−s), together with conjugation symmetry. RH concerns its zeros on Re(s)=1/2. Generalized versions must name a different L-function and its completion.

## Result name

Symmetry alone does not force the critical line

## Result statement

The real polynomial P(s)=[(s−1/4)²+1][(s−3/4)²+1] satisfiesP(1−s)=P(s) and has zeros1/4±i,3/4±i. Multiplying an entire symmetric function by P preserves those elementary symmetries while adding off-line zeros.

## Proof

The substitution s↦1−s exchanges the two quadratic factors, so their product is invariant. Real coefficients give conjugation symmetry, and factoring each positive quadratic over C gives its declared roots. Multiplication by a polynomial preserves entire continuation and does not alter previously known zeros. It does not preserve an Euler product or make the new function an arithmetic L-function. Thus the example refutes only an inference from these insufficient analytic conditions, not RH itself. An operator proof would additionally need a rigorous bijection covering every zero, with the correct multiplicities and domain conditions.

## Worked example: calculation

Any zero genuinely represented this way lies on the critical line. The unsolved step is proving that every relevant zero has such a representation; a few matching gaps or numerical spacings cannot supply surjectivity. The polynomial P explicitly shows why omitted zeros can defeat an otherwise symmetric analytic construction.
