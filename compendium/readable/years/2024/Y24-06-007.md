## Revised question retained in the annual source

What are the actual roots and splitting field of x⁴−2x²+9, and which automorphisms fix each quadratic subfield?

## Status

Classical mathematics with newly authored derivation or finite example and explicit scope

## Summary

The original assistant claimed real-radical roots ±√(1±√8) and Galois group D₄. Both can be tested exactly. The corrected example is especially useful because all roots already lie in a biquadratic field of degree four.

## Definitions

Let α=√2+i and β=√2−i. Then αβ=3, α²=1+2i√2 and β²=1−2i√2.

Let K=Q(√2,i). Its two sign changes σ:√2↦−√2 and τ:i↦−i commute.

## Result name

The recovered quartic has Klein-four rather than dihedral Galois group

## Result statement

The roots are α,−α,β,−β; the splitting field is K and Gal(K/Q)≅C₂×C₂. Its three quadratic fixed fields are Q(i), Q(√2) and Q(√−2), corresponding to σ,τ and στ.

## Proof

The product (x²−α²)(x²−β²) equals x⁴−(α²+β²)x²+α²β²=x⁴−2x²+9. Since Q(√2) is real it cannot contain i, so [K:Q]=4. Conversely 3/α=β, and α+3/α=2√2 while α−3/α=2i. Therefore Q(α)=K, proving that this quartic is irreducible and that its splitting field has degree four, not eight. The two independent sign changes give four automorphisms and every nonidentity has order two. Their fixed quadratic combinations are immediate: στ fixes i√2, whose square is −2.

## Worked example: calculation

The map τ swaps α with β and −α with −β. The map σ sends α to −β and β to −α. Their product sends each root to its negative. None has order four, unlike a rotation in D₄. Substituting the historically stated squared roots would instead use 1±√8, whereas the quadratic equation for x² is y²−2y+9=0 with discriminant −32; the missing imaginary factor explains the failed root formula. The fixed fields of the three distinct order-two automorphisms are exactly Q(√2), Q(i) and Q(√−2). For instance simultaneous sign reversal fixes the product i√2 while changing each factor. Each is a quadratic extension of Q, and the splitting field has degree two over each, exactly matching the subgroup indices.

## Limits

This is one special irreducible quartic. A generic quartic with another coefficient pattern may genuinely have D₄ or S₄ Galois group; no general quartic classification is inferred from this example.
