## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which coherence is lost under local dephasing, and what does repeated projection actually preserve?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered Hamiltonian continuation invoked GHZ coherence and Zeno topology. Finite density-matrix dynamics gives an exact calculation: local phase noise destroys a global off-diagonal term, while ideal repeated projections suppress one specified transition under explicitly conditioned evolution.

## Result name

Exact GHZ coherence decay and a Zeno survival law

## Result statement

The GHZ off-diagonal element decays as e^(−Nγt)/2, while the two diagonal populations remain1/2. For a qubit with H=ΩX, repeatedly testing the initial|0⟩ state N times over total time t gives total survival probability[cos²(Ωt/N)]^N→1.

## Proof

For the operator C=|0…0⟩⟨1…1|, each Z_j contributes Z_jCZ_j=−C, so its generator action is(γ/2)Σ(−2C)=−NγC. Diagonal basis projectors are fixed. Solving the scalar ODE gives the coherence. Since X^⊗N interchanges the two strings, its expectation is e^(−Nγt), while all diagonal Z-correlations are unchanged and therefore cannot certify the surviving global coherence. For the Zeno calculation, e^(−iΩXΔt)|0⟩ has return amplitude cos(ΩΔt). Each successful projective measurement resets the normalized state to|0⟩, so the probability of N successes multiplies to the displayed expression. Expanding log cos²x=−x²+O(x⁴) gives log probability=−Ω²t²/N+O(N^(−3))→0.

## Worked example: setup

TakeN=3 for GHZ dephasing and a fixed total observation time t.

## Worked example: calculation

The coherence witness⟨X⊗X⊗X⟩ is e^(−3γt). The fully dephased mixture has the same computational-basis probabilities as the original pure state, yet this witness is zero. For the separate qubit Zeno model, N=1 gives cos²(Ωt); increasing the number of ideal tests drives the all-success probability toward one.

## Worked example: conclusion

The two effects have exact equations and distinguish unconditional noise from a specified sequence of measurement outcomes.

## Limits

The Zeno limit assumes ideal instantaneous projections and an increasing measurement rate; it is not a topology theorem or a guarantee under finite-bandwidth noise. No physical implementation is proposed.

## Extension

Replace ideal projections by a specified completely positive instrument and compare the unconditional channel with its postselected successful branch.
