## Revised question retained in the annual source

What spectrum results from the recovered time-derivative term in the simplest periodic model?

## Status

Classical result; recovered proposal repaired

## Summary

The historical advanced sum includes iℏ∂t+H(t). Even when this is self-adjoint, it is an operator on a time-function space, not merely an additional spatial potential. Its basic periodic spectrum is unbounded in both directions.

## Definitions

Work on L²(R/TZ)⊗C^d with periodic H¹ domain, letω=2π/T andℏ>0.

Take a constant Hermitian matrix H0 with eigenvectors u_j and eigenvaluesμ_j.

## Result name

Exact periodic-time spectrum

## Result statement

K=iℏ∂t+H0 is self-adjoint and has eigenvectors e^(inωt)u_j with eigenvaluesμ_j−ℏnω, n∈Z. Its heat traceΣe^(−βλ) diverges for everyβ>0.

## Proof

The periodic Fourier basis diagonalizes i∂t: differentiation of e^(inωt) gives inω times the function, so multiplication by i yields−nω. The self-adjoint diagonal operator has domain characterized by square-summability of n times the Fourier coefficients, which is the periodic H¹ domain. The bounded Hermitian term H0 commutes with this Fourier decomposition and preserves self-adjointness. The displayed tensor products form a complete orthogonal basis. For n→+∞, μ_j−ℏnω→−∞; consequently e^(−β(μ_j−ℏnω)) tends to infinity rather than zero. The positive heat-trace sum cannot converge. Quasienergy is often considered moduloℏω in Floquet theory, but that quotient is a separate spectral interpretation, not a convergent Gibbs trace of K.

## Worked example: setup

Let d=1,μ=1/3,ℏ=1,T=2π.

## Worked example: calculation

The eigenvalues are1/3−n, so n=−1,0,1 give4/3,1/3,−2/3. The same physical quasienergy class has infinitely many translated representatives. Inserting these raw eigenvalues into a positive-temperature partition sum makes it diverge.

## Worked example: conclusion

The time term is mathematically meaningful on a defined domain, but it changes boundedness and multiplicity properties that a spatial heat-trace argument may have relied on.

## Limits

A genuinely time-dependent H(t) leads to a monodromy or propagator problem. The constant example does not prove a general Floquet theorem, and it supplies no arithmetic correspondence.

## Extension

Construct the unitary propagator over one period and compare its eigenphases with K moduloℏω. Keep that unitary spectral problem distinct from a semibounded elliptic operator.
