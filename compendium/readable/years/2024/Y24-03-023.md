## Summary

The Schwarzian involves third derivatives but has a particularly simple composition law. Specifying the order of composition matters: postcomposing by a Möbius map leaves it unchanged, whereas precomposition contributes a squared derivative. This entry derives the identities directly rather than relying on a memorized invariance slogan.

## Definitions

For a real C³ function f on an interval with f′≠0, define S(f)=f‴/f′−(3/2)(f″/f′)². The same formula applies to locally univalent holomorphic functions.

Write P(f)=f″/f′. Then S(f)=P(f)′−P(f)²/2, which makes cancellation in the composition rule visible.

A nonconstant Möbius map is M(x)=(ax+b)/(cx+d), with ad−bc≠0, restricted to an interval avoiding its pole.

## Result name

A chain rule and the local zero-Schwarzian classification

## Result statement

S(f∘g)=(S(f)∘g)(g′)²+S(g). Every Möbius map has zero Schwarzian, and locally every C³ map with nonzero derivative and zero Schwarzian is Möbius. Hence S(M∘f)=S(f), with the indicated domain restrictions.

## Proof

The ordinary chain rule gives P(f∘g)=(P(f)∘g)g′+P(g). Differentiate this identity, subtract half its square, and use g″=P(g)g′. The two mixed terms cancel, leaving exactly the displayed formula. To classify S(f)=0, put P=f″/f′; it satisfies P′=P²/2. Local uniqueness for this scalar ODE implies either P is identically zero or, after separating variables, P=−2/(x+c) on a suitable interval. The first case integrates to a nonconstant affine f. The second gives f′=A/(x+c)² and f=B−A/(x+c), again Möbius. Conversely direct differentiation of a Möbius map, or these forms, gives S=0. This is a local statement: poles and coordinate domains must still be tracked when joining intervals.

## Worked example: setup

Compare f(x)=eˣ, g(x)=x² on x>0, and their composite.

## Worked example: calculation

Since all derivatives of eˣ are eˣ, S(eˣ)=−1/2. For x², S(x²)=−3/(2x²). Thus S(e^(x²))=(−1/2)(2x)²−3/(2x²)=−2x²−3/(2x²), equal to −7/2 at x=1. Differentiating e^(x²) independently gives f′=2xe^(x²), f″=(2+4x²)e^(x²) and f‴=(12x+8x³)e^(x²), confirming the same rational expression. The verifier checks this at exact rational x after cancelling the common exponential factors.

## Worked example: conclusion

The third-derivative invariant has a short structural proof, and the example fixes both the sign and the placement of the derivative factor.

## Limits

Nonvanishing f′ and g′ is essential wherever the formula is used. Zero Schwarzian does not imply that a map is affine; reciprocal maps are immediate counterexamples. No global univalence theorem follows merely from this local computation.

## Extension

If y₁,y₂ are independent solutions of y″+Q(x)y=0, their ratio has Schwarzian 2Q where y₂ and the Wronskian do not vanish. This supplies a bridge to projective coordinates, with domains stated before taking the ratio.
