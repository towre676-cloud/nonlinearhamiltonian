## Revised question retained in the annual source

What exact small examples prevent a scalar arithmetic feature from being mistaken for either a class group or a Galois group?

## Status

Classical result; recovered proposal repaired

## Summary

The recovered application list joins class-group and Galois-group computations. These are distinct mathematical tasks. The Gaussian field gives a complete contrast: its ideal class group is trivial while its Galois group overQ has two elements.

## Definitions

The ideal class group of a number field is its nonzero fractional ideals modulo principal ideals.

The Galois group Gal(K/Q) consists of field automorphisms fixingQ, with composition.

## Result name

An exact Gaussian example

## Result statement

ForK=Q(i), Cl(Z[i]) is trivial andGal(K/Q)≅C2. The two invariants cannot be identified even in this simplest quadratic case.

## Proof

To prove the class-group statement, use Euclidean division inZ[i]. For nonzeroβ and anyα, writeα/β=u+iv and choose Gaussian integerq by roundingu andv to nearest integers. Then|α/β−q|²≤1/2<1, soN(α−qβ)<Nβ. The norm is a Euclidean function, hence every ideal is principal: choose a nonzero element of minimum norm in the ideal and divide any other element by it; the smaller remainder must vanish. Thus every fractional-ideal class is trivial. For the Galois group, an automorphism must sendi to a root ofx²+1, so there are at most two possibilities. Bothi↦i andi↦−i extend toQ-automorphisms. The latter has order2, givingC2.

## Worked example: setup

Compare an invariant assigned to the prime37 with these groups.

## Worked example: calculation

Because37=1²+6²=(1+6i)(1−6i), its ideal splits into two principal prime ideals, both trivial in the class group. Complex conjugation exchanges them and is nontrivial in the Galois group. A scalar weight on37 does not specify either the ideal equivalence relation or the field automorphism action.

## Worked example: conclusion

The groups arise from different constructions and can have different sizes. Exact algorithms need the corresponding ideals or field equations.

## Limits

The example does not solve arbitrary class-group or Galois-group computation, nor does it validate an undefined J-based shortcut. Class field theory relates certain abelian Galois groups to ideal-theoretic data, but it does not identify every Galois group with the ordinary class group.

## Extension

Given a number field polynomial, compute its ring of integers and ideal relations separately from its splitting-field automorphisms. If a J-map is proposed as an accelerator, prove which of these data it preserves.
