## Revised question retained in the annual source

How can the recovered Laguerre–spherical expression be made square-integrable and orthonormal?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The historical expression used bare Laguerre polynomials as radial wave factors and invoked spherical orthogonality for a torus. Both require correction. Orthogonality depends on the integration measure and domain, not on the names of the functions.

## Definitions

For α>−1, Laguerre polynomials satisfy ∫₀∞x^αe^(−x)L_n^α(x)L_m^α(x)dx=Γ(n+α+1)δ_nm/n!.

Take normalized spherical harmonics Y_l^m with ∫S²overline{Y_l^m}Y_{l′}^{m′}dΩ=δ_ll′δ_mm′.

## Result name

An explicit radial normalization

## Result statement

For k>0 define R_n(r)=k^(3/2)√(n!/Γ(n+α+1))·(kr)^((α−2)/2)e^(−kr/2)L_n^α(kr). Then ∫₀∞R_nR_m r²dr=δ_nm, and R_nY_l^m is orthonormal in the product measure r²dr dΩ.

## Proof

Substitute x=kr so r²dr=x²dx/k³. Multiplying the radial factors contributes k³, x^(α−2), and e^(−x); together with the measure these give exactly x^αe^(−x)dx. The stated Laguerre norm then cancels the normalization constants for n=m and gives zero otherwise. Angular integration supplies the remaining Kronecker factors. Bare L_n^α(kr) is a polynomial and is not square-integrable on the half-line with measure r²dr; the exponential and power factors are substantive. For α near 2.4 the displayed expression is integrable at zero, but smoothness at the origin and being an eigenfunction of a specific PDE impose additional conditions.

## Worked example: setup

Choose α=2,k=1 and n=0,1 with a common normalized Y₀⁰.

## Worked example: calculation

L₀²=1,L₁²=3−r. Thus R₀=e^(−r/2)/√2 and R₁=e^(−r/2)(3−r)/√6. Their cross inner product is (1/√12)∫r²e^(−r)(3−r)dr=(1/√12)(3·2!−3!)=0; each norm is one by factorial integration.

## Worked example: conclusion

The revised basis has a stated Hilbert space and an exact normalization check.

## Limits

Spherical harmonics are not automatically orthogonal on a torus with its induced area measure. Nor does this basis solve a physical PDE unless the operator and boundaries are separately given. Historical resonator counts do not change those facts.

## Extension

For an actual torus, start from its Laplace–Beltrami operator and metric measure or use a clearly specified periodic flat-torus Fourier basis.
