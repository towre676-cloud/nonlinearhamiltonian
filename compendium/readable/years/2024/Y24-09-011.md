## Revised question retained in the annual source

What well-posed scalar fractional equation replaces a formal fractional beta-function formula?

## Status

Classical result; recovered proposal repaired

## Summary

The historical formula leaves the fractional derivative and its initial data unspecified. A clean model takes t=log(μ/μ0)≥0 and uses the Caputo derivative. Even a linear right-hand side then produces a different evolution law from an ordinary differential equation.

## Definitions

For 0<γ<1, D_C^γg(t)=Γ(1−γ)⁻¹∫₀ᵗ(t−s)^(−γ)g′(s)ds when the integral is defined.

E_γ(z)=Σ_{n≥0}z^n/Γ(γn+1) is the Mittag-Leffler function.

## Result name

Exact linear fractional evolution

## Result statement

The initial-value problem D_C^γg=ag, g(0)=g0 has solution g(t)=g0E_γ(at^γ). Equivalently, g=g0+(a/Γ(γ))∫₀ᵗ(t−s)^(γ−1)g(s)ds.

## Proof

For a monomial with exponent q>0, the beta-integral substitution s=tu gives D_C^γt^q=Γ(q+1)t^(q−γ)/Γ(q+1−γ); a constant has Caputo derivative zero. Apply this identity to the convergent Mittag-Leffler series. Its nth term for n≥1 maps to a times the (n−1)st term, giving the differential equation and the stated initial value. Integrating the Caputo equation by the fractional integral yields the Volterra equation; conversely that equation can be differentiated fractionally under standard local regularity. Uniqueness on compact time intervals follows by iterating the Volterra bound: a difference is bounded by C|a|^nt^(nγ)/Γ(nγ+1) for all n, whose right side tends to zero. At γ=1 the Mittag-Leffler series becomes the ordinary exponential becauseΓ(n+1)=n!, so the model continuously connects to the familiar linear flow in that parameter limit. A constant forcing term b can also be handled explicitly: g(t)=g0E_γ(at^γ)+b t^γE_(γ,γ+1)(at^γ), with the two-parameter function defined by its gamma-denominator series.

## Worked example: setup

Use γ=1/2, a=−1 and g0=1.

## Worked example: calculation

The solution is E_(1/2)(−√t)=e^t erfc(√t). Its expansion begins1−2√t/√π+t−4t^(3/2)/(3√π)+…, so its initial ordinary derivative is singular. For large t it decays like1/√(πt), whereas the ordinary equation g′=−g gives e^(−t). These are mathematically different flows.

## Worked example: conclusion

The fractional order changes the memory kernel and long-time decay, not merely a numerical coefficient in an ordinary beta function.

## Limits

A nonlinear gauge-theory beta function, renormalization interpretation and physical dimensions have not been supplied by this scalar model. Changing to a Riemann–Liouville derivative also changes the initial-data formulation.

## Extension

For a locally Lipschitz nonlinear right-hand side, formulate the corresponding Volterra fixed-point problem and prove local existence on a stated interval before attempting parameter fitting.
