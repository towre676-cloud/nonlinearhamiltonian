## Revised question retained in the annual source

How is e defined as a number, and what equivalence relation turns different triangles into the same geometric form?

## Status

Classical mathematics with newly authored derivation or finite example and explicit scope

## Summary

The user contrasted an ideal circle, mathematical abstraction, e and triangleness. A mathematical answer should define the objects and prove a property of each rather than infer their ontological status. An irrationality proof for e and an affine classification of triangles provide concrete content.

## Definitions

Define e=Σn≥0 1/n!, a convergent real series. A nondegenerate triangle is the convex hull of three noncollinear plane points.

Two ordered triangles are affinely equivalent when one invertible affine map sends their three vertices in the prescribed order.

## Result name

A real constant and a geometric shape require different definitions

## Result statement

The number e is irrational. Any two ordered nondegenerate plane triangles are related by exactly one invertible affine map, although their Euclidean lengths and angles need not agree.

## Proof

Suppose e=a/b. Choose n≥max(b,2). Then n!e and n!Σk=0^n1/k! are integers. Their difference R=Σj≥1 1/[(n+1)…(n+j)] is positive and is strictly less than Σj≥1(n+1)^(−j)=1/n<1, an impossibility for an integer. For triangles, let P=[p₂−p₁,p₃−p₁] and Q=[q₂−q₁,q₃−q₁]. Noncollinearity makes both invertible. The unique map is x↦q₁+QP⁻¹(x−p₁). Any affine map agreeing at the three vertices must have this same linear part and translation.

## Worked example: setup

Map the standard triangle with vertices (0,0),(1,0),(0,1) to (1,1),(3,1),(1,4).

## Worked example: calculation

The map is (x,y)↦(1+2x,1+3y). Areas scale by |det diag(2,3)|=6, while the object remains in the same affine equivalence class of nondegenerate triangles. A triangle boundary is homeomorphic to a circle, but the filled triangle is a disk; the intended shape therefore also depends on whether its interior is included. These definitions are unlike the scalar e, for which equality is ordinary real-number equality. The inverse affine map is ((u−1)/2,(v−1)/3), proving equivalence in both directions. Its determinant is1/6, reciprocal to the forward area factor. This exact invertibility uses nondegenerate triangles; a collinear triple has no invertible two-dimensional affine identification with the standard filled triangle.

## Worked example: conclusion

Numbers, embedded figures and equivalence classes are all mathematical objects, but their properties are tested by different definitions and theorems.

## Limits

The calculation does not settle whether an ideal object exists physically or where a mental representation is located. It also proves irrationality, not the deeper transcendence of e.
