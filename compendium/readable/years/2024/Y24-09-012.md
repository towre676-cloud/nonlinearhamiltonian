## Definitions

Let E/Q be y²+y=x³−x. Its generalized Weierstrass coefficients are a1=a2=a6=0,a3=1,a4=−1.

At a prime p of good reduction, a_p=p+1−#E(F_p), and its Euler factor is (1−a_p p^(−s)+p^(1−2s))⁻¹.

## Result name

## Result statement

The discriminant of E is37. Its point counts at p=2,3,5,7 are5,7,8,9, giving traces −2,−3,−2,−1 respectively.

## Proof

The standard Weierstrass quantities are b2=0,b4=−2,b6=1,b8=−1. Substitution into Δ=−b2²b8−8b4³−27b6²+9b2b4b6 gives64−27=37, so all four listed primes are good. To count, enumerate x,y∈F_p satisfying y²+y−x³+x=0 and add one point at infinity. This is an exact finite computation, not a floating-point fit. The trace formula then gives the displayed a_p. Hasse’s bound |a_p|≤2√p is satisfied in each case. For p=2, x³−x=0 for both x, and y²+y=0 for both y, so there are four affine points and one point at infinity. The local Euler factor forces prime-power coefficient relations: at a good prime, a_(p²)=a_p²−p. Thus the counted a5=−2 determines a25=−1 in the classical L-series. A correction that changes a5 but leaves a25 untouched generally breaks this relation, even if both displayed angles remain within their allowed real range.

## Worked example: calculation

The right sides x³−x for x=0,1,2,3,4 are0,0,1,4,0. The left sides y²+y take the values0,2,1,2,0. Therefore the numbers of y-solutions are2,2,1,0,2, totaling7 affine points. Thus #E(F5)=8 and a5=−2, with cosθ5=−1/√5. Applying a power correction to −2 produces a different number which is not automatically a Frobenius trace of any elliptic curve.
