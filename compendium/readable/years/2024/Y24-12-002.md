## Revised question retained in the annual source

How do transverse and traceless constraints reduce a plane-wave symmetric tensor to two independent components?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered TT conditions are linear constraints on a spatial symmetric perturbation after choosing a propagation direction and gauge. Their two-dimensional solution space can be computed directly without asserting a nonlinear quantum-gravity construction.

## Definitions

Let h be a symmetric 3×3 matrix representing the spatial amplitude of a plane wave with nonzero wavevector k.

The transverse condition is k_i h_ij=0 and the trace condition is Σ_i h_ii=0.

## Result name

TT amplitude space for a fixed direction

## Result statement

For k parallel to the z-axis, every TT amplitude is h=h₊[[1,0,0],[0,−1,0],[0,0,0]]+h×[[0,1,0],[1,0,0],[0,0,0]]. Under a transverse basis rotation by φ, the two components mix by a rotation of angle 2φ.

## Proof

Symmetry begins with six independent entries. Since k_z≠0, transversality forces h_zx=h_zy=h_zz=0. The trace then gives h_yy=−h_xx, leaving h_xx and h_xy as the two free entries. Write the transverse 2×2 matrix as [[a,b],[b,−a]] and transform it by R(φ)^T h R(φ). Multiplication gives a′=a cos2φ+b sin2φ and b′=−a sin2φ+b cos2φ for that passive convention. This double-angle transformation explains the spin-two polarization behavior at the level of the linear representation. The Frobenius squared norm is 2(a²+b²) for real amplitudes and is invariant under the basis change.

## Worked example: setup

Take h₊=1,h×=0 and rotate the transverse axes by φ=π/4.

## Worked example: calculation

cos(π/2)=0 and sin(π/2)=1, so the new components are (0,−1) in the stated passive convention. The original plus pattern becomes a cross pattern of the same norm; a rotation by π returns the same tensor components, while a rotation by π/2 changes both signs.

## Worked example: conclusion

The two polarization degrees of freedom and their transformation law follow from explicit linear constraints.

## Limits

The calculation assumes a fixed nonzero wavevector and a linearized plane-wave setting. Gauge fixing, the linearized field equation, and nonlinear gravitational dynamics are additional questions. A generic symmetric tensor field is not automatically TT.

## Extension

For any nonzero k, form P=I−kk^T/||k||² and the projector h↦PhP−(1/2)P tr(Ph), then verify transversality, tracelessness, and idempotence.
