## Revised question retained in the annual source

How do branching multiplicities determine dimensions in an inductive system of finite-dimensional algebras?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered request connected Bratteli diagrams with Gaussian extensions and dynamical constructions. The basic algebraic content is a multiplicity matrix describing how simple matrix blocks embed, together with path counts. It is useful without identifying unrelated constructions.

## Definitions

Let A_n=⊕_{v∈V_n}M_{d_v}(C).

An edge multiplicity B_{wv} records how many copies of the v-th defining representation occur in the w-th block at the next level.

## Result name

Dimension consistency and path counting

## Result statement

A unital block inclusion requires d′_w=Σ_vB_{wv}d_v. Iterating gives d^(n)=B_{n−1}⋯B_0d^(0); the matrix product entries count directed paths with their edge multiplicities.

## Proof

A representation of ⊕_vM_{d_v} on the next block’s space decomposes into copies of the standard modules C^{d_v}. If there are B_{wv} copies, dimensions add to Σ_vB_{wv}d_v. A unital embedding fills the entire next block, giving equality. Matrix multiplication counts paths because a path from v to u through w has multiplicity B_next(u,w)B_current(w,v), and summing over w counts every possible intermediate vertex exactly once. The same argument inducts over levels. The diagram therefore records inclusion multiplicities, though additional equivalences and ordered K-theory are needed for broad classification claims.

## Worked example: setup

Use two vertices per level, incidence B=[[1,1],[1,0]], and initial dimension vector (1,1).

## Worked example: calculation

The successive vectors are (2,1),(3,2),(5,3). The first inclusion C⊕C→M₂(C)⊕C sends (a,b) to (diag(a,b),a). At the next level the first block combines one copy of dimensions two and one, giving M₃; the second uses the two-dimensional block, giving M₂. The total algebra dimensions as vector spaces are 2,5,13,34, since they are sums of squared block dimensions, not sums of path counts.

## Worked example: conclusion

The distinction between module dimension, algebra dimension, and number of paths is explicit and prevents common bookkeeping errors.
