## Revised question retained in the annual source

Reconstruction: For a simply supported rectangular thin plate, what does a single sine load produce, and how does the Rayleigh quotient bound an eigenvalue and quantify an approximate eigenvector?

## Status

Classical mathematics newly developed from a recovered assistant-only catalog name

## Summary

A plate equation needs its boundary conditions before a Fourier formula is valid. A simply supported rectangle provides a case where each sine mode diagonalizes both the static equation and the vibration equation. The Rayleigh quotient is the corresponding energy ratio, with a small matrix example showing its algebra transparently.

## Definitions

In the linear isotropic Kirchhoff–Love model let D=Eh³/[12(1−ν²)]>0 be bending stiffness and μ=ρh>0 surface mass density. Use DΔ²w+μw_tt=p.

On a rectangle (0,a)×(0,b), Navier simply supported conditions are w=0 and Δw=0 on each straight edge. For a smooth solution they agree with zero normal bending moment because the tangential second derivative of the zero boundary trace vanishes.

For real symmetric A and nonzero v, define R_A(v)=vᵀAv/(vᵀv). For an admissible Navier plate mode the corresponding ratio is D∫(Δw)²/(μ∫w²).

## Result name

Mode amplitudes, vibration frequencies and a residual bound

## Result statement

Let φ_mn=sin(mπx/a)sin(nπy/b), λ_mn=(mπ/a)²+(nπ/b)². Then Δφ=−λφ and Δ²φ=λ²φ. A static load Pφ gives w=Pφ/(Dλ²), and a free vibration in this mode has ω²=Dλ²/μ. Also λ_min(A)≤R_A(v)≤λ_max(A) and dist(R_A(v),spectrum(A))≤||Av−R_A(v)v||/||v||.

## Proof

Differentiate each sine twice to obtain its Laplacian. Both φ and Δφ vanish on all four edges, so the mode satisfies the declared boundary conditions. Substitution reduces the static PDE to Dλ²W=P and the vibration PDE to μW″+Dλ²W=0. For the matrix statements, expand v=Σcⱼeⱼ in an orthonormal eigenbasis. Then R is the weighted average Σλⱼcⱼ²/Σcⱼ², proving the interval bound. The squared normalized residual is Σ(λⱼ−R)²cⱼ²/Σcⱼ², at least the square of the distance to the spectrum. This proves the residual estimate. Two integrations by parts under w=Δw=0 similarly turn ∫wΔ²w into ∫(Δw)², explaining the plate energy quotient. No unproved convergence assertion for an arbitrary Fourier load is needed for the single-mode example.

## Worked example: setup

Take a=b=π, m=n=1, D=3, μ=2 and load p=12sin x sin y. Separately take A=[[2,1],[1,2]], v=(1,2).

## Worked example: calculation

For the plate λ=2, so w=sin x sin y and ω²=6. The matrix has eigenvalues one and three, while R_A(v)=14/5. Its residual is (6/5,−3/5), giving normalized residual 3/5. The distance from 14/5 to the spectrum is 1/5, within that bound. The verifier checks all matrix identities exactly and checks the plate coefficients symbolically by integer arithmetic.

## Worked example: conclusion

Boundary conditions make the PDE mode legitimate, and the quotient supplies a concrete spectral bound rather than merely a suggestive analogy.

## Limits

This uses a thin, small-deflection, linear plate model. Clamped edges require different mode shapes, so the same sine formula cannot be transferred to them. A Rayleigh quotient is an estimate for a specified self-adjoint operator; it need not be an eigenvalue.

## Extension

For a finite span of admissible modes, form stiffness and mass matrices and solve Kc=ω²Mc. With positive definite M this becomes a symmetric matrix problem after conjugating by M^(−1/2), preserving the quotient interpretation.
