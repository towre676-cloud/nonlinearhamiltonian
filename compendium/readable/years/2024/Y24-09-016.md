## Revised question retained in the annual source

What does a small computed eigenpair residual prove, and why can a matching list of zeta ordinates still be inconclusive?

## Status

Classical result; recovered proposal repaired

## Summary

The recovered scripts compare finite eigenvalues with a short list of zeta-zero ordinates. A residual is a useful certificate when the operator is self-adjoint and its domain is controlled. Without those hypotheses even a very small residual may not place an eigenvalue nearby.

## Definitions

For a unit vector v in the domain of A and a real candidate λ, the residual is r=Av−λv.

For self-adjoint A, its spectral measure represents squared norms by integrals over its spectrum.

## Result name

A self-adjoint residual bound

## Result statement

If A is self-adjoint, dist(λ,σ(A))≤||r||. For a nonnormal matrix this conclusion can fail by an arbitrarily large factor.

## Proof

By the spectral theorem, ||(A−λ)v||²=∫|t−λ|²dμ_v(t). The probability measure μ_v is supported on σ(A), so the integral is at least dist(λ,σ(A))²∫dμ_v=dist(λ,σ(A))². Taking square roots gives the bound. This asserts proximity to some spectral point; it does not identify which eigenvalue, prove multiplicity or exclude missing eigenvalues. For a nonnormal counterexample let A=[[0,M],[0,0]] and w=(1,1/M), with λ=1. Then (A−I)w=(0,−1/M), so the normalized residual is1/√(M²+1), while the spectrum is{0} and the spectral distance is1. A certified residual interval may contain several eigenvalues. If the operator has a known isolated spectral cluster and a gap separating it from the rest, stronger subspace-angle estimates become possible. Such a gap must be established independently; choosing the nearest target ordinate afterward does not provide it.

## Worked example: setup

Use M=100 and compare with a diagonal self-adjoint matrix B=diag(1,4).

## Worked example: calculation

The nonnormal matrix has a residual below0.01 at λ=1 despite having no eigenvalue within distance1 of that candidate. For B and v=(√(1−ε²),ε), the residual at λ=1 is3|ε|, and the true eigenvalue1 is indeed present. If a candidate is put into the matrix by construction, a small residual establishes only that construction, not correspondence with an independently defined arithmetic operator.

## Worked example: conclusion

Operator structure turns a residual into a precise spectral statement. Matching finitely many target numbers is a different validation question.

## Limits

The recovered operator and discretization were not specified sufficiently to certify self-adjointness, convergence or a complete zero–eigenvalue bijection. Numerical matches do not prove RH.

## Extension

For a concrete Galerkin scheme, prove a domain-compatible error bound and independently count eigenvalues in each certified interval. Record residuals, truncation error and the target arithmetic statement separately.
