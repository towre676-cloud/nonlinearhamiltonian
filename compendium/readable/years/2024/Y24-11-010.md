## Revised question retained in the annual source

What useful connection between representation theory and differential models follows from an actual symmetry action?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The historical discussion sought a bridge from finite-group blocks to fluid design. A defensible bridge begins with a group acting on a specified state space and an operator commuting with that action. It yields invariant subspaces, not a numerical identity between character heights and Reynolds numbers.

## Definitions

Let a finite group G act unitarily through U_g on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space V.

Define the averaging operator P=|G|^(−1)Σ_g U_g.

## Result name

Reynolds projection and equivariant block decomposition

## Result statement

P is the orthogonal projection onto V^G. If L commutes with every U_g, then LP=PL; more generally L preserves each isotypic component of the representation.

## Proof

For every h, U_hP=P because multiplication permutes G. Thus the image is invariant, and P acts as the identity on invariant vectors. Hence P²=P. Taking adjoints replaces each g by g^(−1), so P*=P and the projection is orthogonal. If LU_g=U_gL for every g, linearity gives LP=PL. Decomposing V into irreducible G-types and using the character central projectors gives preservation of each isotypic piece. Multiplicity spaces may still have nontrivial dynamics; symmetry alone does not diagonalize them. This proof is valid for a finite discretization of a PDE whenever both the discretization and boundary conditions preserve the action.

## Worked example: setup

Let G=C₂ act on R² by swapping coordinates and let L=[[a,b],[b,a]].

## Worked example: calculation

P=(1/2)[[1,1],[1,1]] projects onto span(1,1). The complementary antisymmetric line is span(1,−1). L acts with eigenvalues a+b and a−b on these lines. Conversely a general matrix [[a,b],[c,d]] commutes with the swap exactly when a=d and b=c, so this reduction is a checkable assumption, not an automatic consequence of a geometric picture.

## Worked example: conclusion

Symmetry yields an exact computational reduction once the representation and equivariance are supplied.

## Limits

Complex representations and modular p-blocks involve different coefficient characteristics. No recovered map identifies Brauer defect groups with fluid observables, and the historical analogy does not prove one.

## Extension

For a symmetric domain and boundary conditions, construct the induced action on a finite element space and check the assembled mass and stiffness matrices commute with every generator.
