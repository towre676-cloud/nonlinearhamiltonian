## Definitions

For a locally integrable real b and bounded interval I, let b_I be its average and osc_I(b)=|I|⁻¹∫_I|b−b_I|. The classical BMO seminorm is the supremum over intervals. An H¹ atom on I has mean zero, support in I and L∞ norm at most1/|I|.

## Result name

A sharp local atom pairing and a robust BMO perturbation bound

## Result statement

|∫ab|≤osc_I(b) for an atom a supported on I. If b∈BMO and||e||∞≤ε, then b+e∈BMO and||b+e||_BMO≤||b||_BMO+2ε. Applying the same bound to−e gives the finite two-sided seminorm difference bound.

## Proof

Because the atom has mean zero, ∫ab=∫a(b−b_I). Bounding|a| by1/|I| gives the first inequality. For the perturbation, write(b+e)−(b+e)_I=(b−b_I)+(e−e_I); the triangle inequality gives osc_I(b+e)≤osc_I(b)+osc_I(e). Since|e_I|≤ε, the latter oscillation is at most2ε. Take the supremum over I, then exchange b and b+e to obtain the reverse comparison. The argument identifies exactly which perturbation norm controls the irregularity seminorm.
