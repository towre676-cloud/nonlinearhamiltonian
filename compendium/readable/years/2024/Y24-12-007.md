## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which algebraic conditions define a projection, and why do negative eigenvalue real parts not prove monotone norm decay?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The historical framework listed P†=P, P²=P, and eigenvalue real parts ≤0 near the same operator discussion. These are distinct properties. Orthogonal projections have a very restricted spectrum; stable generators require an evolution equation and a norm estimate.

## Definitions

An orthogonal projection satisfies P*=P and P²=P.

For u′=Au, Euclidean energy decreases for all initial data exactly when A+A* is negative semidefinite.

## Result name

Projection spectrum and the dissipativity criterion

## Result statement

An orthogonal projection has eigenvalues only zero and one. The semigroup exp(tA) is norm-contracting for every t≥0 if A+A*≤0; eigenvalues with negative real parts alone do not imply this contraction.

## Proof

If Pv=λv then λ²v=λv, so λ∈{0,1}. Self-adjointness ensures an orthonormal eigenbasis and ||P||≤1. For an evolving vector, d||u||²/dt=⟨u,(A+A*)u⟩, proving sufficiency of the dissipativity condition. If energy is nonincreasing for every initial vector, evaluating the derivative at t=0 proves necessity. A matrix may nevertheless have all eigenvalues in the left half-plane and have a positive symmetric part because its eigenvectors are nonorthogonal. Such a matrix eventually decays in finite dimensions but may initially amplify vectors. This is a norm-sensitive phenomenon rather than a contradiction of spectral stability.

## Worked example: setup

Take A=[[-1,4],[0,−1]] and u(0)=(1,1).

## Worked example: calculation

Both eigenvalues equal −1. Yet A+A*=[[-2,4],[4,−2]] has eigenvalues 2 and −6, and the initial energy derivative is (1,1)(A+A*)(1,1)^T=4>0. The exact solution is e^(−t)(1+4t,1), confirming transient amplification. By contrast −P generates exp(−tP)=I−P+e^(−t)P, a contraction that preserves the kernel component.

## Worked example: conclusion

Projection, asymptotic spectral stability, and monotone energy stability are mathematically different and can be checked separately.

## Limits

This is finite-dimensional analysis. For unbounded generators, the operator domain and semigroup generation theorem are essential; a formal spectrum alone is even less sufficient.

## Extension

Choose a positive definite metric M and solve A*M+MA≤0 to certify contraction in the weighted norm u*Mu when a Euclidean certificate fails.
