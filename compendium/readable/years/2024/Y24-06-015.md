## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which degrees, orientation and metric define the exterior operations, and when does a closed form have a global potential?

## Status

## Summary

The recovered exterior-calculus passages mixed antisymmetry, volume, differential forms and topology. The key distinctions are degree, closure and exactness. A disk example verifies Stokes directly, while a punctured-plane example shows precisely why local potentials need not glue into one global scalar.

## Definitions

For a p-form α and q-form β, α∧β=(−1)^(pq)β∧α. Thus two one-forms anticommute, but two two-forms commute.

On oriented Euclidean R³ use dx∧dy∧dz as volume and the Hodge star defined by α∧*β=⟨α,β⟩vol. In particular *(dx∧dy)=dz.

## Result name

Wedge products, Hodge duality and a genuine closed-form obstruction

## Result statement

On the plane, η=(−y dx+x dy)/2 satisfies dη=dx∧dy and ∮_(unit circle)η=π. On the punctured plane, ω=(−y dx+x dy)/(x²+y²) is closed but not exact; its circle integral is2π. This is a well-defined topological obstruction to one global potential.

## Proof

Differentiate η term by term: d(−y dx)=−dy∧dx=dx∧dy and d(x dy)=dx∧dy, giving dη=dx∧dy. Parameterize the circle by x=cos t,y=sin t to obtain η=dt/2 and integralπ, equal to the disk area. For ω, the coordinate calculation ∂x[x/(x²+y²)]−∂y[−y/(x²+y²)]=0 gives dω=0 wherever the denominator is nonzero. On the unit circle ω=dt and its integral is2π. If ω=df globally, the integral along any closed parametrized path would equal f at the endpoint minus f at the start, hence zero. The nonzero period disproves exactness. Locally one can use an angle branch, but no single-valued angle exists on the full punctured plane.

## Worked example: setup

Express area and volume using coordinate differentials rather than informal antisymmetric symbols.

## Worked example: calculation

(a dx+b dy)∧(c dx+d dy)=(ad−bc)dx∧dy, so the coefficient is the oriented parallelogram area. In R³, (dx∧dy)∧dz is the volume form and its coefficient changes sign when orientation reverses. A scalar density ρ becomes a mass three-form ρ dx∧dy∧dz; multiplying scalar functions alone does not create a three-form. The Hodge star additionally depends on the metric, whereas wedge multiplication does not.

## Worked example: conclusion

The local operations and the global obstruction are now separate statements with direct calculations.

## Limits

The closed-not-exact example concerns the punctured domain; including the origin makes ω undefined. Hodge theory on general manifolds requires boundary conditions and analytic hypotheses not supplied by these identities.

## Extension

Compute periods on a declared basis of H₁ of a domain and test whether a closed one-form can be exact, rather than using an undefined global-flow obstruction.
