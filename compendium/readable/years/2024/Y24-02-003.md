## Revised question retained in the annual source

Reconstruction: What extra state relation is needed to turn charge into an energy quantity, and how does a specified lumped model account for delivered power and loss?

## Summary

This entry is a new mathematical reconstruction of a recovered qualitative concept. The source does not contain the equations below. ## Definitions

Charge q is measured in coulombs, current I in coulombs per second, voltage in joules per coulomb, energy in joules and power in joules per second.

Let q∈[0,Q] denote remaining charge. Prescribe a continuous open-circuit voltage function V_oc(q) and define the model storage function E(q)=∫_0^q V_oc(s)ds.

For discharge take I(t)≥0, qdot=−I, resistance R≥0 and terminal voltage V(t)=V_oc(q(t))−RI(t). This is a stated lumped model, not a complete electrochemical description.

## Result name

An exact balance law for the prescribed storage model

## Result statement

Along an absolutely continuous charge trajectory with integrable power terms, Edot=−V_oc(q)I almost everywhere. Therefore Edot+P_out+P_loss=0 with P_out=VI and P_loss=RI². Charge capacity Q alone does not determine stored energy: the voltage-state law is also required.

## Proof

The fundamental theorem of calculus gives E′(q)=V_oc(q). Applying the chain rule to qdot=−I yields Edot=−V_oc(q)I. The terminal relation gives P_out=(V_oc−RI)I, and adding RI² recovers V_ocI, proving the identity. Integration from t0 to t1 gives E(q(t0))−E(q(t1))=∫P_out dt+∫RI²dt. The loss is nonnegative because R≥0. To prove nonidentifiability from charge alone, compare two constant laws V_oc(q)=v1 and V_oc(q)=v2 with v1≠v2: both allow the same Q but give E(Q)=v1Q and v2Q. For a variable law the correct quantity is its integral, not an arbitrarily selected endpoint voltage times Q.

## Worked example: setup

As an explicitly constructed example choose Q=3600C, V_oc(q)=3+q/Q volts, R=1/10Ω and constant discharge I=2A until q reaches zero.

## Worked example: calculation

The duration is Q/I=1800s. Initial stored model energy is ∫_0^Q(3+q/Q)dq=(7/2)Q=12600J. Resistive loss is (1/10)·2²·1800=720J. Delivered energy is 11880J, and the balance is exact. Because one watt-hour is 3600J, these values are 3.5Wh, 0.2Wh and 3.3Wh. Terminal voltage decreases from 3.8V to 2.8V and remains positive in this example. These parameters were selected for the worked mathematics and were not recovered from the 2024 passage.
