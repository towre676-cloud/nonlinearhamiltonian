## Summary

The recovered discussion used Galois and Langlands as analogies for a complete integral and operator flow. A precise arithmetic example instead distinguishes automorphism groups, local splitting data, and a proposed correspondence to automorphic representations. The analogy supplies no map by itself.

## Definitions

For a polynomial over Q, its splitting field is the smallest field containing all roots; its Galois group consists of Q-automorphisms.

For Q(i), complex conjugation is the nontrivial automorphism, and unramified odd primes split or remain inert.

## Result name

Elementary quadratic splitting criterion

## Result statement

For an odd prime p, x²+1 has a root in F_p exactly when p≡1 mod4. Thus p splits in Q(i) for p≡1 mod4 and is inert for p≡3 mod4; p=2 is ramified.

## Proof

If a²=−1 mod p, Fermat’s theorem gives 1=a^(p−1)=(−1)^((p−1)/2), so p≡1 mod4. Conversely the finite multiplicative group F_p× is cyclic. If p≡1 mod4 and g generates it, a=g^((p−1)/4) has square g^((p−1)/2)=−1, the unique element of order two. The factorization of x²+1 into distinct linear factors or an irreducible quadratic modulo odd p gives the corresponding prime splitting in the Gaussian integers. At p=2, 2=−i(1+i)², so the prime ramifies. This computes local behavior from one global field; it does not prove a general reciprocity or Langlands theorem.

## Worked example: setup

Compare p=5,13,3,7 and the automorphisms of Q(i).

## Worked example: calculation

2²≡−1 mod5 and 5²≡−1 mod13, while no root exists modulo3 or7. The two automorphisms send i to i and −i. The associated quadratic character χ₋₄ is 1 for odd n≡1 mod4, −1 for n≡3 mod4, and zero for even n; it records precisely these unramified splitting signs.

## Worked example: conclusion

The example has explicit arithmetic objects and calculations. Langlands concerns correspondences between appropriate Galois-type and automorphic objects, not an alternative word for solving a differential equation.

## Limits

No biological, neural, or mass-ratio consequence follows. The general correspondence is not proved here, and neither a complete integral nor an unspecified spectral operator identifies its required representations.
