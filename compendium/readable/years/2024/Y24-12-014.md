## Revised question retained in the annual source

How are heat traces and zeta functions related, and why does the free-space hydrogen spectrum not satisfy the same hypotheses?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered heat-kernel discussion contains a genuine compact spectral construction. Zero modes and trace-class conditions must be handled explicitly. A circle supplies exact formulas and a Mellin-transform proof; an unconfined Coulomb Hamiltonian is a different spectral problem.

## Definitions

Let A=−d²/dx² on a circle of length L, with periodic H² domain.

Remove its zero eigenvalue when defining ζ_A(s)=Σ_{λ>0}λ^(−s).

## Result name

Circle heat trace and Mellin identity

## Result statement

The positive eigenvalues are (2πn/L)² with multiplicity two for n≥1, so ζ_A(s)=2(L/2π)^(2s)ζ_R(2s) for Re(s)>1/2. Also Γ(s)ζ_A(s)=∫₀∞t^(s−1)(Tr e^(−tA)−1)dt in that half-plane.

## Proof

The periodic Fourier basis diagonalizes A, giving the eigenvalues and the zeta formula by direct summation. For λ>0, substituting u=λt in the gamma integral yields ∫₀∞t^(s−1)e^(−λt)dt=Γ(s)λ^(−s). Absolute convergence for Re(s)>1/2 permits interchange with the eigenvalue sum, proving the Mellin identity. The subtraction of one removes the constant zero mode and makes the large-t integral converge. Poisson summation of the Gaussian gives Tr e^(−tA)=L/√(4πt) Σ_{k∈Z}exp(−k²L²/(4t)); thus the leading small-t term is L/√(4πt), with exponentially small corrections. This explains the half-plane threshold and, after subtracting asymptotic terms, the route to meromorphic continuation.

## Worked example: setup

Take L=2π and evaluate the zeta function at s=1.

## Worked example: calculation

ζ_A(1)=2ζ_R(2)=π²/3. The heat trace is 1+2Σ_{n≥1}e^(−tn²), and subtracting its zero mode gives exactly the positive spectrum. By contrast, hydrogen bound energies approach zero from below and have growing degeneracies; Σe^(−βE_n) already diverges on those bound states, and unbounded space adds continuum difficulties.

## Worked example: conclusion

The compact spectral relation is proved in a fully explicit model, and its failure to apply naively to hydrogen is located in the hypotheses.

## Limits

General heat-kernel asymptotic coefficients require a smooth compact manifold and a specified elliptic operator, with boundary conditions if there is a boundary. This entry does not construct a gravitational partition function.

## Extension

Use the small-t expansion to continue the Mellin integral after subtracting each singular term, keeping track of zero modes and possible poles.
