## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which smooth planar deformations preserve constant width, and why is a nonlinear radial correction insufficient?

## Status

Classical result; recovered proposal repaired

## Summary

The geometry in the recovered framework becomes testable when width is expressed through a support function. This also separates a body’s radial distance from the distance of its supporting line, quantities that generally differ.

## Definitions

For a convex body K, h(θ)=max{x·(cosθ,sinθ):x∈K} is its support function. Its width in direction θ is h(θ)+h(θ+π).

A twice continuously differentiable h with h+h″>0 parameterizes a strictly convex curve by x(θ)=h(θ)n(θ)+h′(θ)t(θ).

## Result name

An explicit family and an obstruction

## Result statement

The support function h(θ)=1+εcos3θ has constant width2 and is strictly convex for |ε|<1/8. Applying h↦h+αh^β, with α>0 and 0<β<1 and nonzero sufficiently small ε, generally destroys constant width.

## Proof

Because cos(3(θ+π))=−cos3θ, the sum h(θ)+h(θ+π)=2. Differentiating twice gives h+h″=1−8εcos3θ, which is positive under the stated bound; the curve formula then has positive radius of curvature. For the nonlinear correction, write c=cos3θ. The new width is2+α[(1+εc)^β+(1−εc)^β]. At c=0 it is2+2α. At c=1 strict concavity of u^β yields (1+ε)^β+(1−ε)^β<2, so the width is strictly smaller. Equivalently its Taylor expansion starts2+2α+αβ(β−1)ε²c²+O(ε⁴). The nonconstant second-order term is an explicit obstruction. The support-function curve formula also explains closure: both h andh′ are2π-periodic, so the parameterized endpoints agree. Translating the body adds a first harmonic a cosθ+b sinθ to h; this preserves width because that harmonic is odd under the antipodal shift. The third harmonic changes shape without merely translating it.

## Worked example: setup

Take ε=1/16, α=1/30 and β=2/3.

## Worked example: calculation

The curvature radius lies between1/2 and3/2, so the starting curve is safely convex. Its original width is exactly2. The corrected widths at c=0 and c=1 differ by (1/30)[(17/16)^(2/3)+(15/16)^(2/3)−2], which is negative by strict concavity.

## Worked example: conclusion

The proposed universal correction does not preserve constant width even on a small smooth family. A preservation theorem needs an odd-antipodal support perturbation.

## Limits

A radial function r(θ) cannot be treated as h(θ) without proving the relation for the chosen body. The proof is planar; higher-dimensional support functions have an analogous antipodal condition but different curvature tests.

## Extension

Construct deformations h_t=w/2+g_t with g_t(θ+π)=−g_t(θ), and bound h_t+h_t″ below. This provides an actual admissible family rather than a visual approximation.
