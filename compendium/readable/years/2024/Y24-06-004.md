## Revised question retained in the annual source

How do cardinality, scalar-field dimension, intrinsic topology and ambient placement differ in the proposed number sheets?

## Status

Classical mathematics with newly authored derivation or finite example and explicit scope

## Summary

The user asked whether different kinds of numbers could exist on two- or three-dimensional sheets. A coordinate embedding can place the same numbers in many ambient spaces without changing their algebra. Rational points on a circle give a stronger example: exact rational coordinates can lie densely on a curved geometric object.

## Definitions

Q² is the set of ordered pairs of rationals; Q⊂R can also be placed in R³ by q↦(q,0,0). These are different choices of ambient coordinates.

For rational t set P(t)=((1−t²)/(1+t²),2t/(1+t²)).

## Result name

Rational coordinates do not select an ambient dimension

## Result statement

P(t) is a rational point on x²+y²=1. Every rational point except (−1,0) arises this way. Q² is countable and dense in R², while R is uncountable; none of these facts makes rationals inherently two-dimensional or reals inherently three-dimensional.

## Proof

Squaring and adding the coordinates of P(t) gives [(1−t²)²+4t²]/(1+t²)²=1. Conversely a rational point with x≠−1 has t=y/(1+x), and substitution using x²+y²=1 recovers the two coordinates. Rational t values are dense in R, and continuity of P makes these rational points dense in the circle except for the limiting missing point, which is approached as |t| grows. Countability of Q² follows by enumerating pairs of a countable set, while density follows by choosing a rational number in each coordinate interval. A set injection into an ambient space makes no claim about linear or topological equivalence.

## Worked example: setup

Take t=3/4.

## Worked example: calculation

The circle point is (7/25,24/25), with squared coordinates summing to one. The same pair can be displayed as (7/25,24/25,0) in R³, and the rational number 7/25 can be displayed on a line in either R² or R³. Its rationality has not changed. As Q-vector spaces, however, Q and Q² have dimensions one and two, so a mere enumeration is not a Q-linear isomorphism. The rational subspace topology also matters: no nonempty interval of R is countable, whereas every subset of Q is countable. Dense placement consequently does not make Q an open one-dimensional real manifold or turn a rational sheet into a continuous plane.

## Worked example: conclusion

The proposed sheets can be used as pictures, but algebraic and geometric dimension must be stated separately.

## Limits

This does not assign physical existence to an abstract set or resolve the philosophical status of mental representations. It answers the mathematical embedding and coordinate question only.
