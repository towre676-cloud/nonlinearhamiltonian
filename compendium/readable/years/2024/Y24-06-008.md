## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which weight and index convention determine T₂, and does the supplied q-series satisfy the resulting eigenvalue relation?

## Status

Classical mathematics with newly authored derivation or finite example and explicit scope

## Summary

The recovered formula used a₂n+2a_n without a weight or divisibility condition. In the standard level-one weight-k normalization, the second coefficient term is 2^(k−1)a_(n/2), with zero when n is odd. An Eisenstein series lets every coefficient be checked arithmetically.

## Definitions

Let f(τ)=Σn≥0a_n qⁿ be a weight-k modular form for SL₂(Z). For T₂ use b_n=a_(2n)+2^(k−1)a_(n/2), with a_(n/2)=0 for odd n and b₀=(1+2^(k−1))a₀.

Take E₄=1+240Σn≥1σ₃(n)qⁿ, where σ₃(n)=Σd|n d³.

## Result name

The weight-dependent Hecke formula and an exact Eisenstein eigencheck

## Result statement

T₂E₄=9E₄ coefficientwise. The normalized nonconstant Dirichlet series Σσ₃(n)n^(−s) equals ζ(s)ζ(s−3) for Re s>4, with local factor [(1−p^(−s))(1−p^(3−s))]⁻¹.

## Proof

Write n=2^r m with m odd. Divisor multiplicativity gives σ₃(n)=(1+8+⋯+8^r)σ₃(m). For r=0, σ₃(2n)=9σ₃(n). For r≥1, the finite geometric sums satisfy g_(r+1)+8g_(r−1)=9g_r, proving the Hecke relation at every positive coefficient; the constant coefficient also multiplies by nine. For the Dirichlet series, expand σ₃(n)=Σab=n b³ and interchange absolutely convergent sums when Re s>4, obtaining Σa a^(−s)Σb b^(3−s). Euler products of the zeta functions give the stated local factor.

## Worked example: setup

Use a₁=240,a₂=2160,a₃=6720,a₄=17520.

## Worked example: calculation

The first transformed coefficients are b₁=a₂=2160=9a₁ and b₂=a₄+8a₁=17520+1920=19440=9a₂. The historical expression a₂n+2a_n gives a different result and even places its second term at the wrong index. The series for E₄ itself has coefficients240σ₃(n), so its unnormalized L-series includes an overall240; normalizing the first nonconstant coefficient removes that factor. The constant coefficient obeys the same eigenvalue law: the n=0 formula gives1+8=9. The historical alternative would instead give constant coefficient3, already contradicting that eigenvalue on E₄. For n=2^r m with m odd, σ₃(n)=σ₃(m)(1+8+⋯+8^r); the identity at2n is the finite geometric-sum recurrence. For odd n the second term vanishes. This directly accounts for every index rather than inferring modularity from a few coefficients.

## Worked example: conclusion

The operator, modular weight and L-series normalization must be specified together. A short exact coefficient calculation can reject an incorrect Hecke formula.

## Limits

The coefficient identity here is for the declared level-one normalization. Other conventions and levels modify formulas, and an Eisenstein product is not the same as the degree-two local factor of an elliptic cusp form.

## Extension

Apply the same test to an explicitly supplied cusp-form expansion, using enough certified coefficients and a stated modular-form theorem to upgrade a finite comparison to an identity.
