## Revised question retained in the annual source

What would a spectral operator have to prove in order to encode the Mordell–Weil rank rather than merely restate it?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The corrected historical formula concerns an elliptic curve over Q, its rational points, and the order of vanishing of its L-function. A proposed eigenvalue multiplicity is a separate structure and must not silently turn the rank conjecture into a theorem.

## Definitions

For an elliptic curve E/Q, Mordell–Weil gives E(Q)≅Z^r⊕T with T finite.

If an analytic function f is not identically zero near s=1, ord_1(f) is the least k with f^(k)(1)≠0.

## Result name

What a local determinant actually encodes

## Result statement

For a finite Hermitian matrix A and f(s)=det(A+(s−1)I), ord_1(f)=dim ker A. This is a proved matrix identity; identifying f with a normalized elliptic L-function would require an additional independent theorem.

## Proof

The spectral theorem supplies real eigenvalues λ_1,…,λ_n and f(s)=∏(λ_j+s−1). Exactly the zero eigenvalues contribute factors s−1; each nonzero eigenvalue contributes a factor nonzero at s=1. Therefore the order equals the nullity. Without Hermiticity, the order is algebraic multiplicity and can exceed kernel dimension: a nonzero nilpotent Jordan block already demonstrates the distinction. The BSD rank assertion r=ord_1L(E,s) is a deep arithmetic conjecture in general, with important proved cases; neither the finite determinant identity nor naming an operator proves it. A viable spectral proposal must construct its domain independently, prove self-adjointness or its replacement, and establish an actual determinant or trace formula with convergence and normalization.

## Worked example: setup

Take A=diag(0,0,2) and N=[[0,1],[0,0]].

## Worked example: calculation

det(A+(s−1)I)=(s−1)²(s+1), so the order and nullity are both two. For N, det(N+(s−1)I)=(s−1)² but ker N has dimension one. This exactly exposes the missing semisimplicity assumption in a generic “multiplicity equals rank” sentence.

## Worked example: conclusion

The historical formula can be retained as a conjectural arithmetic target, while the elementary spectral consequence is stated with sufficient hypotheses.

## Limits

No historical elliptic curve, conductor, L-series, or spectral operator was recovered. The general BSD conjecture is not proved here, and a diagonal operator manufactured from desired ranks has no predictive arithmetic content.

## Extension

Choose a specific curve with documented analytic and algebraic rank, compute its rational-point subgroup and L-series independently, and then test a separately defined spectral construction.
