## Revised question retained in the annual source

When does a graded character converge, and what additional data makes a formal path integral an actual measure?

## Status

## Summary

The recovered equations used graded characters and completed mock transformations together with an unspecified higher-field measure. Series, functions, formal power series, and probability measures are different objects. Their domains cannot be supplied merely by placing them in one formula.

## Definitions

For finite-dimensional graded pieces V_n, the formal character is χ_V(q)=Σ_{n≥0}dim(V_n)q^n.

A formal series is algebraic coefficient data; evaluating it analytically requires convergence.

## Result name

Character convergence and a unit-circle obstruction

## Result statement

If dim(V_n)≤C(n+1)^d, then χ_V(q) converges absolutely for |q|<1. If infinitely many dimensions are nonzero, the same series need not converge at a unit-circle point; for one-dimensional V_n, Σq^n fails there because its terms do not tend to zero.

## Proof

For r=|q|<1, the series ΣC(n+1)^d r^n converges by the ratio test, so comparison gives absolute convergence. A necessary condition for ordinary series convergence is that its terms tend to zero. At |q|=1 the magnitudes of q^n are all one, violating that condition in the stated example. At the historical q=e^(iπ/φ), the modulus is exactly one, so an interior q-series cannot simply be evaluated by substitution without a radial-limit, regularization, or analytic-continuation theorem for that particular function. A mock modular completion also includes a specified nonholomorphic correction and multiplier system; writing only a transformation slogan does not define it. Likewise a formal symbol Dφ supplies no countably additive measure until a measurable space and compatible finite-dimensional or other construction are given.

## Worked example: setup

Take V_n=C for every n and compare the formal character with a finite Gaussian integral.

## Worked example: calculation

Inside the unit disk χ_V(q)=1/(1−q) by the geometric series. On the unit circle q≠1, the rational function has a finite analytic value, while the defining series still fails to converge ordinarily. Separately ∫R exp(−x²/2)dx=√(2π) defines a normalized Gaussian after division; the normalization is essential and explicitly finite-dimensional.

## Worked example: conclusion

Analytic continuation can produce a function value without making the original series converge. Formal field notation likewise needs an independent construction.
