## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which derivative does a rotation-based output require, and is the recovered additive weight update actually gradient descent?

## Status

Classical mathematics with newly authored derivation or finite example and explicit scope

## Summary

The quantum-neuron passage supplied qubit states, controlled rotations, a measurement probability and a heuristic learning rule. A one-qubit model is sufficient to compute the correct derivative and produce a counterexample to an update that substitutes an arbitrary feature expectation for that derivative.

## Definitions

Let R_y(w)=[[cos(w/2),−sin(w/2)],[sin(w/2),cos(w/2)]] act on |0⟩. Measure |1⟩ with probability p(w)=sin²(w/2).

For target t∈[0,1], set L(w)=(1/2)(p(w)−t)². The recovered heuristic has the form w_new=w+η(t−p(w))f, where f is a feature expectation.

## Result name

A qubit rotation has an exact gradient and a testable learning rule

## Result statement

The exact derivatives are p′(w)=(1/2)sin w and L′(w)=(p(w)−t)(1/2)sin w. The parameter-shift identity is p′(w)=[p(w+π/2)−p(w−π/2)]/2. Replacing this derivative by a general f does not guarantee descent.

## Proof

Matrix multiplication gives R_y(w)|0⟩=cos(w/2)|0⟩+sin(w/2)|1⟩. Squaring the second amplitude yields p, and elementary differentiation gives p′. Equivalently p=(1−cos w)/2, so evaluating it at w±π/2 gives the parameter-shift identity directly. The chain rule yields L′. For any point with L′≠0, the exact update w−ηL′ decreases L for all sufficiently small positive η by the first-order Taylor expansion. The heuristic is the same gradient update only when f=p′(w), or under another explicitly proved equality that yields an appropriate descent direction; a feature name alone does not establish this.

## Worked example: setup

Choose w=3π/2, t=1, f=1 and η=1/5.

## Worked example: calculation

Initially p=1/2 and L=1/8, while p′=−1/2 and L′=1/4. Gradient descent therefore decreases w. The heuristic instead increases w by1/10. Its new probability is [1−sin(1/10)]/2, approximately0.450083, so the new loss is approximately0.151204, strictly larger than1/8. The strict inequality follows without rounding because sin(1/10)>0. Parameter-shift evaluations still reproduce the negative derivative exactly, identifying the missing sign and factor in the heuristic. A constant feature f=1 is itself an admissible expectation, for example of the identity observable. The counterexample therefore does not depend on an impossible feature value. Its failure arises because this feature is unrelated to the derivative of the specified rotation probability.

## Worked example: conclusion

The recovered output formula now has a complete analytic training calculation and an explicit failure case for an unjustified update.

## Limits

This is a finite quantum-circuit probability model, not a biological-neuron claim or evidence of a quantum computational advantage. Sampling noise and multi-parameter controlled circuits require their own gradient and error analyses.

## Extension

For a specified circuit with one parameter per gate, derive each parameter-shift rule and compare exact loss changes with unbiased finite-shot gradient estimates.
