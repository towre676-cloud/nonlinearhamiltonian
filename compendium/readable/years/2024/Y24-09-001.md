## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which observations identify α and β in a power correction, and which dimensional constraints must they satisfy?

## Recovered original question explicitly supplied in the source

Relationship between α and β

## Status

Classical result; recovered proposal repaired

## Summary

The recovered user fragment asks about α and β and gives approximately 0.0334 and 0.673. Two numerical constants alone do not imply a mathematical relationship. A useful replacement is an identification problem with an explicit observation model and a dimensionless input.

## Definitions

For x>0, write d(x)=αx^β, with α>0 and β real.

If X has units of length, α has units length^(1−β) in X+αX^β. A dimensionless convention is X*=X_P[u+a u^β], u=X/X_P, with dimensionless a.

## Result name

Two-point identifiability

## Result statement

Two exact positive correction measurements d1=d(x1), d2=d(x2), with x1≠x2, determine β=log(d2/d1)/log(x2/x1) and α=d1/x1^β uniquely.

## Proof

Taking logarithms gives log di=log α+β log xi. Subtracting the two equations eliminates log α; the nonzero denominator log(x2/x1) permits division. Substitution then gives α. Conversely, the resulting pair satisfies both observations, so existence and uniqueness hold. With noisy observations, the same transformation becomes linear regression, but its errors are logarithmic errors, not necessarily additive errors in the original units. One observation gives the entire curve α=d1x1^(−β), so it cannot distinguish exponents. A short input range makes log(x2/x1) small and amplifies measurement errors. This is a conditioning issue that extra printed decimal places do not resolve.

## Worked example: setup

Take x1=1, x2=8, d1=1/30 and d2=2/15. These are exact model data for an illustrative exponent rather than historical measurements.

## Worked example: calculation

d2/d1=4, so β=log4/log8=2/3; α=1/30. If the unit is rescaled by X=cY, then X*=c[Y+αc^(β−1)Y^β], and the numerical coefficient becomes α_Y=αc^(β−1). At c=8 and β=2/3 the coefficient halves. The subsequently recovered user-supplied decimal claims can also be checked directly: (1/137)^0.673=0.03647430776545, while0.0334^0.673=0.10150358285425. These do not equal the claimed0.0334 and0.0867. Using the longer recovered β gives about0.036437524 and0.101432200 instead, so extra digits do not repair the discrepancies.

## Worked example: conclusion

The exponent can be invariant under unit changes while the dimensional coefficient changes. A claimed universal numerical α needs a specified normalization.

## Limits

No law relating the recovered decimals is proved. The displayed dimensionless convention uses u consistently; an extra independent x would define a different two-variable model. Negative inputs require a stated real-power convention.

## Extension

Collect at least three exact or interval-valued correction measurements. Fit the logarithmic model, inspect residuals, and propagate input uncertainty before assigning any significance to a proposed algebraic relation between α and β.
