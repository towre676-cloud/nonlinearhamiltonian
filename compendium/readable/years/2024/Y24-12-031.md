## Revised question retained in the annual source

What does a quiver Grassmannian parameterize, and how do its coefficients depend on the actual arrow map?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered quiver discussion assigned subrepresentation coefficients to quantum pathways without specifying a representation. Even the smallest nontrivial arrow shows that dimensions alone do not determine the variety: the arrow map changes the parameter space and its Euler characteristic.

## Definitions

For the quiver1→2 take vector spaces V₁=C²,V₂=C and arrow f:V₁→V₂.

A subrepresentation of dimension vector(1,0) consists of a line U⊂C² and the zero subspace in C with f(U)=0.

## Result name

Exact quiver Grassmannian and Euler count

## Result statement

If f has rank one, Gr_(1,0)(V) is a single point and has Euler characteristic1. If f=0, it is P¹(C) and has Euler characteristic2.

## Proof

The subrepresentation condition is precisely U⊂ker f. For rank-one f, the kernel is one-dimensional, so the only eligible line is ker f itself. For f=0, every line is eligible, giving the ordinary Grassmannian Gr(1,2)=P¹. Its usual affine-cell decomposition is C together with one point at infinity; their compactly supported Euler characteristics are1 and1, giving2, which agrees with its ordinary Euler characteristic as a compact sphere. In coordinates f(z₁,z₂)=z₁, a line[z₁:z₂] qualifies exactly when z₁=0, giving the point[0:1]. This exhibits a proper algebraic subset of the ambient Grassmannian cut out by the arrow compatibility equation.

## Worked example: setup

Compare the same dimension vector(2,1) for f=(1,0) and f=(0,0).

## Worked example: calculation

The vector-space dimensions are identical, but the Euler coefficient for subdimension(1,0) changes from1 to2. At subdimension(1,1), the target subspace is all C, so every source line qualifies for either map and the variety is P¹ with Euler characteristic2. Thus every coefficient must specify both the subdimension and the arrow representation.

The recovered coefficient language now has a concrete geometric definition and two exact computations.

## Limits

No general cluster-character formula is asserted without its hypotheses and normalization. Euler characteristic is a topological integer, not a transition amplitude or a probability without an additional construction.

## Extension

For a small A₂ representation with larger spaces, express the subrepresentation incidence conditions in Grassmannian charts and compute a cell decomposition or finite-field point count where justified.
