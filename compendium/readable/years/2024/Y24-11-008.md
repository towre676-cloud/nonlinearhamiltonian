## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which recovered wave and electrochemical formulas are dimensionally valid, and what can a simulation certify?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered discussion mixed wavelengths, modulation, photon energy, Gibbs energy, Faraday’s law, and numerical models. Correct units expose an erroneous current formula and prevent a frequency from being mistaken for an energy or a reaction rate.

## Definitions

A wave speed v, frequency f and wavelength λ obey λ=v/f in a nondispersive model.

The Faraday constant F carries units C/mol; a molar production rate dn/dt carries mol/s.

## Result name

Charge conservation and dimensional normalization

## Result statement

For a reaction transferring z electrons per molecular event, I=zF dn/dt. An ideal reversible potential is V_rev=ΔG/(zF). A Tafel logarithm must be log(I/I₀), with dimensionless argument.

## Proof

One mole of electrons has charge F. Producing dn moles in time dt with z electrons per event transfers charge dQ=zF dn. Differentiating gives current I=dQ/dt. Dividing Gibbs energy per mole by charge per mole gives joules per coulomb, which are volts. A formula such as I=zFA/C with undefined A and C is not charge conservation and cannot be used without definitions and units. For a wave, one period 1/f transports a phase crest by v/f; this gives wavelength but says nothing about material absorption or reaction kinetics. Likewise E=hf is the energy per photon, whereas molar energy is N_Ahf. A numerical scheme approximates a specified equation; discretization convergence does not validate the physical model or constitutive coefficients.

## Worked example: setup

Use v=299792458 m/s, f=26965000 Hz, z=2, F≈96485 C/mol and ΔG=237000 J/mol.

## Worked example: calculation

The wavelength is approximately 11.11784 m. At 432 Hz the same vacuum-wave formula gives approximately 693964 m; an acoustic wave requires its own speed. The reversible voltage is 237000/(2·96485)≈1.22817 V. A rate 10^(−6) mol/s would correspond to 0.19297 A by charge conservation. These are algebraic examples, not an apparatus design.

## Worked example: conclusion

Frequency, photon energy, electrical potential, and molar rate are distinct quantities connected only by explicitly stated laws.

## Limits

The examples supply no material resonance, efficiency improvement, or device performance. Dispersion, losses, temperature, and kinetics require independent data and governing equations.
