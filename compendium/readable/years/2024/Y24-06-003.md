## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which symplectic form and Poisson bracket are intended, and is the proposed quantity conserved by one flow or by every Hamiltonian flow?

## Status

Classical mathematics with newly authored derivation or finite example and explicit scope

## Summary

The recovered user topic asked for correctness and numerics concerning symplectic phase space, sp(2n), commutators and Casimirs. A canonical oscillator provides a complete calculation and exposes a particularly important distinction: on a connected nondegenerate canonical phase space, a Casimir is constant.

## Definitions

Use coordinates (x₁,x₂,p₁,p₂), J=[[0,I],[−I,0]] and the canonical bracket {f,g}=Σi(f_xi g_pi−f_pi g_xi).

A Casimir C satisfies {C,f}=0 for every smooth f. The symplectic Lie algebra is sp(4,R)={A:AᵀJ+JA=0}.

## Result name

Hamiltonian conservation is not the Casimir property

## Result statement

For H=(|x|²+|p|²)/2 the flow is ż=Jz, preserves H and the symplectic form, and has period2π. It is not true that this nonconstant H is a canonical Casimir. Every differentiable canonical Casimir on a connected domain is constant.

## Proof

The skew matrix J has J²=−I, giving exp(tJ)=Icos t+Jsin t. Its transpose identity exp(tJ)ᵀJexp(tJ)=J follows either from multiplication or differentiating it and using AᵀJ+JA=0. Energy preservation follows from Ḣ=(∇H)ᵀJ∇H=0 because J is skew. For the Casimir claim, use test functions x_i and p_i: {C,x_i}=−C_pi=0 and {C,p_i}=C_xi=0. Thus every first derivative is zero, and connectedness gives constancy. The equality {H,H}=0 only proves conservation under H’s own flow.

## Worked example: setup

Take x₁=cos t, p₁=−sin t, x₂=sin t, p₂=cos t.

## Worked example: calculation

These functions satisfy Hamilton’s equations exactly, have H=1 and angular momentum x₁p₂−x₂p₁=1. But {H,x₁}=−p₁ is not identically zero, so H fails the Casimir test. In quantization, [x_i,p_j]=iℏδijI belongs to an operator algebra; it must not be substituted for the classical Poisson relation {x_i,p_j}=δij without changing the mathematical setting. Angular momentum in this example is another first integral but still not a canonical Casimir. For L=x₁p₂−x₂p₁ the bracket {L,x₁}=x₂ is nonzero. Conservation by the rotationally symmetric oscillator and invariance under every Hamiltonian vector field are therefore different even for this second conserved quantity.

## Worked example: conclusion

The historical oscillator is a valid Hamiltonian example; its existence is not a universal model of flight, and its conserved Hamiltonian is not a universal commutator invariant.

## Limits

Nonconstant Casimirs can occur for degenerate Poisson structures, including Lie–Poisson structures on dual Lie algebras. That is a different space from this canonical symplectic domain.

## Extension

For an explicit degenerate Poisson tensor, solve J(z)∇C=0 and compare its genuine Casimirs with first integrals of one chosen Hamiltonian.
