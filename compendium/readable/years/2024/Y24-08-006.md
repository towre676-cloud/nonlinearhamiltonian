## Summary

The simplest useful development fixes the congruence subgroup and proves its prime-level index. This supplies an actual modular-curve calculation and a moduli interpretation, while leaving the much deeper links to deformation rings and Shimura varieties conditional on named theorems and hypotheses.

## Definitions

Let H be the complex upper half-plane and Γ₀(N)={[[a,b],[c,d]]∈SL₂(Z):c≡0 modN}. It acts byτ↦(aτ+b)/(cτ+d). The open modular curve is the quotient with its appropriate orbifold/coarse-space conventions; X₀(N) denotes its cusp compactification.

## Result name

Prime-level index and a preserved cyclic subgroup

## Result statement

For a prime p, [SL₂(Z):Γ₀(p)]=p+1. A pointτ determines E_τ=C/(Zτ+Z) with the cyclic subgroup generated by1/N; Γ₀(N) preserves the isomorphism class of this pair.

## Proof

Reduction modulo p maps SL₂(Z) onto SL₂(F_p); elementary upper and lower unipotent matrices give the needed generators. The subgroup with c=0 stabilizes the line∞ in P¹(F_p). The finite group acts transitively on its p+1 lines, so orbit-stabilizer gives the index. For the pair interpretation, multiplication by1/(cτ+d) identifies E_τ with E_τ′. The identity1/(cτ+d)=a−cτ′ shows that the image of1/N is congruent to a/N in the new torus when N divides c. Since a is invertible modulo N, this generates the same cyclic subgroup.

## Worked example: setup

Take p=2 and enumerate matrices over F₂.

## Worked example: calculation

There are six determinant-one matrices, and two have c=0, so the index is three. This finite check confirms the general orbit argument in the smallest prime case. It does not calculate the genus or every cohomology group of the compactified curve.

## Worked example: conclusion

The quotient, level and cyclic-subgroup data are now explicit. A named modular curve is no longer standing in for an unspecified arithmetic space.

## Limits

Elliptic stabilizers and boundary conventions matter for a smooth-moduli or stack statement. General Shimura varieties need different group data. No R=T theorem or Eisenstein-ideal identification follows from this index calculation.

## Extension

Choose a level with a specified torsion-free subgroup, calculate cusps and compactification data, and state precisely which deformation problem is related to it.
