## Revised question retained in the annual source

Can linear wave superposition implement XOR, and what does a parameter derivative of a modal layer mean?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered discussion used linear modal fields, neural weights, and interference-based logical gates. Linear amplitudes alone cannot implement XOR as a linear scalar output, but a specified nonlinear intensity readout can. Chain rules must retain the field’s dependence on its weights.

## Definitions

For inputs a,b∈{0,1}, a coherent signed amplitude can be z=a−b.

A linear modal layer is ψ(w)=Σ_iw_if_i; an objective E(w)=||ψ(w)−y||² uses fixed Hilbert-space modes f_i.

## Result name

XOR by intensity and the correct real-weight gradient

## Result statement

The intensity |a−b|² equals XOR on binary inputs. For real weights w_i, ∂E/∂w_i=2 Re⟨f_i,ψ(w)−y⟩; a field variation is δψ=Σ_iδw_i f_i.

## Proof

Enumerate the four input pairs. The amplitudes are 0,−1,1,0 for (0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1); squaring their magnitudes gives 0,1,1,0. The nonlinear step is the square-law readout. If a linear output ℓ(a,b)=αa+βb+γ represented XOR, evaluating at (0,0) forces γ=0, at (1,0) forces α=1, and at (0,1) forces β=1, making ℓ(1,1)=2 rather than zero. For the layer derivative, expand ||r+δw_if_i||²−||r||²=2δw_i Re⟨f_i,r⟩+(δw_i)²||f_i||² and divide by δw_i. This gives the exact chain-rule result. Gradient descent in parameter space uses δw_i=−η∂E/∂w_i, after which δψ follows; it is not a new independent wave equation.

## Worked example: setup

Take orthonormal f₁,f₂, target y=f₁, and w=(0,1).

## Worked example: calculation

The residual is −f₁+f₂, E=2, and ∇E=(−2,2). A step η=1/2 gives w_new=(1,0) and zero error. Separately an AND gate can use amplitude a+b with a threshold above one and below two, again explicitly introducing a nonlinear readout.

## Worked example: conclusion

The logical truth table and learning gradient are exact while the source of nonlinearity is made explicit.

## Limits

The examples are abstract finite computations, not a claim to realize neural hardware or a physical wave channel. Orthogonality and measurements must be verified in any proposed implementation.

## Extension

With nonorthogonal modes, use the Gram matrix to quantify conditioning and derive the gradient-descent step restriction from its largest eigenvalue.
