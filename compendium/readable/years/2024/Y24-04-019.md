## Result name

Lorenz and Rössler labels do not constitute a chaos certificate

## Result statement

Forρ>1, Lorenz equilibria are(±√(β(ρ−1)),±√(β(ρ−1)),ρ−1). Their coefficients areA₁=σ+β+1,A₂=β(σ+ρ),A₃=2σβ(ρ−1). Ifσ>β+1, they are stable exactly for1<ρ<σ(σ+β+3)/(σ−β−1).

## Proof

At equilibrium the first equation givesy=x, the third givesz=x²/β, and the second givesx(ρ−1−z)=0. These yield the origin and the stated pair. Substituting each into the Jacobian determinant gives the cubic coefficients. Their positive-coefficient condition is automatic forρ>1. The remaining Hurwitz expression simplifies toβ[σ(σ+β+3)−ρ(σ−β−1)], giving the strict boundary. The divergence is the constant−σ−1−β, so infinitesimal volume contracts exponentially. This cannot prove chaotic dynamics: the linear systemu̇=−u in three dimensions also has negative divergence and every trajectory tends to one point. For Rössler, equilibrium requiresy=−x/a,z=x/a andx²−cx+ab=0, a different algebraic structure. At the Lorenz origin, the characteristic polynomial factors as(λ+β)[λ²+(σ+1)λ+σ(1−ρ)]. Forρ>1 the quadratic constant term is negative, giving one positive root and proving instability of that equilibrium too. This remains a local statement even when all listed equilibria are unstable.

## Worked example: setup

Take Lorenzσ=10,β=8/3 and compareρ=20 withρ=28. For Rössler choosea=b=1,c=3.

## Worked example: calculation

The Lorenz boundary is470/19≈24.7368. The nonzero equilibria are stable at20 and unstable at28; the latter conclusion alone does not prove a strange attractor. Rössler equilibria have x=(3±√5)/2,y=−x,z=x, and substitution reduces the final equation to x²−3x+1=0 exactly.

## Limits

The threshold excludes nonhyperbolic equality and does not classify all attractors. A positive finite-time numerical Lyapunov estimate is also not automatically a rigorous infinite-time chaos proof. The historical fragment does not supply a numerical experiment to audit.

## Extension

A rigorous chaos certificate can use an explicitly verified return map with expansion and a trapping region, or a suitable established theorem with matched parameters. State the definition of chaos and the required global evidence before attaching that word to a simulation.
