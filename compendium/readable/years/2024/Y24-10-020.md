## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which exact positivity and decay statements follow when a recovered modification is defined as edge weights?

## Status

Classical result; recovered proposal repaired

## Summary

The historical application does not specify the modified Laplacian. A concrete weighted graph model supplies an exact theorem: nonnegative symmetric weights give a positive semidefinite energy and heat evolution, while negative weights can reverse the conclusion.

## Definitions

For symmetric weightsw_ij≥0, define(Lu)_i=Σ_jw_ij(u_i−u_j). The graph connects vertices linked by positive weights.

Heat evolution is u′=−Lu. Constants on each connected component form the expected equilibrium space.

## Result name

Energy identity and decay

## Result statement

uᵀLu=Σ_(i<j)w_ij(u_i−u_j)²≥0. Along heat flow,d(||u||²/2)/dt=−uᵀLu≤0. On the orthogonal complement of componentwise constants, decay is exponential at the smallest positive eigenvalue.

## Proof

ExpanduᵀLu=Σ_iu_iΣ_jw_ij(u_i−u_j). Pair theij andji terms to obtainw_ij(u_i−u_j)² for each unordered edge. Vanishing of all terms forces equality ofu across every positive edge and hence across each connected component. Symmetry allows orthogonal diagonalization; on a positive-eigenvalue component with eigenvalueλ, the solution coefficient is multiplied bye^(−λt). This proves the decay statement, while zero modes remain constant. If a proposed J weight is negative, the nonnegative-square proof fails andL may have a negative eigenvalue; the corresponding “heat” mode then grows.

## Worked example: calculation

L=[[1,−1,0],[−1,2,−1],[0,−1,1]] has eigenvalues0,1,3 with eigenvectors(1,1,1),(1,0,−1),(1,−2,1). Starting from(1,0,−1), heat evolution is exactlye^(−t)(1,0,−1). On two vertices with weight−1, the difference mode instead has eigenvalue−2 and growse^(2t).

## Worked example: conclusion

A valid Laplacian modification needs symmetry and positivity, or an explicit alternative theory. A named arithmetic weight does not automatically possess them.
