## Revised question retained in the annual source

Reconstruction: Which volume or depth is missing from the calculation, what do exact unit conversions give, and can the requested amount be identified from concentration and area alone?

## Status

Developed mathematical repair with exact worked checks; historical evidence is assistant-only

## Summary

The recovered calculation contains a dimensional defect before any numerical approximation is considered. Concentration measures amount per volume, whereas the supplied spatial quantity is an area. Their product has amount per length, so an amount cannot be inferred without a depth or volume model. The molar conversion at the end can be correct while the preceding quantity is ill-defined. This entry develops only the mathematics of that error, not an environmental prescription.

## Definitions

Use C in mol/L, A in m², h in m and M in g/mol. One litre is exactly 10^(−3)m³, so one m³ is 1000L and one km³ is 10^12L.

For the stipulated historical inputs take C=1μmol/L=10^(−6)mol/L, A=10km²=10^7m² and M=55.845g/mol. The numerical M is an input to this calculation, not an exact claim about every material.

A uniform slab has volume V=Ah. For a nonuniform domain use a depth function h(x) and a concentration field C(x,z) in a common consistent unit system.

## Result name

An exact missing-depth law and nonidentifiability

## Result statement

For a uniform slab with the stipulated inputs, m(h)=558.45h kg when h is measured in metres. Without h or an equivalent volume constraint, the amount is not identified. The source fragment’s 10^6L/km³ factor is also incorrect: the exact factor is 10^12L/km³.

## Proof

The volume is 10^7h m³=10^10h L. Multiplying by 10^(−6)mol/L gives 10^4h mol; multiplying by 55.845g/mol gives 558450h g=558.45h kg. At every step the units cancel to the required quantity. Holding C and A fixed while replacing h by sh for any s>0 leaves all supplied concentration and area data unchanged but multiplies the amount by s. Thus those data alone cannot distinguish different amounts. For a variable concentration or depth, integrate the local amount density over the actual volume. Constant C gives C∫_A h(x)dA, so even then the required extra datum is the area-integrated depth. Bounds C_min≤C≤C_max and h_min≤h≤h_max yield corresponding integral bounds, but an area by itself supplies neither.

## Worked example: calculation

The volume is 25,000,000m³=25,000,000,000L. The amount is 25,000mol and the mass is 1,396,125g=1,396.125kg. If one instead chooses h=0.001m, the formula gives 558.45g. This explains which additional depth would reproduce the old final number; it does not show that the missing depth was actually present in the original conversation. Separately, 10^7μmol does equal 10mol, and 10×55.845g/mol equals 558.45g. That final arithmetic does not repair the unsupported amount estimate.
