## Revised question retained in the annual source

What exact determinant–resolvent identity can be established, and why does it not by itself identify a spectral determinant with the Riemann xi function?

## Status

Classical result; recovered proposal repaired

## Summary

The recovered argument links spectral zeta functions, Hadamard products and zero ordinates. Each object can be legitimate while the claimed equality between them remains unproved. A finite-dimensional calculation isolates what follows automatically from spectral algebra.

## Definitions

For a positive Hermitian d×d matrix L with eigenvalues λ_j, define D(t)=det(L+tI) and ζ_L(s)=Σ_jλ_j^(−s).

For infinite spectra, convergence of the series and analytic continuation required for a regularized determinant are additional assertions.

## Result name

An exact logarithmic derivative

## Result statement

For t>−minλ_j, d(logD(t))/dt=Tr((L+tI)⁻¹)=Σ_j1/(λ_j+t), and logD(0)=−ζ_L′(0).

## Proof

Unitary diagonalization yields D(t)=∏_j(λ_j+t). All factors are positive in the stated interval, so ordinary real differentiation gives the sum of reciprocal factors. The spectral theorem identifies that sum with the resolvent trace. Differentiating λ_j^(−s)=exp(−slogλ_j) at s=0 gives ζ_L′(0)=−Σ_jlogλ_j, which proves the determinant formula. In an infinite-dimensional case, these operations cannot be transferred without checking convergence: for λ_n=n², ζ_L(s)=ζ(2s) converges initially only for Re(s)>1/2, while Σlogλ_n diverges. Analytic continuation may define a regularized determinant, but its value depends on a proved continuation and normalization.

## Worked example: setup

Let L=diag(1,4).

## Worked example: calculation

D(t)=(1+t)(4+t)=t²+5t+4. Its logarithmic derivative is(2t+5)/(t²+5t+4)=1/(1+t)+1/(4+t). Also ζ_L(s)=1+4^(−s), so−ζ_L′(0)=log4=logdetL. Every identity is exact and contains no zeta-zero information.

## Worked example: conclusion

A determinant packages a spectrum that has already been defined. To obtain an arithmetic theorem, one must prove an independent identity with an arithmetic entire function, including normalization and all zeros.

## Limits

Prescribing known ordinates as diagonal entries constructs a model from those numbers; it does not prove that all nontrivial zeros lie on the critical line. Hadamard products also require convergence factors and growth control.

## Extension

Formulate a candidate entire-function identity, compare orders and normalization at a regular point, and prove equality through a trace or functional equation that does not assume the desired zero correspondence.
