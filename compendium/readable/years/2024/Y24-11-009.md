## Revised question retained in the annual source

When do differing property values imply an attained maximum, and what does algebraic projection actually preserve?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The historical claim that differing degrees of a property force a maximum is false without restrictions. Compactness, continuity, and the distinction between closed and constructible images give a precise replacement for the suggested Chevalley-based argument.

## Definitions

A supremum is a least upper bound, which need not be a value of the function.

A constructible subset of an algebraic variety is a finite union of locally closed subsets.

## Result name

Attainment under compactness; an algebraic projection counterexample

## Result statement

A continuous real-valued function on a nonempty compact space attains both extrema. However the projection of a closed affine algebraic set need not be closed: the image of xy=1 under (x,y)↦x is A¹ minus {0}.

## Proof

For the analytic assertion, the continuous image of a compact space is compact in R and therefore closed and bounded. Its supremum belongs to that closed image, hence is attained. The hypotheses are essential: f(x)=x on (0,1) has supremum one but never reaches it, and f(x)=x on R is unbounded. For the algebraic assertion, every nonzero x has y=1/x and zero has none, proving the exact image. It is open and constructible but not Zariski closed. Chevalley’s theorem predicts constructibility for finite-type morphisms, not universal closedness. Proper morphisms, including projections with a projective factor, provide stronger closed-image statements.

## Worked example: setup

Compare f(x)=x/(1+x) on [0,∞) with the same f on [0,M], and compare the affine hyperbola projection with a projective parameter space.

## Worked example: calculation

On the half-line, f increases toward one, so sup f=1 but there is no maximizing point. On [0,M], the maximum is M/(1+M). The derivative 1/(1+x)² is never zero, so solving ∇f=0 would find neither the boundary maximizer nor the unattained limit. Algebraic rational functions also require their denominator-nonzero domain to be stated.

## Worked example: conclusion

A degree comparison alone proves neither boundedness nor attainment. The required hypotheses can be checked explicitly.

## Limits

Complex projective varieties are compact analytically, but a rational function may have poles and fail to extend continuously everywhere. A vanishing first Chern class by itself does not supply an optimization theorem.

## Extension

For polynomial optimization on a compact semialgebraic set, include boundary constraints and compare critical values on all strata.
