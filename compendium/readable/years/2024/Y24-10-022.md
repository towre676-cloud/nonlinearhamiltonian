## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which elementary test prevents a proposed categorical height from depending on an arbitrary presentation?

## Status

Classical result; recovered proposal repaired

## Summary

The recovered ∞-topos discussion does not define its new height. A rigorous foundational requirement is invariance under the chosen equivalence relation. A finite chain-complex example shows that counting components of a presentation can change while the represented cohomology does not.

## Definitions

A cochain complex is a sequenceC^i with differentialsd satisfyingd²=0. A quasi-isomorphism is a chain map inducing isomorphisms on all cohomology groups.

The two-term complexK=(R→R), with differential the identity, is acyclic.

## Result name

An exact presentation-invariance obstruction

## Result statement

C andC⊕K are quasi-isomorphic and have identical cohomology, but their total vector-space dimensions differ by2. Any proposed invariant depending only on the derived object must assign them the same value.

## Proof

The projectionC⊕K→C is a chain map. Becauseker(id_R)=0 andcoker(id_R)=0, K has zero cohomology in every degree. Direct sums commute with kernels and images here, soH^i(C⊕K)=H^i(C)⊕H^i(K)=H^i(C). The projection is a quasi-isomorphism. Nevertheless the sum of dimensions of its presentation spaces increases by2. Thus an uncorrected “height” counting those spaces cannot be invariant under quasi-isomorphism. The Euler characteristic avoids this specific problem because the two added terms occur in consecutive degrees with opposite signs, contributing1−1=0. This does not make Euler characteristic an arithmetic height, but it gives a complete model of the invariance check that is missing.

## Worked example: setup

TakeC=R concentrated in degree0.

## Worked example: calculation

C hasH0=R and total dimension1. The complexC⊕K has a two-dimensional degree0 term, a one-dimensional degree1 term and an identity map on the added summand. Its cohomology is againR in degree0 and0 elsewhere, its total dimension is3, and both Euler characteristics equal1.

## Worked example: conclusion

A category-theoretic vocabulary must be accompanied by a defined equivalence-invariant construction. A list of component values does not supply one.

## Limits

This is a finite homological-algebra model, not a definition of heights on ∞-topoi. Equivalence in an ∞-category, convergence of infinite sums and arithmetic metrics require substantially more structure.

## Extension

Define the object category, equivalences, target group and additivity law for the proposed invariant. Test it on contractible or acyclic additions before attaching numerical examples.
