## Revised question retained in the annual source

What domain makes a phase-twisted circle Laplacian self-adjoint, and how does the phase change its spectrum?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The historical equation ψ(x+L)=e^(iθ)ψ(x) is meaningful only with an operator domain. For a second-order operator the derivative boundary condition is equally necessary. The resulting spectrum is elementary and supplies a precise holonomy example.

## Definitions

On L²([0,L]), let A_θ=−d²/dx² with domain {f∈H²: f(L)=e^(iθ)f(0), f′(L)=e^(iθ)f′(0)}.

The boundary phase θ is real and defined modulo 2π.

## Result name

Self-adjoint twisted Laplacian and shifted Fourier modes

## Result statement

A_θ is self-adjoint and has orthonormal eigenbasis L^(−1/2)exp(i(2πn+θ)x/L), n∈Z, with eigenvalues ((2πn+θ)/L)².

## Proof

Integration by parts gives the boundary form [−overline f g′+overline f′g]_0^L. The equal phase factors in function and derivative values cancel, so the form vanishes on the domain. To establish self-adjointness, multiply by exp(−iθx/L), a unitary map to periodic functions. Under this gauge transformation A_θ becomes (−i∂_x+θ/L)² on the periodic H² domain. Its action on the complete periodic Fourier basis is multiplication by real numbers ((2πn+θ)/L)² with the usual square-summability domain, which is self-adjoint. This also proves completeness, not merely a list of candidate eigenfunctions. If only the function boundary condition were imposed, the boundary form would retain uncontrolled derivative terms and symmetry could fail.

## Worked example: setup

Take L=2π and compare θ=0 with θ=π.

## Worked example: calculation

At θ=0 the eigenvalues are n², including a zero constant mode. At θ=π they are (n+1/2)², so the least eigenvalue is 1/4 with multiplicity two and there is no zero mode. The spectra at θ and θ+2π coincide after reindexing n, while their eigenfunctions differ by a gauge relabeling.

## Worked example: conclusion

The boundary twist shifts momentum exactly, with a proved operator domain and no speculative identification of the phase.

## Limits

This is a one-dimensional free operator. Interactions, nonabelian holonomy, and physical superselection require additional specified structures. A boundary phase is not by itself an error-correcting code.

## Extension

For a matrix-valued unitary boundary holonomy, diagonalize the unitary and obtain a union of shifted spectra, while retaining both boundary conditions.
