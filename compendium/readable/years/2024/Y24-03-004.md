## Summary

## Definitions

Use dimensionless x∈[0,1] and t>0. The toy function ρ=x²+t has mass M(t)=t+1/3 and is not a probability density.

Define p(x,t)=(x²+t)/(t+1/3) and differential entropy S(t)=−∫₀¹p logp dx.

A time-integrated entropy-rate expression is a total change over an interval. It is not the same object as a value of Ṡ at one time.

## Result name

Normalize the density before differentiating entropy

## Result statement

The normalized derivative is p_t=(1/3−x²)/(t+1/3)², whose integral is zero. At t=1, S=(7/6)−π/4−log(3/2), and Ṡ=(10−3π)/16>0.

## Proof

Differentiate −p logp under the integral to obtain Ṡ=−∫p_t(logp+1). Normalization removes the last term because ∫p_t=0. At t=1, p=3(1+x²)/4 and p_t=3(1−3x²)/16. The constant log(3/4) also drops from the derivative. Integration by parts gives ∫₀¹(1−3x²)log(1+x²)dx=π−10/3, yielding the exact derivative. A second elementary integration gives ∫₀¹(1+x²)log(1+x²)dx=(4/3)log2−14/9+π/3, which yields S(1). Positivity follows, for example, from π<10/3. These are checks on a declared probability model, not a validation of an added term W. Since p is a probability density on an interval of length one, Jensen’s inequality gives ∫p logp≥0 and S≤0. Thus the negative value of the differential entropy is consistent with its definition: its positive derivative describes approach toward the uniform-density maximum, not a negative probability.
