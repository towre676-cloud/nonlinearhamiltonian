## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which proposed unifications can be demonstrated in a finite model, and which require additional objects and maps?

## Status

## Summary

The historical request combined spectral operators, supersymmetry, arithmetic, geometric Langlands, and topology. A meaningful bridge must specify domains, products, and intertwining maps. Two finite constructions show how to do this without claiming a universal theory.

## Definitions

Let D:C→C² be D(z)=(z,0), with grading Γ=diag(1,−1,−1) on C⊕C².

On a finite abelian group A, a character χ:A→C× is a homomorphism with |χ(a)|=1.

## Result name

Exact graded pairing and character diagonalization

## Result statement

For Q=[[0,D*],[D,0]], H=Q²=diag(1,1,0), and Str(e^(−tH))=Tr(Γe^(−tH))=−1. Separately, finite Fourier characters diagonalize all translation operators on C^A.

## Proof

Block multiplication gives Q²=diag(D*D,DD*) and ΓQ=−QΓ. The two positive-energy states occur once in each grading and cancel in the supertrace; the remaining odd zero mode contributes −1. This proves a finite index relation directly. For the Fourier assertion, define T_bf(a)=f(a+b). A character obeys T_bχ(a)=χ(b)χ(a), so it is a simultaneous eigenvector for every translation. Character orthogonality follows by translating the sum Σ_aχ(a)overline{ψ(a)} by an element on which χ/ψ is not one. The sum must then equal a nontrivial multiple of itself and vanish. Normalized characters form an orthonormal basis.

## Worked example: setup

Take A=Z/3Z with ω=e^(2πi/3) and characters χ_k(a)=ω^(ka).

## Worked example: calculation

The transform F_{ka}=ω^(−ka)/√3 is unitary and F T_1 F*=diag(1,ω,ω²), up to the explicitly chosen transform ordering. In the supersymmetric example the heat supertrace is e^(−t)−e^(−t)−1=−1 for every t. Both identities are exact finite matrix calculations.

## Worked example: conclusion

Each relationship has defined spaces, a precise map, and an identity. Naming spectral and automorphic categories without a functor and hypotheses provides no comparable proof.

## Limits

The finite Fourier transform is an illustration of duality, not a proof of geometric Langlands. The recovered de Rham/Betti/tempered category fragment does not specify a curve, group, stack, or equivalence, so no missing categorical theorem is supplied.

## Extension

To formulate an actual geometric problem, first fix a smooth curve X, reductive G, the relevant moduli stacks and sheaf categories, and the exact proposed functor.
