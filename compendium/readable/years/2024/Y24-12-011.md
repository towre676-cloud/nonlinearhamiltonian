## Revised question retained in the annual source

Does the recovered integrated phase-feedback objective actually depend on phases?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The proposed objective integrates the squared magnitude of a modal sum. Its entire phase dependence is encoded by the Gram matrix. If the modes are truly orthogonal over that same domain and measure, the proposed phase feedback is identically zero.

## Definitions

Let f₁,…,f_N belong to a complex Hilbert space, G_jk=⟨f_j,f_k⟩, with inner product conjugate-linear in the first argument.

Set c_n=a_ne^(iχ_n), a_n≥0, and E(χ)=||Σc_nf_n||²=c*Gc.

## Result name

Exact phase gradient and orthogonality obstruction

## Result statement

The derivative is ∂E/∂χ_n=2 Im(overline{c_n}(Gc)_n). If G is diagonal, E=ΣG_nn a_n² is independent of every χ_n and the gradient vanishes everywhere.

## Proof

Differentiate c_n to get ic_n and its conjugate to get −i overline{c_n}. The first differentiated factor contributes −iz, where z=overline{c_n}(Gc)_n; the second contributes i overline z because G is Hermitian. Their sum is i(overline z−z)=2 Im z. If G is diagonal, z=G_nn|c_n|² is real, proving zero derivative. Thus counting many resonators or using a gradient update cannot create coupling absent from the objective. For nonorthogonal modes, off-diagonal terms are genuine phase interactions. Since E is smooth on the compact phase torus, its gradient has a finite Lipschitz constant; a sufficiently small gradient-descent step decreases E by the descent lemma, but this does not imply reaching a global minimum or synchronization.

## Worked example: setup

Take two unit modes with real overlap ρ∈[0,1), unit amplitudes, and relative phase χ=χ₂−χ₁.

## Worked example: calculation

E=2+2ρcosχ. The derivative with respect to χ₂ is −2ρsinχ, agreeing with the Gram formula. At ρ=0 every phase has energy two and feedback is zero. At ρ>0 energy minimization selects χ=π, destructive interference, whereas maximizing the same objective selects χ=0. The intended objective therefore determines whether phases align or oppose.

## Worked example: conclusion

The recovered feedback law has an exact derivative and a decisive consistency test against its own orthogonality assumption.

## Limits

The domain, measure, boundary conditions, and coupling Gram matrix must be specified. No mechanical, neurological, or field-control performance follows from this finite-dimensional optimization identity.

## Extension

Use a bounded positive self-adjoint localized observation operator W (for example multiplication by a nonnegative bounded window) and optimize c*G_Wc with G_W,jk=⟨f_j,Wf_k⟩ if global orthogonality otherwise removes the desired phase sensitivity.
