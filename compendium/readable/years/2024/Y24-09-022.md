## Revised question retained in the annual source

When is the logarithmic symbol integral a valid determinant limit, and what does it actually compute?

## Status

Classical result; recovered proposal repaired

## Summary

The historical use of an integral of log f resembles a Toeplitz determinant theorem, but it was inserted into an undefined decay model. A positive tridiagonal family gives an exact determinant recurrence and an independently derived limiting logarithm.

## Definitions

Let T_n=tridiag(b,a,b) with a>2|b| and D_n=detT_n; set D_0=1.

Define r_±=(a±√(a²−4b²))/2. For b≠0, 0<r_-<r_+; b=0 is immediate.

## Result name

Exact determinant and limiting geometric mean

## Result statement

D_n=(r_+^(n+1)−r_-^(n+1))/(r_+−r_-), and lim n⁻¹logD_n=logr_+=π⁻¹∫₀πlog(a+2b cosθ)dθ.

## Proof

Expansion along the last row gives D_n=aD_(n−1)−b²D_(n−2), with D_0=1,D_1=a. Solving this second-order recurrence yields the formula, as its two characteristic roots are r_±. Factor r_+^(n+1) to obtain logD_n=(n+1)logr_++log[1−(r_-/r_+)^(n+1)]−log(r_+−r_-). Division by n gives the limit. Independently, the preceding eigenvalue formula and the positivity bound λ_k≥a−2|b|>0 permit the continuous test F=log, so the same limit is the symbol integral. This proves the integral identity without importing a decay interpretation. Positivity supplies a consistent real logarithm and bounds it on the entire spectral interval. Positive definiteness can also be verified without eigenvectors: the quadratic form is aΣu_j²+2bΣu_ju_(j+1), bounded below by(a−2|b|)Σu_j² using2|uv|≤u²+v². Thus every finite determinant is positive under the same hypothesis, independently confirming that its real logarithm is legitimate.

## Worked example: setup

Take a=3,b=1.

## Worked example: calculation

D_0=1,D_1=3,D_2=8,D_3=21,D_4=55. The limiting geometric mean isr_+=(3+√5)/2≈2.61803. For n=4, D_4^(1/4)=55^(1/4), still above that limit. The recurrence gives every finite determinant exactly and a transparent exponential correction through(r_-/r_+)^(n+1).

## Worked example: conclusion

The logarithmic integral computes a normalized determinant in this model. Calling its exponential a decay rate requires a separate, defined dynamical equation.

## Limits

At a=2|b| the symbol touches zero and the continuity argument for log fails, although some limits can still exist after extra work. With a sign-changing or complex symbol, branches, zeros and possible winding become substantive issues.

## Extension

If a proposed Γ uses this determinant, state the finite-n formula relating Γ_n to D_n, check its units and derive its limit. Do not identify a geometric mean with a physical or arithmetic observable by notation alone.
