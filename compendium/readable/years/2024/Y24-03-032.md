## Revised question retained in the annual source

Reconstruction: For a real monic cubic, which coefficient inequalities are equivalent to strict left-half-plane stability, and where does s³+2s²+2s+K lose that property?

## Status

Classical mathematics newly developed from a recovered assistant-only catalog name

## Summary

The stability criterion is most useful when it gives a parameter certificate rather than a list of approximate roots. A cubic is small enough that the criterion can be proved directly from its possible real and complex factors. The equality boundary also reveals why strict inequalities are required.

## Definitions

A real polynomial is strictly Hurwitz when every complex root has negative real part. This is the spectral condition relevant to an autonomous linear ODE with that characteristic polynomial.

Write p(s)=s³+as²+bs+c with real a,b,c. Strict cubic Hurwitz conditions are a>0, b>0, c>0 and ab>c.

When a≠0, the cubic Routh first column is 1,a,(ab−c)/a,c. The proof below establishes the stable cubic case directly, without relying on a general root-count theorem.

## Result name

Necessary and sufficient cubic inequalities

## Result statement

The monic real cubic is strictly Hurwitz if and only if a,b,c>0 and ab>c. In the family p_K=s³+2s²+2s+K, this holds exactly for 0<K<4. At K=4 the factorization is (s+2)(s²+2), so the imaginary pair prevents strict stability.

## Proof

If all three roots are negative real numbers, write their negatives r₁,r₂,r₃>0. The elementary symmetric coefficient formulas make a,b,c positive, and expanding ab−c gives a sum of positive terms. If there is one negative real root −r and a conjugate pair −u±iv with r,u>0, factor p=(s+r)(s²+2us+u²+v²). Its coefficients are positive and direct expansion gives ab−c=2u[(r+u)²+v²]>0. This proves necessity. Conversely assume the inequalities. Positive coefficients imply p(s)>0 for all real s≥0, so no root is a nonnegative real number. If an unstable nonreal pair u±iv with u≥0 existed, the remaining real root would be −r with r>0, since c=r(u²+v²)>0. Factoring p=(s+r)(s²−2us+u²+v²) then gives ab−c=−2u[(r−u)²+v²]≤0, contradicting ab>c. All roots therefore lie strictly left of the imaginary axis. Applying the inequalities to a=b=2 gives K>0 and 4>K, as claimed.

## Worked example: setup

Compare K=2, K=4 and K=5 using exact coefficients.

## Worked example: calculation

For K=2 the Routh first column is (1,2,1,2), and the proved inequalities certify strict stability. For K=4 it is (1,2,0,4), signaling a boundary case; the exact factorization displays roots −2 and ±i√2. For K=5 the coefficient product ab−c is −1. Since all coefficients remain positive there is no nonnegative real root, and the failed strict criterion together with exclusion of an imaginary pair shows that the unstable roots are a conjugate pair with positive real part. The verifier checks the rational columns, inequalities and boundary product exactly.

## Worked example: conclusion

The parameter interval and its boundary are established without trusting floating-point root classifications near the imaginary axis.

## Limits

This proof is for a monic cubic with real coefficients. It is not a proof of the general Routh–Hurwitz theorem or its unstable-root counting rule. An imaginary-axis root means the strict criterion fails; any claim about bounded motion or nonlinear stability would need separate analysis.

## Extension

For a parameter-dependent polynomial, retaining symbolic Hurwitz determinants produces exact semialgebraic stability regions. A meaningful robustness certificate requires positive lower bounds on the relevant determinants throughout a stated parameter box.
