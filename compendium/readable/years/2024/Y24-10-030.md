## Revised question retained in the annual source

What rigorous variational calculation replaces a formula-only least-action flashcard?

## Status

Classical result; recovered proposal repaired

## Summary

The recovered flashcards include least action and Newton’s law. Their mathematical core is a stationary-action calculation, with an important distinction between stationarity and a genuine minimum. An elementary quadratic action permits both to be checked exactly.

## Definitions

For a C² pathx:[0,T]→R with fixed endpoint values, takeS[x]=∫₀ᵀ[(m/2)(x′)²−V(x)]dt, withm>0.

An admissible variationη is smooth and vanishes at0 andT. Stationarity means the derivative ofS[x+εη] atε=0 vanishes for everyη.

## Result name

First and second variation

## Result statement

Stationarity impliesmx″+V′(x)=0. ForV(x)=kx²/2 and zero-endpoint variations, the second variation is∫[m(η′)²−kη²]dt and is positive whenk<mπ²/T².

## Proof

Differentiate under the integral to getδS=∫[mx′η′−V′(x)η]dt. Integration by parts removes the endpoint term becauseη vanishes there, leaving−∫[mx″+V′(x)]ηdt. The fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations forces the continuous bracket to vanish. For the quadraticV, differentiating a second time gives the stated form exactly. The Dirichlet Poincaré inequality∫η²≤(T/π)²∫(η′)² then bounds it below by[m−kT²/π²]∫(η′)². Strict positivity proves a strict minimum for this quadratic functional under fixed endpoints. Ifk>mπ²/T², takeη=sin(πt/T); the second variation is negative, so a stationary path need not minimize the action.

## Worked example: setup

Choosem=1,k=1 andT=π versusT=2π.

## Worked example: calculation

AtT=π, the variationη=sin t has zero second variation. AtT=2π,η=sin(t/2) gives∫η′²=π/4 and∫η²=π, soδ²S=−3π/4. The same stationary zero path is therefore not an action minimum on the longer interval.

## Worked example: conclusion

“Least” action requires an actual second-variation argument. The Euler–Lagrange equation alone proves stationarity.

## Limits

The argument is one-dimensional with smooth data and fixed endpoints. Constraints, gauge symmetry, weak minimizers and boundary variations require revised function spaces and boundary conditions.

## Extension

For a specified field action, calculate the first variation, include all boundary terms and determine the Hessian on the admissible variation space before making a minimization claim.
