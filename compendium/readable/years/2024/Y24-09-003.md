## Revised question retained in the annual source

What algebraic identities follow from a bijective deformation, and which operations must be specified?

## Status

Classical result; recovered proposal repaired

## Summary

The recovered framework applies a common deformation to many algebraic objects. A rigorous interpretation is transport of structure: a bijection can re-express a known algebra without changing its identities. Merely replacing symbols by starred symbols does not determine a new multiplication.

## Definitions

Let φ:R→R be a bijection. Define x⊕y=φ⁻¹(φ(x)+φ(y)) and x⊙y=φ⁻¹(φ(x)φ(y)).

The transported zero is z=φ⁻¹(0), and the transported unit is e=φ⁻¹(1). Scalar coordinates are part of the definition.

## Result name

Transport gives an isomorphic field

## Result statement

The operations ⊕ and ⊙ make R into a field isomorphic to the ordinary real field by φ. In particular, associativity and distributivity are exact, irrespective of whether φ is linear.

## Proof

Apply φ to (x⊕y)⊕w. The result is (φ(x)+φ(y))+φ(w), which equals φ(x)+(φ(y)+φ(w)); injectivity of φ proves associativity. Similarly φ(x⊙(y⊕w))=φ(x)(φ(y)+φ(w))=φ(x)φ(y)+φ(x)φ(w)=φ((x⊙y)⊕(x⊙w)). The remaining field axioms follow by the same argument, with additive inverse φ⁻¹(−φ(x)) and multiplicative inverse φ⁻¹(1/φ(x)) when x≠z. This proof also explains its limit: no new intrinsic field invariant appears, because φ is an explicit isomorphism. Geometry, norms or differentiation added afterward may change, but they need independent definitions. For the cubic example the transported additive inverse remains−x, but the transported sum is not ordinary addition. Its scalar action must likewise be transported: c acting onx becomesφ⁻¹(cφ(x)) if the scalar field itself is left in ordinary coordinates. Mixing these two scalar conventions would create a different structure.

## Worked example: setup

Use φ(x)=x³, a simple exact bijection that avoids numerical inversion.

## Worked example: calculation

x⊕y=(x³+y³)^(1/3), whereas x⊙y=xy. Thus 1⊕1=2^(1/3). Both (1⊕1)⊕(−1) and 1⊕(1⊕(−1)) equal1. Ordinary addition combined with an arbitrary corrected product would not be covered by this calculation.

## Worked example: conclusion

The example supplies an exact associative deformation, with a completely explicit explanation of what has and has not changed.

## Limits

Transport through a coordinate bijection is not a proof that a proposed starred Lie algebra, magic square or mass formula has new representation theory. For vector spaces or noncommutative algebras, one must specify a bijection on the entire underlying set and transport all relevant operations.

## Extension

Test a proposed new multiplication by computing its associator on a basis. If it is transported, exhibit the isomorphism; if it is not, prove the identities directly and identify an invariant distinguishing it from the starting algebra.
