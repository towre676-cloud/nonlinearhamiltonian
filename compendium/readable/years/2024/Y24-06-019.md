## Revised question retained in the annual source

What is the actual scaling operator, its fixed point and the domain of attraction in one rigorously defined example?

## Status

## Summary

The recovered scaling passage is truncated before its recurrence can be reconstructed. A complete replacement should therefore declare a new operator. Independent normalized summation gives an exact renormalization map and explains a genuine universality statement with finite-variance hypotheses.

## Definitions

For a centered random variable X of variance σ², let φ(t)=E exp(itX). Define the law transformation R(X)=(X₁+X₂)/√2 for independent copies.

On characteristic functions this is (Rφ)(t)=φ(t/√2)². A fixed point means equality of probability laws, not equality of individual sample paths.

## Result name

A renormalization map with a proved Gaussian fixed point

## Result statement

The centered Gaussian law of variance σ² is a fixed point of R. For every centered finite-variance law, R^k(X) converges in distribution to that Gaussian. If an r-th cumulant exists, its value after k iterations is2^[k(1−r/2)] times its initial value.

## Proof

Independence gives the product of characteristic functions and therefore the stated operator. A Gaussian has φ(t)=exp(−σ²t²/2), and substitution leaves it unchanged. For a centered finite-variance law, differentiation at zero gives φ(s)=1−σ²s²/2+o(s²). After k iterations n=2^k independent summands are divided by√n, so φ_k(t)=φ(t/√n)^n. Taking a logarithm near1 yields nlogφ(t/√n)=−σ²t²/2+o(1), hence convergence to the Gaussian characteristic function; the continuity theorem converts it to weak convergence. For existing cumulants, logφ has coefficient κ_r(it)^r/r!, and replacing it by2logφ(t/√2) multiplies that coefficient by2^(1−r/2). This proves the cumulant statement, whose existence assumptions are stronger than needed for weak convergence.

## Worked example: setup

Start with a symmetric variable taking±1 with equal probability.

## Worked example: calculation

Its characteristic function is cos t. After k steps it is [cos(t/√(2^k))]^(2^k), tending to exp(−t²/2). The variance remains1, while the fourth cumulant is initially E X⁴−3(EX²)²=−2 and becomes−2/2^k. Odd cumulants vanish by symmetry. The persistence of a fixed variance and decay of this non-Gaussian fourth cumulant demonstrate two different scaling directions explicitly.

## Limits

Finite variance and independence matter. Heavy-tailed laws can have different normalizations and limits; correlated systems and deterministic bifurcation renormalization are not covered. The incomplete historical recurrence is not silently replaced by a claimed original formula.
