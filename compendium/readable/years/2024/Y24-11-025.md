## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which hypotheses turn a formal differential equation into a uniquely solvable weak problem?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered mathematical-physics catalogue included weak formulations and Lax–Milgram. A complete one-dimensional example gives a concrete existence, uniqueness, and stability result, while exposing the difference between coercivity and a merely symmetric expression.

## Definitions

Let H=H₀¹(0,1) with norm ||u||_H=||u′||₂.

For μ≥0 define a(u,v)=∫u′v′+μ∫uv and ℓ(v)=∫fv for f∈L².

## Result name

Unique weak solution and an energy bound

## Result statement

There exists a unique u∈H satisfying a(u,v)=ℓ(v) for every v∈H, and ||u′||₂≤||f||₂/π. If μ=0 and f=1, the solution is u(x)=x(1−x)/2.

## Proof

Poincaré’s inequality ||v||₂≤||v′||₂/π makes ℓ bounded with norm at most ||f||₂/π. Cauchy–Schwarz and the same inequality show a is bounded. Since μ≥0, a(v,v)≥||v′||², so it is coercive. To prove the needed existence rather than merely name it, equip H with the equivalent inner product a. The Riesz representation theorem supplies u with a(u,v)=ℓ(v). Uniqueness follows because a(u−w,u−w)=0 implies u=w. Taking v=u and combining coercivity with the functional bound gives the stated estimate. For f=1,μ=0, differentiating x(1−x)/2 gives −u″=1 and zero endpoint values; integration by parts verifies the weak equation.

## Worked example: setup

Compare μ=0 with the sign-changed form a_−(u,v)=∫u′v′−π²∫uv.

## Worked example: calculation

The function sin(πx) is nonzero but a_−(sinπx,v)=0 for every v∈H by integration by parts. Thus the sign-changed form is not coercive and its homogeneous equation is nonunique. The original solution has u′=(1−2x)/2 and ||u′||₂²=1/12, consistent with the bound ||u′||₂≤1/π.

## Worked example: conclusion

Existence follows from explicit Hilbert-space hypotheses, and the counterexample identifies exactly where uniqueness can fail.

## Limits

This is a linear elliptic result, not a general theorem for nonlinear quantum or gravitational equations. Boundary conditions and coefficient lower bounds are essential.

## Extension

For a finite element approximation, use Galerkin orthogonality and coercivity to derive a best-approximation error estimate in the energy norm.
