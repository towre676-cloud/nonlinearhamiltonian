## Revised question retained in the annual source

When does x↦x+α sign(x)|x|^β define a globally invertible coordinate change, and what regularity does it lose?

## Status

Classical result; recovered proposal repaired

## Summary

The historical radial correction becomes a precise real-variable map once its sign and parameter range are fixed. It is invertible for every α>0 and 0<β<1, but its derivative is unbounded near zero. This distinction matters whenever the map is inserted into a differential equation or metric.

## Definitions

Let φ(x)=x+α sign(x)|x|^β and φ(0)=0.

A map is inverse 1-Lipschitz when |φ⁻¹(u)−φ⁻¹(v)|≤|u−v| for all u,v.

## Result name

Global inversion and regularity

## Result statement

For α>0 and 0<β<1, φ is an increasing homeomorphism R→R. Its inverse is 1-Lipschitz. The map φ is smooth off zero and fails to have a finite derivative at zero.

## Proof

The signed power s(x)=sign(x)|x|^β is continuous and strictly increasing. Thus for y>x, φ(y)−φ(x)=(y−x)+α[s(y)−s(x)]≥y−x>0. This proves injectivity and the inverse Lipschitz inequality. The limits at positive and negative infinity are respectively positive and negative infinity; continuity and the intermediate value theorem give surjectivity. A continuous strictly monotone bijection has a continuous inverse. For x≠0, φ′(x)=1+αβ|x|^(β−1)>1. At zero, φ(h)/h=1+α|h|^(β−1), which diverges as h→0. Consequently a smooth change-of-variables theorem requiring a finite nonzero derivative cannot be invoked at zero. There is a useful local Hölder estimate as well: for0<β<1, the signed power is β-Hölder with constant at most2^(1−β). On a bounded interval, φ therefore has a quantitative modulus of continuity even though it is not Lipschitz at zero. This separates continuity, differentiability and inverse stability.

## Worked example: setup

Take α=1/3 and β=2/3. Evaluate at x=−8,0,8 and examine the inverse close to zero.

## Worked example: calculation

φ(8)=8+4/3=28/3 and φ(−8)=−28/3. For small y, y≈α sign(x)|x|^β, hence φ⁻¹(y)≈sign(y)(|y|/α)^(1/β)=sign(y)(3|y|)^(3/2). The exact inverse inequality bounds any recovered-input error by the output error.

## Worked example: conclusion

The transformation is globally well behaved for inversion, while differentiating it at its cusp requires separate treatment. These are compatible facts.

## Limits

The later logistic-factor formula X+α|X|^βF(X/X_P) is a different function and has not been substituted into this proof. Its negative-input behavior and monotonicity require their own analysis.

## Extension

For an equation in the transformed variable, first derive its weak or integral form. Then state whether trajectories cross zero; a formal chain rule at the cusp can otherwise introduce a nonexistent derivative.
