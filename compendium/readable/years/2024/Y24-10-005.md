## Revised question retained in the annual source

What changes when a linear operator is supplemented by a state-dependent cubic term?

## Status

Classical result; recovered proposal repaired

## Summary

The recovered nonlinear extension cannot be treated as an ordinary linear spectrum. A finite-dimensional constrained energy gives a complete model: its stationary points solve a nonlinear eigenvalue equation, and the multiplier depends on the state.

## Definitions

For real ψ=(x,y) with x²+y²=1, let E(ψ)=(1/2)ψᵀLψ+(g/4)(x⁴+y⁴).

A constrained stationary point satisfies Lψ+g(ψ componentwise cubed)=λψ.

## Result name

An exact nonlinear minimizer

## Result statement

For L=diag(0,1) and g=2, the global minimum is E=7/16, attained when x²=3/4 and y²=1/4. Its multiplier isλ=3/2.

## Proof

Put u=x², so y²=1−u and0≤u≤1. The energy is(1/2)(1−u)+(1/2)[u²+(1−u)²]=u²−(3/2)u+1=(u−3/4)²+7/16. This gives the exact global minimum and all sign choices of its minimizers. For a nonzero coordinate the stationary equation gives2x²=λ and1+2y²=λ. Substitution of the minimizing squares yieldsλ=3/2 in both equations. The original linear matrix has eigenvalues0 and1, so the nonlinear multiplier is neither a new linear eigenvalue of L nor a value recoverable from the linear spectral theorem alone. Multiplying the equation by ψ givesλ=ψᵀLψ+g(x⁴+y⁴), which also verifies the value.

## Worked example: setup

Use the positive representative ψ=(√3/2,1/2).

## Worked example: calculation

Lψ=(0,1/2); the cubic term is2ψ³=(3√3/4,1/4). Their sum is(3√3/4,3/4)=(3/2)ψ. At the boundary state(1,0), the energy is1/2, strictly above7/16. Thus allowing a mixed state genuinely changes the optimizer.

## Worked example: conclusion

An exact nonlinear variational calculation replaces the undefined idea that a cubic correction simply shifts every linear eigenvalue.

## Limits

The model is real and finite-dimensional. Complex phases, PDE compactness, existence of infinite families and linearization stability require additional analysis. No arithmetic zero correspondence follows from a nonlinear stationary equation.

## Extension

Linearize the constrained gradient at each stationary point and compute the tangent-space Hessian. In a PDE model, state the function space and prove existence before comparing numerical nonlinear modes.
