## Revised question retained in the annual source

What does the recovered height formula mean, and what elementary examples show the theorem’s actual content?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered source is Malle, Navarro, Schaeffer Fry, and Tiep’s proof of the remaining odd-prime implication of Brauer’s height zero conjecture. The full theorem is distinguished from the elementary character computations that can be supplied here.

## Definitions

For a p-block B of a finite group G with defect group D, χ(1)_p=|G:D|_p p^{hχ} defines the nonnegative height hχ of χ∈Irr(B).

For an integer n, n_p is its largest power-of-p divisor.

## Result name

Brauer height zero theorem, with an independently proved example

## Result statement

All ordinary irreducible characters in B have height zero if and only if D is abelian. The cited paper establishes the previously open implication for odd p; its classification-based proof is not reproduced. For the p-group Q₈ at p=2, the unique block has four height-zero characters and one height-one character.

## Proof

For a finite p-group the defect group of its unique p-block is the whole group, so |G:D|_p=1 and hχ is the p-adic valuation of χ(1). In Q₈, the quotient by the central subgroup {±1} is C₂×C₂, giving four distinct linear characters. The five conjugacy classes are {1},{−1},{±i},{±j},{±k}, so there are five irreducible characters. The sum-of-squares identity Σχχ(1)²=|Q₈|=8 leaves 8−4=4 for the last degree squared, making its degree two. Its height is one; the four linear degrees have height zero. Q₈ is nonabelian, so the presence of a positive-height character matches the theorem. For an abelian p-group every irreducible character is linear and all heights are zero.

## Worked example: setup

Compare Q₈ with C₈, both of order eight, in characteristic two.

## Worked example: calculation

Q₈ has degree multiset (1,1,1,1,2), with squared sum eight and height multiset (0,0,0,0,1). C₈ has eight degree-one characters and eight zero heights. The common group order does not determine the heights; the character and block structure matters.

## Worked example: conclusion

The recovered formula has a precise arithmetic interpretation, and the example checks it without pretending to reproduce the deep theorem.

## Limits

The elementary p-group block fact and the general theorem are cited background. No relationship between character height and a fluid parameter follows from equal-looking symbols or units.

## Extension

For a chosen non-p-group, supply the actual block partition and defect orders before computing heights; ordinary character degrees alone need not determine block membership.
