## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which conclusions about a running parameter follow from its stated differential equation, without adding an unconstructed field theory?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The historical catalogue invoked renormalization-group flow and scale hierarchies. Once a beta function and convention are fixed, its local consequences are an ordinary differential equation. The integration is exact within that assumed truncation, while physical validity remains a separate question.

## Definitions

Let t=log(μ/μ₀) and consider dg/dt=−b g² with b a fixed real number.

A fixed point is a zero of the specified beta function β(g); a truncation is a chosen approximate beta function, not an exact model by default.

## Result name

Exact solution of the quadratic beta-flow model

## Result statement

For initial g(0)=g₀, g(t)=g₀/(1+bg₀t) on the maximal interval where the denominator is nonzero. For b,g₀>0 it decreases to zero as t→+∞ and has a backward pole at t=−1/(bg₀).

## Proof

If g₀=0 the zero solution is immediate. Otherwise differentiating 1/g gives d(1/g)/dt=b as long as g is nonzero. Integration gives 1/g=1/g₀+bt and hence the formula; direct substitution verifies it. Local uniqueness for the polynomial vector field prevents a nonzero solution from crossing the zero equilibrium. The denominator determines the maximal interval in this model. A pole of a truncated equation does not by itself prove a physical singularity: neglected terms or a different effective description can become important first. Replacing μ by a different dimensional unit leaves t unchanged if μ and μ₀ use the same unit; writing log μ alone would hide a necessary reference scale.

## Worked example: setup

Take b=2,g₀=1/4 and compare scales μ₀ and e²μ₀.

## Worked example: calculation

At t=2 the coupling is (1/4)/(1+1)=1/8. The model’s backward pole is at t=−2, corresponding to μ=μ₀e^(−2). If β(g)=−2g²+cg³ instead, the extra term changes the ODE and may add a nonzero fixed point at 2/c; it is not legitimate to retain the old solution without an error estimate.

## Worked example: conclusion

The recovered scaling language becomes a checkable flow calculation with a defined reference scale and a stated approximation.

## Limits

No interacting continuum quantum field theory is constructed. Numerical coefficients depend on the coupling definition and loop convention, so the example is not a numerical QCD prediction.

## Extension

For a beta function known with a remainder bound, compare the exact and truncated flows by a differential inequality on a compact coupling interval.
