## Revised question retained in the annual source

How can growth and resource calculations be checked without inventing missing weights or mixing time periods?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered calculations mix five-year growth, land allocation, production cycles, and weekly percentages. Each becomes reliable after identifying its denominator and checking conservation of totals. A numerical projection is a conditional calculation, not evidence for its assumptions.

## Definitions

A category weight w_i is nonnegative and the weights sum to one.

A rate has a numerator, denominator, and specified time interval.

## Result name

Weighted growth bounds and normalization

## Result statement

For principal P≥0, integer n≥0, rates r_i≥−1 and weights w_i, A_n=PΣw_i(1+r_i)^n lies between P(1+r_min)^n and P(1+r_max)^n. A weekly total reported as a sum of daily RDI percentages must be divided by 700%, not 100%, to obtain the fraction of the seven-day target.

## Proof

Each growth factor lies between the extreme factors; multiplying by nonnegative weights and summing proves the bound. Conversely every value in the interval is achievable by mixing the two extreme categories. Thus the three rates alone do not determine a portfolio result: at least two independent weights are needed. Normalization follows by adding seven daily denominators of 100%. Changing from a 400-day cycle to a 365-day year multiplies an annualized amount by 365/400. These are consequences of units and convex combinations and do not depend on an economic or nutritional model.

## Worked example: setup

Take P=100000 and recovered rates 8%, 12%, and 18%; also audit the recovered seven land allocations and a 700000-unit annual total shared by 1000 recipients.

## Worked example: calculation

The five-year extreme values are 100000·1.08^5=146932.80768 and 100000·1.18^5=228775.77568. The historical 177543 is feasible but its weights are missing. The listed allocations sum to 74500, exceeding the claimed 70000 by 4500. A weekly total of 245% is 245/700=35% of its weekly target. Finally 700000/1000=700 units per recipient per year, and 700·1300=910000 energy units per recipient, while the aggregate is 910000000.

## Worked example: conclusion

If 99.2 kW is already rated output, a 40% capacity factor gives99.2·8760·0.4=347596.8kWh=347.5968MWh per year; separately multiplying capture and conversion again would require a different definition of rated power. Every discrepancy can be located in a missing weight, a sum, or a denominator.

## Limits

Rates, prices, yields, and compositional coefficients were historical assumptions and are not validated here. Gross revenue is not profit. The botanical and operational contexts are omitted.
