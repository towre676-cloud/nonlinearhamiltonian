## Definitions

A towera^(b^c) means exponentiateb byc first. The parenthesized expression(a^b)^c is a different binary-operation tree.

## Result name

## Result statement

For positivea,(a^b)^c=a^(bc), whereas a^(b^c) generally differs. Ifa≠1, equality holds exactly whenbc=b^c.

## Proof

From the exponential definition,(a^b)^c=exp(c log(exp(b loga)))=exp(bc loga)=a^(bc), sincea>0. For a≠1 the functionr↦a^r is injective, so equality witha^(b^c) is equivalent tobc=b^c. For a=1 all real powers are1. Restrictions are essential when allowing negative bases or complex exponents, because logarithm branches then prevent the same unrestricted argument. A parser records exponentiation as a node with left and right children; choosing right association createsa^(b^c), while left association creates(a^b)^c. No appeal to typographic intuition is needed once the tree is specified.

## Limits

This entry concerns exact finite values. Huge towers require modular arithmetic or logarithmic descriptions, and floating-point exponentiation can overflow even when the mathematical expression is well-defined.

## Extension

For a symbolic-language framework, give exponentiation its own grammar rule and store parentheses structurally. Test the rendered expression against the abstract syntax tree rather than relying on a string of caret symbols.
