## Summary

The historical list ranged from natural numbers through finite fields, p-adics, hyperreals and nonassociative algebras. A rigorous treatment cannot place all of them in one inclusion chain. Small constructions show which axioms distinguish them and why algebraic dimension is not physical space.

## Definitions

The field Q(√2) consists of a+b√2 with a,b∈Q. The finite field F₄ is F₂[t]/(t²+t+1).

Quaternion units satisfy i²=j²=k²=−1 and ij=k=−ji. An ordered field must have every nonzero square positive.

## Result statement

Q(√2) is a two-dimensional Q-vector-space field; F₄ has four elements and characteristic two; the quaternions are a four-dimensional real division algebra but not a commutative field. No ordering compatible with field operations can make C an ordered field.

## Proof

For a+b√2≠0, its product with a−b√2 is a²−2b², a nonzero rational number because √2 is irrational. This gives an explicit inverse and proves field closure. Over F₂, t²+t+1 has no root, hence is irreducible; the quotient is a field. If α is its class, α²=α+1 and α(α+1)=1. In quaternions ij≠ji, so the commutative field axiom fails even though nonzero elements have inverses. Finally, an ordered field has 1>0 and a²≥0; a square root i of −1 would force −1≥0, contradicting the order.

## Worked example: setup

Compare the meanings of the numeral two in Q(√2), F₄ and H.

## Worked example: calculation

In Q(√2), two is invertible with inverse1/2. In F₄, two equals zero because 1+1=0, although α is neither zero nor one. In H, real two is central and invertible, while products of imaginary units depend on order. These differences concern operations and axioms, not the number of coordinates used to display a numeral. The prime subfield of F₄ is only{0,1}; its remaining two elements solve the same irreducible quadratic over F₂. Thus field cardinality four does not mean characteristic four, and its two-dimensional vector-space structure is over F₂, not over R.

## Limits

The recovered list did not provide constructions of all hyperreal, surreal, ordinal or cardinal systems. This entry does not conflate those distinct objects or claim they are all field extensions of R.
