## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which parts of the proposed harmonic filter admit a precise, provable stability statement?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The historical assessment named Littlewood–Paley damping, Lyapunov functions, divisibility, and probabilistic filtering but did not define a single operator or the claimed rank scale. A finite Fourier model isolates a rigorous statement that can actually be checked.

## Definitions

On C^N use the unitary discrete Fourier transform F.

Let P_j be mutually orthogonal Fourier-band projections with ΣP_j=I, and let a_j≥0 be damping rates.

## Result name

Bandwise contraction

## Result statement

For A=Σa_jP_j, the evolution u(t)=exp(−tA)u(0) satisfies ||u(t)||²=Σexp(−2a_jt)||P_ju(0)||² and d||u||²/dt=−2Σa_j||P_ju||²≤0.

## Proof

Orthogonality gives P_iP_j=δ_ijP_j, so every power of A acts separately on each band. Summing the exponential series yields exp(−tA)=Σexp(−a_jt)P_j. Parseval gives the norm identity. Differentiation gives the Lyapunov inequality. If every active rate is at least a>0, the norm decays by exp(−at). If a band has zero rate, its component is preserved, so asymptotic convergence to zero cannot be concluded. For a forward Euler update I−hA, the multiplier in band j is 1−ha_j. Contractivity is equivalent to |1−ha_j|≤1, hence 0≤ha_j≤2 for every band; continuous stability alone does not justify an arbitrary step size.

## Worked example: setup

Choose N=4, retain the constant Fourier mode, and damp the other three modes at rate 2. Let u(0)=(1,0,0,0).

## Worked example: calculation

The constant projection is (1,1,1,1)/4 and its squared norm is 1/4. The orthogonal remainder has squared norm 3/4. Therefore ||u(t)||²=1/4+(3/4)e^(−4t). A forward Euler step is contractive for h≤1, but h=3/2 gives a multiplier −2 on the damped subspace and quadruples that component’s energy.

## Worked example: conclusion

The filter is stable in a stated norm and converges to the undamped subspace, with an explicit discrete-time restriction.

## Limits

No definition of the historical “BS-rank 0.5–1” was recovered. Frequency damping does not prove modular divisibility, nonlinear blow-up prevention, or a probabilistic reliability percentage.

## Extension

For nonlinear u′=−Au+N(u), derive an estimate Re⟨u,N(u)⟩≤c||u||² and compare c with the smallest damping rate.
