## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which operations compose, and what equation must a first-order correction satisfy?

## Status

Revised classical development with scoped worked result

## Summary

An endomorphism operad and a first-order associative deformation make the word correction precise. The example is ordinary algebra, with no claim that the historic rigid or p-adic spaces automatically carry the required operad action. It supplies a complete finite cocycle calculation and a nontrivial deformation rather than an application slogan.

## Definitions

For a vector space V, its endomorphism operad has O(n)=Hom(V^⊗n,V), with insertion of one multilinear map into a chosen input of another. A bilinear multiplication m is associative when m(m(a,b),c)=m(a,m(b,c)). Work over a characteristic-zero field with ε²=0.

## Result name

The first-order associativity equation is a Hochschild cocycle condition

## Result statement

For m_ε=m+εφ, associativity modulo ε² is equivalent to aφ(b,c)−φ(ab,c)+φ(a,bc)−φ(a,b)c=0 for all a,b,c. On A=k[x]/(x²), the normalized correctionφ(x,x)=1, φ(1,a)=φ(a,1)=0 satisfies it and is not removed by a first-order unital basis change.

## Proof

Expand the two parenthesizations and compare their ε coefficients; rearranging gives the displayed equation. For the dual-number algebra it is enough to check triples of basis elements1,x by multilinearity. Triples involving1 cancel by normalization, while the all-x triple givesx−x=0. The deformed algebra is the free rank-two algebra over k[ε]/(ε²) with relationx²=ε, whose quotient multiplication is associative directly. Under x′=x+ε(a+bx), one has(x′)²=ε+2aεx modulo ε², so the scalar ε term cannot be removed; the original undeformed relation x²=0 is not restored.

## Worked example: conclusion

The correction is a genuine first-order algebraic deformation with an explicit equivalence convention.

## Limits

This example does not construct an operadic action on a rigid analytic space, identify a universal deformation or establish higher-order unobstructedness. Higher powers of ε require additional equations.

## Extension
