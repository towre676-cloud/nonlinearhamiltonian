## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which independently fixed quantities make a proposed correction identifiable, and what cannot be determined from the sum alone?

## Status

Classical mathematics with newly authored derivation or finite example and explicit scope

## Summary

The historical gyre fragment adds undefined corrections to beta functions, vacuum energy, a magnetic moment and a holographic entropy. The mathematically defensible development is an identifiability test: an additive symbol is a parametrization until a model defines it and independent information separates it from its baseline.

## Definitions

For one scalar observable let F(θ,g)=θ+g, with both θ and g unknown real parameters. Identifiability means different admissible parameters produce different observable predictions.

For four independent additive observables write F(θ,g)=θ+g on R⁴×R⁴. A local sensitivity matrix is the derivative of the prediction map.

## Result name

An additive correction ansatz does not identify a new mechanism

## Result statement

With no further constraints, the scalar sum has a one-dimensional unidentifiable direction, and the four-observable sum has a four-dimensional one. Knowing the baseline independently can identify a residual, but does not by itself explain or predict that residual.

## Proof

For every real t, F(θ+t,g−t)=θ+g=F(θ,g). Thus each observed sum has an entire affine line of compatible parameter pairs. The Jacobian is[1,1], whose null space is span{(1,−1)} and whose rank is1. In four dimensions the derivative is[I₄ I₄], of rank4, with kernel{(v,−v):v∈R⁴}. This exactly matches the four-parameter family of indistinguishable decompositions. If θ is fixed by an independent calibrated model, then g=F−θ is algebraically determined by the observation. But this is a fitted residual: obtaining future values of g still requires a separately defined predictive rule, and uncertainty in θ propagates directly to uncertainty in the inferred g.

## Worked example: setup

Take an observed value y=7 and leave both baseline and correction unconstrained.

## Worked example: calculation

(θ,g)=(7,0),(5,2),(10,−3) all give y=7. Repeating the same scalar measurement at greater precision cannot remove this structural ambiguity. If a second observation instead has the declared law y₂=θ+2g and yields9, subtracting gives g=2 and then θ=5; the sensitivity matrix[[1,1],[1,2]] has determinant1, so it is invertible. The extra independent equation resolves the ambiguity because it has a different known dependence, not because it is labeled another correction.

## Worked example: conclusion

This is a complete finite theorem about the recovered ansatz form. It identifies the additional mathematical data needed for a testable model.

## Limits

No gyre field, beta-function correction, cosmological constant or new quantum-gravity interaction is derived. The four historical quantities have different dimensions and cannot be added to each other. Even the entropy expression needs its gravitational units and normalization declared.

## Extension

Define an explicit correction function of shared parameters and specified observables, compute its sensitivity rank, and state which parameters remain indistinguishable before attempting estimation.
