Which numerical constraints follow from crystallographic symmetry and an A₂ Cartan matrix?

## Summary

The recovered mineral discussion conflated discrete point groups, root systems, Lie algebras, and Mordell–Weil groups. Their dimensions and allowable integers are exact and different. A concrete A₂ calculation and the planar crystallographic restriction supply reliable replacements.

## Definitions

For simple roots α_i, use a_ij=2(α_i·α_j)/(α_i·α_i), with diagonal entries2 and nonpositive off-diagonal integers.

A planar lattice-preserving rotation acts by an integral2×2matrix of determinant one.

## Result name

A₂ roots and crystallographic rotation orders

## Result statement

The A₂ Cartan matrix [[2,−1],[−1,2]] has six roots; the associated complex Lie algebra has dimension8=6+2. A planar lattice rotation can have only orders1,2,3,4,6.

## Proof

Choose unit simple roots with angle120°, so their dot product is−1/2 and both off-diagonal Cartan entries are−1. The roots are ±α₁,±α₂,±(α₁+α₂), six vectors; adjoining a rank-two Cartan subalgebra gives dimension eight for sl₃. For the rotation restriction, its real eigenvalues over C are e^(±iθ), so its trace is2cosθ. Since it is represented by an integer matrix, the trace is an integer in[−2,2], hence−2,−1,0,1,2. These give θ with rotation orders2,3,4,6,1 respectively. Positive off-diagonal numbers cannot be used as simple-root Cartan entries under this convention. For finite rank-two systems, the product a₁₂a₂₁ is0,1,2,or3, distinguishing disconnected,A₂,B₂,G₂ cases.

## Limits

No invented mineral polynomial or torsion assignment is retained. A continuous Lie group, finite point group, space group, and binary polyhedral group are not interchangeable; the binary icosahedral group has order120 rather than60.
