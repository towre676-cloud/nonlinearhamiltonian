## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which basis represents the density, which region is integrated, and when is an implicit level set a regular surface?

## Status

## Summary

The recovered density, implicit-surface and finite-element discussion becomes concrete on one tetrahedron. Barycentric coordinates give an exact mass formula and an explicit positivity criterion; the regular-value test prevents a singular point from being mislabeled a smooth surface.

## Definitions

Let T be the tetrahedron with vertices v₀=(0,0,0), v₁=(1,0,0), v₂=(0,1,0), v₃=(0,0,1). Its barycentric coordinates are λ₀=1−x−y−z, λ₁=x, λ₂=y, λ₃=z.

An affine density is ρ=Σ_(i=0)^3 ρ_i λ_i. A level set F=0 is regular at a point when ∇F is nonzero there.

## Result name

Affine density integrates exactly on a simplex

## Result statement

For every affine density on a nondegenerate tetrahedron, ∫_Tρ dV=Vol(T)(ρ₀+ρ₁+ρ₂+ρ₃)/4. It is nonnegative everywhere if and only if every vertex value is nonnegative. For F=x²+y²+z²−r², the zero set is a regular two-dimensional surface when r>0, but at r=0 it is one singular point.

## Proof

Affine permutations of the four vertices preserve the tetrahedron and its volume measure. They permute the barycentric functions, so all four integrals ∫λ_i are equal. Since Σλ_i=1, each is Vol(T)/4. Integrating the linear combination proves the mass formula. Since λ_i≥0 and sum to one, ρ is a convex combination of its vertex values; their nonnegativity implies pointwise nonnegativity. The converse follows by evaluating at a vertex. For the implicit surface, ∇F=2(x,y,z). On F=0 with r>0 its norm is2r, nonzero, so the implicit-function theorem gives a local surface chart. With r=0 the only real solution is the origin, where the gradient vanishes and there is no two-dimensional neighborhood.

## Worked example: setup

Integrate ρ=1+x+y+z on the standard tetrahedron.

## Worked example: calculation

The volume is1/6, and the four vertex values are1,2,2,2. Consequently mass=(1/6)(7/4)=7/24. Direct integration gives the same answer: ∫1=1/6 and ∫x=∫y=∫z=1/24. The mean density is7/4, equal to the value at the centroid(1/4,1/4,1/4). Nonnegative nodal coefficients preserve positivity for this affine basis, a property that need not survive an arbitrary oscillatory higher-order basis.

## Worked example: conclusion

The density representation now yields a finite exact calculation rather than an unspecified numerical pipeline.

## Limits

This is exact for affine densities on straight nondegenerate simplices. Curved elements, nonlinear densities and signed Jacobians need additional quadrature and geometry analysis. The regularity test does not assert that every level set is globally connected or sphere-like.

## Extension

For a declared quadratic density use simplex monomial integrals, then compare its exact integral with a stated quadrature rule and check whether positivity remains preserved.
