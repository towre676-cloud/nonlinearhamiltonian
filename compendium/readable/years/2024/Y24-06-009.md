## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which sheaf and cover define the claimed obstruction, and what exactly fails to glue?

## Status

Classical mathematics with newly authored derivation or finite example and explicit scope

## Summary

The historical statement called H¹(M) an obstruction to a global flow without naming a sheaf or complex. A two-arc cover of the circle gives an exact nonzero cohomology calculation and explains why this does not contradict the ordinary sheaf axiom for compatible sections.

## Definitions

Cover S¹ by connected contractible arcs U,V whose intersection has two connected components. Let R_const be the sheaf of locally constant real functions.

The Cech cochain spaces are C⁰=R² and C¹=R², with differential d(a,b)=(b−a,b−a); there is no triple intersection term for this two-set cover.

## Result name

A nonzero H¹ from two arcs without violating sheaf gluing

## Result statement

The Cech groups are H⁰≅R and H¹≅R²/diag(R)≅R. In particular, the overlap cocycle (1,0) is not a coboundary. The calculation agrees with sheaf cohomology because the cover and its finite intersections are acyclic for this sheaf.

## Proof

A locally constant section on each connected arc is one scalar; on the disconnected overlap it is two independently chosen scalars. Restriction of the two arc values gives the same difference b−a on both overlap components. Thus the kernel of d consists of equal pairs, giving H⁰=R. Its image is the diagonal line, and quotienting R² by that line leaves one dimension, detected by the difference of the two coordinates. The pair (1,0) has unequal coordinates and cannot arise from one b−a. The acyclic-cover comparison theorem supplies the identification with sheaf H¹; the finite complex itself can be computed without that general theorem.

## Worked example: setup

Try to solve b−a=1 on one overlap component and b−a=0 on the other.

## Worked example: calculation

No pair of constants a,b can satisfy both equations, which is exactly the nontrivial cocycle. By contrast, if two actual local sections agree on their overlap, then a=b and they glue uniquely to one constant section on S¹, just as the sheaf axiom requires. H¹ concerns gluing data such as torsor transitions, not a failure of the axiom for compatible ordinary functions.

## Worked example: conclusion

A cohomological obstruction is meaningful only after the sheaf, cover and target gluing problem have been specified.

## Limits

This is an ordinary topological site, not a construction of every Grothendieck topology or an etale-flow obstruction. No dynamics is inferred from the isolated notation H¹(M).

## Extension

Define the desired local differential equation or torsor and identify its coefficient sheaf before interpreting its Cech cocycle as a dynamical obstruction.
