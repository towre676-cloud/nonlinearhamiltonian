## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which stated evolution preserves probability, and what changes when an observed coordinate is only part of the state?

## Definitions

A quantum state in this example is a vector Ψ=(a,b)∈C² with Euclidean norm one. Time and coupling κ are expressed in compatible units, with ℏ=1.

Hermitian means H*=H. The source fragment recovers the name quantum evolution but no complete Hamiltonian or state-space definition; the model below is newly chosen, not a recovered formula.

The amplitude a alone describes a projection of the full state. It is not automatically a normalized state of a closed one-dimensional system.

## Result name

Quantum evolution needs an explicit state space and norm

## Result statement

For H=[[0,κ],[κ,0]], κ real, and Ψ(0)=(1,0), the solution is Ψ(t)=(cosκt,−i sinκt). Its norm is one. Eliminating b gives ȧ(t)=−κ²∫₀ᵗa(s)ds, whose solution a=cosκt does not have constant squared modulus.

## Proof

Differentiate Ψ*Ψ and use Ψ̇=−iHΨ to obtain iΨ*H*Ψ−iΨ*HΨ=0. Thus Hermiticity proves conservation for every initial state. In the chosen matrix, ȧ=−iκb and ḃ=−iκa. Integrating the second equation with b(0)=0 gives b(t)=−iκ∫₀ᵗa(s)ds. Substitution gives the memory equation with its negative sign. Differentiating again gives ä+κ²a=0, with a(0)=1 and ȧ(0)=0. The sine coordinate supplies exactly the probability absent from |a|². This derivation identifies what is eliminated rather than assigning an unexplained kernel a unitary interpretation.

## Worked example: setup

Take κ=2 and t=π/8, then compare the whole state with the first coordinate.

## Worked example: calculation

The full vector is (1/√2,−i/√2), with squared norm one. The first-coordinate probability is one half. At t=π/4, a vanishes while the full state remains normalized. These exact trigonometric checks distinguish probability transfer from a violation of the full evolution law.

## Worked example: conclusion

The example supplies a consistent mathematical meaning for one possible quantum-evolution component and exposes a common normalization error.

## Limits

It does not recover the missing historical equation, attach a physical meaning to a consciousness variable, or justify arbitrary nonlinear additions. Infinite-dimensional Hamiltonians require operator domains and self-adjointness hypotheses beyond a matrix calculation.

## Extension

For a block Hamiltonian, solve the hidden block by variation of constants and substitute it into the observed block. The resulting kernel and source term depend on the hidden Hamiltonian and initial condition; derive both before interpreting a reduced evolution.
