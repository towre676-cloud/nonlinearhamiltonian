## Revised question retained in the annual source

Reconstruction: Why does a continuous self-map of a closed ball have a fixed point, why must a continuous tangent field on S² vanish, and what explicit examples show the hypotheses?

## Status

Classical mathematics newly developed from a recovered assistant-only catalog name

## Summary

These are global existence and obstruction theorems, not identities evaluated at a point. A closed ball and its boundary sphere behave differently, and even-dimensional spheres have a parity obstruction absent on S¹. The statements below make those distinctions explicit and identify the classical topological input used in each proof.

## Definitions

Brouwer: every continuous F:Bⁿ→Bⁿ on a closed Euclidean n-ball has a fixed point. Equivalently use the standard simplex Δⁿ={xᵢ≥0,Σxᵢ=1}.

For x∈S²⊂R³, a tangent vector v(x) satisfies x·v(x)=0. A tangent field is continuous when its three coordinate functions are continuous.

Use the classical degree facts deg(id:S²→S²)=1, deg(−id)=−1, and homotopy invariance of degree. These are explicit inputs to the hairy-ball proof.

## Result name

A simplex limiting proof and a sphere-degree contradiction

## Result statement

A continuous self-map of a closed ball has at least one fixed point. Every continuous tangent field on S² has at least one zero; neither assertion guarantees uniqueness, and the sphere itself is not the ball in Brouwer’s theorem.

## Proof

For Brouwer, use arbitrarily fine triangulations of Δⁿ and the classical Sperner lemma: if a vertex on a face receives a label belonging to that face, some small simplex has all n+1 labels. If F has no fixed vertex, label each vertex v by an i with vᵢ>Fᵢ(v); such an index exists because both coordinate sums are one, and vᵢ=0 prevents that label because Fᵢ≥0. A fully labeled small simplex therefore exists. Let its mesh tend to zero and choose a convergent subsequence of its vertices using compactness. Continuity gives xᵢ≥Fᵢ(x) for every i at the common limiting point. Equal coordinate sums force equality in all coordinates, giving a fixed point. This proof uses Sperner’s lemma as a stated classical combinatorial input. For hairy ball, suppose a nowhere-zero tangent field exists and normalize it to w(x). Then H(x,t)=cos(πt)x+sin(πt)w(x) stays on S² because x and w are orthogonal unit vectors. It is a homotopy from id to −id, contradicting their different degrees. This proves the obstruction using the stated degree facts.

## Worked example: setup

On [0,1]² take F(x,y)=(x/2+1/4,y/4+1/4). On S² project the fixed vector e₃ into each tangent plane.

## Worked example: calculation

The square map has fixed point (1/2,1/3), and each coordinate stays in [0,1]. The tangent field v(x,y,z)=e₃−z(x,y,z) has x·v=0 and ||v||²=1−z²; its zeros are exactly the north and south poles. In contrast w(x,y)=(−y,x) is a nonvanishing unit tangent field on S¹. The verifier checks these algebraic examples exactly. It does not pretend to verify a global topological theorem by sampling.

## Worked example: conclusion

Existence is supplied by compactness and topology, while the finite examples reveal the geometric content of the hypotheses.

## Limits

The proof explicitly imports Sperner’s lemma and basic degree theory rather than claiming a self-contained foundation of topology. Continuity, the closed-ball domain, and the even sphere dimension cannot simply be removed. A circle rotation has no fixed point despite being a continuous sphere self-map.

## Extension

In applications a stronger contraction estimate can additionally yield uniqueness and an iteration rate. For the square example the Euclidean Lipschitz constant is 1/2, a quantitative fact that Brouwer alone does not provide.
