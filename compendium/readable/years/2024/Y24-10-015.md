## Revised question retained in the annual source

If a J-based map relabels a cyclic group bijectively, what changes mathematically in its discrete-log equation?

## Status

Classical result; recovered proposal repaired

## Summary

The recovered application list gives no precise group operation or map. A rigorous algebraic analysis can nevertheless identify a broad case: bijective transport produces the same group in new coordinates. This is a structural theorem, with no security claim attached.

## Definitions

Let G=〈g〉 be a cyclic group of ordern andφ:G→S a bijection. Define a⋆b=φ(φ⁻¹(a)φ⁻¹(b)).

The transported power is defined using⋆, including the transported identityφ(1).

## Result name

An exact equivalence of logarithm equations

## Result statement

φ(g) raised to k using⋆ equalsφ(g^k). Thus φ(g)^⋆k=φ(h) if and only if g^k=h. The group and its exponent equation are isomorphic to the originals.

## Proof

Induct on nonnegativek. Atk=0 both sides equalφ(1). If the identity holds atk, then φ(g)^⋆(k+1)=φ(φ⁻¹(φ(g^k))g)=φ(g^(k+1)). Negative exponents follow from transported inverses. Injectivity ofφ proves equivalence of the equations. Associativity and all group identities are inherited through the same calculation. Ifφ andφ⁻¹ are explicitly available, converting between equation instances is exact. If either map is unavailable or many-to-one, this theorem no longer describes the proposal: the resulting operation, representation and possible collisions must be specified separately.

## Worked example: setup

Use the multiplicative group F_7* with generatorg=3, andφ(x)=x² only on a declared subset versusφ(x)=2x mod7 on all nonzero residues.

## Worked example: calculation

The mapx↦2x is a bijection of the six labels. Since3⁴≡4 mod7, its transported equation isφ(3)^⋆4=φ(4), or6^⋆4=1. The label1 is not the transported identity, which is2. In contrastx↦x² is not bijective onF_7*:1 and6 collide, so it cannot serve as this transport without changing the quotient problem.

## Worked example: conclusion

The group law, not a new symbol alone, determines what the exponent equation means.

## Limits

The historical J map, cost model and complete construction were not recovered. This does not establish computational hardness, an attack or any bit-security level; it identifies exact algebraic equivalence in the stated bijective case.

## Extension

Specify the map and operation, test injectivity and derive its kernel or inverse. Only then classify whether the object is a relabeling, a quotient, an extension or a genuinely different problem.
