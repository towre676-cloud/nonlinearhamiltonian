## Revised question retained in the annual source

How do curvature forms yield topological numbers, and why must they not be confused with a variational gravitational action?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered catalogue mentioned Pontryagin terms alongside curvature actions. Chern–Weil theory gives precise invariant integrals; their local variations are boundary terms under the appropriate hypotheses. This is a different role from an Einstein–Hilbert kinetic term.

## Definitions

For a connection A on a complex bundle, F=dA+A∧A and D_AF=0 is the Bianchi identity.

For a Hermitian line bundle write its unitary curvature as iF_real with F_real a real two-form; c₁ is represented by F_real/(2π).

## Result name

An explicit characteristic number and its variation

## Result statement

On the unit four-torus, a line bundle with curvature 2π(n dx₁∧dx₂+m dx₃∧dx₄) has ∫c₁²=2nm. In general δ tr(F∧F)=2d tr(δA∧F).

## Proof

For the line-bundle example, squaring the two-form gives twice the cross term because two-forms commute under wedge product and each individual square vanishes. Integration over the unit oriented four-torus gives 2nm. Integer periods of the normalized curvature encode the line bundle’s Chern class. For the variation, δF=D_AδA. Cyclicity of trace and the even degree of F give δtr(F∧F)=2tr(D_AδA∧F). Apply the covariant Leibniz rule and use D_AF=0; taking a trace removes commutators, leaving an ordinary exterior derivative. On a closed manifold its integral is zero by Stokes, so a constant multiple of this topological term contributes no local bulk Euler–Lagrange equation under smooth variations within the same bundle.

## Worked example: setup

Choose n=2,m=3. Compare a closed torus with a four-manifold with boundary.

## Worked example: calculation

The integral is 2·2·3=12. For the underlying oriented real plane bundle, p₁=c₁² in this convention, so its first Pontryagin number is also 12. On a manifold with boundary, the action variation is the boundary integral 2∫∂M tr(δA∧F), which need not vanish without boundary conditions or a compensating term.

## Worked example: conclusion

A topological curvature integral is invariant under allowed interior deformations, while boundary data can carry genuine variation.

## Limits

Normalization, real versus complex curvature conventions, and trace representation affect constants. No claim is made that a Pontryagin density alone supplies the Einstein equations or a quantum-gravity measure.

## Extension

Compare the variation of ∫tr(F∧*F), whose Hodge star introduces a metric and whose equation is D_A*F=0, with the topological density.
