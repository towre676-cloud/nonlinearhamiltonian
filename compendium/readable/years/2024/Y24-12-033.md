## Revised question retained in the annual source

How can the recovered combinatorial flag-variety language be made concrete with Plücker coordinates?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered Borovik–Gelfand–White discussion named combinatorial flag varieties without a specific flag. A line inside a plane in C³ gives a complete small model: its incidence is one polynomial relation, and the vanishing pattern of minors yields a matroid that can change under specialization.

## Definitions

Represent a two-plane by the row space of[[1,0,1],[0,1,t]].

Its ordered minors are p₁₂=1,p₁₃=t,p₂₃=−1; a line is represented by a nonzero vectoru=(u₁,u₂,u₃).

## Result name

Flag incidence and support specialization

## Result statement

The lineCu lies in the plane exactly whenu₁p₂₃−u₂p₁₃+u₃p₁₂=0. For the plane above this isu₃=u₁+tu₂; its rank-two matroid loses the basis{1,3} precisely att=0.

## Proof

The determinant of the matrix with the two plane rows andu as the third row is zero exactly when the rows are linearly dependent. Since the first two rows are independent(p₁₂=1), vanishing is equivalent to membership ofu in their span. Expanding the determinant along the third row yields the displayed Plücker incidence polynomial. The rank-two matroid’s bases are index pairs whose2×2minor is nonzero. Here{1,2} and{2,3} are always bases, while{1,3} is a basis exactly whent≠0. These are combinatorial support data obtained from actual linear algebra. A flag requires compatibility between the line and plane; independently choosing their supports is insufficient.

## Worked example: setup

Chooseu=(1,1,1+t), which satisfies the incidence equation for everyt.

## Worked example: calculation

At generic t≠0,−1 the line’s support is all three coordinates and the plane has all three bases. Att=0 the plane loses{1,3} but the line still has all coordinates nonzero. Att=−1 the plane retains all its bases, while the line’s third coordinate becomes zero. Thus different components of the flag combinatorics can specialize at different parameter values.

## Worked example: conclusion

The example has an exact incidence certificate and explicit changing support, rather than an undefined state-organization analogy.

## Limits

This is an ordinary complex representable flag, not a proof of a general combinatorial flag-variety classification. The historical framework did not specify a hyperfield-valued flag or its axioms.

## Extension

Push the minors through a support map to a Krasner matroid, then test the appropriate flag compatibility axioms before importing hyperfield terminology.
