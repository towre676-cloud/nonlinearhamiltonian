## Revised question retained in the annual source

Reconstruction: Starting from three independent Gaussian velocity components, what is the normalized speed density and how do its mode, mean and root-mean-square speed differ?

## Status

Classical mathematics newly developed from a recovered assistant-only catalog name

## Summary

A velocity component and a nonnegative speed have different distributions. The factor proportional to speed squared comes from three-dimensional volume, not an arbitrary correction. The derivation below identifies that Jacobian and calculates the distinct characteristic speeds from one normalized model.

## Definitions

Assume an equilibrium classical ideal-gas velocity vector V=(V₁,V₂,V₃) with independent centered Gaussian components of variance σ²=k_BT/m>0.

The vector density is (2πσ²)^(−3/2)exp(−|v|²/(2σ²)) on R³. The speed is R=|V|≥0.

The spherical volume element is r²sinθ drdθdφ. Integrating over directions contributes 4πr², so a speed density must include this factor.

## Result name

Normalization, moments and three characteristic speeds

## Result statement

The speed density is g(r)=√(2/π)r²σ^(−3)e^(−r²/(2σ²)) for r≥0. Its mode is √2σ, mean is √(8/π)σ, and root-mean-square speed is √3σ. More generally E[Rᵖ]=2^(p/2+1)Γ((p+3)/2)σᵖ/√π for p>−3.

## Proof

Integrate the vector density over each sphere of radius r to obtain g. The one-dimensional Gaussian integral can be normalized by squaring ∫e^(−x²/2)dx and changing to polar coordinates in R²; thus the joint Gaussian has total mass one, as does g. For the moments substitute u=r²/(2σ²) into ∫₀∞rᵖg(r)dr. The power of r and dr produce the stated gamma integral, finite near zero exactly when p>−3. Substituting p=1 and p=2 gives the mean and second moment. Alternatively E[R²]=ΣE[Vᵢ²]=3σ², an independent check. For the mode differentiate log g(r)=constant+2log r−r²/(2σ²) on r>0. Its derivative vanishes only at r=√2σ, changes from positive to negative, and the density vanishes at zero and infinity. This proves it is the unique positive maximum.

## Worked example: setup

Choose the nondimensional model σ=1, then compare the three speeds and the first two moments.

## Worked example: calculation

The mode is approximately 1.41421356, the mean 1.59576912 and the root-mean-square speed 1.73205081. The exact second moment is three and the variance is 3−8/π≈0.45352091. The verifier integrates g, rg and r²g over [0,10] with a declared composite Simpson rule, compares to 1,√(8/π),3 and reports the finite integration errors. The unintegrated tail is exponentially small; the proof of normalization and moments is the analytic gamma calculation, not the quadrature.

## Worked example: conclusion

The three commonly quoted speeds answer different mathematical questions, and their ordering is a consequence of the density rather than a convention.

## Limits

This is the explicitly assumed classical equilibrium Gaussian model, not a statement about every velocity sample or every interacting system. The scalar component density has its maximum at zero, while the speed density has its maximum away from zero; confusing them removes the radial Jacobian.

## Extension

For anisotropic independent variances σ₁²,σ₂²,σ₃² the second moment remains their sum, but the speed is generally not Maxwell distributed. This supplies a concrete diagnostic for whether isotropy was used in a calculation.
