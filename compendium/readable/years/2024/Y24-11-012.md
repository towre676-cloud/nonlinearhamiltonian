## Revised question retained in the annual source

What does it mean rigorously to obtain one diagram algebra as a quotient of another?

## Status

Developed classical result with a checked example; historical claims corrected where needed

## Summary

The recovered question asked whether ordinary Temperley–Lieb is a quotient of an affine version. The answer depends on the precise affine presentation and the relation that closes or removes the winding generator. The universal quotient construction can be proved before making that choice.

## Definitions

TL₃(δ) is generated by 1,e₁,e₂ with e_i²=δe_i, e₁e₂e₁=e₁ and e₂e₁e₂=e₂.

For an algebra A and subset R, ⟨R⟩ denotes the two-sided ideal generated by R.

## Result name

Universal property of imposing relations

## Result statement

A homomorphism φ:A→B satisfying φ(r)=0 for all r∈R factors uniquely through A/⟨R⟩. A proposed affine-to-finite quotient therefore requires explicit generator images that satisfy every affine relation.

## Proof

The kernel of φ is a two-sided ideal containing R, hence contains ⟨R⟩. Define φ̄(a+⟨R⟩)=φ(a); if two representatives differ by the ideal their images agree, so this is well-defined. It is a homomorphism, and uniqueness follows because the quotient map is surjective. In TL₃, every word can be reduced using e_i² and alternating triples to a span of 1,e₁,e₂,e₁e₂,e₂e₁: a word with no repeated adjacent generator must alternate, and any alternating word of length at least three has a reducible triple. Diagram representations show these five elements are independent in the abstract diagram algebra, even when special parameter values make a particular matrix representation nonfaithful.

## Worked example: setup

Use rank-one projections P,Q on R² with unit spanning vectors u,v satisfying ⟨u,v⟩=1/δ, and put e₁=δP,e₂=δQ, for real δ≥1.

## Worked example: calculation

e₁²=δe₁ and e₂²=δe₂. Also P Q P=|⟨u,v⟩|²P=P/δ², so e₁e₂e₁=δ³PQP=δP=e₁; the other triple relation is identical. At δ=2 choose u=(1,0),v=(1/2,√3/2), giving a fully explicit representation.

## Worked example: conclusion

The finite relations have a concrete representation and the quotient criterion is exact. An unspecified “set the affine generator to one” instruction is insufficient unless all relations survive.

## Limits

Different affine and annular Temperley–Lieb conventions have different generators, central winding elements, and quotients. No unique affine presentation was recovered, so no convention-dependent quotient is asserted.

## Extension

Write the chosen annular diagram relations, define a filling or evaluation map, and identify its kernel rather than guessing one relation.
