## Revised question retained in the annual source

How can a rigidity statistic be computed exactly on a finite interval, and what information does it retain?

## Status

Classical result; recovered proposal repaired

## Summary

The recovered proposal cites spectral rigidity without defining its regression convention. A finite counting staircase and an ordinary least-squares fit give a fully specified statistic. This makes the dependence on interval placement and unfolding explicit.

## Definitions

For levels x_j, let N(x)=#{j:x_j≤x}. On[a,a+L], define Δ3=L⁻¹min_(A,B)∫[N(x)−Ax−B]²dx.

Any unfolding map applied before this calculation is part of the statistical model.

## Result name

An exact one-level example

## Result statement

For a single level at1/2 in the interval[0,1], the best affine fit to its counting staircase is(3/2)x−1/4 and Δ3=1/16.

## Proof

Here N(x)=0 on[0,1/2) and1 on[1/2,1]. The normal equations are ∫(N−Ax−B)dx=0 and∫x(N−Ax−B)dx=0. The necessary moments are∫N=1/2,∫xN=3/8,∫x=1/2 and∫x²=1/3. Thus A/2+B=1/2 and A/3+B/2=3/8, giving A=3/2,B=−1/4. Orthogonal projection gives the minimum squared error ∫N²−A∫xN−B∫N=1/2−9/16+1/8=1/16. Direct integration of the two quadratic pieces gives the same result. Since L=1, no further normalization is needed.

## Worked example: setup

Compare with an interval containing no level, and with a change of the horizontal coordinate.

## Worked example: calculation

If N is constant on the whole interval, choosing A=0 and B=N gives Δ3=0. A nonlinear unfolding changes both level positions and the measure dx, so it can change the value. It is therefore inappropriate to choose an unfolding after seeing which one most closely resembles a target curve.

## Worked example: conclusion

The statistic measures a defined least-squares deviation. Its value is not an operator identity or proof of spectral correspondence.

## Limits

A single interval is not the averaged Dyson–Mehta statistic usually used in large-sample comparisons. Boundary conventions at jump points have measure zero, but interval selection and averaging do matter. Random-matrix expectations require an independently justified ensemble.

## Extension

Predeclare a smooth counting approximation for unfolding and a family of windows, compute exact staircase integrals, and report the window-to-window spread alongside the mean.
