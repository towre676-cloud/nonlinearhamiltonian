## Revised question retained in the annual source

Could a bounded perturbation of a one-dimensional Laplacian have all squared zeta ordinates as its eigenvalues?

## Status

Classical result; recovered proposal repaired

## Summary

The historical proposal requires more than real eigenvalues: its counting function must match the number of all zeta zeros, with multiplicity. A basic asymptotic comparison rules out a large class of elementary candidate operators. It does not rule out every possible Hilbert–Pólya construction.

## Definitions

For a positive discrete operator L, N_L(Λ) counts eigenvalues at mostΛ, with multiplicity.

The zeta-zero count is N_ζ(T)=T/(2π)log(T/(2π))−T/(2π)+O(logT), counting zeros in the critical strip with positive ordinate.

## Result name

A concrete density mismatch

## Result statement

If L=−d²/dx²+B on(0,π) with Dirichlet conditions and bounded self-adjoint B, then N_L(Λ)=√Λ+O(1). It cannot equal the multiset {γ_n²} of ordinates of all nontrivial zeta zeros.

## Proof

By the min–max principle, n²−||B||≤λ_n(L)≤n²+||B||. Consequently floor√(Λ−||B||)≤N_L(Λ)≤floor√(Λ+||B||), once the expressions are meaningful. Both endpoints are√Λ+O(1). If an exact multiplicity-preserving correspondence λ_n=γ_n² held for all zeros, the required count would instead be N_ζ(√Λ)=√Λ/(2π)log(√Λ/(2π))−√Λ/(2π)+O(logΛ). Dividing by√Λ, the latter grows like(logΛ)/(4π), while the Laplacian ratio tends to1. Equality is impossible. The argument uses the count of all zeros and makes no RH assumption: even off-line zeros have ordinates and contribute to that count.

## Worked example: setup

Compare a diagonal model with eigenvalues n²+c against the required target density.

## Worked example: calculation

Its firstN levels occupy an energy range of orderN². Inverting the zeta count gives roughly γ_N of orderN/logN, so γ_N² has orderN²/log²N. A constant spectral shift c cannot alter this leading discrepancy. Fitting any fixed initial list would leave the asymptotic contradiction intact.

## Worked example: conclusion

A density comparison can reject a candidate before expensive numerical tuning. A finite match is weaker than an infinite counting theorem.

## Limits

This excludes bounded perturbations on a fixed finite interval, not noncompact geometries, singular potentials, nonlocal operators or other spectral settings. Those need their own Weyl analysis and domain construction.

## Extension

Derive the candidate operator’s counting law independently, including lower-order terms, then compare it with the target zero count before discussing a trace formula.
