## Revised question retained in the annual source

Why must an RH-scale prime-counting error use li(x), rather than only its first asymptotic term?

## Status

Classical result; recovered proposal repaired

## Summary

The recovered October22 formula is mathematically incorrect for small positive ε. It omits the second main term, which is much larger than a square-root error. This is independent of any proposed spectral framework.

## Definitions

For x>2 define Li₂(x)=∫₂ˣdt/logt; the conventional li(x) differs by a constant.

For fixed0<ε<1/2, x/log²x grows faster than x^(1/2+ε).

## Result name

The missing second term

## Result statement

Li₂(x)=x/logx+x/log²x+O(x/log³x). The classical prime number theorem with its known stronger error then gives π(x)−x/logx∼x/log²x. Under RH the usual square-root-scale statement is π(x)=li(x)+O(√xlogx).

## Proof

Integration by parts gives Li₂(x)=x/logx−2/log2+∫₂ˣdt/log²t. Repeating gives∫₂ˣdt/log²t=x/log²x−2/log²2+2∫₂ˣdt/log³t. The last integral is O(x/log³x): split at√x, bound the first interval by O(√x), and use logt≥(1/2)logx on the second. The known unconditional bound π(x)−li(x)=O(xe^(−c√logx)) is o(x/log^m x) for every fixedm. Combining it with the expansion proves the asymptotic difference. Finally[x/log²x]/x^(1/2+ε)=x^(1/2−ε)/log²x→∞, so the recovered error claim cannot absorb the omitted term. The RH bound is cited as a separate classical conditional theorem, not proved by this elementary integration.

## Worked example: setup

Take ε=1/4 and compare the scale ratio at x=e^u.

## Worked example: calculation

The ratio is e^(u/4)/u², which tends to infinity. For u=100 it already equals e^25/10000, more than seven million. This compares asymptotic scales rather than approximating a finite prime count, so no numerical prime table is needed to expose the contradiction.

## Worked example: conclusion

Correct main-term bookkeeping is essential. A spectral gap cannot repair an asymptotically missing deterministic term.

## Limits

The unconditional and RH error estimates are classical inputs with their own proofs and hypotheses. The entry establishes the main-term obstruction, not RH. For number fields, constants and possible exceptional-zero effects require a separately stated theorem.

## Extension

Before proposing any improved error bound, expand its main term far enough to identify every omitted term larger than the claimed remainder, or retain the full logarithmic integral.
