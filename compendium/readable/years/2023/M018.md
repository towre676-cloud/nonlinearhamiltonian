## Revised question retained in the annual source

Does the PDE preserve a finite-dimensional invariant space on which a matrix identity can legitimately be used?

## Status

Invariant heat subspace and nonlinear no-go result proved

## Summary

A finite matrix can represent an infinite-dimensional evolution exactly on a proved invariant subspace. A truncation outside such a subspace instead produces an approximation. The distinction can be settled in examples, and a useful degree argument rules out a broad polynomial closure proposal.

## Definitions

A subspace W of observables is Koopman-invariant for a map f when p∈W implies p∘f∈W.

For the periodic heat equation u_t=u_xx, trigonometric modes are eigenfunctions of the generator ∂_xx.

For the obstruction, work with real polynomials as functions on an interval, and let f have degree d≥2.

## Result name

No finite polynomial Koopman space can contain the state coordinate

## Result statement

There is no finite-dimensional subspace W⊂R[x] that contains x and is invariant under p↦p∘f when degf=d≥2. In contrast, the heat equation exactly preserves every fixed finite span of Fourier modes.

## Proof

If W contains x and is invariant, it contains x∘f=f, then f∘f, and inductively every iterate f^(∘n). Their degrees are d^n because the nonzero leading coefficient of a composition cannot cancel. Polynomials with distinct degrees are linearly independent: in any finite linear combination the largest degree has a coefficient contributed by exactly one summand, so that coefficient must vanish, and descending induction eliminates the rest. Thus W would contain infinitely many independent vectors, a contradiction. For heat flow, ∂_xx sin(kx)=−k²sin(kx) and the same holds for cos(kx), while the constant mode has eigenvalue zero. This proves invariance of each chosen finite mode span and gives exact coefficient evolution a_k(t)=e^(−k²t)a_k(0).

## Worked example: calculation

For f(x)=2x(1−x), exact iterates have degrees 1,2,4,8,16,32,64 starting with x. No finite polynomial feature list containing x closes exactly on an interval. For Burgers-type nonlinearity u u_x, the initial mode sinx immediately generates sinx cosx=(1/2)sin2x, so the single-mode span is not invariant even though it is invariant for heat flow.

## Worked example: conclusion

Finite polynomial degree growth gives a proof of impossibility, while Fourier invariance gives a valid exact finite example. Neither conclusion rests on a numerical residual alone.

## Limits

The obstruction does not exclude nonpolynomial coordinate transformations, finite-state restrictions, approximate closures or special higher-dimensional nonlinear systems. On a finite orbit, polynomials are functions modulo additional relations and the interval argument no longer applies.

## Extension

Before applying Cayley–Hamilton to a nonlinear model, certify an invariant feature space or quantify the projection error. Investigate special model classes separately rather than assuming universal finite closure.
