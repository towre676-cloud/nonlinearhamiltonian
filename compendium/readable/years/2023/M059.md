## Revised question retained in the annual source

When does a decomposition preserve the total squared norm, and when does overlap double count information?

## Status

Classical result developed and worked

## Summary

A multiscale norm construction needs a bookkeeping theorem. The Frobenius norm behaves like a Euclidean norm on the list of matrix entries, so squared energies add over disjoint blocks. Overlapping descriptions need multiplicity corrections instead of an unqualified addition rule.

## Definitions

For a real or complex matrix A, ||A||_F²=Σᵢⱼ|aᵢⱼ|²=Tr(A* A). A block here means a subset of entry indices; blocks may have irregular shapes. A partition uses every entry exactly once.

## Result name

Partition additivity and the overlap correction

## Result statement

If entry blocks B₁,…,B_m partition the indices, then ||A||_F²=Σ_b||A|B_b||_F². If they cover entries with multiplicities cᵢⱼ, the uncorrected sum is Σᵢⱼcᵢⱼ|aᵢⱼ|².

## Proof

Expand every block norm as a sum of squared moduli and interchange two finite sums. The coefficient of a given entry is exactly the number of blocks containing it. For a partition that number is one. When every covered entry has positive multiplicity, assigning weight 1/cᵢⱼ to that entry within each occurrence restores the original energy. The argument needs no independence or probabilistic assumptions. An equivalent Hilbert-space statement uses orthogonal coordinate projections P_b and the identity ΣP_b=I.

## Worked example: setup

Let A=[[1,2],[3,4]]. Partition it by rows, then deliberately count its first row twice.

## Worked example: calculation

The complete squared Frobenius norm is 1+4+9+16=30. Row energies are 5 and 25, adding to 30. Repeating the first row gives 35, exposing five units of double counting. Dividing the two first-row occurrences by multiplicity two restores 30. By contrast, the unsquared norms √5+5 do not equal √30.

## Worked example: conclusion

Disjoint block decomposition preserves total squared energy. It does not automatically make values comparable across blocks of different cardinalities.

## Limits

When comparing differently sized systems, one must choose total energy, mean squared entry, an operator norm or a dimensionless normalization. Those choices encode different questions. Orthogonal-coordinate arguments also do not apply unchanged to arbitrary overlapping learned features.

## Extension

For nonorthogonal feature maps F_b, study frame bounds a||x||²≤Σ||F_bx||²≤b||x||² and determine whether a tight-frame normalization can preserve the intended energy.
