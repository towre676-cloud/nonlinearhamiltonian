## Summary

A concrete spectral bridge replaces the unsupported Fourier-to-automorphic-form slogan. In the smallest nontrivial sl₂ example, the Bethe vector, Hamiltonian eigenvalues, Miura potential and cancellation of the apparent singularity can all be computed exactly. A rational three-site continuation also gives a complete eight-dimensional joint eigenbasis and two additional scalar-oper certificates.

## Definitions

On each C² take h=diag(1,−1), e|↓⟩=|↑⟩, f|↑⟩=|↓⟩ and Ω=(1/2)h⊗h+e⊗f+f⊗e. Choose marked points z₁=0,z₂=1.

Let H₁=Ω/(z₁−z₂)=−Ω and H₂=Ω. For weights (1,1), one Bethe root obeys 1/w+1/(w−1)=0.

## Result name

Singlet spectral data and apparent-pole cancellation

## Result statement

The root w=1/2 yields a singlet with (H₁,H₂) eigenvalues (3/2,−3/2). Define u(x)=1/(2x)+1/(2(x−1))−1/(x−1/2). Then (∂−u)(∂+u)=∂²−V, where V=u²−u′=3/[4x²(x−1)²].

## Proof

The Bethe vector is w⁻¹|↓↑⟩+(w−1)⁻¹|↑↓⟩=2|↓↑⟩−2|↑↓⟩. Direct use of Ω gives eigenvalue −3/2, hence the Hamiltonian pair above. Near a root w, write u=−1/(x−w)+a₀+O(x−w). The double pole cancels in u²−u′, and its simple residue is −2a₀. Here a₀=(1/2)(1/w+1/(w−1))=0 precisely by the Bethe equation. Expanding at the physical points gives V=3/(4x²)+3/(4(x−1)²)+(3/2)/x−(3/2)/(x−1). Its simple residues match H₁ and H₂ in the declared normalization.

## Worked example: setup

Treat the scalar differential equation y″−V(x)y=0 on the punctured projective line.

## Worked example: calculation

The apparent point x=1/2 is absent from V. One solution is y₁=(x−1/2)/sqrt(x(x−1)). Reduction of order gives y₂=(x²−x/2+1/4)/sqrt(x(x−1)), with Wronskian one. Both are regular at x=1/2. Around x=0 or x=1 their common square-root factor changes sign, so monodromy is scalar and projectively trivial. The vacuum sector, with no root, instead has simple residues (−1/2,1/2), matching the triplet eigenvalues. For sites (0,1,8/3), the one-root equation reduces to 3w²−(22/3)w+8/3=0, with roots 2 and 4/9. Their eigenvalue triples are (−3/16,6/5,−81/80) and (25/16,−8/5,3/80). The highest vectors have one-down-spin coefficients (1/2,1,−3/2) and (9/4,−9/5,−9/20). Applying total lowering once to each gives two doublets; applying it zero through three times to the vacuum gives a quartet with eigenvalues (−11/16,1/5,39/80). All eight rational vectors are independent and satisfy all three full 8×8 Hamiltonians. For each regular root, with g=∏(x−z_i), u=g′/(2g)−1/(x−w) gives V=(3/4)Σ_i(x−z_i)^(−2)+Σ_iE_i/(x−z_i). Exact residual polynomials vanish. Since g′(w)=0, reduction of order has no logarithm: y₁=(x−w)/sqrt(g), y₂=[−g(w)+(g″(w)/2)(x−w)²+(x−w)³/2]/sqrt(g). Their Wronskian is one, and both ODE residuals vanish identically.

## Worked example: conclusion

The calculation supplies an actual rank-one instance of the Gaudin–oper relationship: eigenvalues are encoded in coefficients of a scalar differential operator. The simple finite example is classical; its explicit replay is an exposition and verification result. The three-site continuation settles this complete finite joint-spectrum example, including its scalar equations, rather than leaving it as a proposed computation.

## Limits

This is an sl₂ example, not a proof of the full Gaudin-algebra spectrum theorem, a global automorphic correspondence or arbitrary Bethe completeness. Oper terminology and Langlands duality must retain the relevant group, singularity and monodromy conditions.
