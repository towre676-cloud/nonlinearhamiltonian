## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which conjugate exponents apply, and when is the bound sharp?

## Status

Classical result developed and worked

## Summary

Conjugate exponents and equality cases turn a generic inequality into a sharp tool. The proof below also states what must be checked before applying it: measurability, the relevant integrability and the distinction between the absolute-value bound and the signed integral.

## Definitions

Let 1<p<∞ and q=p/(p−1), so 1/p+1/q=1. Norms are ||f||_p=(∫|f|^p)^(1/p) and the analogous q-norm, on one fixed measure space.

## Result name

Young and Hölder with sharp equality

## Result statement

For a,b≥0, ab≤a^p/p+b^q/q, with equality exactly when a^p=b^q. Consequently ∫|fg|≤||f||_p||g||_q. For nonzero finite norms, equality holds exactly when |f|^p/||f||_p^p=|g|^q/||g||_q^q almost everywhere.

## Proof

Fix a and minimize h(b)=b^q/q−ab+a^p/p over b≥0. Its derivative is b^(q−1)−a, with its unique minimum at b=a^(p−1), and the minimum equals zero. Normalize nonzero f,g to unit p- and q-norms, apply the scalar inequality pointwise and integrate. Equality in the integrated nonnegative difference requires pointwise equality almost everywhere, which yields the normalized-power condition. To obtain equality for |∫fg| as well, the product must also have an almost-everywhere constant phase or sign.

## Worked example: setup

Choose p=3, q=3/2, a=2 and b=4.

## Worked example: calculation

Here a^p=b^q=8, so Young is exact: ab=8=8/3+8/(3/2). For a finite counting measure, proportional normalized powers supply equally explicit Hölder equality examples. Choosing disjointly supported nonzero functions makes the product integral zero while both norms are positive, showing a strict case.

## Worked example: conclusion

The equality condition identifies when a proposed estimate is sharp and which directions are responsible for slack.

## Limits

The endpoints p=1,q=∞ require essential-supremum language and a different equality statement. If either required norm is infinite, the finite useful estimate is unavailable even when a formal product expression can be written.

## Extension

Use the equality characterization to optimize a linear functional on an L^p unit ball and compare existence and uniqueness in endpoint versus strictly convex cases.
