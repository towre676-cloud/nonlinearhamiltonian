## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which operation remains difficult after exploiting coefficient recurrences, public structure and available algebraic algorithms?

## Status

Algorithm and precision-doubling identity proved

## Summary

Formal reciprocal coefficients are determined recursively. Inversion of a known truncated unit power series is therefore an available algorithm, not an unexplained source of computational hardness.

## Definitions

Work in R[[q]] for a commutative ring R with identity. Let F=1+Σ_{n≥1}a_nqⁿ.

A unit series has an invertible constant coefficient. The normalization one avoids writing a₀⁻¹ in every formula.

A congruence modulo q^m means equality of all coefficients below degree m; no analytic convergence is required.

## Result name

A unique reciprocal and a doubling step

## Result statement

There is a unique G=Σ_{n≥0}b_nqⁿ with FG=1, where b₀=1 and b_n=−Σ_{i=1}^n a_i b_{n−i}. If FG≡1 mod q^m, then G_new=G(2−FG) satisfies FG_new≡1 mod q^(2m).

## Proof

Equating the degree-n coefficient of FG with zero isolates b_n because F has constant term one. This gives existence by recursive construction and uniqueness degree by degree. For doubling, write E=1−FG. Then 1−FG(2−FG)=(1−FG)²=E². If E has order at least m, its square has order at least 2m. The update is valid in any characteristic because it is a polynomial identity; it does not require division by two. Naive coefficient multiplication takes quadratic ring-operation count in the truncation length, while fast multiplication and doubling improve that bound when the coefficient ring admits efficient arithmetic.

## Worked example: setup

Take F=1−q−q² over Z.

## Worked example: calculation

The recurrence is b_n=b_{n−1}+b_{n−2}, with b₀=b₁=1. The inverse begins 1+q+2q²+3q³+5q⁴+8q⁵+13q⁶. The verifier checks the convolution identity exactly through degree twelve.

## Worked example: conclusion

A recipient knowing F can recover the same truncated reciprocal coefficients by a transparent deterministic calculation. A purported one-way map based solely on hiding this inversion would fail its first baseline test.

## Limits

This does not prove every cryptographic proposal involving formal series insecure. Some other specified hidden-data or inversion problem could be difficult. Bit complexity also depends on coefficient growth and cannot be read off solely from the number of ring operations.

## Extension

For a concrete proposal, identify exactly what the adversary knows and which operation is claimed hard. Compare the intended task with coefficient recurrence, Newton inversion, factorization and reconstruction from observed coefficients before making any security assertion.
