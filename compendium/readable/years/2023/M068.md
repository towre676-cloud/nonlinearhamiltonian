## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which matrix norm corresponds to the input and output errors being measured?

## Status

Classical result developed and worked

## Summary

Choosing a norm means choosing how input and output error are measured. A norm catalogue becomes useful only when attached to its induced error inequality. A two-dimensional example shows that several plausible norms report different numerical amplification.

## Definitions

The induced p-norm is ||A||_p=sup_{x≠0}||Ax||_p/||x||_p. The induced 1-norm is the largest absolute column sum, the induced infinity-norm is the largest absolute row sum, and the Frobenius norm is a Euclidean norm on entries.

## Result name

Sharp induced bounds and singular-value comparisons

## Result statement

For every vector x, ||Ax||_p≤||A||_p||x||_p. In Euclidean norm, ||A||₂ is the largest singular value, while ||A||_F² is the sum of squared singular values; hence ||A||₂≤||A||_F≤√r||A||₂ for rank r>0.

## Proof

The first inequality is the definition of the supremum. For p=1, triangle inequalities bound each output coordinate and collecting each |x_j| gives the maximum column sum; choosing a coordinate vector at a maximizing column attains it. The infinity case uses a maximizing row and matching signs. Orthogonal or unitary invariance reduces the Euclidean claims to a diagonal singular-value matrix, where the largest entry bounds their squared sum above and below. The rank-zero case has both norms zero.

## Worked example: setup

Let A=[[1,2],[0,1]].

## Worked example: calculation

Its induced 1- and infinity-norms are both three. Its Frobenius norm is √6. The eigenvalues of AᵀA=[[1,2],[2,5]] are 3±2√2, so ||A||₂=√(3+2√2)=1+√2. These are distinct measures of the same operator. A claim of amplification three is sharp for a 1-norm or infinity-norm question, but overestimates the sharp Euclidean factor.

## Worked example: conclusion

Reporting the input and output norms is part of stating the bound, not a cosmetic implementation detail.

## Limits

Condition numbers also depend on the norm and require invertibility or a specified pseudoinverse/restricted subspace. An entrywise norm cannot replace an induced one without tracking the comparison constants.

## Extension

For a chosen observation map, compute the restricted operator norm on the admissible state subspace and quantify how much a generic full-space bound overestimates the actual errors.
