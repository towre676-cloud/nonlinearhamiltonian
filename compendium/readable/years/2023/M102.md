## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which ordered four points are harmonic, and what precisely is preserved by a projective transformation?

## Status

Classical result developed and example checked

## Summary

Cross-ratio becomes a precise projective invariant once the four-point order and convention are fixed. Harmonicity is then a concrete value of that invariant, not an order-free label.

## Definitions

For distinct projective points a,b,c,d on P¹, choose nonzero representatives v_a,…,v_d and write [u,v]=det(u,v).

Use CR(a,b;c,d)=[v_a,v_c][v_b,v_d]/([v_b,v_c][v_a,v_d]). In an affine chart this is ((c−a)(d−b))/((c−b)(d−a)).

## Result name

Projective invariance and harmonic ordering

## Result statement

Every invertible 2×2 matrix preserves this ordered cross-ratio. Under permutations the possible values lie in {r,1/r,1−r,1/(1−r),r/(r−1),(r−1)/r}.

## Proof

Rescaling any representative multiplies one numerator factor and one denominator factor by the same scalar. Applying M multiplies each bracket by det M; the two determinant factors above and below cancel. Thus CR is both projectively well defined and invariant. The permutation formulas follow by swapping arguments in the affine expression. In particular r=−1 can become 2 or 1/2 after a different ordering, so a harmonic assertion must retain its convention. Homogeneous representatives make the point at infinity routine rather than an exception requiring an invented formula.

## Worked example: setup

Take (a,b;c,d)=(−1,1;0,∞) and T(z)=(2z+3)/(z+2).

## Worked example: calculation

Using v∞=(1,0) gives CR=−1. The transformation has nonzero determinant one and sends the ordered tuple to (1,5/3;3/2,2). The transformed ratio is [(1/2)(1/3)]/[(-1/6)(1)]=−1, verified with rational arithmetic.

## Worked example: conclusion

The example simultaneously checks infinity, ordering and projective invariance. A dual statement for four concurrent lines follows only after identifying their pencil with P¹; duality is not a license to replace undefined point coordinates by arbitrary line expressions.

## Limits

The four points must be distinct for the displayed nondegenerate ratio. Coinciding points require a limiting or compactified configuration-space interpretation. Over a general field, the listed six values can coincide more than they do over the real numbers.

## Extension

Cross-ratio coordinates give a concrete entry into moduli of marked projective lines: normalize three points to 0,1,∞ and the fourth is the remaining parameter. Investigate degenerations by tracking this parameter toward 0,1 or ∞.
