## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which weak-field matching fixes the dimensionless coefficient that units cannot determine?

## Status

Classical result developed and worked

## Summary

The coefficient 8π is determined by matching a conventioned relativistic field equation to Newtonian Poisson gravity. The trace reversal supplies a crucial factor one half. This is a derivation of the classical normalization, not a new gravitational law.

## Definitions

Set Λ=0 for the local weak-field limit. Use signature (−,+,+,+), x⁰=ct and R^ρ_{σμν}=∂_μΓ^ρ_{νσ}−∂_νΓ^ρ_{μσ}+Γ^ρ_{μα}Γ^α_{νσ}−Γ^ρ_{να}Γ^α_{μσ}, with R_σν=R^ρ_{σρν}. Let G_μν=κT_μν initially have an unknown κ.

## Result name

Newtonian matching yields κ=8πG_N/c⁴

## Result statement

For a static weak potential with g_00=−(1+2Φ/c²), slow matter and negligible pressure, R_00≈∇²Φ/c² and the trace-reversed source is T_00−g_00T/2≈ρc²/2. Matching ∇²Φ=4πG_Nρ therefore gives κ=8πG_N/c⁴.

## Proof

Contract the field equation in four dimensions: −R=κT. Substituting back gives R_μν=κ(T_μν−g_μνT/2). For dust at rest, T_00≈ρc², spatial diagonal stresses are negligible, and T≈−ρc²; with g_00≈−1 the displayed source becomes ρc²−ρc²/2. The weak, static connection is Γ^i_{00}≈∂_iΦ/c². In R_00, time derivatives vanish and connection products are higher order, leaving ∂_iΓ^i_{00}≈∇²Φ/c². Thus ∇²Φ=(κc⁴/2)ρ. Equating its coefficient with Poisson’s coefficient fixes κ.

## Worked example: calculation

If one incorrectly equates R_00 with κρc² without trace reversal, the inferred coupling would differ by a factor of two. If one uses time rather than ct, the connection and stress components must both be rescaled. Either mistake can produce superficially plausible but inconsistent equations.

## Worked example: conclusion

The numerical factor and powers of c are fixed together once geometric, matter and Newtonian conventions are stated.

## Limits

This leading-order derivation assumes weak fields, slow motion, small stresses and a regime in which Λ is negligible. Reversing the Riemann-tensor sign requires changing the field-equation sign convention consistently. The result does not derive Einstein’s equation uniquely from Newtonian gravity.
