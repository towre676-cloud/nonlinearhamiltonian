## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which mathematical terms differ, and what dispersion relation follows from each equation?

## Status

Classical result developed and example checked

## Summary

The mathematical comparison starts with two explicitly normalized differential operators. Their plane-wave dispersions, propagation equations and interpretation can then be compared without confusing a classical field equation with the procedure of quantization.

## Definitions

Use coordinates (t,x), speed c and □=c⁻²∂_t²−Δ. The free Klein–Gordon equation is (□+(mc/ℏ)²)φ=0.

A plane wave is exp(i(k·x−ωt)). Its wave vector k has inverse-length units, while ω has inverse-time units.

## Result name

Massive and massless plane-wave dispersions

## Result statement

Substitution gives ω²=c²|k|²+m²c⁴/ℏ². Setting m=0 gives the classical massless wave dispersion ω²=c²|k|².

## Proof

The second time derivative multiplies the wave by −ω² and the Laplacian by −|k|². The PDE therefore reduces to −ω²/c²+|k|²+(mc/ℏ)²=0. Multiplying by ℏ² and writing E=ℏω and p=ℏk gives E²=c²|p|²+m²c⁴. For m>0, differentiating the positive branch gives group velocity v_g=c²k/ω with magnitude below c; at high frequency it approaches c. These are consequences of the chosen PDE and Fourier ansatz, not additional quantum postulates.

## Worked example: setup

In units c=ℏ=1 take m=2 and spatial mode k=(3,0,0).

## Worked example: calculation

The frequencies are ω=±sqrt(13), and each wave has zero PDE residual because −13+9+4=0. The positive-branch group speed is 3/sqrt(13). On a periodic box of side L, allowed wave vectors are 2πn/L, producing a countable family of uncoupled harmonic oscillators at the classical level.

## Worked example: conclusion

Mass changes the relation between frequency and wave number. The periodic Fourier decomposition also identifies the finite-mode truncations that can be computed exactly; it does not by itself choose a Fock space or turn amplitudes into operators.

## Limits

Negative-frequency solutions do not by themselves establish a negative-probability theory. Quantization and the choice of conserved inner product require their own construction. Interactions, curved backgrounds and boundary conditions change the analysis.

## Extension

For a specified potential or curved metric, determine whether a positive self-adjoint spatial operator supplies a stable mode expansion. Retain the operator domain and boundary conditions rather than transferring the flat-space dispersion unchanged.
