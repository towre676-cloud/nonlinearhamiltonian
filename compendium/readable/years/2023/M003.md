## Revised question retained in the annual source

For f(x)=ax+b on Z_p, which fixed points exist, when are they attracting, and what finite congruence data determines the orbit?

## Status

Complete fixed-point and attraction classification for the stated family

## Summary

The affine family can be settled exactly. The hypotheses a,b∈Z_p ensure that f(x)=ax+b maps Z_p into itself and never expands p-adic distance. Attracting, neutral and translational behavior can then be distinguished without metaphor.

## Definitions

A fixed point solves f(x*)=x*.

Attraction means f^n(x)→x* in the p-adic metric for nearby initial states.

Congruence modulo p^N means two states cannot be distinguished at N-digit precision.

## Result name

Affine fixed points and orbit differences

## Result statement

If a≠1, a fixed point in Z_p exists exactly when b/(1−a)∈Z_p, equivalently b=0 or v_p(b)≥v_p(1−a), and is then unique. If a=1, b=0 makes every point fixed and b≠0 gives none. For |a|_p<1 the unique point attracts every state; for |a|_p=1 no distinct state converges to a fixed point.

## Proof

The fixed-point equation is (1−a)x=b, which gives the classification because Q_p is a field and membership in Z_p is exactly nonnegative valuation. Subtract two trajectories: f^n(x)−f^n(y)=a^n(x−y). Taking norms gives |a|_p^n|x−y|_p. This converges to zero precisely in the nonunit case, while for a unit it remains constant whenever x≠y. When |a|_p<1, 1−a is a unit, so every b supplies an integral fixed point. For a≠1, direct induction gives f^n(x)=a^n x+b(1−a^n)/(1−a). Congruent initial states remain congruent at every step, since multiplication by a∈Z_p cannot lower their agreement precision.

## Worked example: setup

Take p=7, a=7 and b=1.

## Worked example: calculation

The fixed point is −1/6∈Z_7. Starting from x_0=0 gives x_n=1+7+⋯+7^(n−1), and x_n−x*=7^n/6. Its valuation is exactly n. Thus n iterations supply n correct base-seven digits. In contrast a=8,b=1 has no fixed point in Z_7 because (1−8)x=1 would require v_7(x)=−1.

## Worked example: conclusion

The same affine notation yields global contraction in one case and a provable integral fixed-point obstruction in the other.

## Limits

Neutral maps may have rich periodic structure modulo p^N; constant separation does not mean every orbit is constant. This classification is for affine maps on Z_p, not arbitrary nonlinear p-adic dynamics.

## Extension

Classify neutral affine cycles modulo p^N, then identify which cycle properties stabilize as N grows. Use gcd divisibility as an independent finite checker.
