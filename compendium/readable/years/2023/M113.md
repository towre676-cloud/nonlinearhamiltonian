## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which couplings run with which scale, and which conclusions survive the chosen approximation and scheme?

## Status

Classical result developed and example checked

## Summary

The beta function must be specified before discussing running couplings. Simple autonomous flows can be solved exactly, and the derivative at a fixed point gives a local quantity that survives smooth invertible reparametrization of the coupling.

## Definitions

Write t=log(μ/μ₀) and dg/dt=β(g). A fixed point g* satisfies β(g*)=0.

A scheme coordinate change is a smooth locally invertible h=h(g). Its beta function is β_h(h(g))=h′(g)β(g). This is a coordinate statement for an exact flow, not a guarantee about truncated calculations. The nonzero-initial-condition formula below assumes g₀≠0; g₀=0 is the constant equilibrium.

## Result name

Flow solutions and fixed-point linearization

## Result statement

For β=bg², g(t)=g₀/(1−bg₀t). For β=−εg+bg² with ε,b>0 and g*=ε/b, g(t)=g*/[1+(g*/g₀−1)e^(εt)] wherever its denominator is nonzero. At a fixed point, dβ_h/dh=β′(g*).

## Proof

The first solution follows by differentiating 1/g. For the second, substitution or separation of variables verifies the displayed logistic-type formula. Linearization gives δġ=β′(g*)δg. Differentiating β_h=h′β with respect to h yields (h″β+h′β′)/h′. At a fixed point β=0, so the derivative equals β′. Thus the local exponent is invariant under the declared regular change of variable, although the numerical coordinate used to label the fixed point can change.

## Worked example: setup

Take ε=b=1 and g₀=1/2.

## Worked example: calculation

Then g(t)=1/(1+e^t). It approaches the Gaussian fixed point zero as μ increases and the nonzero fixed point one as μ decreases. The derivatives are β′(0)=−1 and β′(1)=1, giving the stated ultraviolet and infrared stability directions. For β=g² with g₀=1/2, the toy solution has a pole at t=2.

## Worked example: conclusion

These statements are exact for the selected differential equations. A pole in a one-loop approximation does not prove that an underlying physical theory has an actual singularity there; it marks where the approximation can no longer justify extrapolation.

## Limits

A field-theoretic beta function depends on the specified action, regulator, renormalization prescription and perturbative order. Multiple couplings require a Jacobian matrix, and marginal directions require nonlinear analysis. An arbitrary scalar cost function is not automatically an RG flow.

## Extension

For a specified two-coupling beta vector field, solve the fixed-point equations, classify its Jacobian and compare the invariant eigenvalues under an explicit analytic coordinate change. Report the truncation order with every numerical prediction.
