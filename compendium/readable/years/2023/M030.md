## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which additional structure turns the particular analytic space into an algebraic or projective one?

## Status

Curve criterion proved from Riemann–Roch

## Summary

Projectivity can be demonstrated by producing enough sections of a line bundle to separate points and tangent directions. The curve case gives a concrete replacement for a list of named geometric structures.

## Definitions

Let X be a smooth projective curve over C of genus g and L a line bundle of degree d.

A line bundle is very ample when its complete linear system gives a closed embedding into projective space.

Riemann–Roch says h⁰(L)−h⁰(K_X⊗L⁻¹)=d+1−g. A line bundle of negative degree has no nonzero section.

## Result name

Degree at least 2g+1 guarantees a projective embedding

## Result statement

If d≥2g+1, then h⁰(L)=d+1−g and L separates points and tangent vectors. Hence its complete linear system embeds X into P^(d−g).

## Proof

For any length-two subscheme Z of X, deg(K_X⊗L⁻¹(Z))=2g−2−d+2≤−1. Serre duality therefore gives H¹(L(−Z))=0. The exact sequence 0→L(−Z)→L→L|_Z→0 shows that restriction of sections to Z is onto. Taking two distinct points gives separation of their values, and taking a double point gives tangent separation. The same argument for a length-one subscheme gives base-point freeness. These are the embedding criteria for the complete linear system. The section count follows from the vanishing of H¹(L) and Riemann–Roch. This proof states exactly which positivity replaces an unsupported claim of projectivity.

## Worked example: setup

Take a genus-one curve and a line bundle of degree three.

## Worked example: calculation

The bound is met, h⁰(L)=3, and the resulting embedding is into P² with degree three: a smooth plane cubic. For g=3 and d=7, there are five sections and an embedding into P⁴. These are actual dimensions of spaces of sections, not a metaphorical richness measure.

## Worked example: conclusion

A concrete line bundle can turn an abstract curve into an explicit projective object. Algebraic descriptions and projective embeddings are nevertheless distinct notions.

## Limits

The argument begins with a smooth projective curve and does not prove that every analytic space is algebraizable. An algebraic affine line already shows that being algebraic need not mean being projective. Higher-dimensional embedding criteria require their own positivity and vanishing results.

## Extension

For a particular analytic compactification, identify a candidate line bundle, prove its positivity, obtain the required sections and show that their map is injective and immersive. State separately any algebraization theorem used to enter the algebraic category.
