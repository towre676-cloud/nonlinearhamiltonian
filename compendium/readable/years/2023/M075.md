## Summary

The original torsion comparison combined two unrelated notions of structure: set partitions and elastic deformation. Both can be developed, but a counting theorem does not determine an energy. An explicit discrete energy and continuum limit provide the missing bridge when an actual rod model is intended.

## Definitions

S(N,k) counts partitions of N labeled objects into k nonempty unlabeled subsets. A contiguous partition of an ordered chain instead cuts k−1 of its N−1 gaps. For a rod of length L, θ(x) is an angular displacement and GJ>0 is a torsional stiffness parameter.

## Result name

Distinct partition counts and a consistent torsional energy

## Result statement

Arbitrary partitions satisfy S(N,k)=kS(N−1,k)+S(N−1,k−1); contiguous k-block partitions number C(N−1,k−1). For grid spacing h=L/N, E_N=(GJ/2)Σ_{i=0}^{N−1}[(θ_{i+1}−θ_i)/h]²h equals the continuum Dirichlet energy of the piecewise-linear interpolant.

## Proof

For Stirling’s recurrence, the last label either joins one of k existing blocks or forms a singleton. For contiguous partitions, choosing the cut gaps is a bijection. For energy, the interpolant derivative is constant on each grid interval and equals the difference quotient; integrating its squared derivative gives the formula exactly. Thus strong H¹ convergence of interpolants implies energy convergence. A bounded-energy sequence with a fixed endpoint has an H¹ bound; weak lower semicontinuity gives a lower energy bound at weak limits, while interpolating smooth functions supplies elementary recovery approximations. No set-partition count is needed for this argument.

## Worked example: setup

First take N=4,k=2, then a constant total twist Θ.

## Worked example: calculation

The partition counts are seven and three respectively. For θ_i=Θi/N, every difference quotient is Θ/L and E_N=GJΘ²/(2L) for every N. For L=GJ=1 and θ(x)=x², endpoint interpolation gives E_N=2/3−1/(6N²), converging to (1/2)∫₀¹4x²dx=2/3.

## Worked example: conclusion

The continuum energy follows from a stated stiffness law and interpolation, not from taking a limit of the Stirling numbers.

## Limits

The one-dimensional quadratic energy assumes a chosen linear elastic model. Material nonlinearities, warping, varying stiffness and topology of configurations require additional specification. Algebraic torsion in a group is a different concept again.

## Extension

If partitions represent actual latent material domains, define a weighted sum over contiguous partitions with a stated domain energy and examine its continuum or large-system limit.
