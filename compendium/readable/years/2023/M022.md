## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which random-matrix ensemble matches the dependence and normalization of the matrix being analyzed?

## Status

Moment calculation proved

## Summary

A random-matrix comparison must preserve dependence, centering and normalization. A simple fourth-moment calculation shows why a sparse adjacency matrix cannot be treated as a dense Wigner matrix without qualification.

## Definitions

Let A be a simple random graph on n vertices with independent Bernoulli(p) upper-triangular entries and zero diagonal.

Set W_{ij}=(A_{ij}−p)/sqrt(np(1−p)) for i≠j and W_{ii}=0. Then E W_{ij}=0 and E W_{ij}²=1/n.

A sample covariance S=XᵀX/m is positive semidefinite and belongs to a different ensemble, even if X has independent entries.

## Result name

Exact second and fourth normalized moments

## Result statement

E[Tr(W²)/n]=(n−1)/n. Moreover E[Tr(W⁴)/n]=2(n−1)(n−2)/n²+(n−1)[1−3p(1−p)]/[n²p(1−p)]. Thus p=c/n with fixed c>0 yields fourth-moment limit 2+1/c, whereas np(1−p)→∞ removes this extra term.

## Proof

Expand the traces as sums over closed walks. For the second moment, each of n(n−1) directed edges contributes 1/n. For length four, centering kills walks containing an edge used only once. Two distinct edges traversed twice give two families of n(n−1)(n−2) walks, each with expectation 1/n². Alternating repeatedly on one edge gives n(n−1) walks with E W_{ij}⁴=[1−3p(1−p)]/[n²p(1−p)]. Divide by n to obtain the formula. This derivation depends on upper-triangular independence; it fails for matrices whose entries share hidden common factors.

## Worked example: setup

Compare dense p=1/2 with p=c/n and compare W with a covariance matrix.

## Worked example: calculation

The dense fourth-moment limit is two. The sparse fourth-moment limit retains 1/c. A covariance matrix satisfies xᵀSx=||Xx||²/m≥0, while centered symmetric adjacency matrices can have negative eigenvalues.

## Worked example: conclusion

One observed eigenvalue histogram cannot select its own null ensemble. The normalization and dependence structure must be specified before interpreting outliers.

## Limits

Two moments do not prove a complete limiting spectral law. The sparse calculation demonstrates a mismatch, not a universal law for every sparse network. No prediction about human behavior follows from these moment identities.

## Extension

Estimate the dependence and degree heterogeneity that a null ensemble must preserve. Compare centered, variance-normalized and degree-corrected matrices, and use moment or resolvent bounds matched to each construction.
