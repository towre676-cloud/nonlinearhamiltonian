## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which identities survive nonassociativity, and exactly when does regrouping an octonion product change its value?

## Status

Nonassociativity exhibited and Moufang law certified by all coefficients

## Summary

An analogy involving octonions becomes mathematics once multiplication, regrouping and the surviving identities are explicit. The real octonions are a division algebra but not an associative algebra; consequently their nonzero elements form a Moufang loop rather than a group.

## Definitions

Represent an octonion as a pair (a,b) of quaternions with product (a,b)(c,d)=(ac−conj(d)b, da+b conj(c)).

Use the quaternion convention ij=k, jk=i, ki=j. Set e1=(i,0), e2=(j,0), e3=(k,0), e4=(0,1), e5=(0,i), e6=(0,j), e7=(0,k).

The associator is [a,b,c]=(ab)c−a(bc). The Euclidean norm on R⁸ is multiplicative.

## Result name

A nonzero associator with an exact surviving identity

## Result statement

Under this convention [e1,e2,e4]=2e7. Nevertheless the associator is alternating, and the Moufang identity (ab)(ca)=a((bc)a) holds for all real octonions. In particular any two elements generate an associative subalgebra, the classical alternative-algebra theorem.

## Proof

The displayed associator follows directly from e1e2=e3, e3e4=e7, e2e4=e6 and e1e6=−e7. For an independent finite certificate of alternation, expand the trilinear associator in the basis 1,e1,…,e7. The verifier checks all 512 triples and confirms [a,b,c]+[b,a,c]=0 and [a,b,c]+[a,c,b]=0 at every coefficient. Bilinearity of multiplication then proves alternation for arbitrary real coordinates. The Moufang residual is quadratic in a and linear in b and c. It is therefore enough to check a on each basis vector and each pairwise sum of basis vectors, with b and c on basis vectors: polarization recovers all squared and mixed coefficients. All 2304 evaluations vanish exactly. This finite coefficient certificate verifies the full identity, not merely a random sample.

## Worked example: setup

Regroup e1,e2,e4 and compare the two results.

## Worked example: calculation

The left grouping gives (e1e2)e4=e7 and the right gives e1(e2e4)=−e7. Their difference has norm two. If the third factor instead belongs to the associative quaternion algebra generated by e1,e2, regrouping makes no difference.

## Worked example: conclusion

The example demonstrates both a genuine failure and a precise replacement law. No philosophical interpretation is needed for either statement.

## Limits

Other octonion tables may change basis signs and the displayed associator sign. The convention must travel with every numerical example. Alternativity does not authorize arbitrary reassociation of three unrelated factors.
