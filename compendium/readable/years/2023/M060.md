## Revised question retained in the annual source

Do the weights represent probabilities, frequencies, importance or amplitudes?

## Status

Classical result developed and worked

## Summary

The distinction between probability weights and amplitude weights can be settled before any elaborate multiscale model is built. The former defines an expected square; the latter first attenuates the signal and then squares it. They agree only under special circumstances.

## Definitions

Let p_i≥0 with Σp_i=1 and let a_i be real or complex values. Expected square is E|A|²=Σp_i|a_i|². Entrywise amplitude weighting by p gives ||p⊙a||₂²=Σp_i²|a_i|².

## Result name

Probabilistic weighting is implemented by square-root amplitudes

## Result statement

The probability-weighted squared magnitude equals ||diag(√p_i)a||₂². Weighting the amplitudes by p_i instead yields a different quadratic form. Since 0≤p_i≤1, the second is no larger than the first.

## Proof

Expand the Euclidean norm after applying the diagonal matrix. Each squared coefficient is (√p_i)²=p_i. The ordinary amplitude weight p_i is squared by the norm, producing p_i². The inequality follows term by term from p_i²≤p_i. Equality requires p_i(1−p_i)|a_i|²=0 for every i: nonzero signals can occur only at weights zero or one, apart from degenerate cancellations that are impossible in this nonnegative sum.

## Worked example: setup

Use values a=(2,4) and probabilities p=(1/4,3/4).

## Worked example: calculation

Expected square is (1/4)4+(3/4)16=13. Squared probability-amplitude weighting gives (1/16)4+(9/16)16=37/4=9.25. The weighted mean is 7/2, so the variance is 13−(7/2)²=3/4. None of these three quantities is a substitute for the others.

## Worked example: conclusion

A correct implementation starts by deciding whether the data describe sampling frequencies, deliberate attenuation or an averaging measure. The square-root representation then supplies a precise matrix formula.

## Limits

If some weights vanish, the resulting quadratic form is only a seminorm on the full coordinate space. Importance weights need not sum to one, while signed weights need not produce a positive quadratic form at all.

## Extension

For a general positive measure μ, formulate the same distinction as an L²(μ) norm versus a weighted amplitude map, and establish invariance under a change of sampling representation.
