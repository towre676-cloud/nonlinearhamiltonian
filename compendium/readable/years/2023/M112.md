## Summary

A zero-dimensional quartic model permits an exact derivation of propagator contractions, vacuum normalization, connected coefficients and a rigorous asymptotic remainder. It supplies a fully checkable model of perturbative logic without claiming to construct a continuum field theory.

## Definitions

Let X be a real standard Gaussian and define Z(g)=E[exp(−gX⁴/24)] for g≥0. The normalized two-point function is G₂(g)=E[X²exp(−gX⁴/24)]/Z(g).

Gaussian moments are E[X^(2r)]=(2r−1)!! and odd moments vanish. Wick pairings count those double factorials.

## Result name

Quartic coefficients and a controlled remainder

## Result statement

Z(g)=1−g/8+35g²/384+O(g³), log Z(g)=−g/8+g²/12+O(g³), and G₂(g)=1−g/2+2g²/3+O(g³) as g↓0.

## Proof

Expand the interaction exponential to a fixed order and integrate each Gaussian moment. The nth coefficient of Z is a_n=(−1)^n(4n−1)!!/(24^n n!). Multiplying and dividing the resulting formal series gives the displayed connected and normalized coefficients. For g≥0, Taylor’s integral remainder for exp(−y) has magnitude at most y^(N+1)/(N+1)!; taking y=gX⁴/24 and integrating bounds the partition-function remainder by the next absolute coefficient times g^(N+1). This proves a finite-order asymptotic statement without exchanging an infinite series and the integral.

## Worked example: setup

Calculate through second order using integer Gaussian moments.

## Worked example: calculation

E[X⁴]=3, E[X⁸]=105 and E[X¹⁰]=945. Thus the numerator of G₂ is 1−5g/8+105g²/128+O(g³); division by Z gives 1−g/2+2g²/3. Meanwhile |a_(n+1)/a_n|=(4n+3)(4n+1)/(24(n+1)), which grows without bound.

## Worked example: conclusion

The perturbative series has zero radius of convergence although the original integral exists for every g≥0. Correct finite-order coefficients and an explicit asymptotic bound are therefore stronger and more honest deliverables than a claim that the infinite diagram sum converges.

## Limits

This toy integral has no spacetime ultraviolet divergences, locality or scattering theory. It does not replace renormalization of a continuum model. Its value is the exact separation of algebraic combinatorics, normalization and asymptotic control.

## Extension

Add several Gaussian variables with a stated covariance and derive connected cumulants. For a continuum problem, introduce a cutoff and identify which bounds survive its removal rather than assuming the finite-dimensional argument is uniform.
