## Revised question retained in the annual source

What is the exact optimizer, when is it unique, and how do additional constraints change it?

## Status

Classical result developed and worked

## Summary

The positive-definite inverse formula is only one branch of the quadratic-program problem. The singular case admits a clean complete classification, and inequality constraints can change the optimizer even when the matrix is nonsingular. These are mathematical statements about quadratic forms, not practical allocation recommendations.

## Definitions

Let Σ be a symmetric positive semidefinite real matrix and c≠0. Minimize wᵀΣw subject to cᵀw=1. Write Σ⁺ for its Moore–Penrose inverse and kerΣ for its nullspace.

## Result name

Complete equality-constrained PSD classification

## Result statement

If some z∈kerΣ has cᵀz≠0, the minimum is zero and all minimizers are the feasible null vectors. Otherwise c∈rangeΣ, q=cᵀΣ⁺c>0, the minimum is 1/q, and all minimizers are Σ⁺c/q+z with z∈kerΣ.

## Proof

In the first case, z/(cᵀz) is feasible and has zero objective; positive semidefiniteness rules out a lower value. In the second case, c is orthogonal to the nullspace and hence lies in the range. Set w₀=Σ⁺c/q. It is feasible and Σw₀=c/q. For any feasible w=w₀+h, cᵀh=0, so the cross term 2hᵀΣw₀ vanishes and the objective is 1/q+hᵀΣh. Equality holds exactly when h∈kerΣ. This also proves uniqueness precisely when the feasible tangent space contains no nonzero null vector.

## Worked example: setup

Compare a diagonal matrix, a rank-one matrix, and a nonnegative constraint.

## Worked example: calculation

For Σ=diag(1,4), c=(1,1), w*=(4/5,1/5) and the minimum is 4/5. For Σ=11ᵀ, every feasible w has objective one. For Σ=[[1,2],[2,9]], the unrestricted optimizer is (7/6,−1/6) with value 5/6; imposing w≥0 gives (1,0) with value one. Its KKT multiplier for the second coordinate is positive.

## Worked example: conclusion

Nullspaces, budgets and inequality constraints must be handled together; merely replacing an inverse by a pseudoinverse does not cover every singular case.

## Limits

If Σ is indefinite the convex proof fails and the objective may be unbounded below along feasible directions. Additional bounds or penalties define different programs.

## Extension

For rational inputs, produce exact KKT and nullspace certificates, then study the parameter loci where the active set or nullspace changes.
