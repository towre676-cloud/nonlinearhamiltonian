## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which infinite sequence of distances has a finite sum, and what time parametrization permits the endpoint to be reached?

## Status

Convergence and endpoint behavior proved

## Summary

Infinitely many subdivisions do not determine elapsed time. Once both the distance increments and the time increments are stated, the mathematical ambiguity disappears: an infinite event sequence can accumulate at a finite time, or it can continue for an infinite duration.

## Definitions

Let distance increments be d_n=2^(−n), with milestones x_N=Σ_(n=1)^N d_n.

Let positive time increments be τ_n and milestone times t_N=Σ_(n=1)^N τ_n.

A finite accumulation time exists exactly when the positive time series converges.

## Result name

Finite distance does not fix finite elapsed time

## Result statement

The distances sum to one. Choosing τ_n=2^(−n) reaches milestones at t_N=1−2^(−N) and admits the continuous extension x(1)=1. Choosing τ_n=1/n instead takes infinite total time while approaching the same spatial limit.

## Proof

For the geometric sum, multiply S_N=1/2+⋯+2^(−N) by two and subtract to obtain S_N=1−2^(−N). Given ε>0, choose N>log₂(1/ε); then 0<1−S_N<ε, proving convergence. With equal distance and time increments, the piecewise linear motion is simply x(t)=t on [0,1), so continuity supplies the endpoint x(1)=1. In the harmonic-time model, the subsequence of times diverges because each block from 2^k+1 through 2^(k+1) contributes at least 1/2. A continuous piecewise linear interpolation still approaches spatial position one, but only as t→∞. The number of labeled milestones is identical in both descriptions; its cardinality does not decide the total time.

## Worked example: setup

Compare the first N milestones in the two parametrizations.

## Worked example: calculation

Both have spatial coordinate 1−2^(−N). In the first model the remaining time is 2^(−N). In the second, any finite milestone leaves an infinite tail Σ_(n>N)1/n. Their different conclusions follow from different time assignments, not from a contradiction in summing the same distances.

## Worked example: conclusion

The revised question separates convergence, continuity and parametrization, each of which can be proved with elementary estimates.

## Limits

A mathematical parametrization does not assert that a physical device can execute an unbounded rate of discrete operations. The continuous trajectory requires no such discrete mechanism; a physical implementation would add its own constraints.

## Extension

Study rectifiable curves with infinitely many segments and ask separately whether a chosen parametrization has bounded speed, finite length and a continuous endpoint.
