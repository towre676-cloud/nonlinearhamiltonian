## Revised question retained in the annual source

How does completing Q in the p-adic norm change convergence and numerical precision, and which computations genuinely benefit?

## Status

Simple-root lifting proved and calculated

## Summary

A useful p-adic computation is a compatible sequence of exact congruences with a precision guarantee. The essential advantage in this example is that a simple solution modulo p determines one branch at every higher precision; decimal proximity has no role in the argument.

## Definitions

v_p(x) is the exponent of p in a nonzero rational x, and |0|_p=0.

An approximation x_n modulo p^n certifies a p-adic ball x_n+p^nZ_p.

A simple root modulo p satisfies f(x_1)≡0 and f′(x_1) not≡0 modulo p.

## Result name

Unique next digit in a Hensel lift

## Result statement

For f∈Z_p[x], a simple root x_n modulo p^n has exactly one lift x_n+t p^n modulo p^(n+1), with t∈{0,…,p−1}. The compatible lifts converge to a unique root in x_1+pZ_p.

## Proof

Taylor expansion gives f(x_n+t p^n)≡f(x_n)+t p^n f′(x_n) modulo p^(n+1); all terms of degree at least two are divisible by p^(2n), hence by p^(n+1). Dividing by p^n reduces the lift equation to f(x_n)/p^n+t f′(x_n)≡0 modulo p. The derivative is a unit, so this linear equation has one digit t. Repeating constructs a compatible inverse-limit element. Completeness gives a limit α; polynomial continuity gives f(α)=0. If two roots share the residue x_1, the same unique-digit argument forces agreement modulo every p^n and therefore equality. A finite calculation consequently certifies a ball containing the unique branch, not an approximate real root.

## Worked example: setup

Lift x²−2 from the root 3 modulo 7.

## Worked example: calculation

At precision 7², write x=3+7t. Dividing f(x) by 7 gives 1+6t≡0 modulo 7, hence t=1 and x=10 modulo 49. Next x=10+49t gives 2+6t≡0, hence t=2 and x=108 modulo 343. Directly, 108²−2=34·343. The verifier continues the uniquely selected digits through precision 7^6.

## Worked example: conclusion

The result is a root α∈Z_7 with α≡108 modulo 343, with a proof that no other root in 3+7Z_7 has those initial conditions.

## Limits

If f′ vanishes modulo p, uniqueness and existence require a different argument; the simple-root calculation cannot be recycled unchanged. This example establishes an arithmetic algorithm, not a general computational speedup over real methods.

## Extension

Develop Newton lifting with precision doubling and an independent residual valuation; compare digit lifting and Newton lifting on the same branch.
