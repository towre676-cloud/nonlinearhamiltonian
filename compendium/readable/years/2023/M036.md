## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which Coxeter system and pair of elements define the polynomial, and what theorem links it to the proposed application?

## Status

Classical recurrence implemented exactly

## Summary

Coxeter data and normalization determine the polynomial. The smallest useful nonconstant example supplies an actual computation to replace claims that KL polynomials are universal complexity scores.

## Definitions

Let W=S₄ with simple generators s_i swapping adjacent positions and length ℓ(w) equal to the inversion count.

Use the Hecke convention (T_s−q)(T_s+1)=0 and the usual polynomials P_{u,v}(q), with P_{u,u}=1, P_{u,v}=0 unless u≤v in Bruhat order, and degree at most (ℓ(v)−ℓ(u)−1)/2 for u<v.

For permutations, u≤v exactly when the sorted initial k entries of u are componentwise no larger than those of v for every k.

## Result name

Two singular Schubert examples have P_{e,v}=1+q

## Result statement

The exact right-descent recurrence gives P_{e,3412}(q)=1+q and P_{e,4231}(q)=1+q. In contrast P_{u,4321}=1 for every u∈S₄ in the same normalization.

## Proof

Choose a right descent s of v and let c be one if us<u and zero otherwise. The recurrence is P_{u,v}=q^(1−c)P_{us,vs}+q^cP_{u,vs}−Σ_{u≤z<vs,zs<z} μ(z,vs)q^{(ℓ(v)−ℓ(z))/2}P_{u,z}. Here μ(z,vs) is the coefficient of q^{(ℓ(vs)−ℓ(z)−1)/2}, and is zero when that exponent is not an integer. Every polynomial on the right has a strictly shorter upper permutation, so the recurrence terminates. The verifier implements integer polynomial arithmetic, generates permutations, tests Bruhat order from the prefix criterion and evaluates the displayed examples. It also checks constant terms, degree bounds and coefficient nonnegativity for every comparable pair in S₄. These consistency checks support the implementation but do not replace the mathematical theorem defining the recurrence.

## Worked example: setup

Compute the interval from the identity to 3412, whose length is four.

## Worked example: calculation

The allowed degree is at most one and the resulting coefficient tuple is (1,1). The identity-to-4231 calculation agrees. The full S₄ pass tests 213 comparable pairs and the longest-element smooth case.

## Worked example: conclusion

The output is a polynomial indexed by a specific ordered pair in a specified Coxeter group. Its geometric meaning concerns local intersection-cohomology data under the relevant theorem, not generic algorithmic difficulty.

## Limits

The finite computation proves no unprovided relation to error-correcting codes, traveling-salesperson optimization or cryptographic hardness. Different Hecke conventions can reparameterize the same canonical data.

## Extension

State an explicit map from a proposed application into Bruhat intervals and prove that the statistic of interest is preserved. Without that map, keep the KL calculation as representation-theoretic mathematics in its own right.
