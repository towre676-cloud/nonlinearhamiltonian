## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which Hardy space is meant, and what bounded pairing does BMO provide on that space?

## Status

Classical estimate proved; duality theorem identified

## Summary

The useful connection is a bounded pairing between a cancellation-sensitive signal space and a mean-oscillation space. This supplies an actual inequality; it does not make arbitrary behavior into a Hardy-space object.

## Definitions

A real H¹ atom a is supported in a finite interval I, satisfies ∫a=0 and ||a||∞≤1/|I|. Higher dimensions use cubes or balls.

The BMO seminorm is sup_I |I|⁻¹∫_I|b−b_I|, where b_I is the interval average. Constants have seminorm zero.

The atomic H¹ norm is the infimum of Σ|λ_j| over convergent atomic decompositions f=Σλ_j a_j. This is not the Sobolev H¹ norm.

## Result name

Cancellation converts mean oscillation into a bounded functional

## Result statement

For an atom a and b∈BMO, |∫ab|≤||b||_BMO. Consequently |⟨f,b⟩|≤||f||_H¹,atomic ||b||_BMO. The converse identification of every bounded H¹ functional with BMO modulo constants is the classical duality theorem, not a conclusion of the finite example alone.

## Proof

Subtract the constant b_I inside the pairing: ∫ab=∫_I a(b−b_I), because ∫a=0. The atom bound gives |∫ab|≤|I|⁻¹∫_I|b−b_I|≤||b||_BMO. For a finite atomic sum, apply the triangle inequality and sum the coefficient magnitudes. Completion gives the bounded functional on the atomic space. The quotient by constants is forced by cancellation: adding c to b leaves every atom pairing unchanged. The difficult converse is a representation theorem and requires the established Hardy-space theory; no finite rank calculation can replace it.

## Worked example: setup

Take I=[−1,1], a(x)=−1/2 on (−1,0), a(x)=1/2 on (0,1), and zero outside. Let b(x)=sign(x).

## Worked example: calculation

The atom has zero integral and supremum 1/2. For any interval containing positive fraction t and negative fraction 1−t, the mean oscillation of sign is 4t(1−t)≤1. Symmetric intervals achieve 1. The pairing is ∫ab=1, so the atomic estimate is sharp for this normalization.

## Worked example: conclusion

A normalization, a cancellation condition and a sharp example replace the earlier loose invocation of two named function spaces.

## Limits

Pairings of general distributions require the continuous extension, not an unjustified pointwise integral. Graph analogues need their own measure, Laplacian and atomic definitions; the Euclidean theorem is not automatically inherited.

## Extension

Define graph atoms with respect to an explicit reversible measure, then ask whether the chosen graph-BMO space is the dual of the chosen graph Hardy space. Proving just the atom bound is a useful first stage, but it is not full duality.
