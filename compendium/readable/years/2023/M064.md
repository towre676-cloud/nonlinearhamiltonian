## Revised question retained in the annual source

What initial condition is intended, what are the units of t, and what time solves the stated equation exactly?

## Status

Classical result developed and worked

## Summary

The original numerical claim can be resolved entirely within its assumed exponential model. The corrected answer is a model time, not a prediction of a real population. The factor of one half changes the initial condition and must not be silently discarded.

## Definitions

For discrete-time growth, P(t)=P₀(1+r)^t with r>−1; allowing real t is an interpolation of that formula. A continuous exponential with the same unit-step multiplier has rate λ=log(1+r), not r exactly.

## Result name

Exact target time and rate sensitivity

## Result statement

For P(t)=154000(1.0005)^t/2, the starting value is 77000. The time to P*=10¹² is t*=log(10¹²/77000)/log(1.0005)=32767.109878086… in the specified time units. If only integer times are allowed, the first crossing is 32768.

## Proof

Take logarithms of the positive equation P*=77000(1.0005)^t. Strict monotonicity of the exponential gives a unique real solution, and integer rounding follows from the same monotonicity. For a general multiplier 1+r>1, differentiation yields ∂t*/∂r=−log(P*/P₀)/[(1+r)log(1+r)²]. This makes the long extrapolation highly sensitive to the assumed rate. Replacing log(1+r) by r is only a small-r approximation whose cumulative error should be reported.

## Worked example: setup

Compare the stated discrete interpolation with a continuous model using λ=0.0005.

## Worked example: calculation

The latter target time is log(10¹²/77000)/0.0005≈32758.92083, about 8.18905 units earlier. Substituting t*=32767.109878086 back into the original formula recovers 10¹² to the working floating-point precision. The previously reported value near 11788 does not satisfy the equation.

## Worked example: conclusion

Correct arithmetic settles a question about a formula; it does not supply evidence that the formula remains valid over tens of thousands of units.

## Limits

The model assumes a fixed rate and no carrying capacity, migration, stochasticity or resource constraint. Any logistic replacement needs a stated carrying capacity larger than the target, otherwise the target is never reached in finite time.

## Extension
