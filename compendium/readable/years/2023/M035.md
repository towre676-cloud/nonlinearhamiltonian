## Revised question retained in the annual source

What is being counted by the selected partition statistic or representation dimension?

## Status

Small instance independently counted

## Summary

The shape of a Young diagram is a combinatorial input, and its statistic must be declared. Counting standard tableaux and evaluating a two-parameter symmetric polynomial are different tasks.

## Definitions

A partition λ of n is a weakly decreasing list of row lengths summing to n.

A standard Young tableau fills its cells with 1,…,n, increasing along every row and column.

The hook length of a cell is one plus the number of cells to its right and below. The classical hook-length theorem gives f^λ=n!/Π_c h(c).

## Result name

The corner-removal recurrence gives a separate exact count

## Result statement

Let f(∅)=1. Then f^λ=Σ_{c removable corner} f^(λ∖c). For λ=(3,2), this count is five and matches the hook-length formula.

## Proof

In any standard tableau, its largest entry n cannot have a cell to its right or below, so it occupies a removable corner. Removing it gives a unique standard tableau of the smaller shape. Conversely, insert n into any chosen removable corner of any valid smaller tableau. These inverse operations prove the recurrence without using the hook-length formula. For (3,2), the two smaller shapes are (2,2) and (3,1), with counts two and three; hence five. The hook lengths are 4,3,1,2,1 and their product is 24, so the independent closed formula gives 5!/24=5. The general hook-length theorem is quoted classical background; the recurrence alone is a full independent proof of this finite count.

## Worked example: setup

Count the standard tableaux of shape (3,2) by both methods.

## Worked example: calculation

The verifier recursively removes corners with memoization, computes each hook directly from the diagram and checks that both answers are five. It does not infer the count from a floating approximation or from a representation-dimension claim.

## Worked example: conclusion

The integer five has a definite interpretation: it counts exactly these tableaux and, by the relevant representation theorem, is the dimension of the corresponding irreducible S₅ module over characteristic zero.

## Limits

Semistandard tableaux, Schur-function evaluations, modular representations and Macdonald-polynomial specializations require different weights or hypotheses. The same diagram alone does not make their answers equal.

## Extension

Compute a weighted generating polynomial on the same shape, declare the statistic being weighted and compare its specialization at one with the unweighted count. Then separate a proved representation-theoretic interpretation from a conjectural application.
