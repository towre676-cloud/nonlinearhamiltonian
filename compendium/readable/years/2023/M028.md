## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which compactification preserves which properties, and where do choices enter?

## Status

Curve case constructed; higher-dimensional distinction stated

## Summary

In dimension one the basic boundary operation is tangible: a punctured disk acquires its missing point. Higher-dimensional toroidal compactification adds combinatorial choices that this curve example does not see.

## Definitions

A cusp of width N has local translation τ↦τ+N. The invariant coordinate is q_N=exp(2πiτ/N).

For Imτ sufficiently large, the cusp quotient is a punctured disk 0<|q_N|<ε. Adding q_N=0 gives a smooth disk.

For an arithmetic quotient of a Hermitian symmetric domain, the Baily–Borel construction supplies a canonical projective completion. Toroidal constructions use admissible rational cone decompositions near boundary components.

## Result name

The rank-one cusp fills smoothly, but a fan can create or resolve toric singularities

## Result statement

The smooth compactification of a modular curve fills each cusp with one point; the normal curve completion is unique. In higher dimensions, a toroidal boundary chart is smooth only when its relevant cones are regular in the chosen lattice. Projectivity requires a compatible support-function condition rather than following from any arbitrary subdivision.

## Proof

The q_N coordinate is unchanged by τ↦τ+N and maps a horizontal fundamental strip at large imaginary height bijectively to a punctured disk. The puncture can be filled by the standard complex coordinate. In one complex dimension normality already implies smoothness, which explains why the minimal/toroidal distinction is muted for curves. To see a genuinely higher-dimensional local issue, take a two-dimensional cone generated by (1,0),(1,2). Its primitive generators have determinant two, so the affine toric chart is singular. Inserting the ray (1,1) gives two determinant-one cones and hence smooth charts. This is a local toric illustration, not an assertion that this fan is the fan of a particular unstated arithmetic quotient.

## Worked example: setup

For Y(3), fill its four cusps; separately perform the determinant-two cone subdivision.

## Worked example: calculation

The curve becomes X(3), a smooth projective genus-zero curve. The fan computation changes a singular affine chart into a smooth toric modification. The two calculations demonstrate two distinct roles for boundary construction.

## Worked example: conclusion

Canonical completion, smoothness and projectivity must be checked individually; they are not synonyms for making a space richer.

## Limits

The full Baily–Borel and toroidal existence theorems are cited classical results. The local calculations do not prove their general gluing, arithmetic descent or projectivity statements.

## Extension

Specify a higher-dimensional locally symmetric variety and an admissible fan. Compute stabilizers, lattice regularity and support functions, then compare the resulting boundary strata and contraction to the minimal compactification.
