## Revised question retained in the annual source

How do index range, symmetry, differential identities and gauge freedom affect the equation count?

## Status

Classical result developed and worked

## Summary

There are several counts in general relativity, and conflating them produces incorrect conclusions. Matrix entries, independent tensor components, differential identities, initial-data constraints and physical propagating modes belong to different stages of the analysis.

## Definitions

Use indices μ,ν∈{0,1,2,3}. A symmetric covariant tensor satisfies S_μν=S_νμ. The Einstein tensor obeys four contracted Bianchi identities. In a 3+1 formulation, the spatial metric and its conjugate momentum have six components each.

## Result name

Component count and local vacuum degree-of-freedom count

## Result statement

A symmetric four-by-four tensor has ten independent components: four diagonal and six off-diagonal. The four contracted Bianchi identities are differential identities, not four duplicate entries. For regular vacuum general relativity, four first-class constraints acting on twelve spatial canonical variables leave four physical phase-space variables, or two configuration polarizations per spatial point.

## Proof

For an n-by-n symmetric matrix there are n diagonal entries and n(n−1)/2 unordered off-diagonal pairs, giving n(n+1)/2. At n=4 this is ten, although the full display contains sixteen slots. The Bianchi relations differentiate combinations of field-equation components; they do not identify any selected four slots algebraically. In the canonical count, a first-class constraint imposes one phase-space condition and generates one gauge direction. Four such constraints therefore remove eight of the twelve spatial canonical variables. Dividing the remaining four phase-space variables into coordinate-momentum pairs gives two local propagating degrees of freedom.

## Worked example: calculation

There is no S_44 in the chosen zero-through-three convention. A one-through-four convention may use it, but mixing the two is invalid. In a linearized plane-wave gauge, the remaining transverse-traceless amplitudes can be represented by h_+ and h_×, illustrating rather than replacing the constrained Hamiltonian count.

## Worked example: conclusion

The repaired statement retains all ten independent equations while explaining their differential dependence and gauge structure.

## Limits

Boundary conditions, global moduli, matter fields and singular constraint surfaces can affect global degrees of freedom. Subtracting four identities from ten components is not on its own a derivation of two propagating modes.
