## Revised question retained in the annual source

Does the proposed reduced evolution preserve trace, positivity and complete positivity, and under what approximation?

## Status

Classical result developed and worked

## Summary

An open-system equation must preserve the convex set of density operators, including when an auxiliary system is attached. An exactly solvable qubit channel provides a stronger check than inspection of trace alone. Its Kraus form supplies complete positivity directly.

## Definitions

A density operator is a positive semidefinite matrix ρ with trace one. A map is completely positive when its tensor product with the identity map on any finite auxiliary system preserves positivity. Set ℏ=1.

## Result name

Exact amplitude-damping semigroup

## Result statement

For H=0 and L=√γ |0⟩⟨1| with γ≥0, the Lindblad equation sends ρ=[[a,b],[b*,d]] to [[a+(1−q)d,√q b],[√q b*,qd]], where q=e^(−γt), t≥0. This defines a trace-preserving completely positive semigroup.

## Proof

Direct matrix multiplication in LρL†−{L†L,ρ}/2 gives ḋ=−γd, ḃ=−γb/2 and ȧ=γd, which solve to the displayed formula. Define K₀=diag(1,√q) and K₁=√(1−q)|0⟩⟨1|. Their adjoint squares sum to I, and ΣK_jρK_j† reproduces the solution. Tensoring each K_j with an auxiliary identity shows complete positivity. Since q(t+s)=q(t)q(s), composition satisfies the semigroup law.

## Worked example: setup

Start from |+⟩⟨+|, so a=d=b=1/2.

## Worked example: calculation

At q=1/4 the output is [[7/8,1/4],[1/4,1/8]], with trace one and determinant 3/64>0. More generally detρ(t)=q detρ(0)+q(1−q)d²≥0, giving an additional two-by-two positivity check. As t tends to infinity the state approaches |0⟩⟨0|.

## Worked example: conclusion

The exact solution verifies what the mathematical generator does. It does not establish that this generator describes any proposed physical mechanism.

## Limits

A reduced microscopic model can require weak-coupling, coarse-graining or Markov assumptions before it has this form. Trace preservation alone is weaker than positivity, and positivity alone is weaker than complete positivity. Negative-time inversion is not a physical damping channel.

## Extension

Add a specified Hamiltonian or dephasing operator and test whether an exact solution, a spectral decomposition of the generator, or a controlled splitting error is available.
