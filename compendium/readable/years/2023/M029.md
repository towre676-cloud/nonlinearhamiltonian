## Revised question retained in the annual source

For which group, level and compactification is the canonical divisor big, and what boundary corrections enter?

## Status

Curve family calculated; higher-dimensional problem bounded

## Summary

The canonical divisor of a compactification and the log-canonical divisor of its punctured interior answer different questions. Principal modular curves make the difference explicit without appealing to vague increases in structure.

## Definitions

For a smooth projective curve X of genus g, deg K_X=2g−2. It is of general type exactly when g≥2.

For a reduced divisor D of c punctures, deg(K_X+D)=2g−2+c. The open curve is of log-general type exactly when this is positive.

For N≥3, the effective principal congruence group has index μ_N=|SL₂(Z/NZ)|/2, all cusp widths N, and c_N=μ_N/N. There are no elliptic points.

## Result name

A smooth hyperbolic arithmetic interior need not have a general-type compactification

## Result statement

For X(3), μ=12, c=4 and g=0. Thus deg K=−2 but deg(K+D)=2. For X(7), μ=168, c=24 and g=3, so deg K=4 and deg(K+D)=28. Both interiors are log-general type, but only the latter compact curve is general type.

## Proof

A torsion-free index-μ quotient of the upper half-plane has hyperbolic area μπ/3, because PSL₂(Z) has area π/3. Gauss–Bonnet on the surface with c cusps gives area 2π(2g−2+c), so 2g−2+c=μ/6. Since every cusp has width N, the cusp-width sum gives c=μ/N. These equations calculate g. On a compact curve, a divisor is big exactly when its degree is positive: Riemann–Roch makes its large multiples grow linearly with positive leading coefficient, whereas nonpositive degree cannot have that growth. Applying this to K and K+D proves the distinction. The verifier directly counts determinant-one matrices modulo N for N=3,…,7 and recovers genera 0,0,0,1,3.

## Worked example: setup

Use levels three and seven as contrasting cases and level six as a boundary case.

## Worked example: calculation

At N=6 the effective index is 72 and there are twelve cusps, giving genus one. Its canonical degree is zero while its log-canonical degree is twelve. It lies between the genus-zero and general-type compact examples.

## Worked example: conclusion

A precise level-dependent calculation replaces the unsupported inference that smoothness or hyperbolicity already implies a big canonical divisor.

## Limits

The genus formula uses the classical torsion-free modular-curve geometry. It is not a proof of Tai-type higher-dimensional general-type theorems. Ramification, boundary discrepancy and compactification choice require explicit treatment outside this curve setting.

## Extension

For a specified higher-dimensional congruence quotient, compute a canonical-divisor formula, identify boundary and ramification contributions and prove positivity at declared levels. Keep ordinary and logarithmic canonical divisors in separate columns.
