## Revised question retained in the annual source

What interaction terms must a two-soliton solution contain, and when is the resulting tau-function nonsingular?

## Status

Tau-function interaction and phase shifts derived

## Summary

The interaction term can be determined rather than guessed. A positive tau-function supplies a nonsingular exact solution and gives a clean explanation of why the answer is not the sum of two single waves.

## Definitions

Take k1>k2>0, η_i=k_i x−k_i³t+δ_i and A=((k1−k2)/(k1+k2))².

Set τ=1+e^η1+e^η2+A e^(η1+η2), and u=2∂_x²logτ.

For exponentials, a Hirota bilinear operator acts by parameter differences; write P(k,ω)=kω+k⁴.

## Result name

Interaction coefficient and asymptotic shifts

## Result statement

This τ obeys (D_xD_t+D_x⁴)τ·τ=0 and u solves u_t+6uu_x+u_xxx=0. The fast wave’s spatial shift is −log(A)/k1>0 and the slow wave’s is log(A)/k2<0. The profile mass is 2(k1+k2).

## Proof

Each one-wave term satisfies P(k_i,−k_i³)=0. The only new mixed exponential coefficient is proportional to A P(k1+k2,−k1³−k2³)+P(k1−k2,−k1³+k2³). These two polynomial values are 3k1k2(k1+k2)² and −3k1k2(k1−k2)², so their cancellation forces the stated A. Other coefficients reduce to the one-wave dispersion equations. Dividing the bilinear identity by τ² gives (logτ)_xt+(logτ)_xxxx+6((logτ)_xx)²=0; differentiating in x yields KdV for u. Because all four tau coefficients are positive, τ>0 for real x,t, eliminating zeros and singularities. In the fast moving frame, the effective one-wave factor changes from 1+e^η1 to 1+A e^η1 between early and late times, giving the fast shift. In the slow frame the factor changes in the reverse order, giving the slow shift. Finally ∫u dx=2[(logτ)_x]_(−∞)^∞=2(k1+k2).

## Limits

This is the classical KdV two-soliton family, also a y-independent KP solution. It is not a construction of every KP interaction. Coincident or zero k values require a separate limiting treatment.
