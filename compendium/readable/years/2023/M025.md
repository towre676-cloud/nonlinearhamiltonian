## Revised question retained in the annual source

What are the players, strategy sets, information and payoff functions, and which perturbation changes the equilibrium set?

## Status

Finite equilibria and potential verified

## Summary

A graph becomes a game only after strategies and payoffs are supplied. The four-cycle coordination example also separates a local equilibrium from a globally optimal configuration.

## Definitions

Each vertex of C₄ chooses s_i∈{0,1}. Its payoff is the number of neighbors choosing the same value, minus εs_i.

A pure Nash equilibrium permits no strictly profitable unilateral change. Indifference is allowed.

The potential is Φ(s)=Σ_{edges {i,j}}1[s_i=s_j]−εΣ_i s_i.

## Result name

An exact potential and a perturbation-induced equilibrium change

## Result statement

Every unilateral payoff change equals the corresponding change in Φ. At ε=0 there are six pure equilibria: the two constant profiles and the four rotations of 0011. For 0<ε<2 only the two constant profiles remain; for ε>2 only all-zero remains.

## Proof

Changing s_i changes exactly the edge agreement terms incident to i and its own action cost, proving the potential identity. Since the game is finite, any sequence of strict improving moves terminates. To classify C₄ equilibria, observe that at ε=0 an isolated action has two disagreeing neighbors and can improve, while blocks of two have one agreeing and one disagreeing neighbor and are weakly stable. These give exactly the listed profiles. If 0<ε<2, a boundary vertex choosing one has one neighbor of each type, so switching to zero saves ε without losing agreement. Thus mixed profiles cannot be equilibria. The constant-one profile stays stable until its cost exceeds the loss of two agreements. Constant-zero is stable for every positive ε.

## Worked example: setup

Enumerate all sixteen action profiles and each of their four possible unilateral flips.

## Worked example: calculation

The verifier finds six, two and one equilibria at ε=0,1/2,3 respectively and checks the potential identity for every profile and deviation at ε=1/2. At ε=0, 0011 is an equilibrium with two agreeing edges, while the global optimum has four.

## Worked example: conclusion

Local stability, global optimization and a graph partition are three distinct notions. A small action cost can remove weak equilibria without changing the graph.

## Limits

The boundary ε=2 needs its own weak-inequality treatment; the displayed open-interval statement avoids hiding it. Mixed-strategy equilibria are not classified here, and a different payoff law can give a different game on the same graph.

## Extension

Characterize strict equilibria on a general graph through neighbor majorities, then compare them with potential maximizers. For constrained interventions, optimize which action costs or edge weights eliminate a selected undesirable equilibrium.
