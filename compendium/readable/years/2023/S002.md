## Status

Classical framework with a proved synthesis and worked example

## Summary

The Gudermannian can turn an unbounded coordinate into a bounded angle and simplify a formula. An exact reversible change of observation cannot itself create statistical information. This distinction turns the old activation and geometry questions into an analysis of representation, quantization, noise and numerical precision.

## Definitions

Let X have a regular density p_θ(x), with θ-independent support and square-integrable score s_θ=∂_θlog p_θ. Fisher information is I_X(θ)=E_θ[s_θs_θᵀ].

Let g be a θ-independent differentiable bijection with nonzero Jacobian on its domain. Y=g(X) is the same observation expressed in another coordinate.

## Result name

Fisher information is invariant under an invertible parameter-independent observation chart

## Result statement

Under the stated change-of-variable and regularity assumptions, I_Y(θ)=I_X(θ). Additional quantization or a parameter-independent noisy channel cannot increase Fisher information when the score-conditioning identity and the usual differentiation conditions hold.

## Proof

The transformed density is q_θ(y)=p_θ(g⁻¹(y))|det Dg⁻¹(y)|. The Jacobian factor is independent of θ, so differentiating its logarithm with respect to θ gives zero. Consequently the transformed score is s_θ(g⁻¹(y)), and change of variables in its second moment proves equality. For a further observation Z produced through a θ-independent channel, differentiation under the integral gives s_Z(Z)=E[s_X(X)|Z]. Conditional Jensen yields E[s_Zs_Zᵀ]⪯E[s_Xs_Xᵀ], since their difference is E[Cov(s_X|Z)]. This is a statement about observations; reparameterizing θ has its own Jacobian transformation law and is a different operation.

## Worked example: setup

Let X∼N(μ,σ²) with σ>0 known and take Y=gd(X)=arctan(sinh X), so −π/2<Y<π/2.

## Worked example: calculation

The inverse is x=artanh(sin y), with dx/dy=sec y. Therefore q_μ(y)=exp(−(artanh(sin y)−μ)²/(2σ²))sec y/(σ√(2π)). Its μ-score is (artanh(sin y)−μ)/σ², and its squared expectation is 1/σ², exactly the original information. If Y is then rounded into a finite set of bins, the retained information becomes Σ_j (∂_μP_j)²/P_j and is at most 1/σ². The bundled calculation evaluates this finite-bin quantity directly from Gaussian CDFs.

## Worked example: conclusion

The coordinate transformation is mathematically useful while its statistical information is unchanged. A benefit under a fixed finite-bit or hardware budget is possible, but it must be demonstrated for that budget and distribution.

## Limits

The theorem does not forbid a transform from improving optimization behavior, finite-precision conditioning or a restricted approximation class. It also does not apply unchanged to a transform depending on an unknown θ. A bounded activation used inside a noninvertible network is not merely an observation chart.

## Extension

Compare candidate transforms at a fixed bit budget by optimizing retained information under a declared prior or worst-case parameter range. Include saturation, endpoint roundoff and added sensor noise. This yields a concrete design objective rather than a claim that bounded coordinates are intrinsically more informative.
