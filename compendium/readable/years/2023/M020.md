## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which objects can a chosen heat signature distinguish, and how stable is it to perturbations and sampling?

## Status

Classical pair with exact finite certificate

## Summary

The 4×4 rook graph and the Shrikhande graph supply an exact obstruction to recovering a graph from its heat trace or diagonal heat signature. The full heat operator retains strictly more information.

## Definitions

Both graphs have vertex set Z₄×Z₄. Rook adjacency means sharing exactly one coordinate.

Shrikhande adjacency means the difference lies in {(±1,0),(0,±1),(1,1),(−1,−1)} modulo four.

For adjacency A, L=6I−A and K_t=exp(−tL). The heat trace is Tr K_t; the vertex signature is (K_t)_{vv}.

## Result name

Identical spectra and vertex signatures do not force isomorphism

## Result statement

Both graphs have Laplacian eigenvalues 0 once, 4 six times and 8 nine times, and every vertex in both has signature (1+6e^(−4t)+9e^(−8t))/16. Nevertheless their clique numbers are respectively four and three.

## Proof

The verifier constructs both integer adjacency matrices and checks A²=4I+2J. The all-ones vector has adjacency eigenvalue six. On its orthogonal complement J vanishes, so adjacency eigenvalues are ±2. Trace A=0 and dimension sixteen force multiplicities six and nine. Both graphs are connected because their Cayley generators span Z₄², hence eigenvalue six is simple. Their vertex translations act transitively, so all diagonal entries of any matrix function of A agree and equal its trace divided by sixteen. The verifier exhaustively checks four-vertex subsets: rook four-cliques exist, whereas Shrikhande has none, and Shrikhande has triangles. Clique number is invariant under isomorphism, proving the graphs differ.

## Worked example: setup

Use the two explicit sixteen-vertex adjacency matrices produced by rook_adjacency() and shrikhande_adjacency() in verify.py.

## Worked example: calculation

Exact matrix multiplication verifies the common-neighbor identity. Exhaustive enumeration of C(16,4)=1820 subsets per graph certifies the four-clique distinction. This does not rely on floating eigenvalue comparisons or a graph-isomorphism heuristic.

## Worked example: conclusion

Even the entire curve of diagonal heat signatures can lose structural information. This is a stronger failure than matching a handful of sampled times.

## Limits

The obstruction does not apply to the complete labeled K_t. For any fixed t>0, K_t is symmetric positive definite and its unique real symmetric logarithm gives L=−log(K_t)/t. Equality up to a permutation therefore recovers graph isomorphism. Numerical recovery can still be ill-conditioned when small eigenvalues of K_t are noisy.
