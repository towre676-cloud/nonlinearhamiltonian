## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which sequence of observables satisfies a finite recurrence, and can the minimal polynomial give a shorter recurrence than the characteristic polynomial?

## Status

An exact annihilator theorem with a reduced example

## Summary

Cayley–Hamilton always gives a recurrence for a finite linear state, but the observable behavior may require a strictly smaller polynomial. The correct object is the induced operator after all permanently invisible directions have been quotiented out.

## Definitions

Let V be a finite-dimensional vector space over a field, A:V→V and C:V→Y linear, with outputs y_n=CA^n x.

Let N=∩_(k=0)^(n−1)ker(CA^k), where n=dimV. Cayley–Hamilton makes this the intersection over all k≥0.

A universal output recurrence with polynomial p means CA^k p(A)x=0 for every state x and every k≥0.

## Result name

The observable quotient’s minimal polynomial is the universal recurrence

## Result statement

N is A-invariant, so A induces A_bar on V/N. A polynomial p gives a universal output recurrence exactly when p(A_bar)=0. Thus the unique least-degree monic universal recurrence is the minimal polynomial of A_bar. Its degree is at most dim(V/N); for a scalar observable with an observable quotient it equals that quotient dimension.

## Proof

If v∈N, all outputs CA^kv vanish, hence CA^k(Av)=CA^(k+1)v=0 and Av∈N. Now a recurrence holds exactly when p(A)V⊂N, which is precisely p(A_bar)=0 after applying the quotient map. This proves both directions and minimality. The dimension bound is the standard minimal-polynomial bound. For scalar output, the row span of C,CA,… on the quotient has full dimension by observability. A polynomial of smaller degree would make every later row a combination of fewer initial rows, contradicting full dimension. This last equality need not hold for multiple outputs: A=I with C=I observes an n-dimensional state but has universal polynomial z−1.

## Worked example: setup

Take A=diag(1,2,3) and C=(1,1,0).

## Worked example: calculation

The invisible space is span(e3). On the two-dimensional quotient the minimal polynomial is (z−1)(z−2), so y_(n+2)−3y_(n+1)+2y_n=0 for every initial state. The full characteristic polynomial has a redundant factor z−3. If the particular initial state is e1, its individual sequence satisfies the shorter recurrence y_(n+1)=y_n.

## Worked example: conclusion

This distinguishes full-state, universal-observation and single-trajectory recurrences, rather than conflating their orders.

## Limits

A recurrence fitted to one finite prefix may be accidental. The quotient argument proves a recurrence for all times under the stated model; finite fitting alone does not.

## Extension

Compute exact observability kernels and Hankel ranks, then certify the smallest realization compatible with the desired class of initial conditions.
