## Revised question retained in the annual source

What mathematical object is common to the models, and which transformation preserves the claimed structure?

## Status

Classical result developed and worked

## Summary

A useful unification identifies a common mathematical structure and proves a transportable statement. Gradient flows provide one such structure, but the metric and mobility are part of the model. Sharing an energy symbol does not make two systems dynamically equivalent.

## Definitions

Let E:Rⁿ→R be differentiable. A mobility K(x) is a symmetric positive semidefinite matrix. The dynamics ẋ=−K(x)∇E(x) uses the chosen Euclidean coordinates; positive-definite K defines a corresponding metric interpretation.

## Result name

Energy dissipation and coordinate transport

## Result statement

Every sufficiently differentiable solution satisfies dE/dt=−∇EᵀK∇E≤0. Under an invertible linear change y=Sx, the transported energy Ẽ(y)=E(S⁻¹y) and mobility K̃(y)=SK(S⁻¹y)Sᵀ preserve this dissipation identity.

## Proof

The chain rule gives Ė=∇Eᵀẋ, and positive semidefiniteness gives the sign. For the coordinate change, ∇_yẼ=S⁻ᵀ∇_xE and ẏ=Sẋ=−SKSᵀ∇_yẼ. Substitution recovers the same scalar energy derivative. This calculation explains why transforming only the state while keeping an arbitrary identity mobility generally changes the dynamics. The result requires neither thermodynamic terminology nor a claim that all dissipative systems are gradient flows.

## Worked example: setup

Take E(x)=xᵀAx/2 with symmetric A positive semidefinite, and K=I.

## Worked example: calculation

Then ẋ=−Ax and Ė=−xᵀA²x. A finite weighted-graph heat equation has this form with A equal to the graph Laplacian, while the scalar equation ẋ=−x is its one-dimensional instance. A constant component in the Laplacian nullspace is preserved rather than dissipated, which shows the difference between nonincrease and convergence to a unique state.

## Worked example: conclusion

The shared theorem is energy monotonicity under a specified positive mobility. A proof of equivalence requires an actual state-space map such as S and the correctly transported energy and mobility.

## Limits

Nonsymmetric transport, Hamiltonian components, constraints and nonlocal metrics require extensions. Energy decrease alone does not prove boundedness, uniqueness, convergence rate or identification from observations.

## Extension

Separate a generator into dissipative and skew parts, prove the resulting balance law and ask which observations distinguish the mobility from the energy.
