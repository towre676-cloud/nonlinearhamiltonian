## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which graph, null model and resolution parameter define a community, and does the optimizer remain stable under perturbation?

## Status

Finite optimization solved exactly

## Summary

A small graph already exhibits resolution-dependent optima and nontrivial ties. Reporting one partition without its resolution parameter would conceal both effects.

## Definitions

The graph is C₄ with four edges, degree two at every vertex and total edge weight m=4.

For a vertex partition P and γ≥0, Q_γ(P)=Σ_{B∈P}[e(B)/4−γ(|B|/4)²], where e(B) counts internal undirected edges.

The feasible set contains all fifteen set partitions of four labeled vertices. Rotated partitions are distinct selections even when they have the same score.

## Result name

The complete modularity phase diagram

## Result statement

For 0≤γ<1 the all-in-one partition uniquely maximizes Q_γ=1−γ. For 1<γ<2 the two partitions into adjacent pairs maximize Q_γ=1/2−γ/2. For γ>2 the singleton partition uniquely maximizes Q_γ=−γ/4. At γ=1 there are three maximizers; at γ=2 there are seven.

## Proof

Group partitions by block pattern and internal-edge count. Besides the three candidate lines, an adjacent pair with two singletons has score 1/4−3γ/8; a triple plus singleton has 1/2−5γ/8; opposite pairs have −γ/2; a nonadjacent pair with two singletons has −3γ/8. Comparison of these finitely many affine functions gives the envelope above. The first two candidate lines meet at γ=1. The pair and singleton lines meet at γ=2, where the four adjacent-pair-plus-singletons partitions also tie. No discarded line is higher for γ≥0. The verifier independently enumerates the partitions and evaluates rational scores at interior and boundary values.

## Worked example: setup

At γ=3/2, compare the all-in-one, adjacent-pair and singleton candidates.

## Worked example: calculation

Their scores are −1/2, −1/4 and −3/8 respectively. Exactly two adjacent-pair partitions win. At γ=2 each has score −1/2, as do the four one-pair partitions and the all-singleton partition.

## Worked example: conclusion

The preferred community structure changes under the chosen scale even though the graph has not changed. Boundary multiplicities are mathematically part of the answer.

## Limits

This solves one finite instance, not general modularity maximization. Numerical graph algorithms may miss ties, and graph perturbations require a separate sensitivity analysis.

## Extension

For a weighted graph, compute the exact upper envelope of all partition score lines for manageable n. Identify parameter intervals with a unique optimizer and certify a score gap that bounds stability to weight perturbations.
