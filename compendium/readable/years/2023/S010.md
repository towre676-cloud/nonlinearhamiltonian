## Status

Classical rank-one structure developed into a proved finite-sector theorem with exact rational and exceptional certificates

## Summary

The two-site calculation extends to an explicit theorem for every number of spin-1/2 sites in the one-magnon sector. Simple critical points of one polynomial give a complete joint eigenbasis. For distinct real sites these critical points necessarily interlace and are simple, so this sector has no missing regular Bethe states apart from the explicitly retained uniform descendant. A three-site rational example is solved on the entire eight-dimensional tensor space, and the complex discriminant-zero locus exhibits a genuine Jordan degeneration.

## Definitions

Let n≥2, take pairwise distinct complex sites z₁,…,z_n and put p(x)=∏_i(x−z_i). The one-magnon space has basis e_j, meaning that only spin j is down.

Use Ω_ij=(1/2)h_i h_j+e_i f_j+f_i e_j and H_i=Σ_(j≠i)Ω_ij/(z_i−z_j). Define E_i⁰=(1/2)Σ_(j≠i)1/(z_i−z_j) and the uniform vector u=Σ_j e_j.

For w not equal to a site, define a_j(w)=1/(w−z_j), B(w)=Σ_j a_j(w)=p′(w)/p(w), and v(w)=Σ_j a_j(w)e_j. The scalar equation uses u_w(x)=p′(x)/(2p(x))−1/(x−w) and V_w=u_w²−u_w′. The symbol u_w is a scalar connection coefficient and is distinct from the uniform vector u.

## Result name

Critical points give a complete spectral decomposition under the stated simple-root condition

## Result statement

If p′ has n−1 distinct roots w₁,…,w_(n−1), the vectors u,v(w₁),…,v(w_(n−1)) are a basis of the one-magnon space. Their H_i eigenvalues are E_i⁰ and E_i(w)=E_i⁰+a_i(w), respectively. For distinct real sites, the hypothesis always holds: exactly one critical point lies in each consecutive-site interval. For every critical w, V_w=(3/4)Σ_i(x−z_i)^(−2)+Σ_iE_i(w)/(x−z_i), with no pole at w. Its scalar equation has a rational-over-sqrt(p) fundamental pair of Wronskian one.

## Proof

The action on a pair of sites is explicit: Ω_ij e_i=−e_i/2+e_j, Ω_ij e_j=e_i−e_j/2, while Ω_ij e_k=e_k/2 if k differs from both. Therefore, for k≠i, the kth component of H_i v(w) is E_i⁰a_k+(a_i−a_k)/(z_i−z_k)=(E_i⁰+a_i)a_k. Its ith component equals E_i⁰a_i−a_iΣ_(j≠i)a_j=(E_i⁰+a_i)a_i−a_iB(w). At a critical point B=0, this is the claimed eigenvector. Directly H_i u=E_i⁰u. For any fixed i, the n displayed eigenvalues are distinct because the map w↦1/(w−z_i) is injective and never zero. They therefore give a basis, proving completeness within this finite sector without assuming it. In particular the H_i are simultaneously diagonal in that basis. For real ordered sites, Rolle’s theorem supplies at least one root of p′ in each of n−1 disjoint gaps. Since deg p′=n−1, these are all its roots, each simple. The vectors are orthogonal: uᵀv(w)=B(w)=0 and v(w)ᵀv(v)=[B(w)−B(v)]/(v−w)=0 for distinct critical roots. Their squared norm is −p″(w)/p(w), which is positive for real sites and nonzero whenever the critical root is simple. Thus P_w=v(w)v(w)ᵀ/[−p″(w)/p(w)] and P₀=uuᵀ/n give explicit spectral projectors.

For the scalar equation, a physical site z_i has u_w=1/[2(x−z_i)]+E_i(w)+O(x−z_i), so V_w has double coefficient 3/4 and simple residue E_i(w). At x=w, write u_w=−1/(x−w)+B(w)/2+O(x−w); the double pole cancels and the simple residue is −B(w), hence zero at a Bethe root. Since V_w decays at infinity, its partial fractions are exactly the asserted expression. One solution is y₁=(x−w)/sqrt(p(x)). Reduction of order gives y₂=y₁∫p(x)/(x−w)² dx. Taylor expansion at w has no linear term because p′(w)=0, so an explicit numerator is q₂(x)=−p(w)+Σ_(m=2)^n p^(m)(w)(x−w)^m/[m!(m−1)]. Then y₂=q₂/sqrt(p), and y₁y₂′−y₁′y₂=1. Both solutions are regular at the apparent point w. Their only multivalued factor is a common square root, so the resulting monodromy is scalar and projectively trivial. This is a concrete scalar-oper statement with all normalizations fixed.

## Worked example: setup

Take three sites z=(0,1,8/3), and separately study z=(0,1,t) as a complex parameter family.

## Worked example: calculation

For z=(0,1,8/3), p′ has roots 2 and4/9. Their highest one-magnon coefficient vectors are (1/2,1,−3/2) and (9/4,−9/5,−9/20), giving Hamiltonian triples (−3/16,6/5,−81/80) and (25/16,−8/5,3/80). Each generates a two-dimensional spin doublet under total lowering. The vacuum generates a quartet with triple (−11/16,1/5,39/80). The verifier constructs all eight rational eigenvectors and all three full 8×8 Hamiltonians, verifies joint eigenvector relations and rank eight, and checks both scalar differential equations through identically zero residual polynomials and Wronskians. As another independent exact example, four sites (−7,−1,1,7) have critical roots (−5,0,5), so their complete four-dimensional one-magnon diagonalization and three scalar solution pairs remain rational.

For z=(0,1,t), p′=3x²−2(1+t)x+t and its root discriminant is 4(t²−t+1). The exceptional sites are t=(1±i√3)/2. They are distinct from 0 and1, but the two Bethe roots coalesce at w=(1+t)/3. Here B(w)=B′(w)=0. Differentiating the exact off-shell relation gives (H_i−E_i(w))v′(w)=−a_i(w)²v(w). The right side is nonzero, and v′ is independent of v because distinct sites prevent all a_j from being equal. Together with u these vectors span the three-dimensional sector, so each H_i has a size-two Jordan block there. The checker works exactly in Q(t) with t²−t+1=0 rather than relying on near-coincident numerical eigenvalues.

## Worked example: conclusion

The finite real-site completeness theorem is closed under its stated assumptions. The three-site complex exceptional locus is also explicit, and its loss of diagonalizability is certified by generalized eigenvectors. The scalar-oper formula survives there, but it cannot by itself distinguish the generalized direction in a nonreduced spectral problem.

## Limits

The general theorem covers one-magnon states and the uniform descendant, not every magnetization sector for arbitrary n. The full eight-dimensional completeness statement applies only to the explicitly checked three-site example. General higher-rank Gaudin opers, arbitrary representations and a global Langlands correspondence require additional theory. Projectively trivial scalar monodromy here follows from the explicit fundamental pair, not from a broad assertion that all ODEs associated with a spin system have trivial monodromy.

## Extension

S014 completes the distinct-complex-site one-magnon Jordan classification, including arbitrary critical multiplicities and a closed characteristic formula. For multi-magnon sectors the Bethe equations include root-root interactions. A disciplined next step is to compute a finite highest-weight multiplicity space independently, then compare regular, singular and generalized Bethe vectors with its exact joint spectrum. The exceptional-point calculation shows why a scheme-level spectrum may contain information that a set of distinct eigenvalues loses.
