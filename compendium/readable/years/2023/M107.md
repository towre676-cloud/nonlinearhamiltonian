## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which state variables, conservation laws, observation map and parameter regime support a claimed mechanism or chaotic behavior?

## Status

Classical result developed and example checked

## Summary

A rigorous counterexample is more informative than another catalogue of nonlinear applications. A simple nonlinear ODE has a fully solvable, globally convergent flow and an observation that permanently hides the sign of its initial state.

## Definitions

Consider ẋ=−x³ on the real line for t≥0. The state is x, and first choose the observable y=x².

An observation model is identifiable on a set if no two admissible initial states generate exactly the same observed time history. This is distinct from state stability.

## Result name

Global convergence with an exact observation collision

## Result statement

For every initial x₀, x(t)=x₀/sqrt(1+2x₀²t). All trajectories converge to zero, while x₀ and −x₀ give identical y(t)=x₀²/(1+2x₀²t).

## Proof

For x₀≠0, differentiate x⁻² to obtain d(x⁻²)/dt=2 and solve; x₀=0 gives the equilibrium. The denominator stays positive, giving a global forward solution. With V=x²/2, V̇=−x⁴≤0, and the explicit solution proves convergence. Squaring removes the initial sign for all times. Equivalently y satisfies ẏ=−2y² with the same initial y₀ for both states. Thus the nonlinear system is elementary and convergent, while the chosen observation remains structurally ambiguous.

## Worked example: setup

Compare x₀=1 and x₀=−1, recording y=x² at any finite or infinite collection of times.

## Worked example: calculation

At t=0,1,2 the common observations are 1,1/3,1/5. No increase in temporal sampling resolves the sign because the entire continuous histories coincide. Adding the observable z=x distinguishes the two immediately. The exact rational y values and the observation evolution equation are checked by the verifier.

## Worked example: conclusion

Complexity of the evolution law, richness of sampling and distinguishability of states are separate questions. This example refutes a generic inference from nonlinear equations to chaos and a generic inference from many measurements to complete state reconstruction.

## Limits

This proof concerns one scalar system and one observation. It is not a general criterion for absence of chaos in higher dimensions. Nor does adding a sign-sensitive observation establish practical robustness when measurement noise is large.

## Extension

Classify observation collisions under a symmetry group: if both dynamics and output are sign-equivariant or sign-invariant in the appropriate way, determine whether the output can at best recover a symmetry orbit. Then quantify the additional measurement needed to break it.
