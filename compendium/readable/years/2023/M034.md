## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which specific Macdonald identity is meant, and in which completed ring does its product equal its sum?

## Status

Classical product identity derived and coefficients checked

## Summary

An explicit Jacobi triple product is a tractable affine-root-system identity. It is distinct from an unspecified identity involving Macdonald symmetric polynomials.

## Definitions

Write (a;q)_∞=Π_{m≥0}(1−aq^m). Analytically take 0<|q|<1 and z≠0.

Formally work in q-series whose coefficients are finite Laurent polynomials in z. At every q-degree only finitely many bilateral summands contribute.

The identity is Σ_{n∈Z}(−1)^n z^n q^{n(n−1)/2}=(z;q)_∞(q/z;q)_∞(q;q)_∞.

## Result name

A functional equation fixes all Laurent coefficients up to one factor, and a partition identity fixes that factor

## Result statement

The product satisfies F(qz)=−z⁻¹F(z). Its Laurent coefficients therefore obey c_{n+1}=−qⁿc_n. The constant coefficient is one, giving the entire bilateral series.

## Proof

Shift the product indices: (qz;q)_∞=(z;q)_∞/(1−z) and (1/z;q)_∞=(1−1/z)(q/z;q)_∞. Their ratio is −1/z, yielding the functional equation. To normalize, expand (z;q)_∞=Σ_{a≥0}(−1)^a q^{a(a−1)/2}z^a/(q;q)_a; this expansion follows by its own product shift and constant term one. The analogous expansion for (q/z;q)_∞ shows that the z⁰ coefficient of F is (q;q)_∞Σ_{k≥0}q^{k²}/(q;q)_k². Every integer partition decomposes uniquely into its maximal k×k Durfee square, the cells to its right and the cells below. Those two remaining partitions have at most k parts, giving generating series 1/(q;q)_k². Thus the sum is 1/(q;q)_∞ and the normalizing coefficient is exactly one.

## Worked example: setup

Truncate the product at q-degree twelve while retaining exact Laurent powers of z.

## Worked example: calculation

The verifier obtains exactly the bilateral terms with n(n−1)/2≤12, including positive and negative n. All other product coefficients cancel. The proof explains why agreement persists beyond the finite truncation.

## Worked example: conclusion

The ring, normalization and combinatorial mechanism are explicit. This supplies a real model for revisiting a named affine identity.

## Limits

This is a classical identity, not a new Macdonald formula. It does not supply identities for every P_λ(x;q,t), and analytic manipulations beyond the stated convergence region need justification.

## Extension

Choose a higher-rank affine root system and specify its product and lattice sum. Use a low-rank proof, exact coefficient checks and a clear convergence/completion convention before claiming a general transport principle.
