## Revised question retained in the annual source

Is there a specific q-difference identity involving gd, or are these two independently chosen tools?

## Status

An exact secant integral and uniform bounds

## Summary

There is a clean identity connecting q-differentiation and gd, but it is an instance of the fundamental theorem of calculus rather than evidence of a special learning mechanism. It allows the q-difference quotient to be bounded and its ordinary-derivative limit to be controlled.

## Definitions

For real q>0, q≠1 and x≠0, define D_qf(x)=(f(qx)−f(x))/((q−1)x).

Define D_qgd(0)=1 by continuous extension.

Let m=min(1,q) and M=max(1,q). On the interval between x and qx, the absolute coordinate lies between m|x| and M|x|.

## Result name

Exact integral representation and bounds

## Result statement

D_qgd(x)=∫_0^1 sech(x(1+s(q−1)))ds. Consequently sech(M|x|)≤D_qgd(x)≤sech(m|x|). As q→1 these quotients converge to sech x uniformly on compact x-intervals.

## Proof

Since gd′(t)=sech t, subtracting its values gives gd(qx)−gd(x)=∫_x^(qx)sech t dt. Substitute t=x+s(q−1)x; the oriented integral and denominator cancel even when q<1 or x<0, proving the formula. The function sech t is positive, even and decreasing as |t| increases, so its extrema on this same-sign interval give the bounds. For x in a compact interval and q in a sufficiently small neighborhood of one, all arguments remain in a common compact interval. Uniform continuity of sech there makes the integrand converge uniformly, hence the averaged quotients converge uniformly. At x=0 the integrand equals one, proving the claimed extension without division by zero.

## Worked example: setup

Compare q=1/2 and q=2 at x=2.

## Worked example: calculation

The first quotient averages sech between 1 and 2; the second averages it between 2 and 4. Both are positive, and the verifier checks their values against the exact endpoint bounds. These are secant averages over different intervals, not two names for the same local derivative.

## Worked example: conclusion

The relation is exact and has a precise limit. It distinguishes an honest q-difference calculation from a purely verbal association.

## Limits

Replacing an ordinary gradient by D_q changes the optimization method and its chain rule. For products, D_q(fg)=g(x)D_qf+f(qx)D_qg; the ordinary Leibniz formula is recovered only in the limit. No improved learning performance follows from positivity or the bounds alone.
