## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which power law follows from conserved radial flux, and which metric factor follows from a coordinate projection?

## Status

Two distinct inverse-power mechanisms calculated

## Summary

A coordinate projection and a conserved flux are mathematically different constructions. Each can generate a position-dependent factor, but the factors belong to different measures and have different meanings. Writing both down makes the proposed identification testable.

## Definitions

In Euclidean R^d with d≥2, a radial field has the form J(x)=F(r)e_r for r=||x||>0.

Conserved outward flux across a sphere means F(r) times its area is independent of r.

Stereographic coordinates (u,v) on the unit sphere use r²=u²+v² and X=(2u,2v,r²−1)/(1+r²).

## Result name

Flux power law versus a projection Jacobian

## Result statement

A rotationally symmetric conserved flux in dimension d has F(r)=C/r^(d−1), giving inverse-square magnitude in d=3. The unit-sphere stereographic metric is ds²=4(du²+dv²)/(1+u²+v²)² and its area measure is 4du dv/(1+u²+v²)². The latter is not a derivation of the former.

## Proof

The sphere of radius r has area ω_(d−1)r^(d−1). If its flux is Φ, then Φ=ω_(d−1)r^(d−1)F(r), yielding F(r)=Φ/(ω_(d−1)r^(d−1)). Equivalently, away from a source, the radial divergence is r^(1−d)∂_r(r^(d−1)F), which must vanish. For the projection, differentiate the displayed map X(u,v), take Euclidean inner products of its two coordinate derivatives and obtain a diagonal pullback metric with both diagonal entries 4/(1+r²)². Taking the square root of the determinant supplies the stated area factor. This second calculation concerns how one surface is charted, not how a physical flux changes across a family of spheres.

## Worked example: calculation

A three-dimensional radial flux decays as physical radius R^(−2). The stereographic area density decays as chart radius r^(−4). These statements do not contradict each other: R labels different spheres in a physical space, whereas r labels positions within a chart of one unit sphere. Identifying them would discard the very geometric distinction being calculated.

## Worked example: conclusion

The original question now has a derivation of each factor and a concrete reason the coordinate formula does not imply the conservation law.

## Limits

A projected representation of a conserved field can of course be constructed. It must transform the field and the integration measure together; conservation is then transported, not created by projection.
