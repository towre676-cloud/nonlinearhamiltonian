## Revised question retained in the annual source

What assumptions yield an inequality, and what extra completeness makes it an equality?

## Status

Classical result developed and worked

## Summary

The missing distinction in the original inequality list is completeness. Bessel’s inequality is an exact statement about projection error; Parseval’s equality appears when the chosen orthonormal family spans the relevant Hilbert space. A residual formula makes the practical meaning immediate.

## Definitions

Use an inner product linear in its first argument. For an orthonormal family e₁,…,e_m, define P_mx=Σ⟨x,e_j⟩e_j. Its span is a closed finite-dimensional subspace of the Hilbert space H.

## Result name

Projection residual and completeness criterion

## Result statement

For every x, ||x||²=Σ_{j=1}^m|⟨x,e_j⟩|²+||x−P_mx||². For a countable orthonormal family, the infinite coefficient sum is at most ||x||². Equality for every x holds exactly when its closed linear span is H.

## Proof

The residual is orthogonal to every e_j because taking its inner product with e_j subtracts the same coefficient. Pythagoras therefore splits the norm into projection and residual, and orthonormality gives the coefficient sum. Finite partial sums are increasing and bounded, so their limit exists. The partial projections form a Cauchy sequence, with squared differences equal to sums of omitted squared coefficients. Their limit projects onto the closed span. Equality means the residual projection onto the orthogonal complement vanishes. If equality holds for all x, that complement is zero and the span is dense.

## Worked example: setup

Take x=(2,3,6) in R³ and initially observe only e₁,e₂.

## Worked example: calculation

The observed squared coefficient sum is 4+9=13, total squared norm is 49, and residual squared norm is 36. Adding e₃ removes the residual exactly. Omitting e₃ never becomes harmless merely because the first two coefficients were calculated to high precision.

## Worked example: conclusion

This is both an approximation certificate and a statement of what the measurement family cannot see.

## Limits

An infinite orthonormal family need not be complete. For nonorthogonal features, coefficients from raw inner products are not automatically orthogonal projections, and frame or Gram-matrix analysis is needed.

## Extension

Develop a frame analogue with lower and upper frame bounds, or identify the cheapest observation family whose residual vanishes on a specified finite-dimensional model class.
