## Summary

The heat-kernel questions contain two distinct issues. A compressed signature can lose information exactly, whereas the full kernel can be complete but poorly conditioned for reconstruction. The exact collision in M020 and the bounds below make both statements concrete.

## Definitions

Let L and M be real symmetric positive semidefinite matrices. At t>0 define K_L(t)=exp(−tL). Matrix norms below are Euclidean operator norms.

For a labeled undirected weighted graph, L is its combinatorial Laplacian. A heat trace, the diagonal of the heat kernel and the full heat kernel are different observations.

## Result name

Forward heat evolution is Lipschitz while its logarithmic inverse can amplify noise

## Result statement

For t≥0, ||exp(−tL)−exp(−tM)||≤t||L−M||. For t>0, the full positive-definite kernel determines L uniquely by L=−log(K_L(t))/t. If t>0 and ||L||,||M||≤Λ, then ||L−M||≤e^(tΛ)||K_L(t)−K_M(t)||/t.

## Proof

Differentiate F(s)=exp(−(t−s)L)exp(−sM) with respect to s. Since F(t)−F(0)=exp(−tM)−exp(−tL), integration gives exp(−tL)−exp(−tM)=∫₀ᵗexp(−(t−s)L)(M−L)exp(−sM)ds. Each exponential has norm at most one because the generators are positive semidefinite; integrating norms proves the first bound. For positive-definite A and B with eigenvalues at least a>0, the resolvent integral for the logarithm and the identity U⁻¹−V⁻¹=U⁻¹(V−U)V⁻¹ give ||log A−log B||≤||A−B||∫₀∞(a+s)⁻²ds=||A−B||/a. The spectral lower bound for either heat matrix is e^(−tΛ); substitute a and divide by t. The principal logarithm is unique for a positive-definite matrix, which proves exact completeness of the full labeled kernel.

## Worked example: setup

The 4×4 rook graph and the Shrikhande graph have the same Laplacian spectrum 0,4 with multiplicity 6, and 8 with multiplicity 9, but different clique numbers.

## Worked example: calculation

Both are vertex-transitive, so every diagonal heat value equals (1+6e^(−4t)+9e^(−8t))/16 for every t. Thus even all-time vertex heat signatures cannot distinguish these two graphs. Their full heat matrices do differ up to graph isomorphism, because equality after a vertex relabeling would force equality of their recovered Laplacians. For the full-kernel inverse bound, e^(tΛ)/t is minimized at t=1/Λ when Λ>0 and the absolute kernel-error budget is held fixed.

## Worked example: conclusion

There can be exact blindness in a compressed signature and severe numerical sensitivity in a complete observation. Completeness and robustness must be evaluated separately.

## Limits

The inverse bound applies to exact heat kernels of symmetric positive semidefinite generators with a common norm bound. An arbitrary noisy matrix need not remain positive definite; reconstructing a nearest valid generator is a separate constrained problem. The time 1/Λ minimizes this particular upper bound, not every practical estimator’s risk.

## Extension

Combine the exact observation partition with perturbation bounds: first determine which states are distinguishable in principle, then select times and probes that provide a specified margin under the declared noise model. This avoids optimizing a highly precise score on a fundamentally incomplete signature.
