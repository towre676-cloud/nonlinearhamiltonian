## Revised question retained in the annual source

Can a proposed layer construction bound products of Jacobians better than a conventional layer, and under which input distribution?

## Status

Vanishing demonstrated and a residual-depth bound derived

## Summary

A bounded activation does not protect its derivative from saturation. For gd the derivative is explicitly sech, so useful depth bounds can be derived without guessing from wave-propagation analogies. A residual scaling offers a separate, conditional way to control conditioning as depth grows.

## Definitions

For scalar layers h_l=gd(w_l h_(l−1)+b_l), write z_l=w_l h_(l−1)+b_l.

The input derivative is J=∏_(l=1)^L w_l sech z_l.

For vector layers the Jacobian is D_lW_l with D_l diagonal and entries sech of the preactivations. For the residual construction take T>0 and K≥0. Singular-value bounds must use all relevant coordinates.

## Result name

Saturation bound and a depth-scaled residual alternative

## Result statement

If |w_l|≤W and every scalar |z_l|≥a>0, then |J|≤(W sech a)^L. For residual maps x_(l+1)=x_l+(T/L)F_l(x_l) with ||DF_l||₂≤K and L>TK, every total-Jacobian singular value lies between (1−TK/L)^L and (1+TK/L)^L. For L≥2TK, the bounds e^(−2TK) and e^(TK) are uniform in depth.

## Proof

The scalar chain rule gives the product, and monotonicity of sech on nonnegative arguments supplies the first estimate. In a residual layer, write its Jacobian I+E with ||E||≤TK/L. For every vector v, the triangle and reverse triangle inequalities give (1−||E||)||v||≤||(I+E)v||≤(1+||E||)||v||. Multiply the singular-value bounds across layers. The upper uniform estimate follows from log(1+s)≤s. For s≤1/2, log(1−s)≥−2s gives the lower estimate. These are worst-case deterministic bounds, conditional on a common derivative bound K; they make no distributional or performance claim.

## Worked example: setup

Use h_(l+1)=gd(h_l+2), h_0=0, with L=20.

## Worked example: calculation

Every preactivation is at least two. Therefore |J|≤sech(2)^20≈3.09865×10^(−12). Direct accumulation of logarithmic derivatives gives approximately 1.82740×10^(−24), far smaller still. The calculation avoids underflow and illustrates that the proved upper bound need not be sharp for a particular trajectory.

## Worked example: conclusion

The original concern is now expressed as a measurable derivative contraction, alongside a different architecture with an explicit conditional depth guarantee.

## Limits

The residual lower bound does not imply successful training, identifiability or useful representations. The gd upper bound for a vector layer requires control of the operator norm of its whole diagonal derivative matrix, not merely one saturated coordinate.

## Extension

Test whether a proposed layer family maintains the required K and singular-value conditions on its actual state domain; compare those certificates with measured gradients.
