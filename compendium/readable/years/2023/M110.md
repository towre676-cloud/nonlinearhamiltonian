## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which parts are field equations, which are differential identities and which transformations leave observables unchanged?

## Status

Classical result developed and example checked

## Summary

Differential forms separate what follows from a potential from what must be imposed as a sourced equation. Gauge redundancy becomes a precise kernel of the map from potentials to field strength, with topology supplying an important qualification.

## Definitions

On an oriented spacetime with a fixed metric, A is a one-form and F=dA is a two-form. The Hodge star depends on that metric and orientation.

Use normalization dF=0 and d⋆F=J, where J is a source three-form. Constants can be restored according to electromagnetic unit conventions.

## Result name

Gauge invariance and current closure

## Result statement

A↦A+dχ leaves F unchanged. Whenever d⋆F=J holds, dJ=0. Locally, every closed F is a potential field on a contractible coordinate patch.

## Proof

The identity d²=0 gives d(A+dχ)=dA. Applying d to the sourced equation gives dJ=d²⋆F=0, so source conservation is a compatibility condition. The local converse dF=0⇒F=dA is the Poincaré lemma under the contractibility assumption. It is not a global theorem on arbitrary manifolds: nontrivial degree-two cohomology can obstruct a single globally defined potential. The coordinate form of dF=0 is the homogeneous Maxwell system; the Hodge-star equation contains the metric-dependent dynamical content.

## Worked example: setup

On flat coordinates (t,x,y,z), choose A=a cos(kx−ωt)dy and use units c=1.

## Worked example: calculation

Then F=−ak sin(kx−ωt)dx∧dy+aω sin(kx−ωt)dt∧dy. Direct exterior differentiation gives dF=0 because the two mixed terms cancel. The vacuum sourced equation requires ω²=k². Adding dχ for χ=λtx changes components of A but contributes no change to F.

## Worked example: conclusion

The example distinguishes the automatic homogeneous identity, the dispersion imposed by the vacuum field equation and a gauge change of the potential. It also shows why covariant notation alone is not a quantization procedure.

## Limits

Global potentials, boundary conditions and gauge fixing require separate analysis. A flat connection can have nontrivial global holonomy while being locally pure gauge. A globally exact potential A=dχ with single-valued χ has no such loop holonomy; these cases must not be conflated.

## Extension

Develop the same problem on a torus or a punctured domain and calculate closed forms that are not exact. This makes the topology of gauge data explicit before introducing a quantum theory.
