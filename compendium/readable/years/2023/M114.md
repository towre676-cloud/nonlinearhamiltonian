## Revised question retained in the annual source

When does the threshold define a smooth boundary, and what fails when the spatial derivative vanishes?

## Status

Classical result developed and example checked

## Summary

An implicit boundary can be differentiated only where its defining level is regular. The same calculation quantifies sensitivity and gives an exact example where a boundary is born and its graph velocity diverges.

## Definitions

Let f(x,t) be continuously differentiable and consider the level set f(x,t)=c. A regular spatial level has ∇ₓf≠0.

For a one-dimensional graph r(t), the condition is f_r(r(t),t)≠0. Choose the unit normal n=∇f/|∇f| when discussing normal velocity.

## Result name

Implicit velocity and perturbation sensitivity

## Result statement

Along a regular graph r′=−f_t/f_r. In several dimensions a moving level set has normal velocity v_n=−f_t/|∇f|. A small additive level perturbation δf produces first-order normal displacement −δf/|∇f|.

## Proof

Differentiate f(r(t),t)=c to obtain f_r r′+f_t=0. For a path X(t) on a spatial level, ∇f·X′+f_t=0; taking the normal component gives the velocity. For the perturbation, Taylor-expand f(X+δr n,t)+δf=c and use ∇f·n=|∇f|. The denominator therefore measures conditioning as well as existence of the local graph. Tangential motion is a parametrization choice and is not fixed by the level equation.

## Worked example: setup

Take f(x,y,t)=x²+y²−t and c=0.

## Worked example: calculation

For t>0 the boundary is a circle of radius sqrt(t), and its outward normal speed is 1/(2sqrt(t)). At t=0 the spatial gradient vanishes at the origin. For t<0 there is no real level set, at zero there is one point, and for t>0 there is a circle. The one-dimensional version r²−t=0 has two branches ±sqrt(t), neither a differentiable graph through the critical event.

## Worked example: conclusion

The diverging speed is not a contradiction in the implicit-function theorem; it occurs exactly where its nonzero-gradient hypothesis fails. The same inverse-gradient factor explains why threshold localization becomes unstable near a flat or critical level.

## Limits

These are local regularity and first-order sensitivity statements. They do not guarantee a global graph, a unique topology, or robustness under large perturbations. Nonsmooth defining functions need generalized notions of normal and solution.

## Extension

For a specified smooth family, locate critical levels by solving f=c and ∇f=0 simultaneously, classify the local Hessian, and track which topology changes are compatible with those critical events.
