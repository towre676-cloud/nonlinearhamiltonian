## Status

Classical framework with a proved synthesis and worked example

## Summary

The squared-residual, covariance and observation questions admit an exact common model for Gaussian mean changes. In this model one can calculate precisely what a linear sensor panel preserves and what it loses. The same calculation gives a robust margin and an impossibility statement for universal dimension reduction.

## Definitions

Let X∼N(μ+δ,Σ), where the baseline μ and positive-definite covariance Σ are known and δ is an unknown mean shift. Let B be a k×d matrix of full row rank and observe Y=BX.

Whiten the shift as d₀=Σ^(−1/2)δ. Define C=BΣ^(1/2) and the orthogonal projection P=Cᵀ(CCᵀ)⁻¹C onto the row space of C.

## Result name

A linear panel retains exactly the projected noncentrality

## Result statement

The statistic T_B=(Y−Bμ)ᵀ(BΣBᵀ)⁻¹(Y−Bμ) has a noncentral χ²_k law with noncentrality λ_B=||P d₀||². Full observation has λ_full=||d₀||². Hence λ_B≤λ_full, with equality exactly when d₀ lies in the retained row space. If k<d, some nonzero shifts have λ_B=0.

## Proof

Write X−μ=Σ^(1/2)(Z+d₀) with Z standard normal. Multiplying by (CCᵀ)^(−1/2)C gives a k×d matrix with orthonormal rows. It maps Z to a standard k-dimensional normal, while the squared norm of the transformed shift is d₀ᵀCᵀ(CCᵀ)⁻¹Cd₀=||P d₀||². This proves the distribution and noncentrality formula. The Pythagorean identity ||d₀||²=||P d₀||²+||(I−P)d₀||² proves the inequality and equality condition. If k<d, the projection has a nontrivial kernel, giving a nonzero invisible shift. For perturbations d=d₀+e with ||e||≤ε, triangle inequality gives ||Pd||≥max(||Pd₀||−ε,0). Equality is attained by choosing e opposite to Pd₀ within the retained subspace, or by cancelling it completely when ε is large enough. Thus the exact worst-case noncentrality is max(||Pd₀||−ε,0)².

## Worked example: setup

Take Σ=I₃ and let B retain the first two coordinates. Then P=diag(1,1,0).

## Worked example: calculation

A shift δ=(3,4,0) has λ_full=λ_B=25. A shift δ=(0,0,5) also has λ_full=25 but λ_B=0: the selected panel sees the null distribution exactly. For the first shift with whitened uncertainty radius ε=2, the worst-case retained noncentrality is (5−2)²=9, attained by perturbation e=(−6/5,−8/5,0). Under the null, T_B∼χ²₂, with mean 2 and variance 4; the full statistic has three degrees of freedom. Comparing only raw residual scores without this calibration can be misleading.

## Worked example: conclusion

Exact optimization for a declared finite list of shifts is useful and checkable. Preserving every possible shift while reducing dimension is impossible in this model. Robustness can be expressed as a proved geometric margin rather than a hope that selected probes generalize.

## Limits

Known Gaussian covariance, mean-shift alternatives and linear measurements are strong assumptions. Estimated baselines change calibration, non-Gaussian tails can invalidate χ² laws and nonlinear features need their own analysis. The noncentrality comparison alone is not a universal theorem about every detector at every significance level.

## Extension

For a finite target family {d_j}, choose a minimum-cost panel subject to max(||P_B d_j||−ε_j,0)²≥τ_j. Solve a small instance exactly and report every target and uncertainty set. This produces a concrete experimental-design certificate whose scope is visible.
