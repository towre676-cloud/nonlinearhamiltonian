## Revised question retained in the annual source

On what domain is the expression a probability, and can its parameters be identified from observations?

## Status

Range failure proved and a finite-design model specified

## Summary

The original product can be analyzed before any modeling claim is made. A formula is a probability only on an admissible domain, and fitted coefficients are meaningful only when the observation design can distinguish them.

## Definitions

For the original expression P(x,y)=(1+x)(1−y), assume x,y∈[0,1].

For the alternative logistic model let z=(1,x,y), η=z·β and σ(η)=1/(1+e^(−η)).

A finite design matrix X has rows z_i and identifies β when distinct coefficients give distinct modeled probability vectors.

## Result name

Exact admissible domain and logistic identifiability

## Result statement

On [0,1]² the product lies in [0,1] exactly when y≥x/(1+x). A logistic model yields probabilities strictly between zero and one for every finite β. At a fixed design, its coefficient vector is identifiable exactly when X has full column rank.

## Proof

Nonnegativity of the product is automatic on the square. The upper bound is (1+x)(1−y)≤1, equivalent to y≥x/(1+x) because 1+x>0. The point (1,0) violates it with P=2. For the logistic family, σ is a strictly increasing bijection R→(0,1). Thus equal probability vectors are equivalent, after applying logit, to Xβ=Xβ′. Full column rank makes this imply β=β′; a nonzero null vector otherwise supplies indistinguishable coefficients. For independent Bernoulli observations the log likelihood is Σ[y_iη_i−log(1+e^η_i)]. Its Hessian is −XᵀWX with W diagonal and positive at finite β. Full rank gives strict concavity, but does not guarantee that a finite maximizer exists: complete separation can send the optimum to infinity.

## Worked example: setup

Use the three design points (0,0),(1,0),(0,1).

## Worked example: calculation

Their augmented rows form an invertible 3×3 matrix. β=(0,log3,−log3) gives probabilities (1/2,3/4,1/4). Taking logits yields (0,log3,−log3), from which β is uniquely recovered by solving the linear system. Repeating only the first point would identify β_0 but leave the other two coefficients free.

## Worked example: conclusion

The example separates probability range, structural identifiability and existence of an estimator—three questions the original toy formula left unresolved.

## Limits

Choosing a logistic family does not establish empirical truth, independence or adequate calibration. Identifiability is conditional on the design and model; it is not a performance guarantee.

## Extension

Add a specified observation model and investigate design conditioning or separation before optimizing the likelihood.
