## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which algebra of fields is intended, and how does its formal local expression relate to a geometric formulation?

## Status

Classical result developed and example checked

## Summary

A worked Fock-space model supplies actual operators, a vacuum and a singular operator product. It shows what the abstract vertex-algebra vocabulary is organizing, without claiming that a few words or one OPE alone constitute the full theory.

## Definitions

Let F=C[x₁,x₂,…], with each polynomial involving finitely many variables. Set a₋ₙ=xₙ by multiplication and aₙ=n∂/∂xₙ for n>0; let a₀ act as zero.

The vacuum is 1. The current is J(z)=Σ_(n∈Z)aₙz^(−n−1), interpreted as a formal field. Normal ordering moves creation modes a₋ₙ to the left.

## Result name

Fock commutator and current contraction

## Result statement

[a_m,a_n]=mδ_(m+n,0)I. The singular contraction of J(z)J(w) is 1/(z−w)², in the formal expansion associated with |z|>|w|.

## Proof

For positive m,n, derivatives commute and multiplications commute. The only nonzero mixed commutator is [m∂ₓₘ,xₙ]=mδ_mn. Splitting the field into annihilation and creation parts gives [J₊(z),J₋(w)]=Σ_(n≥1)n z^(−n−1)w^(n−1). Differentiating the formal geometric series Σ_(n≥0)wⁿz^(−n−1)=1/(z−w) produces the claimed double pole. The normally ordered remainder has no additional singular contraction. This calculation is a complete local commutator derivation in a defined representation.

## Worked example: setup

Use the normally ordered stress tensor T(z)=(1/2):J(z)²: and vacuum states of low degree.

## Worked example: calculation

The mode L₋₂ sends 1 to x₁²/2. The only contribution of L₂ on this state is (1/2)a₁², giving L₂L₋₂1=1/2. With L₀1=0, the Virasoro convention [L₂,L₋₂]=4L₀+cI/2 yields c=1. Translation on generators is represented by T₀=Σ_(n≥1)n xₙ₊₁∂ₓₙ, so [T₀,a₋ₙ]=n a₋₍ₙ₊₁₎.

## Worked example: conclusion

The example fixes normalization of the central term and makes the relation between formal fields and modes explicit. The verifier checks the displayed operator relations on a finite set of polynomial states.

## Limits

The finite checks do not prove all vertex-algebra axioms. The complete Heisenberg vertex algebra follows from the usual reconstruction construction, with vacuum, translation and locality stated on the full formal space. A coordinate-free chiral-algebra construction additionally needs a curve and coordinate transformation laws.

## Extension

Develop conformal blocks for this explicit algebra on a chosen curve, or prove a stated reconstruction theorem. Such a continuation has concrete input data rather than a generic appeal to string theory.
