## Revised question retained in the annual source

Is there an actual operator correspondence with matched units, spectra and observables?

## Status

Classical result developed and worked

## Summary

A spectral analogy becomes mathematical only when state spaces, operators, units and observables are related by an explicit map. A small periodic hopping model supplies a genuine Bloch calculation, while a similarity-transform example separates shared eigenvalues from shared dynamics.

## Definitions

Consider a two-site unit cell on an infinite one-dimensional lattice with real hoppings t₁,t₂. Its Hermitian Bloch matrix is H(k)=[[0,t₁+t₂e^(−ik)],[t₁+t₂e^(ik),0]], where k is a dimensionless crystal phase. Hopping parameters and eigenvalues have a common energy unit.

## Result name

An explicit band gap and a warning about spectral equivalence

## Result statement

The two bands are E_±(k)=±√(t₁²+t₂²+2t₁t₂cosk). Their minimum separation is 2||t₁|−|t₂||. Equality of eigenvalue sets in another model does not establish equivalence of observation maps or Euclidean amplification.

## Proof

The characteristic polynomial is E²−|t₁+t₂e^(−ik)|². Expanding the squared modulus gives the dispersion relation. Its minimum is (|t₁|−|t₂|)² because cosk ranges over [−1,1]. If B=SAS⁻¹, A and B share characteristic polynomials, but exp(tB)=S exp(tA)S⁻¹ and Euclidean norm bounds acquire the condition number of S. Only a unitary S preserves every Euclidean norm and the inner-product structure automatically. Observables must also transform, for example C_B=C_A S⁻¹, to preserve measured outputs.

## Worked example: setup

Take t₁=2,t₂=1 and compare an unrelated rescaled two-state model.

## Worked example: calculation

The bands at k=0 are ±3 and at k=π are ±1, so the gap is two. To transfer this statement, another model must supply a compatible periodic operator, an energy-scale conversion and a map respecting the relevant measurements. A match between an energy number and some unrelated flux number has no meaning until units and maps are supplied.

## Worked example: conclusion

This example yields an actual operator theorem and a precise test for proposed cross-domain correspondences.

## Limits

The toy model does not establish that another physical system has Bloch periodicity, the same Hilbert space or the same operator domain. Isospectral operators can remain distinguishable by eigenvectors, locality and response functions.

## Extension

Classify intertwiners BS=SA that also preserve a chosen observation map, and determine which spectral or transport identities survive after taking dimensionless variables.
