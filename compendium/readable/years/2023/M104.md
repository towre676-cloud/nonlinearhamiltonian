## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which periodic spin chain and normalization produce the equations, and which eigenstates do the regular roots describe?

## Status

Classical result developed and example checked

## Summary

The corrected Bethe equations are tied to one Hamiltonian normalization and checked against a finite matrix. The resulting calculation verifies a sector, while keeping singular-root and completeness questions explicit.

## Definitions

Take N=4 periodic spin-1/2 sites and H=Σ_(j=0)^3(I−P_(j,j+1)), where P swaps adjacent spins and the last edge wraps around.

In the one-down-spin sector, |j⟩ denotes the down spin at site j. A plane wave has coefficients exp(ipj), with p=2πm/4.

## Result name

Exact one-magnon diagonalization

## Result statement

H|p⟩=(2−2cos p)|p⟩, so this four-dimensional sector has energies 0,2,4,2. The finite regular Bethe roots are λ=1/2,0,−1/2, giving energies 1/(λ²+1/4)=2,4,2.

## Proof

Only the two bonds incident on the down spin act nontrivially, giving H|j⟩=2|j⟩−|j−1⟩−|j+1⟩. Substitution of the plane wave yields the dispersion. In the periodic XXX convention the regular roots obey ((λ_j+i/2)/(λ_j−i/2))^N=∏_(k≠j)(λ_j−λ_k+i)/(λ_j−λ_k−i). For one root the right side is one. Writing exp(ip)=(λ+i/2)/(λ−i/2) gives λ=(1/2)cot(p/2), and the energy agrees with the plane-wave dispersion. The p=0 state corresponds to the infinite-root limit.

## Worked example: setup

Use real vectors in the ordered basis |0⟩,…,|3⟩.

## Worked example: calculation

The vectors (1,1,1,1), (1,0,−1,0), (0,1,0,−1) and (1,−1,1,−1) form a basis with eigenvalues 0,2,2,4. Exact integer matrix multiplication verifies all four. For λ=1/2,0,−1/2 the complex Bethe ratio is i,−1,−i respectively; its fourth power is one.

## Worked example: conclusion

This establishes agreement between a direct operator calculation and the regular Bethe description of the nonuniform one-magnon states. It also demonstrates why omitting the infinite-root state would lose an actual eigenvector.

## Limits

The calculation does not prove completeness in other magnetization sectors or for arbitrary N. Roots at ±i/2 and coincident roots need a regularization or a carefully specified admissibility convention. Including the self-index k=j would add a spurious factor and changes the equations.

## Extension

For the two-magnon sector, diagonalize the six-dimensional matrix first, then match admissible Bethe solutions and explicitly account for singular solutions and descendants. The matrix spectrum supplies an independent target.
