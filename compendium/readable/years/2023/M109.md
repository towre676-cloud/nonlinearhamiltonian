## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which scales and field assumptions justify reducing a relativistic spinor equation to a nonrelativistic one?

## Status

Classical result developed and example checked

## Summary

The nonrelativistic limit must preserve spin and the magnetic coupling. Removing the rest-energy phase and eliminating the small spinor component gives the leading Pauli equation, together with an explicit statement of the scale assumptions.

## Definitions

Let π=p−qA with p=−iℏ∇ and B=∇×A. Use H_D=cα·π+βmc²+qΦ and a four-spinor ψ=e^(−imc²t/ℏ)(ϕ,χ)ᵀ.

The nonrelativistic regime requires typical |π|/(mc)≪1 and energies and time variation small compared with mc², together with fields smooth enough for the expansion.

## Result name

Leading spin-sensitive Hamiltonian

## Result statement

At leading order χ≈(σ·π)ϕ/(2mc), and iℏϕ̇=[π²/(2m)−(qℏ/(2m))σ·B+qΦ]ϕ.

## Proof

After extracting the rest-energy phase, the two components satisfy iℏϕ̇=qΦϕ+cσ·πχ and iℏχ̇=(qΦ−2mc²)χ+cσ·πϕ. The large 2mc² denominator permits the stated leading elimination of χ when the remaining terms are small. Pauli-matrix multiplication gives (σ·π)²=π²+(i/2)Σε_ijkσ_k[π_i,π_j]. Since [π_i,π_j]=iqℏε_ijkB_k, the second term is −qℏσ·B. Inserting this identity into the first equation produces the Hamiltonian and fixes the sign convention.

## Worked example: setup

First take A=Φ=0 and a plane wave of momentum magnitude p.

## Worked example: calculation

The exact positive relativistic energy is sqrt(m²c⁴+c²p²)=mc²+p²/(2m)−p⁴/(8m³c²)+O(p⁶/(m⁵c⁴)). Removing mc² leaves the Schrödinger kinetic term at leading order. For a magnetic field, the additional Pauli spin term survives at that same effective order; a scalar wave equation would miss it.

## Worked example: conclusion

The result specifies both the limiting equation and the leading neglected kinetic correction. Its small parameter is p/(mc), not simply the statement that an experiment looks classical.

## Limits

This is a formal asymptotic derivation for a prescribed background field. A uniform error theorem over long times needs operator estimates and additional regularity. Strong fields and pair-production regimes invalidate the elementary elimination.

## Extension

Choose a bounded momentum sector and smooth bounded fields, then derive a finite-time norm estimate comparing projected Dirac dynamics with the effective Pauli evolution. That is the appropriate next step toward a controlled approximation theorem.
