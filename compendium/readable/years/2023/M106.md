## Revised question retained in the annual source

What event is being estimated, and what joint assumptions justify multiplying the probabilities?

## Status

Classical result developed and example checked

## Summary

The arithmetic correction is only the first step. An all-success probability cannot be identified from marginal accuracies alone. Exact dependence constructions show the full gap between what the individual probabilities say and what independence adds.

## Definitions

Let A₁,…,A_n be success events with P(A_i)=p_i. The target is P(∩A_i), a joint probability.

Mutual independence means P(∩_(i∈S)A_i)=∏_(i∈S)p_i for every subset S. Pairwise independence checks only subsets of size two.

## Result name

Fréchet bounds and an independence counterexample

## Result statement

max(0,Σp_i−(n−1))≤P(∩A_i)≤min_i p_i. If all 104 marginals are 0.95, these bounds are 0 and 0.95, whereas independence gives 0.95^104≈0.00482230805327.

## Proof

The upper bound follows because the intersection is a subset of every A_i. For the lower bound apply the union bound to the failure events: P(∩A_i)=1−P(∪A_iᶜ)≥1−Σ(1−p_i). Probabilities are also nonnegative. The product formula follows only after imposing mutual independence. To see why pairwise independence is insufficient, let X,Y be independent fair bits and set Z=X xor Y. Any pair is independent, but P(X=Y=Z=1)=0 rather than 1/8.

## Worked example: setup

Construct joint laws for 104 events, each with success probability 0.95.

## Worked example: calculation

For the maximal case take every A_i={U≤0.95} for one uniform U; then all-success probability is 0.95. For a zero case let failures be arcs of length 0.05 on the unit circle, starting at i/104. Since adjacent starts are less than 0.05 apart, the arcs cover the circle, so at least one failure always occurs, although each event still succeeds with probability 0.95. Independent Bernoulli variables give the intermediate product.

## Worked example: conclusion

The same individual accuracy is compatible with no chance of complete correctness, a 95 percent chance, or the independent-model value. Replacing a dependency model by repeated multiplication can therefore be a major modeling error even after arithmetic is repaired.

## Limits

The numerical examples are abstract probability constructions, not estimated properties of a genealogy or any real audit. Real dependence requires observations or structural assumptions. Pairwise correlations alone generally do not determine the required high-order intersection.

## Extension

For a specified dependency graph, derive an interval using available joint moments or solve a finite linear program over atomic outcomes. Report the identified interval if the joint probability remains underdetermined.
