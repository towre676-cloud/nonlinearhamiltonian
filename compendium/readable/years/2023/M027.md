## Revised question retained in the annual source

What does neatness ensure for Γ acting on D, and which singularities can still occur after compactification?

## Status

Definition clarified; rank-one example derived

## Summary

Neatness removes finite-order eigenvalue phenomena in an arithmetic group. It controls the open quotient, while boundary smoothness remains a separate geometric issue.

## Definitions

An element is neat when the multiplicative subgroup generated by its complex eigenvalues contains no nontrivial root of unity. A group is neat when each of its elements is.

For N≥3, let Γ(N)={γ∈SL₂(Z):γ≡I mod N}, acting on the upper half-plane H by fractional linear transformations.

Write Y(N)=Γ(N)∖H, using the image of Γ(N) in PSL₂(R) when discussing effective actions and indices.

## Result name

Γ(3) has a free action on H and its quotient is a smooth noncompact curve

## Result statement

For γ=I+3A∈Γ(3), determinant one implies Trγ=2−9det A. Consequently the possible elliptic integer traces are excluded except the identity/unipotent trace two case. Γ(3) is neat in its defining two-dimensional representation and acts freely on H.

## Proof

The identity det(I+3A)=1+3TrA+9detA yields TrA=−3detA. A real SL₂ matrix fixing a point of H is elliptic; an integer elliptic trace lies in {−1,0,1}, apart from the central finite-order endpoints. None is congruent to two modulo nine, and −I has trace −2 and is absent. Nonidentity trace-two unipotents have no interior fixed point. Hyperbolic eigenvalues λ,λ⁻¹ have |λ|≠1, so their generated subgroup has no nontrivial torsion; unipotents contribute only eigenvalue one. Proper discontinuity and absence of stabilizers make the quotient smooth. This argument is specific and does not replace the general theorem that arithmetic groups contain neat finite-index subgroups.

## Worked example: calculation

SL₂(F₃) has 24 elements, so the effective PSL₂ index is twelve. There are four cusps, each of width three. The torsion-free genus relation 2g−2+c=μ/6 then gives g=0 for the smooth compactification X(3). The verifier directly enumerates determinant-one residue matrices.

## Worked example: conclusion

The open curve is smooth and hyperbolic, yet its compactification is genus zero. These properties are compatible.

## Limits

Torsion-free and neat are distinct notions in general linear groups; their coincidence in this rank-one calculation must not be generalized indiscriminately. Smoothness of the open quotient alone does not determine the geometry of a chosen higher-dimensional boundary.

## Extension

For a specified higher-rank group, compare level conditions that guarantee neatness with cone decompositions that guarantee regular toroidal charts. These are two separate certificates.
