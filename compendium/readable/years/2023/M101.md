## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which nonsingular conic defines the point-line correspondence, and what is its dual conic?

## Status

Classical result developed and example checked

## Summary

The corrected construction identifies the quadratic form first, then derives polarity, the dual conic and coordinate covariance. It removes the undefined polar-line formula and the invented idea that one point dualizes to a conic.

## Definitions

Let Q be an invertible symmetric 3×3 matrix over a field of characteristic different from two. Points are nonzero column vectors modulo scale; lines are nonzero covectors modulo scale.

The nonsingular conic is C={x:xᵀQx=0}. The polar line of p has covector ℓ=Qp, because its equation is pᵀQx=0.

## Result name

Polarity and the dual quadratic form

## Result statement

The polarity p↦Qp is invertible, incidence is reciprocal, and the tangent-line locus in the dual projective plane is ℓᵀQ⁻¹ℓ=0.

## Proof

At a conic point a, differentiating xᵀQx gives tangent covector 2Qa, which defines the same projective line as Qa. Conversely any ℓ has pole p=Q⁻¹ℓ. That line is tangent precisely when its pole lies on C, which is exactly ℓᵀQ⁻¹ℓ=0. Reciprocity follows from pᵀQq=qᵀQp. Under x′=Mx, Q′=M⁻ᵀQM⁻¹ and ℓ′=M⁻ᵀℓ; hence Q′p′=M⁻ᵀQp. The construction therefore respects projective coordinate changes rather than depending on one affine drawing.

## Worked example: setup

Use the real unit-circle conic x²+y²−z²=0 and p=(2,0,1).

## Worked example: calculation

Here Q=diag(1,1,−1), so the polar is 2x−z=0. Its intersections with the conic are a±=(1/2,±√3/2,1). The tangents at these points have covectors (1/2,±√3/2,−1), and each contains p because their pairing with p is zero. The polar itself is not tangent: its dual quadratic value is 2²−1²=3.

## Worked example: conclusion

The polar joins the two contact points of tangents drawn from the exterior point. Applying Q⁻¹ to its covector returns p. This verifies the point-line correspondence and the dual-conic test separately.

## Limits

Invertibility of Q and the characteristic assumption matter. A degenerate quadratic form does not produce a global polarity. Real exterior/interior terminology has no direct analogue over every field; projective incidence survives, while real tangent counts may change.

## Extension

For higher-dimensional quadrics the same matrix argument defines polar hyperplanes. A useful further problem is to classify how the construction degenerates as det Q tends to zero and which incidence statements remain valid.
