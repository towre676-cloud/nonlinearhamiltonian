## Revised question retained in the annual source

In which projective space does a smooth hypersurface of degree d have trivial canonical bundle?

## Status

Canonical bundle and characteristic classes computed

## Summary

The phrase two-dimensional Calabi–Yau quintic was not a sufficiently defined geometric object. Adjunction determines which degree and ambient dimension actually produce trivial canonical bundle.

## Definitions

Let X_d be a smooth degree-d hypersurface in complex projective n-space. Its complex dimension is n−1.

Write H=c₁(O_X(1)); the canonical bundle of Pⁿ is O(−n−1).

The conormal sequence is 0→O_X(−d)→Ω¹_Pⁿ|_X→Ω¹_X→0. Smoothness is essential to this vector-bundle form.

## Result name

Adjunction and the smooth quintic calculation

## Result statement

Taking determinants gives K_X=O_X(d−n−1). A smooth quintic in P⁴ has complex dimension three, real dimension six and trivial canonical bundle. A smooth plane quintic instead has genus six. A smooth quartic in P³ is the familiar surface-degree case.

## Proof

The determinant of the conormal sequence is K_Pⁿ|_X=O_X(−d)⊗K_X, yielding the displayed formula. For a plane curve, taking degrees gives 2g−2=d(d−3); d=5 yields g=6. For a smooth quintic threefold, the normal tangent sequence gives c(TX)=(1+H)^5/(1+5H). Expand through degree three: c₁=0, c₂=10H² and c₃=−40H³. Since ∫_X H³=5, its topological Euler characteristic is ∫c₃=−200. Each step depends on the stated smooth hypersurface geometry rather than a guessed number of spatial dimensions.

## Worked example: setup

Compare degree-five hypersurfaces in P² and P⁴.

## Worked example: calculation

The first is a one-complex-dimensional genus-six curve with K=O(2). The second is a three-complex-dimensional hypersurface with K=O. The verifier independently expands the Chern polynomial and recovers coefficients 1,0,10,−40 and Euler characteristic −200.

## Worked example: conclusion

The ambient projective dimension and degree determine the canonical twist; the word quintic alone determines neither a surface nor a Calabi–Yau manifold.

## Limits

A trivial canonical bundle is not the entire definition of Calabi–Yau under every convention. Additional cohomological or holonomy conditions must be stated. The Chern-class calculation supplies no cryptographic hardness claim and no enumeration of all Calabi–Yau geometries.

## Extension

For a smooth complete intersection of multidegree (d₁,…,d_r) in Pⁿ, repeat adjunction to obtain K=O(Σd_i−n−1). Compute its dimension n−r and characteristic classes before proposing algorithms on its coordinate ring or cohomology.
