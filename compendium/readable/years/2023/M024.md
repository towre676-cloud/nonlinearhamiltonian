## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which operator is diagonalized by which polynomial family, with respect to what positive measure?

## Status

Classical family with exact low-degree verification

## Summary

A recurrence with positive coefficients constructs a spectral measure and explains orthogonality. This is a precise operator-polynomial connection rather than a generic appeal to q-special functions.

## Definitions

Fix 0<q<1. Let H₀=1, H₁=2x and H_{n+1}=2xH_n−(1−qⁿ)H_{n−1}.

On ℓ² of the nonnegative integers, define a bounded self-adjoint Jacobi operator J by off-diagonal entries a_n=sqrt(1−qⁿ)/2 for n≥1 and zero diagonal.

Let μ_q be the probability spectral measure of J at e₀. Since ||J||≤1, its support lies in [−1,1].

## Result name

Orthogonality and exact norms

## Result statement

The recurrence gives H_n(J)e₀=Π_{j=1}^n sqrt(1−qʲ)e_n. Hence ∫H_mH_n dμ_q=δ_{mn}Π_{j=1}^n(1−qʲ). In particular μ_q has second moment (1−q)/4 and fourth moment (1−q)(2−q−q²)/16.

## Proof

The spectral theorem identifies integration of a polynomial with its matrix element at e₀. Induct on n using 2Je_n=sqrt(1−q^{n+1})e_{n+1}+sqrt(1−qⁿ)e_{n−1}; the recurrence cancels the lower basis vector. Orthogonality then follows from orthogonality of e_n, and the norm is the squared coefficient product. For moments, count weighted return walks of length two and four from zero. At length two there is one path; at length four the paths 0→1→0→1→0 and 0→1→2→1→0 contribute a₁⁴ and a₁²a₂².

## Worked example: setup

Take q=1/2 and compute H₀ through H₅ using rational coefficients.

## Worked example: calculation

The probability measure has moments μ₂=1/8 and μ₄=5/128. Exact weighted-walk moments verify every pairing of H₀,…,H₅; squared norms begin 1,1/2,3/8,21/64. The finite computation does not numerically approximate an infinite oscillatory integral.

## Worked example: conclusion

A recurrence, an operator and a measure now support the same calculation. The cited family name is backed by a fully stated normalization.

## Limits

The Jacobi construction proves the displayed orthogonality; it does not itself reproduce every q-difference identity or special-function representation. Changing q outside the stated interval may change positivity or moment determinacy.

## Extension

Set x=sqrt(1−q)z/2 and divide H_n by (1−q)^(n/2). The recurrence becomes K_{n+1}=zK_n−[n]_qK_{n−1}, which tends coefficientwise to the probabilists’ Hermite recurrence as q→1. Analyze convergence of the rescaled measures as a separate limiting problem.
