## Revised question retained in the annual source

Which feedback law generates instability or amplification, and what quantity controls its threshold?

## Status

Classical result developed and worked

## Summary

Feedback questions become testable once an update matrix and a measured norm are fixed. Eigenvalues determine asymptotic decay in finite dimension, but they do not bound all finite-time amplification. A two-dimensional Jordan block makes the distinction completely explicit.

## Definitions

A discrete linear system is xₙ₊₁=Axₙ. Spectral radius ρ(A) is the largest eigenvalue modulus. The Euclidean amplification at step n is the induced norm ||Aⁿ||₂. A real matrix is normal when AAᵀ=AᵀA.

## Result name

Exact transient amplification in an asymptotically stable system

## Result statement

For A=aI+bN with N=[[0,1],[0,0]], N²=0 and 0<a<1, Aⁿ=aⁿI+n aⁿ⁻¹bN. Although ρ(A)=a<1 and Aⁿ→0, intermediate norms can be arbitrarily large as |b| increases.

## Proof

The binomial theorem applies because I and N commute and every power Nᵏ with k≥2 vanishes. Only the terms k=0 and k=1 survive. The scalar sequences aⁿ and n aⁿ⁻¹ both tend to zero, proving asymptotic stability. Acting on e₂ yields Aⁿe₂=(n aⁿ⁻¹b,aⁿ), so its Euclidean norm supplies a lower bound on ||Aⁿ||₂. At a fixed step n this lower bound grows linearly in |b| without changing either eigenvalue.

## Worked example: setup

Choose A=[[1/2,4],[0,1/2]] and initial state e₂.

## Worked example: calculation

At one step the output is (4,1/2), with norm √65/2>4. At two steps the output is (4,1/4), although the eigenvalues remain 1/2. The exact formula predicts eventual decay after this transient. The corresponding continuous example A_c=[[-1,4],[0,−1]] has exp(tA_c)=e^(−t)[[1,4t],[0,1]].

## Worked example: conclusion

A stability statement and a peak-amplification statement answer different questions. A mechanism for a large transient can be proved without asserting any probability distribution for external shocks.

## Limits

The calculation assumes a fixed finite matrix. Nonlinear saturation, delayed feedback, time-varying matrices and stochastic inputs require additional models. A large matrix norm does not by itself imply a particular event occurs.

## Extension

Optimize supₙ||Aⁿ|| over a parameter family, then compare Euclidean bounds with a quadratic Lyapunov norm that contracts at every step.
