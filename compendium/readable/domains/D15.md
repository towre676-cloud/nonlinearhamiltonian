## Organizing question

What can be recovered or controlled from declared observations, at what cost and with what guarantee?

## Overview

The central object in this domain is a requested target seen through an observation. Full-state reconstruction is only one possible target and often demands unnecessary measurements. In finite dimensions, exact target recovery is equivalent to a kernel inclusion and a factorization G=LH. The minimum recovery norm quantifies the amplification of bounded measurement error. A violating invisible vector is an equally useful certificate: it proves that two states have the same data and different targets. The late WIND and observation-geometry topics sharpen this perspective, while the earlier recurrence questions already show its algebraic roots.

Present recovery, prediction and nonlinear identifiability are successive obligations. A present factorization does not make the observation a closed dynamical state; HA=TH additionally requires the invisible kernel to be invariant. Moving observation coordinates acquire B′. A vanishing hidden directional derivative at one point does not establish target constancy on an entire nonlinear fibre. Counting the coefficients of a local wind ansatz does not guarantee a full-rank beam design. These distinctions organize practical sensing questions into exact mathematical tasks instead of letting one rank statistic carry all of their meaning.

Control introduces the dual constructive problem: choose inputs that change the state or target with a certified cost. Gramians characterize finite-horizon reachability and observability without assuming asymptotic stability. Lyapunov identities establish decay in a compatible metric, including nonnormal examples whose Euclidean energy grows transiently. Completion of squares gives full scalar Riccati value proofs with terminal conditions. Descriptor pencils show why the actual constrained model matters: singular E can encode a regular problem, while adding εI can create an unstable artificial mode. These examples make matrix identities useful certificates of behavior, not merely computational diagnostics.

Inverse problems replace exact inversion by controlled approximation. The diagonal models expose the bias-noise split and matching indistinguishable-pair lower bounds. Polynomially decaying singular values give a power rate; exponentially decaying values require a logarithmic rate and an explicit safety margin. Tikhonov’s parameter and Landweber’s stopping time are two ways to choose the same compromise. The guarantee always names a source class and noise model. Neither a tiny residual nor a leading-order cutoff equivalence supplies those assumptions.

Finally, the experiment-design family asks what information should be purchased next. A finite Bellman theorem gives a policy and its matching worst-case lower bound when tests are deterministic and targets explicit. Correlated ellipsoids and hidden conditional variation explain why some extra observations are useful while repeated deficient directions remain irrelevant. L01 combines a fixed linear quotient test with cheapest static probe panels, L04 explores cutoff envelopes, and L08 retains shared uncertainty in robust decisions. These labs demonstrate exact bounded capacities. General nonlinear reconstruction, continuous noisy adaptive design and historical sensor-performance claims require their own supplied models, data and validation arguments.
