## Organizing question

Which state, product, coherence equation or spectral model is actually implemented, and what finite certificate proves its claimed behaviour?

## Overview

This domain brings together quantum states and channels, finite noncommutative systems, deformation quantization, braid and fusion data, integrability and formal obstruction theory. Their common feature is that an appealing symbolic relation becomes useful only after its model class and normalization are fixed. The six families supply explicit finite examples and derive their identities, while distinguishing mathematical structure from physical realization, asymptotic convergence and general classification.

Quantum states make the atlas-wide observation problem especially concrete. The Bell state and its dephased mixture agree on all diagonal observations and both one-qubit marginals, but an off-diagonal correlation separates them. This gives a complete finite collision and a constructive additional measurement. Kraus formulas then supply the correct transformation standard: complete positivity is preserved with arbitrary ancillas, and the completeness equation preserves total probability. Instruments retain every measurement branch, so a conditional normalized success state cannot conceal its success probability. Error correction similarly depends on the specified error list, as the repetition code's exact bit-flip correction coexists with quantum distance one.

Finite Weyl systems identify what can hold exactly in finite dimension. Trace forbids the canonical additive commutator, and determinants force a multiplicative commutation phase to be a root of unity. Clock and shift matrices then generate a full finite matrix algebra. Binary symplectic kernels compute Pauli symmetries, while finite quadratic forms and Gauss sums retain the central phase in a projective modular relation. These calculations distinguish finite rational models from irrational operator algebras and distinguish alternating commutation forms from symmetric quadratic polarizations.

Polynomial Moyal calculus supplies the most direct implemented interaction. Its formal star product has exact finite falling-factorial coefficients for monomials. Degree bounds determine which odd corrections can survive, so the exact degree-four family and a genuine fifth-order obstruction are compatible. The new bounded sextic monomial atlas exhausts a declared 784-pair universe and exposes four cases missed by the cubic test. Its complete bounded scope is valuable precisely because general polynomial cancellations and unbounded higher degree remain separate questions.

Braid and fusion examples then make coherence visible. An algebraic Hecke matrix needs no unitarity, a fusion ring needs no associator, and an associator needs pentagon and hexagon equations. The four-object pointed model provides complete finite data and proofs, while Temperley–Lieb Gram forms test positivity independently. Integrable models provide another bridge between descriptions: a finite XXX matrix agrees with regular Bethe roots when the infinite-root state is included, and a two-site Gaudin eigenvector matches a scalar differential potential. KdV tau-functions illustrate nonlinear interaction through a verified exponential coefficient identity.

Finally, obstruction theory explains why first-order success is insufficient. A characteristic-two character cannot lift, a free rank-two family has jumping Hochschild cohomology and an order-twelve equation can fail at order thirteen. These are exact worked models, not negative verdicts about unrecovered larger objects. The domain's interactive capacities are therefore bounded and auditable: compute, classify within a stated finite universe, inspect a witness and identify the next unresolved equation.
