## Organizing question

Which objective, feasible set and certificate turn a proposed choice into a justified global decision?

## Overview

Optimization unifies much of the archive only after its inputs are typed. An objective assigns preference, a feasible set defines admissible alternatives, and a certificate supplies a bound valid over all of them. Algorithms search for candidates but do not replace these definitions. This domain is arranged from global convex certificates through constructive updates and finite search to robust decisions under shared uncertainty. It connects the practical question of choosing an action with the mathematical question of which uncertainties can change that action.

Convex duality supplies the cleanest starting point. A feasible primal point and a feasible dual bound can prove global optimality when their values agree. The constrained covariance quadratic makes nullspaces central: a feasible zero-energy null vector changes the answer completely, while a budget orthogonal to the nullspace leads to a pseudoinverse formula. Fenchel conjugates expose the effect of domains and nonsmooth penalties. An SDP zero-gap pair provides a finite exact certificate even when an iterative solver's convergence is irrelevant. In contrast, a stationary cubic equation or an indefinite quadratic requires a separate global analysis.

Proximal and projected methods turn an objective into a reproducible update. Soft thresholding, radial shrinkage and ridge scaling differ because their regularizers differ. The projection variational inequality and smooth descent lemma give a direct finite-step bound. A resolvent step damps curvature according to its eigenvalues and needs a positive quadratic model. Graph heat flow makes the distinction between continuous dissipation and discrete stability explicit. Metric projections, subspace restrictions and Wasserstein pushforwards add useful geometry while preserving their own feasibility and contraction hypotheses. These ideas explain why clipping a gradient, projecting a state and constraining a trajectory are different operations.

Objective design and finite search then expose the combinatorial side. The total-scatter identity can collapse seemingly independent clustering weights into the same objective. Capacity constraints and weighted preferences require different formulations. Pareto nondominance contains legitimate alternatives that no positive linear selector can choose. Exact QUBO enumeration and min-plus transfer provide finite certificates of a chosen problem. Frontier and equivalence-class compression are valid when they preserve complete continuation behavior, not merely a current best answer. Search error, physical-model error and measurement error consequently remain separate parts of an implementation claim.

The final family asks for the cheapest information that settles a decision. When two scores share nuisance uncertainty, subtract them at the same state before optimizing. Their joint difference can have a strict positive lower bound even when separately computed intervals overlap widely. Measurements restrict the compatible-state set, and a finite experiment tree can stop when each remaining fibre certifies one action. The implemented L08 model makes this cancellation visible without claiming a general optimizer or empirical forecast. This endpoint joins the other domains: exact counting can enumerate alternatives, observability can expose decisive fibres, covariance can describe noise, and duality can certify a robust margin. The result is an organized program of mathematical choices whose guarantees state their actual scope.
