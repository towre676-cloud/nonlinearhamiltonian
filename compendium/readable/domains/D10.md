## Organizing question

What is the complete law, and which operations preserve or transform the uncertainty needed by the question?

## Overview

The probability material is organized by mathematical operation rather than by a single chronological narrative. A model first specifies its sample space and law. Expectations then integrate against that law, dependence connects its coordinates, transitions evolve it in time, and limit theorems approximate it under explicit scalings. This order makes the archive readable without making every probabilistic topic an instance of one favored physical metaphor. The four families progress from compatible outcomes to combinatorial randomness, from processes to asymptotic simplification.

The first family supplies the indispensable foundation. Marginal event probabilities determine an expected count but usually leave the probability of complete success unresolved. Population weights enter an expected square through square roots in its Euclidean representation; amplitude attenuation is a different operation. Posterior predictive dependence gives a second mechanism: future outcomes independent conditional on a latent parameter become dependent after it is integrated out. These exact finite distinctions continue through the three-item surplus model at the end of the source period. Their significance is constructive: the revised question can ask for the range over all admissible couplings instead of assuming an unprovided independence law.

Random sampling and filtrations move the same discipline into combinatorics and topology. Weighted fixed-cardinality samples are normalized by elementary symmetric polynomials, and inclusion probabilities yield expected Euler characteristic by linearity. A random barcode needs more: the actual birth rule and boundary ranks. The four-cycle fixture makes one complete persistence distribution accessible, while the general weighted formula remains an explicit mathematical recipe. This is an important boundary between a finite worked lab and a universal persistent-homology tool.

Stochastic dynamics specify how the law changes. A generator determines expectation evolution, while Itô's second derivative produces an extra term invisible to deterministic differentiation. The OU process provides exact solutions, stationary variances and a transparent comparison with Euler–Maruyama. Linear SDE propagation separates present posterior uncertainty from fresh process noise. Sensor choices are meaningful only when compared against one prior, one target and one terminal horizon. A driver sensor can beat a direct present-target sensor for predicting a future target without violating any monotonicity theorem.

The final family studies simplification under scaling. Finite variance yields the Gaussian averaging fixed point; Cauchy stability shows why that conclusion cannot be universal. Logarithms turn random products into sums with their own transform threshold. Trace-summable Gaussian covariances permit genuine Hilbert-space samples, whereas finite-dimensional consistency alone permits only cylindrical data. Direct couplings give a different approximation method, bounding Lipschitz observables without a central limit theorem. Across all four families, exact finite identities, limiting laws, numerical approximations and empirical calibration remain distinct claims. The atlas executes representative model checks and labels the broader extensions as recipes.
