## Organizing question

What becomes computable once the metric, form, connection and symmetry are explicitly declared?

## Overview

This domain connects local geometric structure to calculable global behavior. The first input is a declared metric, differential form or connection. Coordinates alone supply none of these. Once a metric is given, one can derive length, geodesics, curvature, volume growth and spectral scaling; once a symplectic form and Hamiltonian are given, one can derive a vector field and test conservation. This order of construction turns the archive’s geometric vocabulary into a set of mathematical interfaces with explicit assumptions and outputs.

Metric geometry separates representation changes from genuine geometric deformation. The hyperbolic half-plane has computable Christoffel symbols, vertical unit-speed geodesics and curvature minus one. Its exponential volume growth prevents a global Euclidean doubling argument even though every smooth point is locally Euclidean in the appropriate sense. Constant scaling links length, curvature and spectral thresholds through exact powers. Discrete transport curvature and prefix ultrametrics then show how different metric inputs answer different questions: probability measures matter for Ollivier curvature, while digit carries obstruct the inverse inference from real distance to prefix agreement. Neither a positive weight table nor an unexplained singular value is a metric-independent invariant.

Symplectic and contact geometry organize mechanics and reduction. Conservation of one Hamiltonian does not make it a Casimir. Removing a cyclic coordinate is a legitimate reduction only at a fixed momentum level, where the restricted two-form’s null directions are precisely the symmetry directions being quotiented. A regular moment-map quotient can be identified with a cotangent bundle in explicit charts. The oscillator demonstrates another kind of geometric change: a generating function blows up at a projection caustic while the underlying phase-space trajectory remains smooth. A Legendrian curve and a thermodynamic potential similarly require the contact form and tangent condition before their consequences follow.

Connections supply transport, curvature measures local two-dimensional noncommutation, and holonomy records a global loop. A flat circle system can have nontrivial monodromy; a curved connection on the plane can live on a trivial bundle; a Berry eigenline over the sphere can obstruct a global smooth potential. The exact shear loop makes noncommutativity and gauge conjugacy visible in finite matrices. It is the appropriate worked model for the interactive atlas, with a clear gap between this calculation and an empirical connection whose actual matrices or two-dimensional refinement are unavailable.

Curves and variational geometry complete the domain by integrating local data into shapes. Frenet frames are rotation equations tied to regularity, moving affine frames introduce explicit derivative terms, and linear curvature uniquely minimizes one declared curvature-rate functional. Endpoint positions or tangents add constraints and create a different problem. The same care with assumptions governs Willmore normalization and the distinction between immersions and embeddings. The source dates support continuity from 2023 Berry and affine questions through the 2024 geometric forms to the 2026 exact transport and reduction examples. The present proofs unify those dated topics without attributing new historical arguments. The open frontier is the construction of declared higher-dimensional metrics, transports and quotient data with quantitative verification.
