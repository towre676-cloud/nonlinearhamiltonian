## Organizing question

Which global features survive local changes, and which exact obstruction prevents a global construction?

## Overview

The topological domain is organized around two constructive tasks: calculate an invariant of a specified space, and identify an obstruction to assembling local information globally. The first begins with cells and boundaries. A graph, the surface on which it is drawn and a filling of its cycles are different spaces. Their boundary matrices make the distinction exact. A triangle boundary has a one-dimensional cycle; attaching its face kills that class. The same principle scales to flag complexes, polyhedral boundaries and singular links, where the problem is to state the actual attachment data before assigning a homology group.

Coefficients determine what the calculation can see. Rational Betti numbers retain free rank but erase torsion. Integer normal forms supply the finite abelian groups hidden by that compression, and reduction modulo a prime can reveal a different pattern again. Reduced homology and intersection homology refine different aspects of a space: one adjusts the degree-zero basepoint contribution, while the other controls allowable chains near specified singular strata. The contractible cone on a torus makes the latter concrete, because ordinary bounding disks may be disallowed at the apex. These distinctions improve classification questions by matching the invariant to the equivalence relation and phenomenon under study.

Persistence adds maps and time to this framework. It retains finite-lived classes that disappear in the eventual space. A legal filtration orders cells after their faces, and induced maps determine births and deaths. Small exact examples support a clear interactive model, while shifted sublevel inclusions give a direct perturbation certificate. Random entry times connect topological lifetimes to order statistics in the simple square example. The scope stays explicit: a stable barcode is a controlled invariant of the declared filtration, not a complete reconstruction of the geometry or a validation of the mechanism generating the data.

The local-to-global side starts with the sheaf axiom’s actual content: compatible data glue uniquely. Arbitrary discrepancies need not be compatible, and their failure can occupy a nonzero cohomology group. Incidence equations, cycle periods and rank-one transport give complete exact examples. Discrete Hodge theory chooses harmonic representatives after an inner product is declared; it does not make every harmonic vector a zero class. Consequently a small residual or a successful test vector cannot certify disappearance of an entire obstruction space. A comparison between local models needs a cochain equivalence if it is to preserve those classes.

Periods, group multipliers and characteristic classes connect this algebraic computation to geometric invariance. Rephasing changes a multiplier by a coboundary, nonvanishing loop homotopies preserve winding, and normalized bundle curvature represents characteristic classes. The family treatments keep their degrees and coefficients separate while exposing their shared local-choice versus global-defect mechanism. This makes the domain’s interactive capacity concrete: supply a finite complex or transport system, and return a cycle, a potential, a barcode or a nonzero obstruction certificate. The frontier is to recover missing attachment, coefficient and comparison data in larger historical proposals rather than infer topology from terminology.
