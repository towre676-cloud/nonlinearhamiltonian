## Organizing question

What algebraic structure is specified, and which exact maps or identities justify the invariant being claimed?

## Overview

Algebra provides the atlas with a language for separating a structure from its numerical shadow. A carrier with a multiplication law is more information than a cardinality; a representation is more information than a dimension; a spectral decomposition is more information than an eigenvalue list. Across the source years, the strongest developments begin when the multiplication, action, field and equivalence relation become explicit. The six families in this domain organise those developments around operations, linear certificates, symmetry, bracket structure, finite local models and operator states.

Typed operations come first because they determine which equations even make sense. Transporting a field through a bijection produces a valid isomorphic field, while merely changing notation or mixing transported and ordinary operations does not. Capped counters create finite monoids with an absorbing top layer and a minimal multiplication ideal; they do not automatically create a vector-space nullspace. Ring quotients, maximal ideals and squarefree branch decompositions then show how discarded information is certified by an actual homomorphism. The finite cases are useful because every product and every ideal condition can be replayed.

Linear structure supplies a second layer of certificates. Rank needs both upper and lower witnesses, singular values need declared metrics, and projectors need their idempotent and annihilation equations. A squarefree annihilating polynomial yields a decomposition only after checking the polynomial identity, and every advertised spectral point still needs nonzero support. These distinctions connect algebra to observation and stability throughout the atlas. They also prevent exact rational rank, integral torsion and floating effective rank from being treated as the same quantity.

Symmetry becomes productive through actual actions. Finite averaging gives invariant projectors; orbit counts give stabilizers; characters and branching specify which modules occur. The B₄ action over F₃ has a complete Hamming-weight classification, while a signed-permutation invariant quadratic form must be scalar. Neither result forces an empirical dataset rank or a unique frequency ratio on a multiplicity space. Exceptional symmetry is retained at its honest level: the E₈ root accounting and 120+128 decomposition are exact, but an exceptional bracket needs equivariant maps and Jacobi beyond a dimension count. Octonions sharpen this lesson by providing a nonassociative product with a sharp, convention-sensitive defect theorem.

Finite local and path algebras add the geometry of relations. The four-dimensional jet algebra needs cubic closure; its symmetric Hilbert function coexists with a two-dimensional socle. The rank-thirteen quiver pencil has a global path-bundle proof of flatness, while its full Hochschild profile remains another calculation. Finally, faithful finite operator states supply modular flow and KMS identities in a setting where all formulas can be derived directly. These finite models connect outward to categories, geometry, quantum theory and inference through explicit maps. The domain therefore aims at inspectable structure and proof, with larger classification and infinite analytic problems stated as precise frontiers rather than inferred from familiar names.
