## Central question

Which graph features survive an observation, and what extra measurement separates a known collision?

## Synopsis

The graph thread develops from a warning about incomplete spectral observations into an exact hierarchy of finite invariants. A graph can be encoded by its adjacency matrix, its Laplacian, a heat operator, a heat trace, diagonal heat signatures, binary cut statistics or integer-height energy coefficients. These are different observation maps. Equality at one level need not imply equality at a richer level, and a numerical agreement at several parameters does not prove equality of a polynomial invariant. The productive method is to construct a full exact collision, identify the discarded correlations, then design a stronger finite observation. Incidence identities provide a parallel constructive route: singular values of a point-line incidence matrix determine the spectrum of its bipartite Levi graph. The centered Green matrix adds a variational capacity, translating a graph operator into an exact energy required to force one coordinate while maintaining zero mean.

## Definitions

For a simple graph G, L=D−A and E_G(h)=hᵀLh=Σ_{uv∈E}(h_u−h_v)^2.

The binary census records all pairs (Σh_i,E_G(h)) for h∈{0,1}^V; on binary heights E_G is a cut boundary size.

The formal integer-height series F_G(x,q)=Σ_{h∈Z_≥0^V}x^{Σh_i}q^{E_G(h)} has finitely many terms at every fixed x-degree.

For λ>0 and Π=I−11ᵀ/n, the centered Green matrix K=Π(λI+L)^{-1}Π is the inverse of λI+L on 1⊥.

## Theorem

The explicit seven-vertex graphs G and H below have identical complete binary size-energy censuses but different integer-height series: [x³](F_G−F_H)=q³(1−q)(1−q³)²(1−q⁴)². Separately, for any graph with n≥2, min{vᵀ(λI+L)v:1ᵀv=0,v_i=a}=a²/K_ii.

## Proof

For the collision, enumerate every one of the 128 binary subsets and store its size and cut size as an integer histogram. For total height R enumerate all weak compositions of R into seven coordinates, recording energy. Exact subtraction at R=0,1,2 is zero; at R=3 its coefficient polynomial factors as stated. This finite computation is a proof certificate for these explicit graphs. For the variational theorem, QK=Π on 1⊥. Thus v_i=(Ke_i)ᵀQv and Cauchy–Schwarz in the positive Q-inner product gives a²≤K_ii vᵀQv. Equality holds at v=aKe_i/K_ii; its mean is zero and its ith component is a. The inverse is positive on 1⊥, so K_ii>0. This proves both the sharp bound and its unique minimizer.

## Worked example: setup

Use G={(0,3),(0,4),(0,6),(1,2),(1,3),(2,3),(2,4),(3,5)} and H={(0,1),(0,4),(0,6),(1,5),(1,6),(2,6),(3,6),(4,5)}. For a second model use the path P₃ and λ=2.

## Worked example: calculation

The census collision is exact over all binary assignments. At q=1/2, the first height separation equals (1/8)(1/2)(7/8)^2(15/16)^2=11025/262144. Support-three terms already cancel because they are binary; support-one terms cancel through the degree census, so the discrepancy comes from support two. For P₃, exact inversion gives diag K=(1/5,2/15,1/5). Forcing an endpoint to a under zero mean therefore costs 5a², while forcing the center costs 15a²/2. As a separate incidence example, the Fano matrix satisfies BBᵀ=2I+J, giving Heawood adjacency eigenvalues ±3 and ±√2, with the latter each sixfold.

## Worked example: meaning

The same graph can support exact reconstruction obstructions, new distinguishing observations and sharp constrained energy calculations; each conclusion is tied to a particular observation map.

## Limits

Finite spectral moments need not determine a graph. The five-vertex uniqueness theorem uses its finite simple-graph scope; it is not a universal inverse spectral theorem. A distance-regular intersection array yields a conditional spectrum only if the graph and relation matrices satisfy the declared axioms. No unspecified Euler product, singularity or graph-to-knot correspondence is inferred.
