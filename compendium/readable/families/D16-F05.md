## Central question

How can an integrability claim be anchored simultaneously in an explicit operator and a verified scalar equation?

## Synopsis

The integrable strand contains two complementary constructive bridges. A finite quantum spin chain can be diagonalized directly and compared with its Bethe parameterization. A classical nonlinear wave can be built from a tau-function whose bilinear identity fixes its interaction coefficient. In both cases a normalization connects descriptions that would otherwise remain a slogan. The bridge is useful because each side supplies an independent exact residual.

The four-site XXX chain has a one-magnon sector where the Hamiltonian becomes the cycle Laplacian. Fourier modes diagonalize it, and the finite regular Bethe roots reproduce all nonuniform modes. The uniform mode is present as an infinite-root limit, exposing why a list of finite roots need not be complete. The two-site Gaudin model offers a different spectral-to-differential bridge: a Bethe root supplies a singlet eigenvector and a Miura potential in which the apparent pole cancels exactly. The eigenvalues appear as residues at the physical marked points. This is a complete small calculation, rather than a proof of arbitrary Bethe completeness or a global automorphic correspondence.

The tau-function side similarly distinguishes interaction from superposition. For KdV, two independent one-soliton exponentials need a mixed exponential term, and its coefficient is forced by the Hirota polynomial. Positivity of the coefficients makes the real tau-function nonsingular. The same construction gives phase shifts and mass by asymptotics and boundary values of logτ. A y-independent solution embeds in the chosen KP equation, but transverse stability and general two-dimensional interactions are additional problems. The unifying capacity is not a universal solver: it is the ability to compare a finite spectral model, a scalar differential operator or a finite exponential identity under explicitly matched conventions.

## Definitions

In the periodic XXX one-magnon sector, H|j⟩=2|j⟩−|j−1⟩−|j+1⟩ with indices modulo N.

For the two-site sl₂ Gaudin model at z₁=0,z₂=1, Ω=h⊗h/2+e⊗f+f⊗e, H₁=−Ω,H₂=Ω.

For k₁>k₂>0, η_i=k_ix−k_i³t+δ_i and τ=1+e^{η₁}+e^{η₂}+A e^{η₁+η₂}, A=((k₁−k₂)/(k₁+k₂))².

Use u=2∂_x²logτ and the KdV convention u_t+6uu_x+u_xxx=0.

## Theorem

For N=4, the one-magnon XXX sector has eigenvalues 0,2,2,4, with finite regular Bethe roots 1/2,0,−1/2 for the nonzero modes. The stated KdV tau-function satisfies (D_xD_t+D_x⁴)τ·τ=0; A is forced by the mixed exponential coefficient.

## Proof

For a plane wave with p=2πm/N, substitution into the cycle difference gives E=2−2cos p. With N=4 this is 0,2,4,2. The one-root Bethe relation is ((λ+i/2)/(λ−i/2))⁴=1, so λ=(1/2)cot(p/2), giving 1/2,0,−1/2; p=0 is the infinite limit. For KdV use P(k,ω)=kω+k⁴. Each individual exponent obeys P(k_i,−k_i³)=0. The only new mixed condition is A·P(k₁+k₂,−k₁³−k₂³)+P(k₁−k₂,−k₁³+k₂³)=0. The two polynomial values are 3k₁k₂(k₁+k₂)² and −3k₁k₂(k₁−k₂)², which force A. The remaining exponential coefficients reduce to these equations. Dividing the bilinear identity by τ² and differentiating gives KdV. Since all four tau coefficients are positive, τ>0 on real x,t, so this solution has no real tau zeros.

## Worked example: setup

Use the integer cycle Hamiltonian at N=4 and the two-site Gaudin Bethe root w=1/2.

## Worked example: calculation

The vectors (1,1,1,1),(1,0,−1,0),(0,1,0,−1),(1,−1,1,−1) have exact eigenvalues 0,2,2,4 and form a basis. For Gaudin, w⁻¹|↓↑⟩+(w−1)⁻¹|↑↓⟩=2|↓↑⟩−2|↑↓⟩ is a singlet with Ω eigenvalue −3/2. Thus H₁,H₂ have values 3/2,−3/2. With u(x)=1/(2x)+1/(2(x−1))−1/(x−1/2), direct rational simplification gives u²−u′=3/[4x²(x−1)²]. The x=1/2 pole disappears because the Bethe equation 1/w+1/(w−1)=0 cancels its residue. This gives a scalar-oper certificate tied to the same eigenvector.

## Worked example: meaning

Independent operator and scalar identities are a strong finite bridge. Their scope is the stated sector, solution family and marked-point normalization.

## Limits

No arbitrary-sector Bethe completeness, full Gaudin theorem or all-KP classification is proved. Coincident soliton parameters and singular roots require their own limiting treatment. The source years do not evidence a continuous year-by-year integrability project.

## Open questions
