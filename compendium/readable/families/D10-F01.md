## Central question

Which parts of a conclusion follow from marginal probabilities, and which require a full joint law?

## Synopsis

A probability model is an assignment of mass to compatible outcomes before it is a formula for an average. The earliest finite probability questions, the distinction between population and amplitude weights, reliability calculations, Bayesian prediction and the final three-item surplus model all converge on this point. Linearity of expectation survives dependence, whereas the probability of a conjunction generally does not. The most productive revision is to preserve the entire compatibility structure until the requested functional has been calculated. A mean, a variance, a tail probability and an optimal action use different parts of that structure. This family therefore treats uncertainty as a mathematical input with explicitly stated moments, rather than as a single decorative error bar.

## Definitions

A finite law is p(ω)≥0 with Σωp(ω)=1; E f=Σωp(ω)f(ω). A covariance requires finite second moments.

For Bernoulli indicators B_i, marginal success probabilities p_i=E B_i specify only n linear constraints on their joint table.

Population-weighted energy is Σp_i|a_i|²=‖diag(√p)a‖²; replacing √p by p changes the quantity.

A posterior predictive probability integrates a conditional law against the posterior; its repeated outcomes can be dependent after the latent parameter is integrated out.

## Theorem

For events A_1,…,A_n with probabilities p_i, max(0,Σp_i−n+1)≤P(∩A_i)≤min_i p_i. For any integrable B_i, EΣB_i=ΣE B_i without independence. If p is random, conditionally independent future Bernoulli outcomes have unconditional covariance Var(p), so their joint success probability is E p² rather than (E p)².

## Proof

The upper intersection bound follows from containment. For the lower bound apply the union bound to the complementary events: P(∩A_i)=1−P(∪A_i^c)≥1−Σ(1−p_i), then combine with nonnegativity. Linearity is a finite rearrangement of sums, or the linearity of the Lebesgue integral. For two future indicators, conditional independence gives E[B_1B_2|p]=p², and the tower property yields E B_1B_2=E p². Each mean is E p; subtraction gives Var(p). Thus independence conditional on one variable cannot be silently transported through marginalization. The proof also identifies the missing datum: a coupling or a latent-parameter distribution.

## Worked example: setup

Three indicators each have success probability 4/5, and an illustrative surplus is Π=10(B_1+B_2+B_3)−95/4.

## Worked example: calculation

The mean is 30·4/5−95/4=1/4 for every compatible coupling. Profit requires three successes, whose probability lies between 2/5 and 4/5. The lower endpoint is attained by assigning mass 2/5 to 111 and 1/5 each to 110,101,011. The upper endpoint is attained by mass 4/5 at 111 and 1/5 at 000. Independence instead yields 64/125. In a Beta(4,2) posterior, the mean success probability is 2/3 but the probability of two future successes is 4·5/(6·7)=10/21, exceeding 4/9 by 2/63.

## Worked example: meaning

A positive expectation and a frequent positive outcome are separate objectives. These exact fixtures give a practical diagnostic: ask whether the claimed conclusion remains constant over all joint laws with the supplied marginals. If it does not, the dependence model belongs in the question itself. This is also the common starting point for distributionally robust decisions.

## Limits

Finite moment calculations do not establish that observations follow the proposed law. A Cauchy sample mean remains Cauchy because its characteristic function is stable under averaging, so even increasing sample size does not repair a nonexistent mean. Signed weights need not define probability or positive energy. Marginal confidence statements also require simultaneous coverage before they define a valid joint uncertainty set.

## Open questions

Characterize extremizing couplings when both marginals and pairwise intersections are fixed.

For a specified posterior family, bound the gap E p^k−(E p)^k and state how it changes a chosen finite loss.
