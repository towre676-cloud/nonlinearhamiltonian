## Central question

How can a declared system carry a complete stability, reachability or optimal-control certificate?

## Synopsis

Control claims become concrete only after the plant, allowed inputs, output and state domain are specified. A positive Lyapunov matrix proves decay in one chosen metric; stable eigenvalues alone need not make Euclidean energy decrease at every instant. Finite-horizon Gramians quantify reachability and observation without requiring asymptotic stability. Riccati equations certify quadratic control values through completion of squares, including the terminal condition. Descriptor systems add algebraic constraints: a singular mass matrix can encode a regular constrained evolution, and replacing it by an invertible approximation can introduce a wholly new unstable mode. The common constructive capacity is to certify the property of the actual model with exact matrix identities. It is not enough to import a gain, a condition number or a contraction claim from a nearby surrogate. Norm bounds and dynamics must refer to the same metric and the same invariant domain.

## Definitions

For x′=Ax, V(x)=xᵀPx with P≻0 is a quadratic energy. AᵀP+PA≼−Q, Q≻0, gives a quantitative decay estimate.

For x′=Ax+Bu, W_c(T)=∫_0^T e^{At}BBᵀe^{Aᵀt}dt; the observation Gramian is W_o(T)=∫_0^T e^{Aᵀt}CᵀCe^{At}dt.

A descriptor system E x′=Ax has regular pencil when det(sE−A) is not identically zero. Consistent initial states must satisfy its algebraic constraints.

For scalar x′=u and cost ∫(x²+u²)dt, a value coefficient P solves a Riccati equation; the admissible horizon and terminal value are part of its definition.

## Theorem

If P≻0 and AᵀP+PA=−Q with Q≻0, then V(t)≤e^{−λ_min(Q)t/λ_max(P)}V(0). For any T>0, W_c(T)≻0 iff [B,AB,…,A^{n−1}B] has rank n; the analogous observation statement holds. For x′=u on [0,T] with zero terminal cost, P(t)=tanh(T−t) gives the optimal feedback u=−P(t)x and value P(0)x(0)².

## Proof

Differentiate V to get V′=−xᵀQx≤−[λ_min(Q)/λ_max(P)]V and integrate. For the Gramian, vᵀW_cv=∫||Bᵀe^{Aᵀt}v||²dt. It vanishes exactly when this analytic function is zero on the interval, equivalently Bᵀ(Aᵀ)^kv=0 for every k. Cayley–Hamilton reduces this to k<n, proving the rank equivalence and the dual observation result. For the Riccati model, P′=P²−1 and P(T)=0. Along any admissible control, x²+u²+d(Px²)/dt=(u+Px)². Integration gives cost=P(0)x(0)²+∫(u+Px)²dt because the terminal term vanishes. The proposed feedback makes the square zero and therefore attains the exact lower bound.

## Worked example: setup

Use the nonnormal stable matrix A=[[-1,4],[0,−1]], then the constrained pencil E=diag(1,0), A_d=diag(−1,1).

## Worked example: calculation

At x=(1,1), d||x||²/dt=4>0 although both eigenvalues are −1. The exact positive matrix P=[[1/2,1],[1,9/2]] has determinant 5/4 and satisfies AᵀP+PA=−I. It supplies a valid metric certificate without denying transient Euclidean growth. For the descriptor pencil det(sE−A_d)=−(s+1), so its only finite eigenvalue is −1 and consistent solutions obey x₂=0. Replacing E by E+εI produces a new second eigenvalue 1/ε. This unstable mode belongs to the altered model. For A=diag(−1,−2),B=(1,1)^T, the controllability determinant is −1, and the infinite-horizon Gramian [[1/2,1/3],[1/3,1/4]] has determinant 1/72.

## Worked example: meaning

Exact energy and rank identities reveal which property is proved and which apparent instability or stability belongs only to a changed model.

## Interactive contract: inputs

Declared A,B,C or descriptor E,A; positive metric and horizon.

## Interactive contract: outputs

Exact Lyapunov identities, rank conditions, Gramian energy or Riccati value; descriptor consistency checks.

## Interactive contract: validation

Standalone rational matrix verification checks P, transient growth, Gramian determinant and descriptor roots; the Riccati square identity is derived analytically.

## Limits

Stability of a transfer function need not imply internal stability in canceled modes. A singular-vector projection is an iteration limit only under appropriate spectral hypotheses. A descriptor intervention gradient assumes a simple eigenvalue and nonzero left-right normalization. Historical benchmark margins, gains and speedups require the unrecovered system and measurements.
