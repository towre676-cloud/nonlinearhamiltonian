## Central question

What does a spectrum actually determine, and which operator-domain and multiplicity data are needed before using it?

## Synopsis

The spectral strand moves from concrete Jacobi and Toeplitz matrices to unbounded operators, residual estimates, heat traces, zeta transforms and spectral dimensions. Its organizing object is not a free-standing list of eigenvalues but an operator with a domain and a functional calculus. Self-adjointness makes a residual meaningful as distance to the spectrum; nonnormal matrices behave differently. Multiplicities change counting functions and commutants even when a list of distinct eigenvalues looks unchanged. Fixed-time trace class is also distinct from short-time heat asymptotics. A diagonal Hilbert-space model makes all these distinctions constructive and supplies a precise bridge between counting growth, heat decay and a zeta convergence half-plane. It is an exact model, not a proposed identification with zeros of a number-theoretic function.

## Definitions

On ℓ²(N), define Ae_n=n²e_n with domain D(A)={x:Σn⁴|x_n|²<∞}. This domain makes A self-adjoint and nonnegative.

The counting function with multiplicity is N_A(Λ)=#{n:n²≤Λ}. The heat operator H_t=e^(−tA) has diagonal entries e^(−tn²).

The spectral zeta series is ζ_A(s)=Σn^(−2s), initially defined where it converges absolutely.

## Theorem

For the specified A, N_A(Λ)=⌊√Λ⌋ for Λ≥1, H_t is trace class for every t>0, and √t Tr(H_t)→√π/2 as t↓0. The defining zeta series converges absolutely exactly when Re s>1/2. Its positive heat trace obeys ∫₁∞e^(−tx²)dx≤Σ_{n≥1}e^(−tn²)≤∫₀∞e^(−tx²)dx.

## Proof

Coordinatewise multiplication by a real sequence is self-adjoint on its maximal square-summability domain; equivalently the adjoint condition forces the same weighted square sum. Its resolvent at −1 has diagonal entries 1/(n²+1), and finite coordinate truncations converge in operator norm because the remaining supremum tends to zero. Thus the resolvent is compact. The counting identity is immediate from n²≤Λ. For t>0, the diagonal entries of H_t are positive and summable by the integral test, so its trace equals their sum. The function x↦e^(−tx²) decreases on [0,∞); rectangle comparison gives the two integral bounds. Their difference is at most ∫₀¹1 dx=1. Multiplying by √t and substituting u=√t x makes both bounds tend to ∫₀∞e^(−u²)du=√π/2. This proves the leading heat asymptotic without asserting an unproved full expansion. Finally the absolute zeta series is Σn^(−2Re s), and the p-series test gives precisely Re s>1/2. Repeating every eigenvalue n times would instead give N(Λ) of order Λ and heat growth of order t^(−1), so multiplicity changes the inferred dimension. The proof exposes where positivity, compactness and the actual counting law enter.

## Worked example: calculation

The partial trace is e^(−1)+e^(−4). Its omitted tail is Σ_{n≥3}e^(−n²), bounded above by ∫₂∞e^(−x²)dx≤e^(−4)/4, using x/2≥1 for x≥2. The true leading exponent at small t is t^(−1/2), while for every fixed t the omitted tail tends to zero as the coordinate cutoff grows.

## Worked example: meaning

A certified finite approximation and a dimension limit answer different questions. Their order of quantifiers must be specified when both a time parameter and a spectral cutoff vary.

## Limits

The diagonal model omits continuous spectrum, nonself-adjoint pseudospectra and singular perturbations of the domain. Mellin residues need a justified continuation and are not automatically integer-valued indices.
