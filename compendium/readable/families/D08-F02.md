## Central question

Which finite approximation preserves the model’s invariants, and what bound connects it to the intended continuous object?

## Synopsis

Approximation becomes usable when its error sources are separated and bounded. The archive includes positive diffusion stencils, CFL conditions, distributed halo exchange, lattice anisotropy, FFT differentiation, Newton error and inverse cutoffs. They share a need to distinguish consistency from stability and implementation correctness. A correct communication pattern cannot repair an unstable time step; a stable matrix cannot recover frequencies never observed. The periodic heat stencil below has sharp positivity conditions, exact mass conservation and an explicit Fourier symbol. It is therefore a fully inspectable worked model. Its continuum error additionally needs smoothness and a consistency estimate. The inverse-cutoff lab L04 uses a different, explicitly declared observation model: bias decreases with cutoff while noise amplification increases. The common capability is selecting a finite scale under a proved error budget, not one universal algorithm.

## Definitions

For N≥3 with indices modulo N, define (T_r u)_j=ru_(j−1)+(1−2r)u_j+ru_(j+1), r=κΔt/(Δx)².

Positivity means T_r maps every coordinatewise nonnegative vector to a nonnegative vector. Fixed-grid identities use the sum-of-squares norm; continuum error rates use the normalized norm (N^(−1)Σ|u_j|²)^(1/2).

Consistency compares the discrete equation with a sufficiently smooth exact solution; it is distinct from finite-dimensional conservation and positivity.

## Theorem

T_r preserves Σu_j for every r. It preserves nonnegativity exactly for 0≤r≤1/2. In that range it contracts the discrete ℓ² norm, with Fourier amplification 1−4r sin²(πk/N). For a smooth heat solution, the forward-Euler centered-space residual is O(Δt+Δx²) on a fixed bounded time interval with bounded derivatives.

## Proof

Cyclic summation makes each old value appear with coefficient r+(1−2r)+r=1, proving mass conservation. If all three coefficients are nonnegative, every new coordinate is a convex combination. Conversely a coordinate basis vector detects a negative off-diagonal coefficient when r<0 or a negative central coefficient when r>1/2, proving the sharp positivity condition. Convexity of z↦|z|² then gives |(T_ru)_j|²≤r|u_(j−1)|²+(1−2r)|u_j|²+r|u_(j+1)|². Sum over j to get contraction. Substituting the discrete character e^(2πikj/N) gives the amplification symbol r e^(−2πik/N)+(1−2r)+r e^(2πik/N)=1−4r sin²(πk/N), an independent spectral check. Finally Taylor expansion yields [u(t+Δt)−u(t)]/Δt=u_t+O(Δt), and [u(x+Δx)−2u(x)+u(x−Δx)]/Δx²=u_xx+O(Δx²). The heat equation cancels the leading terms. In the stable range, summing the resulting one-step defects gives a global fixed-time error of the same order under the stated smoothness in the normalized grid norm, with a uniform bound on the sampled consistency defect. A reaction term or boundary update changes these steps and must be analyzed separately.

## Worked example: setup

Use N=5, r=1/4 and u=(1,0,0,0,0).

## Worked example: calculation

The next vector is (1/2,1/4,0,0,1/4). Its mass remains one, and its squared norm falls from one to 1/4+1/16+1/16=3/8. At r=3/4 the same central value becomes −1/2, disproving positivity immediately. For a separate cutoff recipe with bound B(N)+δS(N), a valid choice must drive both terms to zero; specifying only N→∞ is insufficient.

## Limits

For odd N the sharp ℓ² spectral stability threshold can exceed the positivity threshold; the two notions are not equivalent. The O estimate requires bounded derivatives, a fixed time interval and consistent initial data, not arbitrary nonsmooth inputs.
