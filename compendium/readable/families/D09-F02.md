## Central question

Which changes of stability can be derived from a return map, and what additional work separates a local bifurcation from global chaos?

## Synopsis

The dynamics archive repeatedly returns to logistic period doubling, oscillator locking, pitchfork regulation, scalar flip equations and return-map jets. These are best organized around a local map or flow, its invariant branches and its multipliers. A nonlinearity is not itself chaos, a symbolic analogy is not a conjugacy, and a local germ is not a global attractor. The logistic family offers an unusually complete positive example: the first fixed-point instability, the born two-cycle and its entire linear-stability interval follow from exact algebra. Later 2026 questions enrich this with explicit transcendental flip candidates, square-root branching and cubic coefficients of the second iterate. Those mechanisms can be compared once the local coordinates and nondegeneracy conditions are stated.

## Definitions

Let f_r(x)=rx(1−x), r>0. A point has exact period two when f_r²(x)=x and f_r(x)≠x.

The multiplier of a k-cycle is the product of derivatives along it. Absolute multiplier less than one proves local attraction; equality is a boundary case requiring higher-order analysis.

A local flip occurs when a fixed-point multiplier crosses −1 transversely with an appropriate nonzero nonlinear coefficient.

## Theorem

The nonzero logistic fixed point is stable for 1<r<3. For r>3 its genuine two-cycle has points x_±=[r+1±√((r−3)(r+1))]/(2r) and multiplier m₂=−r²+2r+4. It is linearly attracting exactly for 3<r<1+√6.

## Proof

Solving f_r(x)=x gives zero and x*=1−1/r. At x*, f_r′(x*)=r(1−2+2/r)=2−r, whose absolute value is less than one exactly when 1<r<3. To locate period-two points, expand f_r²(x)−x and divide by the known fixed-point factors. The remaining equation is r²x²−r(r+1)x+(r+1)=0. Its discriminant is r²(r−3)(r+1), giving the displayed distinct real roots for r>3. Their sum is (r+1)/r and product is (r+1)/r². Consequently f_r′(x_+)f_r′(x_-)=r²[1−2(x_++x_-)+4x_+x_-]=−r²+2r+4. For r>3 this polynomial decreases strictly, starts at +1 at r=3 and reaches −1 at r=1+√6. Hence |m₂|<1 precisely on the stated interval. At the first endpoint the two-cycle merges with the fixed branch; at the second, its multiplier reaches the next flip boundary. This calculation establishes neither every later doubling nor Feigenbaum universality. A complementary jet calculation explains why higher terms matter: if R(s)=−s+as²+bs³+O(s⁴), direct composition gives R²(s)=s−2(a²+b)s³+O(s⁴); the quadratic term cancels and the sign or vanishing of a²+b is relevant. A multiplier of −1 alone omits that information.

## Worked example: setup

Take r=16/5. Also compare the regulator m↦tanh(am) near a=1.

## Worked example: calculation

The logistic two-cycle is (21±√21)/32, and its multiplier is 4/25. It is therefore locally attracting. For the regulator, tanh(am)=am−a³m³/3+O(m⁵); a nonzero fixed point satisfies m²=3(a−1)/a³+O((a−1)²) as a↓1. This square-root branch is a pitchfork mechanism, distinct from the logistic two-cycle despite a shared critical-scaling vocabulary.

## Worked example: meaning

Exact branch formulas make hypotheses testable. They provide a foundation for a numerical bifurcation diagram, while the diagram itself cannot certify an infinite cascade or a global chaotic invariant set.

## Limits

All stated nonlinear stability conclusions are local unless a global invariant region and basin argument are supplied. Chaotic horseshoes, positive entropy and universality require different theorems from the first two logistic thresholds.
