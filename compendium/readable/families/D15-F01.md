## Central question

Which hidden directions matter for the requested target, and when can the observation itself evolve as a closed state?

## Synopsis

The strongest common thread in the later archive is that full-state recovery is often unnecessary. An observation can leave a large invisible fibre while still determining a particular target exactly. The right question asks whether the target is constant on those fibres. In finite-dimensional linear algebra this becomes a kernel inclusion, a factorization and a sharp stability inequality. This also separates three levels frequently merged in informal discussions: present target identifiability, stable noisy recovery and closed predictive dynamics. Present factorization G=LH does not imply that Hx can predict its own future; closure requires the invisible space to be invariant under the state dynamics. In nonlinear models a derivative test is only local and can miss second-order target variation along a hidden fibre. These distinctions guide sensor design constructively: retain or measure directions that change the target, then verify the closure or robustness property actually required by the task.

## Definitions

For state x∈V, observation H:V→W and target G:V→Z, a fibre is {x:Hx=y}. The target is identifiable if Hx=Hx′ implies Gx=Gx′.

The invisible space of a linear observation is ker H. Target-relative observability is ker H⊆ker G, not necessarily ker H={0}.

For dynamics x′=Ax, observation closure means there is T with HA=TH. A moving observation B(t) closes when B′+BA=TB.

For Euclidean finite-dimensional spaces, the minimum-norm recovery on im H is L₀=GH†; its extension can vanish on the orthogonal complement of im H.

## Theorem

The following are equivalent: G is identifiable from H; ker H⊆ker G; G=LH for some linear L; and GᵀG≼λHᵀH for some finite λ≥0. The smallest such λ is the squared minimum recovery norm. Separately, HA=TH exists exactly when ker H is A-invariant.

## Proof

Identifiability applied to x∈ker H and x′=0 gives the kernel inclusion. Conversely, equal observations differ by a vector in ker H, so their targets agree. Define L(Hx)=Gx on im H; the inclusion makes this independent of the chosen representative and gives linearity. Extend L to W. Factorization gives ||Gx||≤||L||||Hx|| and hence the quadratic inequality. Conversely the inequality forces Gx=0 whenever Hx=0. The least possible norm is the supremum of ||Gx||/||Hx|| over nonzero observed vectors, exactly the least square-root constant in the inequality; the zero extension attains it in finite dimensions. For closure, HA must vanish on ker H, which is equivalent to A(ker H)⊆ker H. Apply the same factorization construction with target HA. In moving coordinates differentiate Bx and obtain (B′+BA)x; this is a new target to which the criterion applies.

## Worked example: setup

Observe Hx=(x₁+x₂,x₂+x₃) and request Gx=x₁+2x₂+x₃. Then compare a nonlinear target at a tangent point.

## Worked example: calculation

The observation kernel is span((1,−1,1)); G annihilates it, so Gx=y₁+y₂ is exactly recoverable even though x is not. The minimum recovery norm is √2 because H is onto R² and L=(1,1). Requesting x₁ instead fails: x and x+(1,−1,1) have the same observation and different first coordinates. For H(x,z)=x and nonlinear target g(x,z)=z², the hidden derivative ∂_z g vanishes at z=0, yet g changes along that same observation fibre. Thus a zero directional derivative at one point is not finite-amplitude identifiability. In periodic velocity reconstruction, divergence and curl similarly leave a constant harmonic mean invisible unless it is separately observed.

## Worked example: meaning

A target can be recovered with fewer channels than a whole state, but stable recovery, future prediction and nonlinear global identifiability each need their own theorem.

## Limits

In infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces kernel inclusion alone need not give bounded recovery without a range/majorization condition. Local Jacobian rank does not prove a nonlinear target constant on entire fibres. Fifteen coefficients in an affine wind model require a full-rank observation design, not just fifteen arbitrarily repeated readings. Exact optimization on development data does not prove sensor reduction or out-of-sample accuracy.

## Open questions

For a supplied target, what is the smallest additional observation subspace that removes all target-relevant hidden directions?

When can local differential factorization be integrated into a global fibre-constant target on a restricted nonlinear state manifold?
