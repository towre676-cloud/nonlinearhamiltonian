## Central question

Which identities survive a nonassociative or graded product, and what additional data turn exceptional dimensions into a Lie algebra?

## Synopsis

This family joins two recurring themes: bracket consistency and exceptional symmetry. The starting point is a declared multiplication convention. Associative multiplication produces a commutator satisfying Jacobi; graded associative multiplication produces the super-Jacobi identity with parity signs. Octonionic multiplication is alternative rather than associative, so its commutator carries a nonzero Jacobiator. These are different algebraic structures, each with identities that can be derived and tested exactly.

The octonion model is especially productive because it turns regrouping into a measurable defect. Under a fixed Cayley–Dickson convention, the triple e₁,e₂,e₄ has associator 2e₇. Norm multiplicativity then yields a sharp universal bound for every triple, with a complete equality criterion. This gives a rigorous invariant of the multiplication, while preventing an illicit reassociation of three unrelated factors. The same discipline applies to superalgebras: in gl(1|1), the bracket of the two odd off-diagonal matrix units is an anticommutator and equals the identity, not an ordinary commutator.

Exceptional Lie algebras enter at the representation level. The decomposition so₁₆⊕S⁺ has dimension 120+128=248, and its D₈ roots together with the half-spin weights form the 240-element E₈ root set. That is a substantial and checkable weight calculation. It is still distinct from constructing the bracket: the so₁₆ action on S⁺ and an equivariant map Λ²S⁺→so₁₆ must be specified with a normalization satisfying spinor Jacobi. The magic-square dimension arithmetic likewise organises candidate structures without replacing their multiplication. This synthesis keeps the genuine exceptional mathematics while making its missing construction obligations visible.

## Definitions

Use quaternion units ij=k, jk=i, ki=j and octonion product (a,b)(c,d)=(ac−conj(d)b, da+b conj(c)).

The associator is [a,b,c]=(ab)c−a(bc); the octonion norm is multiplicative.

For homogeneous parity |X|∈{0,1}, [X,Y]_s=XY−(−1)^{|X||Y|}YX.

The E₈ root set is {±e_i±e_j:i<j} together with {(±1/2,…,±1/2):an even number of minus signs}.

## Theorem

For real octonions, ||(ab)c−a(bc)||≤2||a||||b||||c||. For nonzero norm product, equality holds exactly when (ab)c=−a(bc), and the constant two is sharp. Independently, the displayed E₈ root set has 240 vectors of squared length two.

## Proof

Write u=(ab)c and v=a(bc). Multiplicativity gives ||u||=||v||=n=||a||||b||||c||. Hence ||u−v||≤||u||+||v||=2n. If n>0, equality in the triangle inequality requires u and −v to have the same direction; equal norms make this exactly u=−v. The declared multiplication gives e₁e₂=e₃, e₃e₄=e₇, e₂e₄=e₆ and e₁e₆=−e₇, proving sharpness. For the root count, choosing two positions among eight and two independent signs gives 4·binom(8,2)=112 integer roots. Of the 2⁸ half-integral sign vectors exactly half have even parity: flipping one chosen sign pairs the two parity classes. Thus there are 128 such roots and 240 total. Each integer root has norm squared 1+1=2; each half-integral vector has 8/4=2. No Jacobi identity for an exceptional Lie bracket is implied by this count.

## Worked example: calculation

For (e₁,e₂,e₄), the two groupings are e₇ and −e₇, so the defect is two. For (e₁,e₂,e₃), both groupings equal −1, so the defect is zero. In gl(1|1), Q₊=E₁₂ and Q₋=E₂₁ are odd. Their superbracket is E₁₁+E₂₂=I, while Q₊²=Q₋²=0. Taking N=E₁₁ gives [N,Q₊]_s=Q₊ and [N,Q₋]_s=−Q₋. Expanding associative triple products with parity signs proves super-Jacobi. A finite exact verifier can check the octonion basis associators and all E₈ root lengths without asserting that either computation constructs a physical model.

## Worked example: meaning

Convention-sensitive multiplication and root enumeration are complete finite tasks. A dimension match remains a consistency check until the actual bracket maps are present.

## Limits

The octonion defect theorem does not classify every zero-associator orbit. Root counts do not prove anomaly cancellation, physical field content or a full E₈ bracket. Real forms and normalization must accompany any further comparison.
