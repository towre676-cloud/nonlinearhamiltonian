## Central question

When does a first-order algebraic change extend to a genuine family, and which structural invariants can still jump?

## Synopsis

Deformation is a family of equations, not an adjective attached to a new notation. Its data include a coefficient ring, an object, an equivalence relation and a compatibility equation at every order. In associative algebra, the first-order equation is a Hochschild cocycle condition. In a differential graded Lie model it becomes a Maurer–Cartan equation, with a second-order cohomology class measuring obstruction. Neither a tangent vector nor a long finite truncation proves an all-order extension without a theorem controlling the remaining equations.

Three small models make the distinctions explicit. A characteristic-two character of C₂ has a nontrivial tangent direction that cannot lift one order further. A dual-number multiplication can be deformed to x²=t as a free rank-two algebra, yet its positive-degree Hochschild cohomology jumps at the special fibre. A reflection-deformed derivative on polynomials supplies an exact represented relation [Dκ,X]=I+κs for every parameter; its proof uses the monomial action rather than an unsupported universal classification. These are constructive examples of obstruction, flatness with changing derived invariants, and a genuinely defined deformation law.

Poisson wall transport adds a geometric version. A skew lattice pairing gives an automorphism x^m↦x^m f(x^γ)^{B(γ,m)}. Skew symmetry and B(γ,γ)=0 ensure the distinguished monomial is fixed and make the inverse and bracket compatibility work. Replacing the pairing by an arbitrary matrix loses the proof. The same principle links back to Moyal exactness: each higher equation must actually vanish, and its degree or filtration bound determines whether a finite check is complete. The atlas presents finite obstruction calculations with exact coefficient rings and reports the boundary between formal existence, finite truncation, convergence and physical interpretation.

## Definitions

A first-order associative deformation is m_t=m+tφ modulo t², with multiplication unit and equivalence under basis changes specified.

Its cocycle equation is aφ(b,c)−φ(ab,c)+φ(a,bc)−φ(a,b)c=0.

For a differential graded Lie algebra in characteristic zero, α satisfies dα+[α,α]/2=0; coefficients at each order give extension equations.

A flat rank-two family A_t=k[x]/(x²−t) is free on 1,x over its parameter ring; assume characteristic different from two for the stated Hochschild comparison.

## Theorem

Over a characteristic-two field, the C₂-character tangent u=1+ε modulo ε² satisfies u²=1 but cannot lift to u=1+η+bη² modulo η³. Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic different from two, A_t=k[x]/(x²−t) has HH^n=0 for t≠0 and n>0, while at t=0 every positive-degree HH^n has dimension one.

## Proof

In characteristic two, (1+ε)²=1 because 2ε=ε²=0. For a prospective lift, (1+η+bη²)²=1+η² modulo η³: doubled terms vanish and the η² coefficient cannot be changed by b. Thus the tangent relation has an unavoidable next-order obstruction. For the flat algebra family, division by the monic polynomial leaves a unique representative a+bx, proving freeness of rank two. At t≠0, choose a²=t; x−a and x+a are coprime, so Chinese remainders give A_t≅k×k, a separable algebra with no positive-degree Hochschild cohomology. At t=0 the standard two-periodic bimodule resolution has maps x⊗1−1⊗x and x⊗1+1⊗x. Applying Hom over A^e gives alternating maps zero and multiplication by 2x. In positive degrees the kernels or cokernels have dimension one: ker(2x)=kx and A/(2x)=k. Thus flatness of the underlying module does not force derived-invariant constancy.

## Worked example: calculation

The scalar equation s¹³=0 is satisfied by s=ε modulo ε¹³, so it passes all coefficients through order twelve. The same leading direction fails modulo ε¹⁴ because its ε¹³ coefficient is one; no higher correction changes that leading term. This gives a precise finite-truncation boundary. For the reflection model Dκx^n=[n+(κ/2)(1−(−1)^n)]x^{n−1}. Subtracting XDκ from DκX gives coefficient 1+κ on even n and 1−κ on odd n, exactly I+κs. At the dual-number fibre x²=0, D(x)=x is a nonzero derivation; it represents the HH¹ direction that disappears in the split generic fibre.
