## Central question

How can multiplicative arithmetic become a finite lattice calculation with a complete certificate?

## Synopsis

Prime factorization converts multiplication into addition of exponent vectors. Divisibility becomes a coordinate order, gcd and lcm become coordinate minimum and maximum, and a monomial equation becomes an integer linear system at every prime. This does more than simplify computation: it states exactly which sign cases, valuation bounds and prime supports a complete answer must cover. Divisor incidence then transports scalar arithmetic identities into matrix factorizations. The same identity n=Σ_(d|n)φ(d) controls a positive gcd kernel, its determinant, and a sieve implementation. Thus arithmetic, combinatorics and finite-dimensional energy geometry meet through a declared transform, not merely because they share the word prime. The family includes factorial valuations and prime-stripped block products: one computes the p-power removed, the other computes the remaining unit, and their recombination reconstructs the original factorial exactly.

## Definitions

For n=∏p_i^a_i, a divisor corresponds to exponents 0≤b_i≤a_i. Its divisor lattice is the product of chains C_(a_i+1).

Dirichlet convolution is (f*g)(n)=Σ_(d|n)f(d)g(n/d). The incidence matrix E has E_(i,d)=1 when d divides i, and zero otherwise.

Legendre’s formula gives v_p(n!)=Σ_(j≥1)⌊n/p^j⌋. The stripped unit U_p(n)=n!/p^v_p(n!) is an integer not divisible by p.

## Theorem

For G_(i,j)=gcd(i,j), 1≤i,j≤N, one has G=E diag(φ(1),…,φ(N))Eᵀ. Consequently G is positive definite and det G=∏_(d=1)^Nφ(d). Moreover positive solutions of x²y³=2²⁰3¹² are exactly x=2^(10−3r)3^(6−3s), y=2^(2r)3^(2s), with 0≤r≤3 and 0≤s≤2.

## Proof

The (i,j) entry of the displayed matrix product is Σ_(d|i,d|j)φ(d)=Σ_(d|gcd(i,j))φ(d)=gcd(i,j). The identity Σ_(d|m)φ(d)=m follows by grouping 1,…,m according to gcd with m. E is lower triangular with diagonal one; every φ(d)>0, giving positive definiteness and the determinant. For the monomial equation, a prime outside {2,3} has 2v_p(x)+3v_p(y)=0, forcing both exponents zero. At p=2, nonnegative integral solutions to 2a+3b=20 require b=2r and a=10−3r, with r=0,1,2,3. At p=3 the same argument gives b=2s and a=6−3s, with s=0,1,2. Independent choices prove both existence and completeness of the twelve solutions. A bounded search would show existence of examples but would not establish that no extra prime or exponent pattern occurs.

## Worked example: calculation

For N=4 the diagonal weights are (1,1,2,2), so det G=4. The row for i=4 in E is (1,1,0,1), explaining gcd(4,2)=φ(1)+φ(2)=2. For n=72=2³3², the rank polynomial is (1+t+t²+t³)(1+t+t²), whose coefficients are 1,2,3,3,2,1; its value at one is 12 and its degree is five. For 10!, v_3=3+1=4, so U_3(10)=3628800/81=44800. The recursion U_3(10)=(1·2·4·5·7·8·10)U_3(3), with U_3(3)=2, reproduces 44800.

## Worked example: meaning

A positive kernel, a complete Diophantine parametrization and an efficient arithmetic block algorithm arise from distinct but compatible uses of divisor structure.

## Limits

Factorization is an input cost, and exact arithmetic operation counts are not bit-complexity bounds. Real or complex solutions of a monomial equation do not inherit the integer valuation classification. A positive matrix constructed from divisors does not automatically encode an arithmetic L-function.

## Open questions

For a general nonnegative exponent matrix, which finite target supports produce a bounded polytope of integer valuation vectors?

Can the O(N log N) divisor-kernel action be paired with a bit-complexity bound and an exact sparse factorization certificate?
