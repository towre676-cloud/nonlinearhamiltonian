## Central question

How do local incidence constraints produce global cycles, and when does a short witness prove an optimal graph action count?

## Synopsis

Incidence is the common finite language for conservation, cycle spaces, graph gauge classes and many covering arguments. An oriented edge is sent to the difference of its endpoint vertices; cancellation at internal vertices explains why a path has only source and sink boundary. Subtracting two paths therefore produces a circulation. The converse requires hypotheses: in a finite source-to-sink directed acyclic graph with every edge covered by a path, these differences span the entire real circulation space. Acyclic direction does not erase undirected cycles; a diamond already has a one-dimensional incidence kernel. The complementary optimization theme is that an admissible construction establishes an upper bound, while a matching or other dual witness establishes a lower bound. A support size or observed action count has no optimality meaning until the permitted action is stated. This family makes that action model and its certificate visible.

## Definitions

For an oriented graph with n vertices, m edges and c connected components, B is the vertex-by-edge matrix having −1 at a tail and +1 at a head.

The circulation space is ker B. Over any field its dimension is m−n+c; over the integers a spanning forest gives a fundamental cycle basis.

A vertex-call covers every edge incident to the chosen vertex. A matching consists of edges with disjoint endpoints, so any one vertex-call covers at most one matching edge.

For a graph regarded as a one-dimensional complex, H_1=ker B because there are no two-cells. Adding filled faces changes the quotient by their boundaries.

## Theorem

The incidence rank is n−c, hence the circulation dimension is m−n+c. On a finite directed acyclic s-to-t graph with every edge on an s-to-t path, ker B is spanned over R by χ(p)−χ(p₀). In the vertex-call model on K_{a,b}, the exact minimum call count is min(a,b).

## Proof

On each connected component, incidence columns sum to zero and hence rank is at most n_component−1. A spanning tree contributes independent columns: remove a leaf and inspect its unique incident coefficient, inductively forcing every coefficient to zero. Summing components proves the rank formula. Every path difference has zero boundary. For the converse sum covering path indicators to obtain a strictly positive feasible flow f. For z∈ker B, both f and f+εz are nonnegative for small enough ε. A finite DAG permits a decomposition of each into nonnegative s-to-t path flows: follow positive flow, use conservation internally, and subtract the least path-edge value. At least one edge is removed at each stage. Their source divergence is the same, so subtracting the decompositions gives a combination of path differences. Finally assume a≤b. An a-edge matching demands at least a vertex-calls, and all a left vertices cover every edge. Matching lower bound and cover upper bound meet.

## Worked example: setup

Use the diamond with edges (sa,at,sb,bt), then K_{3,3} and the call model K_{4,7}.

## Worked example: calculation

The diamond has four vertices and four edges, so ker B is one-dimensional, generated by (1,1,−1,−1), the difference of its two directed paths. It is a DAG even though this undirected cycle survives. For K_{3,3}, m−n+1=9−6+1=4; a spanning tree and each of its four non-tree edges produce an integral fundamental cycle. For K_{4,7}, 28 edges are covered by four left-vertex calls. A four-edge matching proves that three calls cannot suffice. If a call instead selected one edge, the answer would be 28, demonstrating why action semantics changes the optimization problem.

## Worked example: meaning

Global invariants become replayable through integer matrices, explicit cycles and matching witnesses; pictures alone do not establish homology or cost.
