## Central question

Which information quantity is monotone under a declared transformation, and what geometry does it induce?

## Synopsis

Entropy appears across finite chains, communication channels, softmax models, quantum states and gradient diffusion. The synthesis needs a reference measure and a transformation before it needs a slogan about disorder. Shannon entropy can decrease under a nonuniform Markov equilibrium while relative entropy to that equilibrium decreases as well. Mutual information describes a joint distribution and contracts along a Markov channel; it does not assign a causal arrow. At small scale, relative entropy generates Fisher geometry, whose null directions express parameter redundancy. These facts connect probabilistic coarse-graining, differential geometry and optimization through explicit maps rather than through a shared word.

## Definitions

For positive finite q, D(p||q)=Σp_i log(p_i/q_i), with 0 log0=0; Shannon entropy is H(p)=−Σp_i logp_i.

A stochastic channel T maps input distributions to output distributions. Mutual information is D(p_XY||p_Xp_Y).

For logits u, A(u)=logΣe^{u_i}, p_i=e^{u_i−A(u)}, and F=diag(p)−ppᵀ. A common additive logit is unidentifiable.

For a faithful density matrix σ, its modular Hamiltonian is −logσ. Matrix entropy and classical entropy agree on diagonal states, but their full differential geometry requires operator calculus.

## Theorem

Every finite stochastic channel satisfies D(Tp||Tq)≤D(p||q). For softmax probabilities, F≥0, F1=0, and D(p(u)||p(u+h))=(1/2)hᵀFh+O(‖h‖³). At uniform p on m outcomes, F=(I−11ᵀ/m)/m and H=logm.

## Proof

For each output j, apply the log-sum inequality to a_i=T_{ji}p_i and b_i=T_{ji}q_i. Summing the inequalities over j and using Σ_jT_{ji}=1 gives contraction. Equality requires the likelihood ratio p_i/q_i to be constant across every collection of inputs mixed into a positive output. For the geometric statement, differentiating A gives ∇A=p and ∇²A=F. The quadratic form vᵀFv=Σp_iv_i²−(Σp_iv_i)² is a variance, hence nonnegative and zero on constants. The KL expression is exactly A(u+h)−A(u)−p(u)·h; Taylor expansion supplies the local quadratic term. This proves the geometry on the quotient by common logits rather than pretending the full Fisher matrix is invertible.

## Worked example: setup

Take a Markov matrix whose every row is π=(9/10,1/10), and initial p=(1/2,1/2). Then compare a fair bit passed through two binary symmetric channels with flip probability 1/10.

## Worked example: calculation

One Markov step sends p to π, reducing H from log2 to −.9log.9−.1log.1, while D(p||π)=log(5/3) falls to zero. In the channel cascade the effective flip probability is 2(.1)(.9)=.18. Information therefore falls from 1−h_2(.1)≈.531004 bits to 1−h_2(.18)≈.319923 bits. At m=97 uniform classes, entropy is log97≈4.574711 nats and the 96 nonzero Fisher eigenvalues are 1/97. Neither uniform entropy nor a small parameter step reports classification accuracy.

## Worked example: meaning

The correct monotone depends on the experiment: relative entropy for a fixed reference law, mutual information for a channel, or KL locally for a parameterized model. This provides a useful common language while preserving their distinct arguments, units and equality conditions. An information quantity can be mathematically exact even when its proposed application lacks data.

## Limits

Finite alphabets avoid differential-entropy coordinate issues. Continuous densities must first be normalized and have adequate integrability for differentiation. Mutual information alone does not identify causal direction, and a proposed logarithmic transform of mutual information need not be a metric. Fisher, empirical Fisher, Gauss–Newton and the objective Hessian are different matrices outside special model assumptions.
