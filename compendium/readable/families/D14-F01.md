## Central question

When can two internal states be merged while preserving every permitted future observation?

## Synopsis

The correct state of a computation is relative to its observer and action alphabet. Two memories that currently print the same value may behave differently after a tick, reset, allocation or later query. Future equivalence compares every such continuation. In a finite deterministic machine, its apparently infinite definition has a finite certificate: iterative partition refinement stops at a stable partition, and stability implies agreement for every word. Weighted futures require immediate costs as well as visible outputs to descend. Bisimulation supplies a useful transition-level proof principle but must be stated for the actual operational semantics. The resulting quotient is an executable machine, not merely a partition of a log. This family is the central bridge between semantic state reduction, target-relative observations and efficient separator states. It explains both why some reductions are exact and why a dramatic reduction in state count alone establishes no behavioral equivalence.

## Definitions

A deterministic observation machine has finite states S, actions A, transitions δ_a:S→S and output o:S→O. A word acts in its given order.

Future equivalence is s∼t iff o(δ_w(s))=o(δ_w(t)) for every finite word w, including the empty word.

Depth-r equivalence checks words of length at most r. Refinement maps a state to its present output together with the current classes of all its successors.

With additive action costs, C_s(aw)=c(s,a)+C_{δ_a(s)}(w). A cost-preserving quotient must preserve these immediate costs and all successor classes.

## Theorem

Future equivalence is the coarsest equivalence respecting outputs and every action transition. Finite partition refinement computes it and has at most |S|−1 strict refinements. Once two successive partitions agree, the stable partition checks all word lengths. Equality of all accumulated continuation costs similarly defines a right congruence for deterministic weighted machines.

## Proof

If s and t have equal futures, then their present outputs agree. For every action a and every word w, the output after aw agrees; hence δ_a(s)∼δ_a(t), making the quotient transition independent of representatives. Conversely, any output-preserving transition congruence preserves the output after every word by induction, so it refines future equivalence. Starting from output classes, the next refinement checks exactly one more leading action by the same induction. A stable partition therefore preserves transitions and already equals the full relation. Every strict refinement increases the number of nonempty blocks, giving the finite bound. For additive costs, compare one-letter words first to obtain c(s,a)=c(t,a), then subtract this finite common value from the equality for aw to obtain future equality at the successors. This subtraction argument needs finite deterministic costs; nondeterministic min–plus machines require their own semantics.

## Worked example: setup

Use a TTL counter with states 0,1,2. Tick sends positive values down by one and leaves zero fixed. Read costs one at zero and zero otherwise, then resets the counter to two.

## Worked example: calculation

States 1 and 2 have identical one-step costs for read and tick, but tick-read costs one from 1 and zero from 2. Present-output merging therefore loses a future distinction. Refinement retaining both action-cost vectors and successor classes separates all three states. In a separate four-state cycle δ(s)=s+1 mod 4 with output s mod 2, the exact quotient has two classes {0,2} and {1,3}, swapped by the action. Changing only the output to the indicator of state 3 makes every state distinguishable within three actions. Thus the same transition graph has different minimal observable quotients for different observers.

## Worked example: meaning

A quotient certificate combines a declared alphabet, output map and closure proof. A finite logged prefix is evidence only within a proved completeness bound.

## Limits

Changing the action alphabet can split previously valid classes. Trace equivalence and bisimulation are distinct for nondeterministic systems. Infinite-state refinement may not terminate. A broad tropic name is not sufficient to specify weighted, min–plus or nonlinear transition semantics. No unprovided TTL machine table or benchmark quotient count is reproduced.
