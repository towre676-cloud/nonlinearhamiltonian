## Central question

When do finitely many output words determine a hidden state, and how does that injectivity become optimal error correction?

## Synopsis

The coding thread begins with concrete binary codes whose distance can be independently enumerated and culminates in a universal certificate for a specified 107-bit raw generator. It is important to separate three achievements. Alphabet capacity gives a necessary observation count. A short-vector exclusion proves injectivity for every state, rather than only tested fixtures. Finally a distance argument converts injectivity of every five-position projection into optimal correction of corrupted words. The large proof has a compact trusted core: integer lattice membership, an exact determinant and rational inverse-column bounds. A basis reduction algorithm may discover useful matrices, but the checker does not trust that search. This makes the raw-state theorem a strong example of portable exact certification. The accepted-output sequence has hidden rejected steps and remains a different observation model; its earlier threshold claim cannot inherit the raw theorem.

## Definitions

Set M=2^107−1, A=3^121 mod M, b=2^26, x_{i+1}=Ax_i mod M and y_i=x_i mod b, with 0≤x_i<M.

For five known positions p_0<…<p_4 in {0,…,8}, let g_j=p_j−p_0 and Λ_p={d∈Z^5:d_j≡A^{g_j}d_0 mod M}. Set D=floor((M−1)/b)=2^81−1.

The raw trajectory code C_n consists of the first n output words of every state. Hamming distance counts changed words, even when many bits in a word are changed.

A supplied row basis B_p is certified by lattice row membership, |det B_p|=M^4, and DΣ_i|(B_p^{-1})_{ij}|<3/8 for every column j.

## Theorem

For every five-position subset of the first nine raw steps, the selected words uniquely determine the initial state. Four words cannot determine every state. For 5≤n≤9 the raw trajectory code has minimum distance exactly n−4. Thus seven words correct one arbitrary word error and nine correct two, and these lengths are optimal for any code representing all M states over this alphabet.

## Proof

The map d↦(d_j−A^{g_j}d_0)_{j=1}^4 onto (Z/MZ)^4 has kernel Λ_p, so the lattice index is M^4. Membership and matching determinant establish that the supplied rows span the whole lattice. If two states produce the same selected words, their selected state differences are bd_j with |d_j|≤D. Since b is invertible modulo M, d∈Λ_p, hence d=cB_p for integer c. The dual bound gives |c_j|≤DΣ_i|(B_p^{-1})_{ij}|<1, forcing c=0 and then equality of the initial states because A is invertible. Four words are impossible by b^4<M−1. Distinct length-n codewords agree in at most four positions, so d_min≥n−4. Puncturing d_min−1 positions is injective, giving M≤b^{n−d_min+1}; since M>b^4, d_min≤n−4. Disjoint radius-t Hamming balls require d_min>2t. Puncturing 2t positions then gives n≥5+2t, proving optimal lengths.

## Worked example: setup

Use the actual 126 supplied five-position bases. For decoding a received exact five-word vector, express selected states as y_j+bz_j with 0≤z_j≤D.

## Worked example: calculation

The congruences define an affine lattice z∈v+Λ_p. Applying B_p^{-1} to the bounded z-box places each integer coefficient in an exact rational interval of width below 3/8. Each contains at most one integer. Exact endpoint rounding therefore yields at most one candidate; re-encoding verifies every state bound and observation. The annual certificate checks all 126 bases, and 630 designated decoder fixtures include states 0,1,1234567890123456789,floor(M/3),M−1. For one or two corruptions, enumerate five-position subsets, decode candidates, re-encode and retain a state within the allowed word radius. The distance theorem proves uniqueness. The small analogue M=31,A=3,b=8 has 8,24,31 distinct one-, two-, three-word outputs and independently illustrates why capacity and actual injectivity are separate.

## Worked example: meaning

The universal theorem rests on exact lattice checks; fixtures separately exercise decoder code. This is observability and error correction for a specified deterministic raw recurrence, not a claim of cryptographic unpredictability.

## Limits

The raw-output theorem assumes the stated recurrence, output convention and known positions. No recovered acceptance/rejection rule supports the previous six-accepted-output claim. A designed distance lower bound for an algebraic-geometric code is not an exhibited minimum-weight codeword or efficient decoder. Structural transport can preserve composition while changing Hamming distance.
