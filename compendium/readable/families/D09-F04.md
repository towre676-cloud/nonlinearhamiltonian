## Central question

When does a reduced state determine its own future, and what exact memory or symbolic data repair a failure of closure?

## Synopsis

A final dynamics strand connects hidden-variable elimination, damped recurrences, rotations, substitutions, cellular automata and path-dependent accumulators. The shared question is what information must be retained for a well-defined next state. Eliminating a hidden coordinate usually creates a memory kernel and an initial-state source. Forgetting either can turn an exact reduction into a different equation. Symbolic models pose the discrete analogue: an observed label may not encode the phase or history needed for the future. At the same time, deterministic aperiodicity can be highly structured. A rotation coding has exact balance, and a dense quasiperiodic orbit can have zero entropy. The family therefore organizes recurrence by a declared state, observation and equivalence relation rather than grouping every irregular-looking sequence under chaos.

## Definitions

Consider x′=−ax+by and y′=cx−dy with constants a,b,c,d and given (x₀,y₀). A closed scalar evolution in x alone must determine x′ from retained data.

The memory convolution (K*x)(t)=∫₀ᵗK(t−s)x(s)ds retains past x values. The source be^(−dt)y₀ carries the hidden initial condition.

A mechanical word is w_n=⌊(n+1)α+ρ⌋−⌊nα+ρ⌋ for 0<α<1. It codes a rotation with an explicitly retained phase.

## Theorem

Eliminating y gives exactly x′(t)=−ax(t)+be^(−dt)y₀+bc∫₀ᵗe^(−d(t−s))x(s)ds. For a=d=b=c=1 and (x₀,y₀)=(1,0), x(t)=(1+e^(−2t))/2. Moreover every length-m block of the mechanical word has either ⌊mα⌋ or ⌈mα⌉ ones.

## Proof

Multiply the y-equation by e^(dt). Its derivative is c e^(dt)x(t); integrate from zero to t and divide by e^(dt) to obtain y(t)=e^(−dt)y₀+c∫₀ᵗe^(−d(t−s))x(s)ds. Substitution into the x-equation proves the exact Volterra equation and shows why the initial source cannot be omitted. In the stated symmetric example, s=x+y satisfies s′=0 and r=x−y satisfies r′=−2r. With s₀=r₀=1, x=(s+r)/2=(1+e^(−2t))/2 and y=(1−e^(−2t))/2. Dropping the memory would give e^(−t), which has the wrong long-time limit. This is an exact closure failure, not an approximation estimate. For the mechanical word, sum from n=j to j+m−1: the floors telescope to ⌊(j+m)α+ρ⌋−⌊jα+ρ⌋. Writing jα+ρ as integer plus θ∈[0,1) gives ⌊mα+θ⌋, which is either floor or ceiling of mα. Any two length-m blocks therefore differ in their number of ones by at most one. Dividing the prefix count by m yields limiting frequency α. If α is irrational, a periodic binary word would have rational frequency, so the word is aperiodic. These two examples demonstrate distinct constructive repairs: keep a memory term for the continuous reduction, or keep the rotation phase for the symbolic coding. Neither irregular recurrence nor aperiodicity alone proves positive entropy or unpredictability.

## Worked example: setup

Compare the exact continuous reduction at t=log2 with a mechanical word of slope α=(√5−1)/2 and ρ=0.

## Worked example: calculation

At t=log2, x=(1+1/4)/2=5/8, while the memory-free approximation is 1/2. The mechanical word begins 0,1,0,1,1,0,1,0; its first eight symbols contain ⌊8α⌋=4 ones. Every block of eight contains four or five ones. The compact floor formula supplies all finite blocks without asserting an independent random-symbol model.

## Worked example: meaning

The archive’s symbolic Perron models, substitutions and relative periodic cellular automata can be compared through their declared coding maps and retained state. They are not automatically interchangeable realizations of the same process.

## Limits

The memory formula is exact for the stated linear system. A Markovian approximation needs an additional scale-separation error bound. The balance proof establishes frequency and aperiodicity for irrational slope, not a blanket entropy theorem for all symbolic systems.

## Open questions
