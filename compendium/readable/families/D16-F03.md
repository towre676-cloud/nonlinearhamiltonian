## Central question

Exactly when does a polynomial Moyal bracket equal its Poisson bracket, and when does a vanished cubic correction hide a fifth-order defect?

## Synopsis

The Moyal strand has a particularly strong cross-year synthesis because it begins with a formal product and ends with a finite coefficient classification. Fixing the Weyl convention makes every polynomial calculation exact. Antisymmetry removes the even bidifferential orders, while division by iℏ leaves only even powers ℏ^{k−1}. A different power convention cannot be mixed with these coefficients. For monomials, falling factorials turn differentiation into an integer formula; exactness is then the simultaneous vanishing of every active higher odd coefficient.

Two apparently conflicting source ideas become compatible after the degree bound is respected. The family (qp³,q^{3r+2}p^r) has vanishing cubic correction, and all fifth or higher derivatives of the first input vanish because its total degree is four. It is therefore an infinite exact family. Higher-degree pairs can genuinely hide a fifth-order obstruction. The pair (qp⁵,q⁵p³) gives a nonzero ℏ⁴ term despite S₃=0. The atlas adds a smaller witness entirely inside degree six and an exhaustive bounded monomial classification.

This added sextic result is a present synthesis, not a claim about what an earlier conversation proved. Among the twenty-eight monomials of total degree at most six, 504 of 784 ordered pairs are exact and 280 have a defect; four are invisible to the cubic test alone. Among the seven monomials of exactly degree six, fifteen of forty-nine ordered pairs are exact. These statements are complete for the declared finite monomial universe. Arbitrary linear combinations can cancel coefficients across different monomial pairs, so this enumeration is not a classification of all sextic polynomial pairs. At degree seven and above, S₇ and further terms become possible and must be retained.

## Definitions

Set f⋆g=f exp[(iℏ/2)(←∂_q→∂_p−←∂_p→∂_q)]g and [f,g]_M=(f⋆g−g⋆f)/(iℏ).

For f=q^ap^b,g=q^cp^d, define S_k=Σ_{r=0}^k(−1)^r binom(k,r)(a)_{k−r}(b)_r(c)_r(d)_{k−r}, with falling factorials zero beyond the exponent.

The order-k term has coefficient (−1)^{(k−1)/2}ℏ^{k−1}S_k/(2^{k−1}k!) for odd k and output q^{a+c−k}p^{b+d−k}.

A bounded sextic monomial universe means nonnegative exponents with a+b≤6 and c+d≤6; it does not mean arbitrary sextic polynomial coefficients.

## Theorem

For monomials of total degrees at most six, Poisson=Moyal if and only if S₃=S₅=0. The exhaustive universe has 504 exact and 280 defective ordered pairs; four defective pairs have S₃=0. For every r≥1, [qp³,q^{3r+2}p^r]_M=−(8r+6)q^{3r+2}p^{r+2}.

## Proof

Expand the exponential and binomially expand its two commuting left-right derivative operators. Differentiating monomials gives the displayed falling-factorial sum. Swapping f and g changes the order-k bidifferential by (−1)^k, so even orders cancel and odd orders double. Division by iℏ gives the stated coefficient. Each input receives exactly k derivatives, so k exceeds neither total degree; with both degrees at most six, only k=1,3,5 survive. Distinct formal ℏ powers force S₃=S₅=0 separately. Enumerating all nonnegative a+b≤6 gives 28 inputs and 784 ordered pairs; the attached verifier evaluates both coefficients with exact integers and independently recomputes the antisymmetric star expansion. For the infinite family set c=3r+2. Only two terms of S₃ survive and give 6c(c−1)[3r−(c−2)]=0. Orders k≥5 vanish by degree four. The Poisson coefficient is r−3(3r+2)=−8r−6, proving the family uniformly rather than by sampling.

## Worked example: setup

Use the newly stipulated sextic pair f=qp⁵, g=q⁴p², and compare the recovered exact family at r=1.

## Worked example: calculation

For a=1, odd coefficients factor as S_k=(b)_{k−1}(c)_{k−1}[kd−(b−k+1)(c−k+1)]. At b=5,c=4,d=2, S₃=(5)₂(4)₂[6−3·2]=0. At k=5, S₅=(5)₄(4)₄[10−1·0]=120·24·10=28800, giving coefficient 28800/(16·120)=15. The exact bracket is −18q⁴p⁶+15ℏ⁴p². The four cubic-blind ordered exponent pairs are (1,5;4,2),(2,4;5,1) and their reversals. In contrast (qp³,q⁵p) has bracket −14q⁵p³ with no correction. The higher-degree source replacement (qp⁵,q⁵p³) has S₅=201600 and coefficient 105, so it gives −22q⁵p⁷+105ℏ⁴qp³.

## Worked example: meaning

Exactness is a finite coefficient statement with a declared universe. The cubic term is sufficient under a genuine degree-four bound, but not throughout degree six.

## Limits

The bounded classification covers monomial pairs only. General sextic polynomial pairs require coefficient equations allowing cross-pair cancellation. No complete unrestricted high-degree classification, convergence theorem for arbitrary smooth functions, or new historical attribution is claimed.
