## Central question

Which quantities and scaling laws survive a change of coordinates, units or time parameter?

## Synopsis

The modeling strand includes mechanics, population and asset recurrences, conductance gates, mass balances and renormalization flows. Its common discipline is to identify state variables, units, conserved quantities and the meaning of the time parameter before interpreting a formula. Dimensional consistency narrows possible equations but does not determine numerical coefficients, boundary conditions or a physical mechanism. Hamiltonian mechanics supplies a precise constructive invariant: energy conservation. A simple turning point then exhibits two different exponents for physical time and abbreviated action. Their difference is caused by the measure being integrated, not a contradiction. The RG examples use the same chain-rule discipline to distinguish an invariant linearization exponent from coordinate-dependent higher coefficients. These are typed bridges between calculations, rather than a claim that every model in the family describes the same physics.

## Definitions

For a unit-mass one-dimensional Hamiltonian H(q,p)=p²/2+V(q), Hamilton’s equations are q′=p and p′=−V′(q).

At energy E, a simple turning point q* has E−V(q)≈F(q*−q) with F>0 on the allowed side.

A scalar autonomous flow g′=β(g) transforms under h=φ(g), φ′≠0, to β_h(h)=φ′(g)β(g). The meaning of the time variable remains part of this definition.

## Theorem

For the stated Hamiltonian, H is conserved. If V(q)=E−F(q*−q) exactly on q≤q*, then with δ=q*−q the remaining travel time is T(δ)=√(2/F)δ^(1/2), while the action length is S(δ)=(2/3)√(2F)δ^(3/2). At any scalar-flow fixed point, dβ_h/dh=β′(g*) under a smooth invertible state-coordinate change.

## Proof

Energy conservation is a direct chain-rule calculation: H′=V′(q)q′+pp′=V′p−pV′=0. On the increasing branch p=√[2(E−V(q))]=√(2Fδ). Physical travel time integrates dq/p, so T(δ)=∫₀^δ(2Fs)^(−1/2)ds=√(2/F)√δ. The abbreviated action integrates p dq, giving S(δ)=∫₀^δ√(2Fs)ds=(2/3)√(2F)δ^(3/2). The two powers therefore concern different observables. For a smooth potential with a nonzero turning derivative, Taylor’s theorem produces these leading terms with controlled local remainders; a degenerate turning point has different exponents. For the scalar coordinate calculation, differentiate β_h=φ′β with respect to h using d/dh=(φ′)^(−1)d/dg. The result is β′+(φ″/φ′)β. At a fixed point β(g*)=0, the second term vanishes and the linearized exponent is unchanged. This conclusion uses a state-coordinate change with φ′ nonzero and the same time. A rescaling of time multiplies the rate and must not be silently included. Exact flow integration makes the same point: for g′=bg², 1/g(t)=1/g₀−bt on every interval avoiding its pole. The finite-time wall is a property of that stated flow, not a physically meaningful singularity without an identified scale convention.

## Worked example: setup

Let F=2 in the turning-point model, and separately let g′=g² with g₀=1/4.

## Worked example: calculation

The travel time from distance δ to the turn is √δ, whereas the action is (4/3)δ^(3/2). The exact flow is g(t)=1/(4−t) for t<4. Replacing g by h=2g changes the beta function to h′=h²/2, but the same solution and wall time remain. At a nonzero fixed point of a flow with β(g)=−εg+bg², the derivative is ε, and the state-coordinate argument preserves that number.

## Worked example: meaning

Each formula carries its own dimensions and clock. A mass balance similarly needs an actual volume, molar conversion and yield; an area alone cannot recover an amount.

## Limits

The turning-point theorem assumes a simple nondegenerate turn and the stated mass normalization. Time-dependent forcing breaks this energy conservation unless its work is included. Phenomenological recurrences retain their stipulated interpretation and are not validated by algebra alone.
