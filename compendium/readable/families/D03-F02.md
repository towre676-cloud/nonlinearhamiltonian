## Central question

Which finite identities certify rank, spectrum and decomposition, and which metric is each certificate using?

## Synopsis

Linear algebra is a common language across the source years, but a useful synthesis must preserve the question encoded by each matrix. Rank counts independent directions, singular values quantify amplification after metrics are fixed, Jordan structure measures nilpotent mixing, and a projector certifies a subspace only through its defining identities. A large Frobenius norm, a symmetry label and an eigenvalue list cannot replace one another. The revised framework starts with a domain, codomain and scalar field, then attaches the appropriate certificate.

The exact approach is particularly effective when a numerical claim can be reduced to polynomial identities. A verified factorization A=UV supplies a rank upper bound; a nonzero minor supplies a lower bound. Reduction modulo a prime can certify that a rational minor is nonzero, while a rank drop modulo that prime can arise from arithmetic cancellation. A spectral polynomial p(T)=0 gives projectors only when its roots are distinct; actual nonzero support witnesses are still needed to certify that every advertised eigenvalue occurs. These are constructive, inspectable statements rather than a generic reliance on diagonalization software.

Metrics are part of this structure. A diagonalizable matrix with real spectrum can be self-adjoint in a suitable positive metric without being symmetric in the original coordinates. Conversely a nontrivial Jordan block cannot be made self-adjoint by a positive metric, because an isometric change to ordinary Euclidean coordinates would make it diagonalizable. The same rule governs anisotropy and whitening: coordinate changes preserve metric singular values only when both source and target metrics transform. This family supplies algebraic certificates which the inverse, control and statistical chapters can then use with their own observation and error models.

## Definitions

A rank certificate pairs an exact rank-r factorization with a nonzero r×r minor.

A positive metric η defines ⟨x,y⟩_η=x*ηy; η-self-adjointness is A*η=ηA.

For commuting idempotents P_i, define E_ε=∏_i P_i^{ε_i}(I−P_i)^{1−ε_i}, ε∈{0,1}^m.

For a squarefree polynomial p(t)=∏_{λ∈S}(t−λ), Lagrange polynomials e_λ(t)=∏_{μ≠λ}(t−μ)/(λ−μ) define spectral projectors when p(T)=0.

## Theorem

If P_1,…,P_m are commuting idempotents on a finite-dimensional vector space, the E_ε are pairwise annihilating idempotents summing to I, and im(E_ε) is the simultaneous eigenspace P_i v=ε_i v. Hence the nonzero blocks give an exact direct-sum decomposition.

## Proof

All factors in E_ε commute, and each factor is idempotent, so E_ε²=E_ε. If ε≠δ, some index i has one product containing P_i and the other containing I−P_i. Their product is zero, proving E_εE_δ=0. Expanding ∏_i(P_i+(I−P_i)) gives Σ_εE_ε=I. Multiplication by P_i gives P_iE_ε=ε_iE_ε. Conversely, if v satisfies all simultaneous eigenvalue equations, every factor of E_ε acts as identity on v, so E_εv=v. Thus its image is exactly that eigenspace. Any v equals ΣE_εv; applying each projector to a supposed zero sum proves directness. The proof requires commutativity: arbitrary idempotents can have incompatible images and the product construction can fail even to be idempotent.

## Worked example: setup

Use P=diag(1,1,0,0), Q=diag(1,0,1,0), and T=2P−Q.

## Worked example: calculation

The Boolean blocks E_11,E_10,E_01,E_00 are the four coordinate projections. T has respective eigenvalues 1,2,−1,0, so p(T)=T(T−I)(T−2I)(T+I)=0. Every block is nonzero, witnessed by its coordinate basis vector. A separate rank example is A=[[1,2,3],[2,4,6],[0,1,1]]: its factorization into its first two independent columns times coefficient rows gives rank at most two, while the minor on rows 1,3 and columns 1,2 equals one. It remains nonzero modulo every prime. In contrast, [[2]] has rational rank one and rank zero modulo two; modular rank loss is not a rational vanishing certificate.

## Worked example: meaning

The examples provide independent upper and lower witnesses. The eigenvalue list is supported by actual vectors, and no conditioning statement is inferred from exact rank alone.

## Limits

Finite exact identities do not settle infinite operator domains or asymptotic boundedness. Rational rank, integral torsion and floating-point effective rank remain different invariants.

## Open questions
