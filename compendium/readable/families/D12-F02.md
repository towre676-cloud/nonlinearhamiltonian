## Central question

Which update respects the feasible geometry and comes with a descent or contraction theorem?

## Synopsis

The source development repeatedly distinguishes an intended variational objective from an informal update rule. Soft thresholding, radial shrinkage, ridge regularization, projected gradient, Mahalanobis steps, resolvents and Wasserstein proximal maps all become coherent when the update is defined as a minimizer. This family uses that minimization property to derive an inequality, then studies what remains invisible in a projected or singular geometry. Continuous dissipation and discrete stability are related but separate claims. A small gradient, a clipped direction or an Armijo line search is not a substitute for the exact hypotheses needed by the chosen method. Exact proximal examples also show what the objective preserves: radial shrinkage keeps direction, soft thresholding can change support, and a subspace projector can permanently preserve invisible components.

## Definitions

For a proper convex g, prox_{ηg}(z)=argmin_x[g(x)+‖x−z‖²/(2η)] where the minimizer exists; the quadratic supplies strict convexity.

Projection Π_C is the proximal map of the indicator of a nonempty closed convex set C.

An M-weighted step uses ‖d‖_M²=dᵀMd with M>0; the metric is part of the algorithm.

For differentiable f with L-Lipschitz gradient, the descent lemma is f(y)≤f(x)+∇f(x)·(y−x)+(L/2)‖y−x‖².

## Theorem

For x∈C closed convex and x+=Π_C(x−η∇f(x)), f(x+)≤f(x)−(1/η−L/2)‖x+−x‖². Thus 0<η<2/L gives strict descent unless the step vanishes. For a quadratic local model with Hessian H, the resolvent step δ=−η(I+ηH)^{-1}g is the unique minimizer when I+ηH>0.

## Proof

The projection variational inequality gives (x−η∇f(x)−x+)·(x−x+)≤0. Rearranging yields ∇f(x)·(x+−x)≤−‖x+−x‖²/η. Substitute this into the descent lemma to obtain the bound. Convexity is used for the projection inequality; smoothness controls the objective. The resolvent follows by differentiating q(δ)=g·δ+(1/2)δᵀHδ+‖δ‖²/(2η). Its Hessian H+I/η is positive definite under the stated condition, so the stationary point is the global unique minimizer. In an H eigenbasis each component is −ηg_i/(1+ηλ_i), making curvature damping and a possible negative-curvature obstruction explicit.

## Worked example: setup

For z=(3,4), compare the minimizers of (1/2)‖x−z‖²+λ‖x‖_1, with λ‖x‖_2, and with (λ/2)‖x‖² at λ=1. Also inspect a four-cycle quadratic energy.

## Worked example: calculation

The three updates are respectively (2,3), (12/5,16/5), and (3/2,2). Componentwise sparsity, radial shrinkage and uniform rescaling are different objectives. For a graph Laplacian with spectrum (0,2,2,4), a gradient step has factors 1,1−2η,1−2η,1−4η. At η=.4 every nonconstant mode contracts; at η=.6 the top factor is −1.4 and grows. For η>0 and convex potential a|x|²/2 with a≥0, the quadratic Wasserstein proximal map is the pushforward x↦x/(1+ηa). W_2 distances scale exactly by q=1/(1+ηa); this is strict contraction when a>0 and the identity when a=0.

## Worked example: meaning

The source sequence now has a common constructive workflow: state the objective and metric, derive its minimizing map, then prove feasibility, descent and any stronger convergence property separately. A projected update can vanish because the gradient points into an excluded subspace, even when the original objective is not stationary. That may be a desired constraint, but its interpretation must be declared.

## Limits

The projection theorem does not cover arbitrary nonconvex sets or projecting the gradient instead of the updated state. BFGS positive definiteness needs yᵀs>0; Armijo decrease alone does not ensure it. Fisher, Gauss–Newton and Hessian substitutions need their own approximation analysis. These worked maps are not an implementation of a general optimizer or Wasserstein solver.
