## Central question

Which drift, noise and observation model determines the uncertainty at a future time?

## Synopsis

The stochastic strand becomes coherent when a process is specified before its spectrum, stationary law or prediction error is computed. Finite reversible chains, a two-spin Gibbs model, Ornstein–Uhlenbeck dynamics, bridge functionals and exact linear propagation supply complementary levels of resolution. The key unification is the generator: transition rates in a finite state space and second derivatives in a diffusion both encode how expectations evolve. They do not make deterministic gradient descent, white noise and colored fluctuations interchangeable. The prediction problem adds a second layer: an observation changes the present conditional law, then a common transition propagates that law and introduces fresh process noise. Reversibility supplies a complementary finite certificate: detailed balance makes a Markov generator symmetric after weighting by its stationary law, so its relaxation modes can be checked spectrally. That self-adjoint reduction is specific to the declared chain and is not available for arbitrary driven dynamics.

## Definitions

An Itô diffusion satisfies dX=b(X,t)dt+σ(X,t)dW; its generator is Lf=b·∇f+(1/2)tr(σσᵀD²f).

For dX=AXdt+LdW with deterministic coefficients, F=e^{AΔ} and Q=∫_0^Δe^{As}LLᵀe^{Aᵀs}ds describe the exact transition.

An OU process has drift −κX, κ>0; a stationary variance solves the scalar Lyapunov equation −2κv+σ²=0.

A Brownian bridge B_t=W_t−(t/T)W_T conditions the endpoint, changing covariances and all integrals sensitive to constants.

## Theorem

For dX=−κXdt+σdW and deterministic X_t=x, E[X_T²|X_t=x]=e^{−2κτ}x²+σ²(1−e^{−2κτ})/(2κ), τ=T−t. More generally a linear Gaussian prior covariance P propagates to FPFᵀ+Q. A common propagation preserves Loewner order between two posterior covariances.

## Proof

The integrating factor gives X_T=e^{−κτ}x+σ∫_t^Te^{−κ(T−s)}dW_s. The stochastic integral has mean zero and, by Itô isometry, variance σ²∫_0^τe^{−2κs}ds. Squaring and taking expectations gives the displayed expression. The generator gives the same conclusion: L(x²)=−2κx²+σ², where the constant σ² is the Itô correction. For the matrix process use the variation-of-constants formula, independence of future Wiener increments and the matrix isometry. If P_1≤P_2, then F(P_2−P_1)Fᵀ≥0. Adding the same Q preserves the order. Different terminal times require different F and Q and therefore are outside that comparison.

## Worked example: setup

Let X=(position,driver), A=[[0,1],[0,0]], prior P=I, horizon Δ=10, and a scalar sensor with noise variance r=1/100. Compare measuring position with measuring driver, both at the present time.

## Worked example: calculation

F=[[1,10],[0,1]]. A measurement of position gives posterior diag(1/101,1), so terminal position variance is 100+1/101. A driver measurement gives diag(1,1/101), hence variance 1+100/101=201/101. The indirect sensor is better for this future target. If L=(0,1)ᵀ, process covariance is [[Δ³/3,Δ²/2],[Δ²/2,Δ]]; at Δ=2 it has determinant 4/3 and must be added for either sensor. For OU Euler–Maruyama, stationary variance is σ²/(2κ−κ²h), existing only for 0<κh<2.

## Worked example: meaning

A good present measurement and a good future-target measurement are different design questions. The exact transition separates structural ignorance, current sensor noise, future noise and numerical discretization bias. Each has a different remedy and a different mathematical certificate.

## Limits

Linearity and Gaussianity make these covariance calculations closed. Nonlinear filtering needs a different theorem. Spectral color additionally needs stationarity or a declared integrated process and finite cutoffs when variance would diverge. A bridge cannot be treated as independent Brownian noise: its constant test functional vanishes. None of the illustrative matrices validates a historical weather or sensor-performance number.

## Open questions

Bound finite-horizon prediction error when A and L range over a declared compact uncertainty set.

For an OU discretization, choose a step to control stationary variance bias and computational cost simultaneously.
