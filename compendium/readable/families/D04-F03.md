## Central question

What information survives in Tor, Ext and a tangent complex that an ordinary product or dimension count discards?

## Synopsis

Derived constructions retain the effect of relations that ordinary algebra has suppressed. Tensoring a resolution and applying Hom to it produce Tor and Ext; these groups measure failures of exactness rather than furnishing decorative refinements of a product. A particular extension belongs to an Ext group, so the dimension of the ambient group cannot determine whether that extension splits. Likewise a nonzero obstruction space is a location where an obstruction may live, not a proof that every first-order motion fails. The actual class or nonlinear obstruction map must be computed.

This principle unifies three distinct lines of the source corpus. Dual numbers provide an exact module model with nonzero derived tensor groups in all positive degrees. A singular hypersurface supplies an explicit cotangent complex and a failed second-order lift. A differential graded Lie algebra organizes nonlinear compatibility through the Maurer–Cartan equation. These constructions share a mechanism: solve at first order, identify the next defect as a cocycle, and ask whether a correcting term makes it a boundary. Their coefficient rings and degree conventions remain different. A deformation of a scheme, an extension of modules and a flatness problem for an algebra must therefore be connected by an actual functor or comparison theorem before their obstruction groups are identified. The archive’s elliptic self-extension example is especially useful as a boundary: stable objects can have nontrivial self-extensions, whereas an honestly semisimple abelian category has none in positive Ext degree.

## Definitions

For R=k[ε]/(ε²), let k=R/(ε). A free resolution of k has all positive differentials equal to multiplication by ε.

An element of Ext¹_R(C,A) classifies an extension 0→A→B→C→0 up to the stated equivalence; the zero class is the split extension.

For A=k[x]/(x²) in characteristic zero, the cotangent complex is [A --2x→ A] in degrees −1 and zero. Its derived dual at x=0 is the tangent complex.

In a differential graded Lie algebra, dα+[α,α]/2=0. Writing α=ta+t²b makes the first two equations da=0 and db=−[a,a]/2.

## Theorem

For dual numbers, Tor_n^R(k,k)≅k and Extⁿ_R(k,k)≅k for every n≥0. The extension 0→k→R→k→0 is nonsplit. At the double point x²=0, a first-order displacement x=at lifts modulo t³ only if a²=0.

## Proof

The resolution is exact because the kernel and image of multiplication by ε are both εR. Tensoring it with k makes every differential zero, as does applying Hom_R(−,k), proving the Tor and Ext assertions. An R-linear section of R→k would send 1 to a vector v with εv=0, hence v∈εR, whose quotient is zero; it cannot map back to 1. The direct-sum extension with middle term k⊕k does split although its endpoint Ext group is identical. For the singularity, differentiating x² gives 2x dx. At x=0 this map becomes zero, so the tangent and first obstruction spaces are each k. Direct substitution gives (at+bt²)²=a²t² modulo t³. This computes the actual obstruction map a↦a²; over a field it kills only a=0. In a differential graded Lie algebra, the derivation and Jacobi identities make [a,a]/2 closed when da=0. The next equation is solvable precisely when this cocycle class vanishes, which is a class-level criterion.

## Worked example: setup

Use a finite deformation model L¹=Qa⊕Qb, L²=Qc, with da=0, db=c, [a,a]=2c and all other relevant brackets zero.

## Worked example: calculation

The first-order term ta is closed. Its quadratic defect is t²c, a nonzero vector that is nevertheless the boundary d(t²b). The correction α=ta−t²b satisfies dα+[α,α]/2=−t²c+t²c=0 exactly. Removing b from the complex makes c a nonzero H² class and prevents the same correction. Thus the raw size of the residual does not decide whether the deformation is obstructed.

## Worked example: meaning

The constructive output is a lift or a nonzero quotient class. It specifies exactly which information the derived layer adds, and distinguishes algebraic failure from numerical error in a candidate representative.

## Limits

The finite Maurer–Cartan model is a complete example, not the missing controlling algebra of a historical proposal. A potential obstruction space can be nonzero while the actual class vanishes. Derived equivalence, formal smoothness and physical stability are distinct claims.

## Open questions

For a specified singularity, compute the nonlinear obstruction map through third order and determine which first-order directions lift.

For a proposed obstruction energy, show that its zero test factors through the relevant cohomology class rather than depending on a chosen cocycle representative.
