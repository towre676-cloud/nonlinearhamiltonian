## Central question

Which geometric information is preserved by a parametrization, a birational coordinate change or a normalization?

## Synopsis

An equation is not yet a complete specification of the geometric object being studied. An affine curve may be singular, its normalization smooth, and its projective closure may acquire additional points at infinity. A parametrization can be injective and still lose differential information. Conversely, an explicit rational change of coordinate can identify function fields even when the displayed equations look unrelated. This family builds a hierarchy of certificates: substitution proves that a map lands on the curve; an inverse on a dense open proves birationality; an integral-closure argument proves normalization; Jacobian or local-ring calculations detect singularities; ramification on the smooth compact model determines genus. Cross-ratio symmetry supplies a finite quotient of ordered configurations, but singular special values and ramification must be handled before treating a parameter as a moduli coordinate.

## Definitions

A normalization replaces an integral curve by the curve whose coordinate ring is integrally closed in its function field. A birational map is an isomorphism of function fields, not necessarily an everywhere-defined isomorphism.

For a plane curve F=0 in characteristic zero, a point with F=F_x=F_y=0 is singular. Local intersection multiplicity measures the length of the corresponding local quotient when finite.

For squarefree P of degree m and d≥2, the superelliptic curve means the smooth projective normalization of y^d=P(x), with δ=gcd(d,m) points over infinity.

## Theorem

The cusp y²=x³ is normalized by t↦(t²,t³), and its intersection multiplicity with y=0 at the origin is three. For squarefree P of degree m, the smooth projective model of y^d=P(x) has genus g=((d−1)(m−1)−gcd(d,m)+1)/2.

## Proof

The ring A=C[t²,t³] has fraction field C(t), since t=t³/t², and t is integral over A because it satisfies T²−t²=0. C[t] is integrally closed and finite over A, so it is the normalization. The cusp is singular at the origin because the two partial derivatives of y²−x³ vanish there; the parametrization derivative is also zero there, explaining why injectivity does not imply an immersion. Quotienting the local equation by y gives C[x]_(x)/(x³), whose length is three. In the blowup chart y=ux, the total equation is x²(u²−x)=0 and the strict transform u²−x=0 is smooth. For the superelliptic genus, each of the m simple finite zeros has ramification index d, contributing d−1. At infinity there are δ points, each of index d/δ, contributing d−δ in total. Riemann–Hurwitz gives 2g−2=−2d+m(d−1)+(d−δ). Rearrangement yields the stated formula. Both the squarefree assumption and the use of the smooth compact normalization are essential.

## Worked example: calculation

For y²=x³−x, d=2,m=3,δ=1 gives genus one. For y³=x⁴−1, d=3,m=4,δ=1 gives genus three; its compact surface therefore has Euler characteristic −4. The rational function R(z)=(1−z)³/z² on P¹ has divisor 3[1]−2[0]−[∞]. Setting u=(z−1)/z transforms R(z)=1 into u³−u+1=0, with inverse z=1/(1−u). The equation excludes z=0,1 and u=0,1, so the denominators introduce no missing or extraneous solutions. A matching discriminant would not have proved this coordinate identification; the forward and inverse substitutions do.

## Worked example: meaning

Each model supports a different valid question: singular local structure, smooth genus, ordered branch data or arithmetic field identity. The chosen object must remain explicit throughout.

## Limits

The genus formula does not apply unchanged to repeated roots, positive characteristic dividing d or an unreduced affine equation. A normalization is not automatically an embedding, and a blowup chart does not by itself prove global resolution. General singularity classification requires more than a handful of invariants.

## Open questions

For a proposed parameter family, where do repeated roots or denominator poles change the normalization and branch profile?

Can every claimed moduli identification exhibit its exact finite symmetry group and distinguish generic fibres from special ramified fibres?
