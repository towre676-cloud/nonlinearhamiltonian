## Central question

How do conservation, reduction and projection follow from a specified geometric form?

## Synopsis

Symplectic geometry makes dynamical rules structural: a nondegenerate closed two-form converts the derivative of a Hamiltonian into a vector field. Contact geometry replaces this even-dimensional structure with a maximally nonintegrable hyperplane distribution in odd dimension. The archive’s examples become coherent when these forms are written before their consequences. Hamiltonian conservation follows from antisymmetry, a Casimir is a much stronger object, and a cyclic coordinate can be removed only after its conjugate momentum is fixed. A dimension count for a quotient needs a regular constraint and a free action, not just a formal subtraction.

The oscillator links Hamilton’s equations, Hamilton–Jacobi graphs and Riccati evolution without identifying their coordinate descriptions. Its full phase-space flow is smooth for all times. The generating function for the image of one initial graph fails at a caustic because projection to configuration space ceases to be invertible. The divergence is in the graph representation rather than in the trajectory itself. The quiver-style cotangent quotient supplies an equally concrete reduction: solving a moment-map equation and quotienting a free scaling action produces cotangent coordinates, including their canonical one-form. Contact examples then add a compatible but distinct structure. A Legendrian curve satisfies a tangent condition such as dz−y dx=0; on a thermodynamic equilibrium surface the same contact logic yields a potential identity and a Maxwell relation, provided the stated differentiability and change of variables hold.

## Definitions

Use ω=Σdq_j∧dp_j and define i_{X_H}ω=dH, giving q̇_j=∂H/∂p_j and ṗ_j=−∂H/∂q_j.

A canonical Casimir has zero Poisson bracket with every differentiable function; nondegeneracy forces it to be constant on a connected domain.

For a symmetry generated by translation of q₂, its momentum is p₂. The reduced space at p₂=μ is the momentum level modulo that translation.

A contact form α in dimension 2n+1 satisfies α∧(dα)ⁿ≠0. A Legendrian submanifold has dimension n and tangent bundle inside kerα.

## Theorem

For H=(p₁²+p₂²)/2+V(q₁), reduction at p₂=μ yields ω_red=dq₁∧dp₁ and H_μ=p₁²/2+V(q₁)+μ²/2. For the scaling action on (z,w)∈Cᵐ⊕(Cᵐ)*, the regular quotient {w(z)=0,z≠0}/C* is T*P^{m−1}.

## Proof

On the level p₂=μ every tangent variation has dp₂=0, so the pullback of ω is dq₁∧dp₁. Its kernel is precisely the q₂ translation direction. Quotienting removes that kernel and leaves a nondegenerate form. The restricted H is independent of q₂, so it descends; the projected full equations are exactly Hamilton’s equations of H_μ. In the complex quotient, z selects a line ℓ. The constraint makes w vanish on ℓ, and z⊗w defines an invariant map Cᵐ/ℓ→ℓ, canonically a cotangent vector at ℓ. Conversely a cotangent vector has such a representation after choosing a nonzero z, with different choices exactly the scaling action. The moment-map derivative is surjective on z≠0 by varying w, and the action is free there, giving dimension 2m−2. In a chart z₁=1 with z=(1,u), the constraint gives w=(−up,p) and the canonical one-form w·dz is p du.

## Worked example: setup

For the unit oscillator H=(q²+p²)/2 start with p₀=0, and compare the full trajectory with its Hamilton–Jacobi graph.

## Worked example: calculation

Hamilton’s equations give q=q₀cos t, p=−q₀sin t. When cos t≠0 this graph is p=−q tan t, with generating function S=−q²tan t/2. Direct substitution gives S_t+(S_q²+q²)/2=0. At t=π/2 all initial q₀ project to q=0 while their momenta differ, so no single-valued p(q) describes the line. The phase-space map is still the exact symplectic rotation. For α=dz−y dx, γ(t)=(t,t,t²/2) satisfies γ*α=t dt−t dt=0 and is Legendrian.

## Worked example: meaning

Reduction removes a declared symmetry after fixing conserved momentum. A caustic changes a coordinate description. Neither phenomenon means that the original smooth dynamics has ceased to exist.

## Limits

The cotangent quotient is a Higgs-type complex symplectic model; identifying a hyperkähler metric or a Coulomb branch needs additional constructions. Singular momentum levels and nonfree actions require stratified reduction. The oscillator graph is valid only away from its caustic.

## Open questions

For a specified symmetry action, prove regularity and freeness on the chosen momentum level, and calculate the reduced form in compatible charts.

For a proposed Hamilton–Jacobi model, locate every projection singularity and replace failed graph coordinates with another local generating family.
