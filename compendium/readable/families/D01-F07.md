## Central question

How do multiplicative identities become analytic formulas without losing signs, prime powers, convergence or main terms?

## Synopsis

The divisor algebra gives formal identities; absolute convergence permits their interpretation as analytic functions. This transition is where several recovered proposals needed repair. Pointwise μΛ is a negative prime indicator weighted by logarithm, while convolution μ*log equals the positive prime-power function Λ. A prime-ideal sum changes again when norms and splitting types enter. An Euler product in Re(s)>1 is nonvanishing there, but this fact does not settle zeros after analytic continuation. Likewise π(x) and x/log x are not interchangeable at square-root error scale: the omitted x/log²x term is much larger. The family organizes these issues around exact operation and domain. Spectral determinants, sampled zeta layouts and prime oscillatory tones can be valid mathematical objects while their proposed identification with arithmetic zeros remains an additional, explicitly missing theorem.

## Definitions

The von Mangoldt function Λ(n) is log p when n=p^k, k≥1, and zero otherwise. The Möbius function μ vanishes on integers with a squared prime factor.

A Dirichlet series is absolutely convergent where Σ|a_n|n^(−σ) is finite; only in a justified domain may one freely multiply, rearrange or differentiate the relevant series.

The arithmetic prime sums θ(x)=Σ_(p≤x)log p and ψ(x)=Σ_(p^k≤x)log p differ by prime-power contributions. A spectral zeta requires its own operator, eigenvalue domain and summability.

## Theorem

The exact convolution identity μ*log=Λ gives −ζ′(s)/ζ(s)=ΣΛ(n)n^(−s) for Re(s)>1. Also Σφ(n)n^(−s)=ζ(s−1)/ζ(s) for Re(s)>2, and for real σ>2 its tail after N is at most N^(2−σ)/(σ−2).

## Proof

For n=∏p_i^a_i, log n=Σ_(d|n)Λ(d), since the prime powers dividing n contribute Σ_i a_i log p_i. Convolution with μ, using μ*1=ε, gives μ*log=Λ. Absolute convergence in σ>1 justifies differentiating the Euler product and summing its geometric prime-power expansions. By contrast μ(n)Λ(n) is −log p at n=p and zero elsewhere, because μ(p^k)=0 for k≥2; its sum is −θ(x), not ψ(x). For the totient identity, n=Σ_(d|n)φ(d) means id=1*φ. Multiplication of absolutely convergent Dirichlet series in σ>2 gives ζ(s−1)=ζ(s)Σφ(n)n^(−s); the Euler product ensures ζ(s)≠0 there. Since 0≤φ(n)≤n, the remaining tail is bounded by Σ_(n>N)n^(1−σ)≤∫_N^∞x^(1−σ)dx. This explicitly states both the stronger half-plane required by φ(n) and an error certificate for truncation.

## Worked example: setup

Compare the two operations at n=12 and bound a real totient series.

## Worked example: calculation

The divisors of 12 with nonzero μ are 1,2,3,6. Thus (μ*log)(12)=log 12−log 6−log 4+log 2=0, agreeing with Λ(12)=0. At n=8 the convolution is log 8−log 4=log 2, while μ(8)Λ(8)=0. At n=3 the latter product is −log 3. For σ=4 and N=100, the totient tail is at most 100^(−2)/2=1/20000. This is a deterministic truncation error, not an empirical convergence observation or a zero-location theorem.

## Worked example: meaning

A single symbol change between multiplication and convolution changes the arithmetic object; a single half-plane change can invalidate an analytic manipulation.

## Limits

The worked integer checks certify arithmetic identities, while the convergence proof supplies infinite validity. No Riemann hypothesis, BSD conclusion or arithmetic-spectral identification is inferred. Floating-point samples on a line do not certify unsampled zeros or a global distribution.
