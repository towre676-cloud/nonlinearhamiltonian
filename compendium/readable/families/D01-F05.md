## Central question

Which field, ideal and lattice data are sufficient to turn a norm identity into a complete arithmetic statement?

## Synopsis

A defining polynomial is the beginning of a number-field calculation. Irreducibility, the polynomial-order index, ramification, unit structure and the actual sublattice representing the original equation each impose additional requirements. The squarefree discriminants −23 and −283 give unusually clean maximal-order certificates, while nonabelian Galois closure supplies a decisive obstruction to cyclotomic containment. The class-field examples add a second kind of obstruction: an extension with the right degree can still ramify at finite or real places. Finally, the 2026 norm forms show why unit transport alone need not classify a two-variable equation. Multiplication preserves a cubic norm surface, but often leaves the binary plane that encoded the original problem. Retaining the embedding lattice makes this loss visible and turns a plausible reduction into a mathematically correct one.

## Definitions

For an algebraic integer α, the discriminant of Z[α] equals the field discriminant times [O_K:Z[α]]². A squarefree polynomial discriminant therefore forces index one.

The field norm N_(K/Q)(x) is the determinant of multiplication by x on K. In a specified integral order, norm ±1 implies a unit of that order.

A binary cubic equation can embed in a norm equation only together with a rank-two lattice condition; the full norm surface has an extra coordinate. An exceptional unit is an algebraic integer z for which both z and 1−z are units.

## Theorem

For f(T)=T³−T+1, irreducibility and discriminant −23 imply S₃ Galois closure and exclude every root from every cyclotomic field. Separately, in the order Z[z] with z³=−4, N(a+bz+cz²)=a³−4b³+16c³+12abc; norm-one transport need not preserve c=0.

## Proof

The only possible rational roots of the monic cubic are ±1, neither of which is a root. Its discriminant is −4(−1)³−27=−23, not a rational square. An irreducible cubic has transitive Galois group A₃ or S₃; the discriminant-square criterion excludes A₃. Every subfield of a finite abelian Galois extension is itself Galois because every subgroup is normal. A nonnormal cubic with S₃ closure therefore cannot be contained in a cyclotomic field. For the norm formula, multiplication by a+bz+cz² in basis (1,z,z²) has matrix [[a,−4c,−4b],[b,a,−4c],[c,b,a]]. Its determinant is the displayed form, giving an exact integral certificate without approximate roots. The unit ε=1−z−z² has norm one. Multiplying the binary element −1−3z by ε gives −13−2z+4z². Both have norm 107, but the latter has nonzero third coordinate. Consequently unit-orbit completeness in the ambient order, even if independently proved, would still require an exact return-to-plane test to solve u³+4v³=107.

## Worked example: calculation

For K=Q(√10), the quadratic discriminant is 40 and the class number is two. The biquadratic L=Q(√2,√5) has discriminant 8·5·40=1600=40², so its relative discriminant has norm one; L is totally real, giving no real-place ramification. The alternative K(i) has discriminant 40·(−4)·(−40)=6400 and relative norm four, so it is ramified. For H(u,v)=−3u³+9uv²−2v³ and β³−27β−18=0, determinant expansion gives N(−3u−βv)=9H(u,v). The relevant lattice is −3Z+Zβ, not every norm element of the field.

## Limits

A norm-one element is a unit of the chosen order, but a displayed unit pair need not generate the full unit group. Ramification and class-field assertions use stated classical inputs. No uniqueness of a field from a small discriminant or a polynomial list is claimed.
