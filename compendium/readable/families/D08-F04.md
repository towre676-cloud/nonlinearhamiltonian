## Central question

What does a finite asymptotic expansion determine, and how can its remainder and missing exponential contribution be made explicit?

## Synopsis

The asymptotic strand includes renormalization flows, spectral gaps, elliptic logarithms, cubic folds, Mathieu perturbation, and a factorial Borel model. Its common requirement is to state the small parameter, the approach sector and the uniformity of a remainder. A formal series can be useful while diverging everywhere; convergence and asymptotic accuracy are distinct. Conversely, a few perturbative coefficients cannot determine a global continuation, a Stokes constant or a nonlinear front law. The one-pole Borel example makes the distinction fully constructive. Its coefficients, exact integral, finite error bound and exponentially small solution ambiguity are all explicit. The cubic fold supplies a complementary algebraic scale: coalescing critical points produce a three-halves action difference and a two-thirds transition parameter. Each model has its own hypotheses and should not be promoted to a universal description by analogy.

## Definitions

The formal series F(z)=Σ_{n≥0}n!z^(n+1) has zero radius of convergence. Its Borel transform is B(t)=Σt^n=1/(1−t) near t=0.

For a>0 define the nonsingular negative-ray sum F(−a)=−∫₀∞e^(−u/a)/(1+u)du.

An asymptotic expansion controls the error after each fixed finite truncation as a parameter tends to a limit; it need not converge when infinitely many terms are added.

## Theorem

For every a>0 and N≥1, |F(−a)−Σ_{n=0}^{N−1}n!(−a)^(n+1)|≤N!a^(N+1). The corresponding Laplace sums solve z²F′−F=−z. Any two solutions differ by Ce^(−1/z); with above-minus-below bypasses of the positive Borel pole, the lateral difference is 2πi e^(−1/z).

## Proof

The exact finite geometric identity gives 1/(1+u)=Σ_{n=0}^{N−1}(−u)^n+(−u)^N/(1+u). Integrate against −e^(−u/a). The nth term evaluates to (−1)^(n+1)n!a^(n+1) by the Mellin moment. The absolute remainder is bounded by ∫₀∞e^(−u/a)u^Ndu=N!a^(N+1), since 1/(1+u)≤1. This proves a genuine finite-order error estimate on the negative ray even though the coefficient ratio makes the infinite power series divergent for every nonzero argument. Differentiation under the convergent integral, or integration by parts in its Laplace form, gives the inhomogeneous equation. Subtracting two solutions gives H′=H/z², hence H=Ce^(−1/z), which is smaller than every power as z↓0 on the positive ray. To compare the two lateral integrals, their difference closes clockwise around t=1. The residue of e^(−t/z)/(1−t) is −e^(−1/z), giving +2πi e^(−1/z) in that convention. The exponential ambiguity is therefore visible without guessing it from several factorial coefficients. A complete sectorial asymptotic proof must keep the bypass and decay contour fixed in a compatible sector; the explicit negative-ray bound is the directly verified remainder used here.

## Worked example: setup

Use a=1/10 and N=5. Separately consider Φ(x;t)=x³/3−tx for t>0.

## Worked example: calculation

The Borel-model truncation is −1/10+1/100−2/1000+6/10000−24/100000=−11455/125000=−0.09164, with error at most 120/10⁶=0.00012. The fold saddles are x_±=±√t and Φ(x_−;t)−Φ(x_+;t)=4t^(3/2)/3. Scaling x=h^(1/3)y in Φ/h yields y³/3−(t/h^(2/3))y, so the nontrivial transition occurs at t of order h^(2/3).

## Worked example: meaning

These examples attach a bound or exact scaling identity to an asymptotic statement. They do not identify the Stokes data or thimbles of an unspecified amplitude.
