## Central question

What separate certificates establish a smooth quotient, a well-behaved boundary, positivity and an actual projective embedding?

## Synopsis

The early arithmetic-geometry material becomes coherent when organized around distinct geometric properties. Neatness removes finite stabilizer phenomena in an open arithmetic quotient. A compactification adds boundary strata. Regular cones make local toric charts smooth. A positive canonical or log-canonical divisor controls a birational growth property. A sufficiently positive line bundle supplies sections that define an embedding. None of these steps is interchangeable with the others. Modular curves make this hierarchy unusually explicit: X(3) is a smooth genus-zero compact curve, while its four-punctured interior is hyperbolic and of log-general type. Divisor intersection on P¹×P¹ then gives a second exact arena for positivity and invariant classes. The family’s strongest result is a curve embedding criterion whose proof states exactly why enough sections separate both points and tangent directions.

## Definitions

For a smooth compact curve X of genus g, deg K_X=2g−2; with c reduced boundary points D, deg(K_X+D)=2g−2+c.

A line bundle L is very ample when its complete linear system gives a closed projective embedding. On a smooth curve, separation of every length-two subscheme is the relevant section criterion.

A two-dimensional toric cone with primitive generators u,v is regular when |det(u,v)|=1. Smoothness is local lattice regularity; projectivity additionally requires a compatible support function.

## Theorem

For a smooth projective curve of genus g and a line bundle L of degree d≥2g+1, h⁰(L)=d+1−g and the complete linear system embeds X into P^(d−g). On the principal modular curve X(3), deg K=−2 while deg(K+D)=2; at level seven these degrees are four and 28.

## Proof

For any length-two subscheme Z, Serre duality gives H¹(L(−Z)) dual to H⁰(K_X⊗L⁻¹(Z)). Its degree is 2g−2−d+2≤−1, so it has no nonzero section. The restriction sequence therefore makes H⁰(L)→H⁰(L|_Z) surjective. Distinct points and doubled points give point separation and tangent separation; the length-one version also gives base-point freeness. The standard embedding criterion then proves very ampleness. Vanishing of H¹(L), followed by Riemann–Roch, yields h⁰(L)=d+1−g. For a torsion-free effective modular group of index μ and c cusps, Gauss–Bonnet gives 2g−2+c=μ/6. Principal level N has c=μ/N. At N=3, μ=12,c=4,g=0; at N=7, μ=168,c=24,g=3. The degree formulas then distinguish ordinary from logarithmic general type. These arguments use the declared smooth projective curve and classical modular-curve inputs; they do not algebraize an arbitrary analytic space.

## Worked example: calculation

The cone generated by (1,0),(1,2) has determinant two and a singular toric chart. Adding (1,1) gives determinant-one adjacent cones, producing a smooth local toric modification. On P¹×P¹, classes (a,b) have intersection pairing (a,b)·(c,d)=ad+bc and self-intersection 2ab; for a,b≥0, h⁰(O(a,b))=(a+1)(b+1). Swapping the factors fixes the line a=b in the real divisor space. For g=1,d=3, the embedding theorem gives three sections and a plane cubic. For g=3,d=7, it gives five sections and an embedding into P⁴.

Boundary regularity, divisor intersections and sections now have computable meanings rather than being treated as interchangeable claims of geometric richness.

## Limits

The degree criterion is sufficient, not necessary. A projective curve is an input to its proof, so the theorem cannot establish algebraization from scratch. A local toric subdivision is not a proof of a global arithmetic compactification. Higher-dimensional general type needs explicit boundary and ramification contributions.

## Open questions

For a specified higher-dimensional arithmetic quotient, what canonical-divisor formula and admissible fan give simultaneous smoothness, projectivity and a proved positivity bound?

Can an intended analytic compactification exhibit a concrete ample bundle and enough sections to verify its claimed algebraization and embedding?
