## Central question

How should finite resolution be chosen when inversion amplifies measurement error?

## Synopsis

Inverse problems make a precise tradeoff visible: a richer inverse reduces approximation bias but amplifies noise. A source class says which signals are admissible; a deterministic noise ball says which data can be trusted. The archive’s strongest late analytic development derives matching upper and lower recovery rates for concrete diagonal models rather than asserting convergence from a suggestive cutoff formula. Mild singular-value decay gives a power-law rate, severe decay gives a logarithmic rate, and the latter requires a lower-order safety margin. Tikhonov and Landweber express the same controlled approximation through different spectral filters. The constructive payoff is a parameter or stopping rule with a declared worst-case bound. A rate is not just a fitted slope: an indistinguishable pair shows that no recovery map can uniformly do better on the stated signal class.

## Definitions

Observe y^δ=Ax+η in Hilbert norm with ||η||≤δ and x in a source class M. A linear reconstruction R has bias B_M(R)=sup_{x∈M}||(RA−I)x||.

For diagonal Ae_k=σ_ke_k, spectral cutoff R_N retains k≤N and divides those coordinates by σ_k.

Use the ellipsoid M_r={x:Σk^{2r}|x_k|²≤1}. Mild decay is σ_k=k^{−p}; severe decay is σ_k=e^{−ck^β}, with all parameters positive.

Tikhonov R_α=(A* A+αI)^{-1}A* and Landweber early stopping are bounded spectral filters approximating the unbounded inverse.

## Theorem

For any bounded R, the worst-case error is at most B_M(R)+δ||R||. Mild cutoff N=floor(δ^{−1/(p+r)}) has minimax order δ^{r/(p+r)}. Severe cutoff with L=log(1/δ) and N=floor(((L−(r/β)log L)/c)^{1/β}) has minimax order L^{−r/β} for sufficiently large L. Merely requiring N∼(L/c)^{1/β} does not guarantee convergence.

## Proof

Decompose Ry^δ−x=(RA−I)x+Rη and use the triangle inequality. On M_r the cutoff bias is (N+1)^{−r}; its noise norm is δ/σ_N. For polynomial singular values, balance N^{−r} with δN^p to obtain the power-law upper bound. Choose K of order δ^{−1/(p+r)} and x_±=±K^{−r}e_K. Both signals lie in the ellipsoid and have ||Ax_±||≤δ after a harmless integer adjustment, so the same datum zero is admissible for both. Any estimator must err by at least K^{−r} on one, proving the matching order. For severe decay, the stated safety margin gives δe^{cN^β}≤L^{−r/β} while N^{−r} has the same order. A K of order (L/c)^{1/β} gives the analogous two-point lower bound. But with c=β=1,δ=e^{−j},N=j, the leading asymptotic cutoff holds and noise amplification equals one; convergence fails.

## Worked example: setup

Compare mild p=r=1 at δ=10^{-4} with severe c=β=r=1 at δ=e^{-100}. Then inspect two alternative filters.

## Worked example: calculation

Mild cutoff N=100 gives error ≤1/101+1/100<0.02, while the indistinguishable signals ±e_100/100 force worst-case error ≥0.01. Severe safe cutoff gives N=95 and bound 1/96+e^{−5}<0.01716; N=100 instead has noise bound one. Under x=(A*A)^νw, ||w||≤ρ and 0<ν≤1, Tikhonov gives bias ≤ρc_να^ν and noise ≤δ/(2√α), where c_ν=ν^ν(1−ν)^{1−ν} and c_1=1. Balancing gives rate δ^{2ν/(2ν+1)} with the corresponding ρ scaling. For Landweber with 0<ω≤||A||^{-2}, m steps give bias ≤ρ(ν/(eωm))^ν and noise ≤δ√(ωm). More iterations can therefore worsen the total guarantee after the bias-noise balance.

## Worked example: meaning

The admissible class, operator spectrum and noise model jointly determine useful resolution; a cutoff or iteration count has no universal meaning on its own.

## Limits

Theorems assume the declared source class and norm-bounded noise. Arbitrary smoothness does not transfer through Tikhonov beyond its qualification without additional treatment. Tuning formulas using ρ assume ρ>0; if ρ=0 the source is known zero. A local Newton enclosure requires its own domain, inverse-Jacobian and contraction checks. Small numerical residuals alone neither establish a root count nor certify inversion.

## Open questions

Can a data-dependent parameter choice achieve the same guarantees without knowing source smoothness?

Which non-diagonal observation models admit computable upper bounds and matching indistinguishable-pair lower bounds?
