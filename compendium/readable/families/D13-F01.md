## Central question

How can a finite combinatorial population be counted, optimized and indexed without confusing objects with their statistics?

## Synopsis

The recurring constructive idea is to make an object contribute exactly once to a finite sum. A partition contributes a monomial through its part multiplicities; a tableau contributes through the position of its largest entry; a project selection contributes through one factor for every labeled project. These constructions give independent descriptions of the same population rather than a fit to its reported size. They also explain why equal weights do not merge labeled selections and why a terminal in a reduced decision diagram can represent many assignments. Once completion counts are available, enumeration becomes an address system: the same integers support counting, conditional queries, exact uniform sampling, and an optimum proved by a threshold population of size one. The mathematical advance is the unification of these operations under a single disjoint decomposition. It does not require trusting an optimizer or assuming that a compact diagram has already retained every multiplicity.

## Definitions

A completion count C(q) is the number of feasible objects extending a prefix q; it counts original objects, unless a quotient population has explicitly been chosen.

For labeled binary choices with nonnegative vector weights w_i and capacity b, C(i,b) counts feasible assignments of coordinates i,…,n−1.

The generating polynomial ∏_i(1+z^{w_i}t^{p_i}) records vector weight and integer profit with multiplicity; a coefficient counts choices, not distinct numerical sums.

A species a_1≥a_2≥…≥0 corresponds bijectively to differences d_j=a_j−a_{j+1}; its least representative is ∏_j P_j^{d_j}, where P_j is the jth primorial.

## Theorem

For nonnegative weights, C(i,b)=C(i+1,b)+C(i+1,b−w_i), with C(i,b)=0 if a capacity coordinate is negative and C(n,b)=1 otherwise. These counts yield inverse bijections rank:Ω→{0,…,|Ω|−1} and unrank. A feasible object of profit v is the unique maximizer if the count of feasible objects with profit at least v equals one.

## Proof

Split each population according to its next bit. The two pieces are disjoint and correspond exactly to the smaller residual capacities, proving the recurrence by induction. If the entire suffix fits, every remaining bit is free and contributes 2^{n−i}; an omitted block of g coordinates therefore contributes a factor 2^g. In lexicographic order the zero branch occupies an initial address interval of length C(q0); the one branch occupies the next interval. Unranking compares an address with C(q0), subtracting that count in the one branch; ranking performs the inverse additions. Induction down the tree proves a bijection, including empty branches. A uniform address consequently induces a uniform object. Finally a singleton threshold population includes the supplied feasible witness, excludes every higher-profit object and excludes every competing equal-profit object. This proves optimum and uniqueness together.

## Worked example: setup

Use three distinct projects with weights (2,3,4), capacity 5 and profits (3,4,5). Also compare a small partition problem.

## Worked example: calculation

The feasible words in zero-before-one order are 000,001,010,100,110. Their profits are 0,5,4,3,7, so addresses 0,…,4 enumerate the complete population and 110 is uniquely optimal. The prefix 1 has two completions. Three unbiased random bits can sample an address by rejecting 5,6,7; direct reduction modulo 5 instead gives probabilities (2,2,2,1,1)/8. Independently, for species with least representative at most 12, the inequality 2^{d_1}6^{d_2}≤12 gives representatives 1,2,4,6,8,12. A partition dynamic program gives p(5)=7, while corner removal gives five standard tableaux of shape (3,2). These different integers count different declared objects.

## Worked example: meaning

An exact count is useful infrastructure: it supports reversible inspection, unbiased sampling and optimality witnesses while preserving which population was actually counted.
