## Central question

How does a topological feature persist through a filtration, and what perturbation or randomness can be certified?

## Synopsis

Persistence studies a sequence of spaces together with the maps between them. A barcode records the life of a class, including classes that disappear before the terminal space is reached. This is why the colimit cannot replace a persistence module: a finite bar contributes information during its lifetime even though its eventual image is zero. The archive moves from explicit small filtrations to diagram distances, stability and random birth times. These developments fit one constructive interface: input a legal filtration, compute boundary maps and induced maps on homology, and then declare which comparison theorem applies.

The legal-filtration requirement is essential. A simplex cannot enter before one of its faces. Assigning a dramatic label such as singularity or cavity to a parameter value does not change homology; the chain complex changes when cells or maps change. A metric between persistence diagrams also needs the diagonal. An unmatched point can die by matching to its closest diagonal point, with cost half its lifetime in the usual ℓ∞ convention. Stability then has an exact elementary core: uniformly close functions give mutually shifted sublevel inclusions. Passing to barcode stability adds finiteness or tameness assumptions. Random filtrations supply another useful bridge, because a feature born when all required edges are present has a birth time given by a maximum of random variables. This connects a topological lifetime to an exact order-statistic law without claiming that all random topology reduces to one scalar distribution.

## Definitions

A filtration K_s⊆K_t for s≤t induces a persistence module H_k(K_s)→H_k(K_t) over a fixed field. A bar [b,d) records a feature born at b and killed at d.

For diagrams with the standard bottleneck distance, match points using ℓ∞ cost and allow arbitrary diagonal points. A point (b,d) has diagonal cost (d−b)/2.

For functions f,g on one space, their sublevel filtrations are F_t={f≤t}, G_t={g≤t}. A uniform bound ||f−g||∞≤ε gives an ε-interleaving by inclusions.

## Theorem

A triangle whose vertices enter at zero, all edges at one and its face at two has one essential H₀ bar, two H₀ bars [0,1), and one H₁ bar [1,2). For a square whose four edge times are independent uniform variables on [0,1] and whose face enters at one, its H₁ birth B has P(B≤t)=t⁴ and expected lifetime 1/5.

## Proof

Before time one the triangle has three components; at one its edge boundary matrix has rank two, merging them and leaving the one-dimensional cycle kernel. At two the face boundary spans that kernel. This proves the bars, including the two simultaneous deaths. For the random square, the unique graph cycle exists exactly when every edge has arrived, so B=max(U₁,U₂,U₃,U₄). Independence gives P(B≤t)=∏P(U_j≤t)=t⁴. The face kills it at one, hence expected lifetime is E(1−B)=∫₀¹P(B≤t)dt=1/5. At a fixed t<1 the first Betti number is its birth indicator, with expectation t⁴. For stability, if f(x)≤t then g(x)≤f(x)+ε≤t+ε, giving F_t⊆G_{t+ε}. The reverse inclusion is identical, and the composites are exactly the ordinary inclusion maps shifted by 2ε. This is the direct interleaving certificate before any diagram theorem is invoked.

## Worked example: setup

Use D={(0,3),(1,2)} and E={(1/10,16/5),(4/5,23/10)} with rational coordinates and the standard diagonal convention.

## Worked example: calculation

Pairing long bar to long bar costs max(1/10,1/5)=1/5. Pairing short bar to short bar costs max(1/5,3/10)=3/10, so the bottleneck is at most 3/10. The point (1,2) has no match of cost below 3/10: its closest listed point has that cost and its diagonal cost is 1/2. Therefore the bottleneck is exactly 3/10. Exhaustive finite matchings including diagonal removals certify this small case.

## Worked example: meaning

The stable statement concerns the declared filtration and distance. It controls the persistence invariant; it does not prove a geometric deformation is physically valid or recover all fine geometry from bars.

## Limits

Barcode decompositions require an appropriate finite or tame setting. The linked lab uses one finite filtration, not arbitrary point-cloud persistence. Expected Euler characteristic is an alternating aggregate and does not determine expected Betti numbers separately.

## Open questions

For a specified noisy filtration, derive a uniform perturbation bound and state the exact tameness assumptions needed to pass from interleaving to diagram distance.

For a multi-cycle random complex, calculate joint births and deaths rather than inferring a barcode law from expected Euler characteristic.
