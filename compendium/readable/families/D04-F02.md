## Central question

How can a reduction preserve homology, and what finite identities certify that nothing essential was lost?

## Synopsis

A chain complex records permitted boundaries and the relations among them. Its defining equation d²=0 is the first useful certificate: every boundary is a cycle, so a quotient of cycles by boundaries exists. The archive repeatedly approaches more elaborate constructions through this same requirement. Total complexes need Koszul signs; mapping cones need a genuine chain map; a blocked representation needs maps and a chain homotopy before it can inherit the original cohomology. These are constructive conditions. They provide explicit witnesses that a smaller calculation preserves the invariant instead of only producing similar output on an example.

The key distinction is between changing a presentation and changing an object. Adding a contractible pair increases the number of coordinates while preserving homology. Consequently total chain dimension cannot define an invariant of the derived object. Conversely a cochain map can annihilate a nonzero class, so the fact that an operation is linear or functorial does not prove that it preserves obstruction spaces. The finite Hodge decomposition gives a third view: with declared positive inner products, harmonic vectors represent homology, but that representative depends on the metric while the quotient space is invariant under chain equivalence. The supertrace index then becomes an exact cancellation statement, because all nonzero differential modes occur in paired even and odd degrees. No asymptotic heat approximation is required in the finite model.

## Definitions

A cochain complex has d:Cⁿ→Cⁿ⁺¹ with d²=0 and Hⁿ=ker dⁿ/im dⁿ⁻¹. A cochain map f satisfies d_D f=f d_C.

A contraction onto H consists of maps i:H→C, p:C→H and degree −1 h with pi=Id and Id−ip=dh+hd. This convention fixes the sign of h.

For commuting raw horizontal and vertical differentials on C^{p,q}, the total differential is δ+(−1)^p∂. Cone(f)ⁿ=Dⁿ⊕Cⁿ⁺¹ has differential (u,v)↦(d_Du+fv,−d_Cv).

## Theorem

A contraction makes i and p inverse on cohomology. Signed totalization squares to zero, and the stated cone differential squares to zero exactly when f is a cochain map. In particular Cone(Id) is contractible.

## Proof

For a cocycle c, the contraction identity gives c−ip(c)=d h(c), hence the classes of c and ip(c) agree. Since pi=Id, the induced maps are inverse. In a bicomplex the two mixed compositions have coefficients (−1)^p and (−1)^{p+1}; they cancel because the raw differentials commute. The remaining squares are zero. Applying the cone differential twice yields ((d_D f−f d_C)v,0), displaying the precise defect. When f=Id, define h(u,v)=(0,u); then dh(u,v)+hd(u,v)=(u,v). This is an explicit contracting homotopy. In finite inner-product complexes, orthogonality gives Cⁿ=im dⁿ⁻¹⊕ker Δ_n⊕im(dⁿ)*, because the orthogonal complement of the two images is exactly the simultaneous kernel of d and d*. Every cohomology class has one harmonic representative, although changing the metric changes that vector.

## Worked example: setup

Tensor two complexes Q --Id→ Q concentrated in degrees zero and one, using degree-one coordinates ordered horizontal, vertical.

## Worked example: calculation

The total dimensions are (1,2,1). The exact matrices are d⁰=(1,1)ᵀ and d¹=(−1,1). Their product is zero, both maps have rank one, and the Betti vector is (0,0,0). Replacing the second matrix with the unsigned (1,1) produces d¹d⁰=2, so there is no complex over Q. Separately C⁻¹=Qe, C⁰=Qu⊕Qv with de=u contracts onto Qv: set p(u)=0, p(v)=v, i(v)=v, h(u)=e, h(v)=0. Both sides of Id−ip=dh+hd send u to u and v to zero. If de=(1+ε)u, the same certificate survives with h(u)=e/(1+ε) exactly when ε≠−1.

## Worked example: meaning

The failure at ε=−1 is a real change of homology: the formerly contractible pair becomes two surviving classes. This makes perturbation a testable algebraic question and identifies the parameter where the reduction stops being valid.

## Limits

The finite Hodge statement assumes positive definite inner products. Rational ranks do not retain integer torsion. A general perturbation lemma needs convergence or nilpotence conditions; the one-pair rational rescaling above proves only its own elementary model. Source conventions using ip−Id change the homotopy sign.
