## Central question

What must be measured to certify the decision, rather than reconstruct every uncertain score?

## Synopsis

The final decision strand ties the archive together especially well. Earlier finite thresholds, singular covariance optimization, normalized residual margins and later target-observation questions lead to a precise endpoint: compare the competing actions under the same compatible state. Independent score intervals can erase cancellation and make an easy decision look unresolved. A decision difference may depend on only a small part of a high-dimensional uncertainty set. The best experiment can therefore be one that resolves the sign of that difference, even if it leaves most of the system unknown. This is a mathematical design principle with deterministic quantifiers, distinct from a posterior preference or an empirical forecast.

## Definitions

Let X be a nonempty uncertainty set and J_A,J_B:X→R be scores, with larger preferred; δ(x)=J_A(x)−J_B(x).

A strict robust certificate is a proved m>0 with δ(x)≥m for every x∈X; the infimum need not be attained.

A bounded-error measurement h(x)≈z restricts compatibility to X_z={x∈X:|h(x)−z|≤ε}.

A decision-sufficient finite test makes each nonempty outcome fibre certify one action. Its objective may be worst-case cost rather than expected estimation error.

## Theorem

Joint comparison satisfies inf_X(J_A−J_B)≥inf_XJ_A−sup_XJ_B, often strictly. Restricting to a measurement fibre cannot decrease the lower margin for A. In the shared model J_A=c+d, J_B=c, c∈[0,100], d∈[7/4,9/4], the exact robust A-margin is 7/4 although subtraction of separate score intervals yields lower bound −393/4.

## Proof

For every x, J_A(x)≥inf J_A and J_B(x)≤sup J_B, so subtraction and then infimization prove the inequality. Separate extrema may occur at incompatible x, explaining strictness. If X_z⊆X, every lower bound valid on X remains valid on X_z. In the shared example δ=d because the common c cancels before minimization. The independent ranges are J_A∈[7/4,409/4] and J_B∈[0,100], whose separate lower difference is 7/4−100=−393/4. The distinction is about preserving the same state in both scores. In a finite test tree, a recurrence V(S)=min_t[c_t+max_oV(S∩t^{-1}(o))], with V=0 on decision-certified states, computes the cheapest guaranteed procedure when the admissible tests and stopping certificates are fixed and a terminating strategy exists.

The L08 worked model uses a common nuisance score and an interval for the decision difference. A second fixture measures d with absolute error at most 1/10.

## Worked example: calculation

For c∈[0,100] and d∈[7/4,9/4], every compatible state prefers A with margin at least 1.75, regardless of nuisance width. Changing c's range changes the separate-score bound but leaves the joint margin unchanged. A measurement z=1/4 implies d≥z−1/10=3/20>0, certifying A. A result z=1/20 yields d∈[−1/20,3/20], which certifies neither direction. With affine δ=aᵀx+b on a box, its exact lower bound is b+Σ_j min(a_jl_j,a_ju_j); shared-variable simplification should precede this bound.

## Worked example: meaning

This creates a bridge from information to action: the required observation need distinguish states only when they demand different actions. Reconstructing nuisance coordinates can be unnecessary. Conversely a confident score estimate without a defensible compatibility set cannot become a robust theorem. L08 exposes the common-variable cancellation directly; arbitrary semialgebraic positivity and automatic measurement-tree synthesis remain specified extensions. L08 centers the nuisance interval as [−C,C]; C=50 gives the same width and independent lower bound as [0,100], with both scores translated by −50. It does not implement the bounded-error measurement or test-tree extensions.

## Limits

An uncertainty set inferred from data needs its own coverage justification. A posterior credible set and a uniform deterministic error bound are different inputs. Infinite utilities require a controlled limiting model; even a common limiting prior may produce different preferences if utility scales simultaneously. A strict sign certificate differs from a numerical optimizer returning a small positive value. These examples concern abstract scores and carry no empirical or financial-performance claim.

## Open questions

Produce an exact positivity certificate for a supplied rational or polynomial δ on a compact semialgebraic set.

Given a finite family of bounded-error tests with costs, solve and certify the cheapest terminating decision tree.
