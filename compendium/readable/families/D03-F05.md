## Central question

How do relations determine a finite algebra, and which properties remain constant when those relations vary?

## Synopsis

The local and quiver examples supply a concrete algebraic laboratory for the atlas. A finite Hilbert function is not determined by a few initial coefficients; it comes from the whole ideal. A constant vector-space dimension is not determined by a reported cochain profile; it comes from a basis or a locally free presentation. A symmetric Hilbert function is not enough to prove Gorenstein duality; the socle and multiplication pairing must be inspected. These distinctions turn several ambitious geometric discussions into tractable structural calculations.

The first model begins with two quadrics. An invertible change of coordinates converts them into u² and uv, revealing an infinite tail of powers of v. Adding cubic closure kills v³ and produces the intended four-dimensional algebra. Its basis gives the Hilbert function, radical powers and socle directly. The second model starts with a finite quiver and a square-zero loop. Path enumeration gives fourteen independent paths, and one projective relation removes exactly one dimension because its source and target prevent further left or right multiplication. The resulting rank thirteen is therefore uniform for a structural reason, not a sampling pattern.

These models also connect finite algebras to representation categories. Dual numbers and their matrix rings have different raw dimensions but equivalent module categories; radical layers and Ext groups retain structure that a state count misses. Conversely, a flat family can change Hochschild cohomology even while its basis size stays fixed. The family is organised so that basis certificates come first, module invariants second and deformation claims third. This ordering makes it possible to build interactive normal-form explorers without advertising an unimplemented general Gröbner, quiver-deformation or moduli solver.

## Definitions

For a graded algebra A, H_A(d)=dim_k A_d is its Hilbert function.

For a local Artinian algebra with maximal ideal m, soc(A)={a:ma=0}; a one-dimensional socle is required for the usual commutative Artinian Gorenstein condition.

A quiver path algebra has vertices as orthogonal idempotents and directed paths as a basis before imposing relations.

A finite algebra family is flat when its underlying module is locally free in the finite presentations used here; constant fibre dimension alone is not a general flatness proof.

## Theorem

Over any field, k[u,v]/(u²,uv,v³) has basis 1,u,v,v², Hilbert function (1,2,1), maximal ideal cube zero and socle span{u,v²}. The two quadratic relations alone give Hilbert function (1,2,1,1,…).

## Proof

The monomial ideal (u²,uv) kills exactly monomials divisible by u² or uv. Its surviving monomials are 1,u,v,v²,v³,…, so it has one surviving monomial in every degree at least two. Adding v³ kills all powers of v of degree at least three and leaves precisely 1,u,v,v². Their independence follows because a monomial ideal contains a polynomial exactly when each of its monomials belongs to the ideal. The maximal ideal is span{u,v,v²}; its square is span{v²} and its cube is zero. For a=a₀+a₁u+a₂v+a₃v², multiplication by u gives a₀u and multiplication by v gives a₀v+a₂v². Both vanish precisely when a₀=a₂=0. Hence the socle is span{u,v²}, of dimension two, and the algebra is not Gorenstein despite its symmetric Hilbert function. The coordinate change u=a−λb,v=b is invertible for every λ and turns (a²−λ²b²,ab−λb²) into (u²,uv), including in characteristic two.

## Worked example: setup

Compare the jet algebra with the projective quiver pencil having arrows α:0→1, γ:1→1, β,β′:1→2 and relations γ²=0, uβγα−vβα=0.

## Worked example: calculation

Before the projective relation, count three vertex paths, four arrows, five length-two paths and two length-three paths, for fourteen total. The two paths βγα and βα both run from source 0 to sink 2. Thus the nonzero relation line generates exactly a one-dimensional two-sided ideal for every [u:v]∈P¹, and every fibre has dimension thirteen. Globally this is a nowhere-zero O(−1) subbundle in a trivial rank-two path summand; its quotient is O(1). The full path bundle is therefore O¹²⊕O(1), a locally free rank-thirteen module. The local chart bases change, but flatness has an explicit bundle certificate.

## Worked example: meaning

Basis size, socle type and flatness are separately proved invariants. None determines the full Hochschild profile or a claimed high-order Kuranishi calculation.

## Limits
