## Central question

What finite operator relations can hold exactly, and how are their commutation and central phases certified?

## Synopsis

Finite quantum algebra has a precise boundary. The canonical relation [Q,P]=iℏI cannot hold for finite matrices when ℏ≠0, because the trace of a commutator is zero. Clock and shift operators provide a consistent finite replacement, with multiplicative relation UV=qVU. Taking determinants forces q to be a root of unity, so a rational phase is an exact existence condition, whereas an irrational phase cannot have an exact finite invertible representation. Rational approximation changes the algebraic parameter; it does not remove this obstruction.

The finite models connect naturally to symplectic geometry. Pauli operators modulo scalar phase are labelled by binary vectors, and their commutation sign is an alternating bilinear form. Centralizers of a specified nonzero support become exact symplectic kernels. Sector transition counts are images of another pairing map and therefore require its actual kernel, not a superficially similar quotient. These finite linear maps are a constructive common core of symmetry reduction, stabilizer codes and operator selection rules.

Fourier and Gauss operators add projective phases. A quadratic refinement produces a symmetric polar form, while Pauli commutation uses an alternating form. At odd order these cannot be identified indiscriminately. The finite Weil relation (ST)³=γC records a Gauss phase γ that must be computed rather than normalized away without explanation. For the order-three example γ=i. This family therefore keeps three distinct certificates together: trace and determinant obstructions; exact finite operator bases; and central phases in group relations. It does not promote one finite matrix pair into categorical coherence or an infinite irrational rotation algebra.

## Definitions

For q of order L, Ue_j=q^je_j and Ve_j=e_{j+1 mod L} form a clock–shift pair.

For n qubits, Pauli labels v=(x,z)∈F₂^{2n} have symplectic form ω(v,w)=x·z′+z·x′.

For nonzero Pauli support S of H, its Pauli centralizer labels are span(S)^⊥.

A finite quadratic form Q has polar form b_Q(x,y)=Q(x+y)−Q(x)−Q(y); the associated Gauss sum is γ=|A|⁻¹/²Σ_x exp(2πiQ(x)).

## Theorem

Finite invertible matrices U,V satisfying UV=qVU in dimension d force q^d=1. If q has order L, the displayed L-dimensional clock–shift pair exists and generates M_L(C). For H=Σ_{s∈S}h_sP(s), with distinct labels and nonzero h_s, P(v) commutes with H exactly when ω(v,s)=0 for every s∈S.

## Proof

Taking determinants in UV=qVU yields detU detV=q^d detV detU, and invertibility permits cancellation. Conversely the stated pair satisfies UV e_j=q^{j+1}e_{j+1}=qVUe_j, including wraparound. U has distinct eigenvalues; Lagrange interpolation in U gives every coordinate projection E_jj. Multiplying by powers of V then gives all matrix units, proving full generation and scalar commutant. For Pauli operators, XZ=−ZX gives P(v)P(s)=(−1)^ω(v,s)P(s)P(v). Vanishing pairings imply commutation term by term. Conversely, in the commutator the anticommuting terms are nonzero multiples of distinct Pauli basis elements labelled v+s. They cannot cancel, so every pairing must vanish. Nondegeneracy of ω then gives kernel dimension 2n−rank_F₂(S). This counts Pauli labels, not the full vector-space dimension of the operator commutant.

## Worked example: setup

Take n=2 and H=Z₁+Z₂. Compare the finite pair at order three and the order-three Gauss phase.

## Worked example: calculation

For H, the two support equations force x₁=x₂=0 while z₁,z₂ remain free. The four Pauli symmetries are I,Z₁,Z₂,Z₁Z₂ modulo phase. The full operator commutant is larger because the zero eigenvalue is degenerate, so the binary dimension two must not be called its complex matrix dimension. For a primitive cube root ζ, the nine products U^aV^b are trace-orthogonal: a nontrivial shift has zero trace and 1+ζ+ζ²=0. Thus they form a basis of M₃(C). For Q(x)=x²/3 on Z/3, γ=(1+2ζ)/√3=i, and the finite Fourier/Gauss matrices obey S²=C, (ST)³=iC. The phase is part of the exact relation.

## Worked example: meaning

The finite system has a complete algebraic identity and an explicit symplectic label geometry. Projective phases and representation dimensions remain separate invariants.
