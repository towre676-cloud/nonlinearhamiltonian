## Central question

Which norm or storage function certifies decay when eigenvalues, Euclidean growth and switching tell different stories?

## Synopsis

The stability strand is unusually consistent across the years: feedback can be asymptotically stable and still amplify an initial perturbation, a spectral gap depends on the modes actually observed, and changing metrics can reveal coercivity absent in the Euclidean symmetric part. A positive quadratic Lyapunov function supplies a constructive certificate. Its matrix also quantifies the cost of a coordinate change, forcing, switching and resets. These are capabilities with distinct inputs: an eigenvalue calculation, a transient-gain calculation and a hybrid dwell-time bound are related but answer different questions. The two-dimensional triangular model below is deliberately small enough to solve exactly. It proves decay in one metric while displaying temporary growth in another, giving a positive explanation for the archive’s repeated cautions about nonnormality.

## Definitions

For P=Pᵀ>0, define V(x)=xᵀPx and ||x||_P=√V(x). A Hurwitz matrix has all eigenvalues with negative real part.

A quadratic Lyapunov certificate is AᵀP+PA≤−2αP for α>0. It implies ||e^(tA)x||_P≤e^(−αt)||x||_P.

For hybrid switching, a metric jump ratio ρ≥1 bounds V_after≤ρV_before; an average dwell-time bound controls how often this cost can be incurred.

## Theorem

For A=[[-1,3],[0,−1]] and P=[[1/2,3/4],[3/4,11/4]], P>0 and AᵀP+PA=−I. Since P≤3I, ||e^(tA)x||_P≤e^(−t/6)||x||_P. Nevertheless the Euclidean squared norm has positive derivative at x=(1,1).

## Proof

The leading principal entry of P is 1/2 and det P=11/8−9/16=13/16>0, so the two-by-two Sylvester criterion proves positive definiteness. Direct multiplication gives AᵀP+PA=−I. Along x′=Ax, V′=xᵀ(AᵀP+PA)x=−||x||₂². The matrix 3I−P has positive first entry 5/2 and determinant (5/2)(1/4)−9/16=1/16>0, so P≤3I. Therefore ||x||₂²≥V/3 and V′≤−V/3. Integration and square roots give the rate 1/6 in the P norm. In contrast, at x=(1,1), Ax=(2,−1), so d||x||₂²/dt=2xᵀAx=2>0. The eigenvalues of A are both −1; their location does not prohibit this initial Euclidean amplification. Because A=−I+3N with N²=0, its exact exponential is e^(−t)(I+3tN), explicitly displaying the polynomial transient. If continuous segments obey V′≤−2αV and jumps cost at most ρ, then V(t)≤ρ^N(t)e^(−2αt)V(0). Under N(t)≤N₀+t/τ, this becomes ρ^N₀ exp[−2(α−logρ/(2τ))t]V(0). Thus switching consumes a precise logarithmic decay budget rather than preserving stability merely because each mode is stable.

## Worked example: setup

Use x₀=(0,1) for the continuous model, and compare two rank-one orthogonal projections whose lines meet at angle θ.

## Worked example: calculation

The exact trajectory is x(t)=e^(−t)(3t,1). At t=1 its Euclidean norm is √10/e>1 although it eventually tends to zero. For projections P₀ onto (1,0) and Q onto (cosθ,sinθ), P₀QP₀=cos²θ P₀. Thus a cycle restricted to im P₀ contracts by cos²θ, while ||[P₀,Q]||=|sinθ cosθ|. These are different quantities, even though both depend on the same angle.

## Limits

The exact matrix certificate is global for its stated linear model. A Jacobian certificate for a nonlinear system is usually local unless its inequality holds throughout a forward-invariant region. Common storage, reset inequalities and switching bounds are additional hybrid hypotheses.
