## Central question

How can local transport and global loop effects be distinguished and checked without confusing flatness with triviality?

## Synopsis

A connection supplies a rule for differentiating and transporting sections. Curvature measures its local failure to commute in two independent directions; holonomy measures transport around a loop. They interact, but neither is interchangeable with the other or with the topology of the underlying space. A one-dimensional circle supports no curvature two-form and can still have nontrivial monodromy. On a simply connected surface, a nonflat connection can have loop holonomy arising from curvature even when the bundle is topologically trivial. A nontrivial bundle can additionally obstruct a global gauge potential. These distinctions give the archive’s connection and Berry material a common, precise architecture.

Gauge transformations change coordinates in the fibers. They conjugate loop holonomy at its base point, leaving conjugacy-invariant information such as trace, determinant and eigenvalues unchanged. The ordered product matters for matrices; scalar circulation cannot stand in for a nonabelian calculation. The source’s nilpotent shear example is especially effective because its exponentials terminate and its loop product is an exact polynomial. Berry transport adds a geometric realization: eigenlines form a bundle over a parameter space, and local eigenvector choices are gauge choices. A Chern number requires that declared bundle and a normalized curvature integral; it cannot be recovered from a phase function on an unidentified state space. For the spin-half sphere example the integral is one, proving that no globally smooth potential can represent that curvature on the whole sphere.

## Definitions

For ∇=d+A on a vector bundle, curvature is F=dA+A∧A and ∇² acts by F. Parallel sections satisfy Fv=0, a necessary condition rather than a general converse.

For edge transport U_j:V_j→V_{j+1}, basis changes h_j act by U_j↦h_{j+1}U_jh_j⁻¹. Ordered loop holonomy is the composite returning to its base fiber.

Flat means F=0. Trivial holonomy means every relevant loop acts as identity. Topological triviality means the underlying bundle has a global trivialization; these are different properties.

## Theorem

A loop holonomy transforms by conjugation at the base vertex. For X=[[0,1],[0,0]] and Y=[[0,0],[1,0]], the loop (I+xX)(I+yY)(I−xX)(I−yY) equals [[1+xy+x²y²,−x²y],[xy²,1−xy]]. Its mixed quadratic coefficient is [X,Y]=diag(1,−1).

## Proof

Compose transformed edge maps in path order. Each interior h_j⁻¹ cancels its neighboring h_j, leaving only h₀Hh₀⁻¹; this proves gauge covariance and explains why entries themselves are not invariant. Since X²=Y²=0, exp(xX)=I+xX and exp(yY)=I+yY exactly. Multiplying the four factors gives the displayed polynomial, whose determinant is one and trace is 2+x²y². Setting x=y=ε yields I+ε²[X,Y] plus explicit cubic and quartic terms. This is the exact algebraic mechanism behind a small plaquette limit; a smooth curvature interpretation additionally needs specified base directions, derivative terms and a transport sign convention. On a circle A=iα dθ is flat by dimension, but with parallel transport defined by s′+iαs=0 its holonomy is exp(−2πiα), nontrivial for noninteger α. Thus flatness alone does not force global trivial monodromy.

## Worked example: setup

Take x=y=1 in the shear loop and a base gauge h=diag(2,1). Compare it with a rank-one flat circle connection.

## Worked example: calculation

The shear holonomy is H=[[3,−1],[1,0]], with determinant one and trace three. Conjugation gives hHh⁻¹=[[3,−2],[1/2,0]], retaining both invariants. For α=1/2 the flat circle holonomy is −1. As a separate curved example on R², A=ix dy has F=i dx∧dy. Parallel transport around a positively oriented unit square is exp(−i) in the stated convention by the scalar Stokes theorem, despite the bundle and base being topologically trivial.

## Worked example: meaning

These examples give three independent checks: fiber gauge covariance, local curvature and global monodromy. A numerical loop becomes geometric evidence only after these inputs and conventions are declared.
