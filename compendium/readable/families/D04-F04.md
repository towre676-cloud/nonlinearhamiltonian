## Central question

How much of a total object can be recovered from graded pages, truncations and inverse systems?

## Synopsis

A filtration is an organized sequence of partial views. Passing to the associated graded records the layers but suppresses how they interact. A spectral sequence progressively restores some of that interaction through differentials. Even after every differential is determined, reconstructing an integral group or ring can require extension and multiplicative data. The source sequence develops this distinction through fibrations, filtered complexes, cohomological shifts and inverse limits. Their common question is not whether the displayed table looks plausible, but which connecting maps remain unknown.

The Hopf fibration and the product S²×S¹ have the same initial Serre page but different transgressions. The finite model below makes the same phenomenon an exact two-coordinate computation. It also exposes a second loss: Z/4 and Z/2⊕Z/2 have the same two graded layers, although only the former has an element of order four. A filtration therefore supports a partial reconstruction interface, with each unknown differential and extension listed as an unresolved input. Perverse shifts and t-structures add a related bookkeeping lesson. The declaration of a heart or a support/cosupport condition changes which cohomological degrees count as an object; it is not recoverable from an unshifted label. Finally an infinite inverse system has genuinely limiting information. The doubling tower of integers has vanishing ordinary inverse limit but a nonzero derived limit; no finite truncation alone certifies that infinite result. These examples justify a hierarchy of outputs: graded dimensions, actual graded groups, filtered groups, rings, and geometric objects.

## Definitions

Use an increasing filtration F_pC preserved by the differential. A component lowering filtration by r may first appear as the corresponding page differential after earlier components vanish.

An associated graded group is gr_F A=⊕F_pA/F_{p−1}A. It does not include a chosen splitting of the filtration.

For a countable inverse system A₀←A₁←⋯, define Δ:∏A_n→∏A_n by Δ(a)_n=a_n−f_n(a_{n+1}); then lim A=kerΔ and lim¹A=cokerΔ in this setting.

## Theorem

There exist filtered complexes with identical associated graded differential but different total cohomology. There exist nonisomorphic integral groups with the same associated graded. For the doubling tower Z ←×2 Z ←×2 ⋯, lim=0 and lim¹≅Z₂/Z.

## Proof

Let C⁰=Qv and C¹=Qw with v in filtration one and w in filtration zero. Both d=0 and dv=w induce zero on each same-filtration quotient, so their initial graded complexes agree. For dv=w the next filtration-dropping differential is an isomorphism and the total is acyclic; for d=0 both classes survive. For the extension example filter Z/4 by 0⊂2Z/4⊂Z/4 and filter (Z/2)² by one coordinate; each graded layer is Z/2, but their exponents differ. For the inverse limit, a compatible sequence satisfies a₀=2ⁿa_n for all n, hence a₀=0 and all a_n=0. Define S(b)=Σ2ⁿb_n in Z₂, convergent in the 2-adic topology. If b=Δ(a), partial sums telescope to a₀−2^{N+1}a_{N+1}, so S(b)=a₀∈Z. Conversely if S(b)=m∈Z, set a_n=(m−Σ_{j<n}2ʲb_j)/2ⁿ. The numerator is divisible by 2ⁿ, so a_n is an integer and a_n−2a_{n+1}=b_n. Binary digits make S onto Z₂. Thus S induces exactly cokerΔ≅Z₂/Z.

## Worked example: calculation

For dv=w the ranks are one and the Betti vector is (0,0); for d=0 they are zero and the Betti vector is (1,1). Yet each associated graded has one generator in its listed bidegree and zero initial differential. In the group comparison, enumerate element orders: Z/4 has orders (1,2,4,4), whereas (Z/2)² has (1,2,2,2). The order-four test detects an extension invisible to the common graded ranks.

## Worked example: meaning

A page is useful evidence with a declared reconstruction stage. It becomes a complete answer only after the required differentials, convergence, extensions and products have been supplied.

## Limits

The proof of lim¹ is an infinite algebraic argument, while the executable tests check only finite supporting identities. A finite experiment cannot verify convergence of an arbitrary tower. Multiplicative structures require their own signs, coefficient ring and extension analysis.
