## Central question

Which global defects survive changes of local choices, and what makes their values topological?

## Synopsis

A cocycle expresses consistency of repeated composition; a coboundary expresses a defect removable by changing local choices. Periods and characteristic classes provide ways to detect what remains. The archive contains several versions: winding of a nonvanishing loop, projective phases of group actions, transition functions of bundles and characteristic-form integrals. They share an invariance mechanism, but their coefficients, degrees and products differ. A useful synthesis keeps the actual equation visible rather than treating every nonzero loop quantity as the same obstruction.

Winding is protected by avoidance of the origin: a continuous nonvanishing loop has an integer degree, and a homotopy through nonvanishing loops preserves it. A curl flux is instead a boundary integral depending continuously on the field; equality for two surfaces with the same boundary is a Stokes statement, not automatic quantization. Group projective phases satisfy a two-cocycle equation because multiplication is associative. Rephasing the operators changes the cocycle by a coboundary, while the commutator phase for a commuting pair is unchanged. Bundle transition functions obey their own triple-overlap identity, and characteristic forms connect curvature to integral topology only for a declared bundle and normalization. The source corpus’s four-torus example makes the latter quantitative: if c₁=n dx₁∧dx₂+m dx₃∧dx₄ on the unit torus, then ∫c₁²=2nm, with the factor two arising from degree-two commutativity. A topological density can have a variation that is entirely a boundary term, so it need not furnish a bulk field equation.

## Definitions

For a nonvanishing piecewise smooth complex loop γ, winding is (2πi)⁻¹∮γ⁻¹dγ. The integral depends on its homotopy class in the punctured complex plane.

For an abelian group written additively, a normalized multiplier σ satisfies σ(g,h)σ(g+h,k)=σ(h,k)σ(g,h+k). Rephasing by b(g) multiplies σ by b(g)b(h)/b(g+h).

A closed differential form is exact only if it has a global primitive; nonzero integral over a closed cycle certifies nonexactness.

## Theorem

For G=F₂² and σ((a,b),(c,d))=(−1)^{bc}, σ is a two-cocycle which is not a coboundary. The angular form (−y dx+x dy)/(x²+y²) on the punctured plane is closed but not exact.

## Proof

For the cocycle, write k=(e,f). The left exponent is bc+(b+d)e, while the right exponent is de+b(c+e); they agree modulo two. Hence the multiplication is associative. If σ were an abelian coboundary, it would be symmetric in g,h because b(g)b(h)/b(g+h) is symmetric. But for g=(1,0), h=(0,1), σ(g,h)=1 and σ(h,g)=−1, a contradiction. The alternating commutator ratio therefore detects the nontrivial projective class and survives rephasing. For the angular form, differentiation gives zero on the punctured plane. Restrict to the unit circle x=cos t,y=sin t: the form becomes dt and its integral is 2π. Every globally exact form has zero integral on a closed loop by the fundamental theorem, proving nonexactness. This is the same local-to-global pattern as gluing, with a de Rham coefficient model rather than finite incidence coordinates.

## Worked example: setup

Enumerate the four elements of F₂² and the sixty-four triples, then evaluate the multiplier and its commutator.

## Worked example: calculation

Every triple passes the cocycle identity. The chosen coordinate generators anticommute projectively: their multiplier ratio is −1. In the geometric model γ(t)=e^{ikt} with integer k, γ⁻¹γ′=ik, so the winding is k. Under a homotopy that crosses zero, γ⁻¹ is undefined at the crossing and the certificate ceases to apply. For the unit four-torus with n=2,m=3, the normalized characteristic integral is 2·2·3=12, independently of the chosen connection representing that fixed line-bundle class.

## Worked example: meaning

The invariant is the class with its hypotheses and normalization. Local changes of gauge alter representatives, while crossing a singularity, changing coefficients or changing the underlying bundle can alter the class itself.

## Limits

The finite multiplier is an explicit example, not an identification of every historical phase model. Characteristic forms require declared degree, orientation, trace and 2π conventions. A finite cover multiplies a nonzero integer circle class by its degree; it does not erase it.

## Open questions

For the proposed transition data, compute the actual cocycle and determine whether a gauge change can trivialize it.

For any claimed integer characteristic value, exhibit the bundle, orientation, integral cohomology class and normalized representative.
