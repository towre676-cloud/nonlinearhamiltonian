## Central question

Which information survives a realization, and what additional structure is required before two cohomology theories can be compared?

## Synopsis

Cohomology provides several forms of organized information: dimensions, graded pieces, mixed structures, integral torsion, Frobenius action and functorial maps. A numerical realization may preserve addition and multiplication while forgetting almost everything else. This is why an Euler characteristic can be zero for a nontrivial elliptic curve, and why multiplying it by Shannon entropy cannot automatically become a positive complexity index. The same caution governs comparison theorems: a characteristic-p variety has no complex analytic space until appropriate characteristic-zero data have been supplied, and a motivic chain index is not a singular cohomology degree. A finite monodromy model makes the loss of information especially concrete. Rational ranks can remain unchanged while integer coinvariants gain torsion; the nilpotent operator determines a weight filtration but not an entire limiting mixed Hodge structure.

## Definitions

The Grothendieck ring of complex varieties imposes [X]=[Z]+[X minus Z] for closed Z and multiplication by Cartesian product. A motivic realization is a declared ring homomorphism out of that ring.

Compact-support Euler characteristic is χ_c(X)=Σ_i(−1)^i dim H_c^i(X); it is additive but need not be positive or separating.

A monodromy weight filtration centered at one obeys N(W_k)⊆W_(k−2) and N^j:Gr_(1+j)^W→Gr_(1−j)^W isomorphically. A Hodge filtration and polarization are extra data.

## Theorem

For V=Qe₁⊕Qe₂ and N(e₂)=e₁,N(e₁)=0, the centered weight filtration is W_k=0 for k<0, W₀=W₁=Qe₁ and W_k=V for k≥2. For integral monodromy M_n=I+nN, n>0, the coinvariants are Z⊕Z/nZ, although all these rational models have the same ranks.

## Proof

The image and kernel of N are both Qe₁. The proposed filtration lowers by two under N, and its only nonzero graded pieces are Gr₀ and Gr₂, both one-dimensional. N maps the class of e₂ to the class of e₁, an isomorphism, while all higher required isomorphisms concern zero spaces. Thus the defining conditions are satisfied. On the integer lattice, (M_n−I)Z²=nZe₁, so the quotient is generated freely by e₂ and by e₁ modulo n. Tensoring with Q removes the latter torsion. For realization loss, the scissor relation A¹={0}⊔G_m gives [G_m]=L−1. Compact-support cohomology of G_m has one copy of the coefficient field in degrees one and two, so its Euler characteristic is zero despite nonzero cohomology. A projective genus-g curve has Betti numbers (1,2g,1) and Euler characteristic 2−2g. Therefore χ_c(X)H(p) can vanish or be negative for a positive entropy H(p). Additivity alone gives no positivity or faithful geometric encoding.

## Worked example: setup

Test comparison types on P¹ and exhibit a failure of an unrestricted motivic–Betti isomorphism.

## Worked example: calculation

P¹ over F₅ has six points and its standard smooth lift has cohomological ranks (1,0,1). Frobenius acts by 1 in degree zero and 5 on the hyperplane class in degree two, giving trace count 1+5=6; over F₂₅ it gives 1+25=26. This is a useful consistency test, not a proof of the general comparison theorem. At Spec C, the weight-one motivic group H^{1,1}=C× is nonzero, whereas singular H¹ of a point vanishes. Even after tensoring with C the source is nonzero, since log|·| maps C× onto R and R⊗_Z C maps nontrivially to C. The universal isomorphism claim therefore fails at a point.

## Worked example: meaning

Small examples discriminate a valid comparison with explicit coefficients and hypotheses from a false formula that merely passes a rank coincidence.

## Limits

The finite matrix model is not a general degeneration or mixed-Hodge solver. A limiting Hodge filtration, polarization, geometric origin and admissibility remain extra requirements. Étale–crystalline comparison uses stated lifting or period-ring hypotheses; numerical agreement of a cellular test object is insufficient.

## Open questions

Can a proposed comparison specify an actual functor and a correctly graded natural map before asking whether it is an isomorphism?

Which integral or filtered invariants must a proposed geometric complexity measure retain to distinguish objects with identical Euler characteristic?
