## Central question

Which operations, equations and equivalences actually determine an algebra, and which information survives quotienting?

## Synopsis

The algebraic backbone of the collection is the move from a named collection of symbols to a multiplication law whose identities can be proved. A field, a monoid, a ring and a partial resource algebra have different admissible operations. Their quotients therefore require different compatibility conditions. The useful revised question is not whether a construction looks algebraic, but whether its carrier, operations, units, domains of definition and notion of equality have all been specified. This viewpoint makes the number-system comparisons, transported operations, finite maximal ideals, capped trace monoids and squarefree branch decomposition parts of one coherent programme.

Transport of structure is the first constructive tool. Given a bijection, pulling all operations back creates an isomorphic object, so associativity and distributivity are inherited by proof. This gives a complete model of a coordinate deformation while explaining why it supplies no new intrinsic field invariant. Quotients then remove information in a controlled way: a ring congruence is determined by a two-sided ideal; a monoid quotient requires a multiplication-compatible equivalence; a partial algebra needs its composability relation retained. Approximate numerical closeness cannot silently serve as any of these equalities.

Finite models make the distinction executable. Enumerating ideals of a finite ring can produce maximal ideals and field quotients. Capping an additive counter produces an absorbing top layer, but does not turn its semigroup kernel into a linear nullspace. The latter distinction matters throughout the atlas: a minimal two-sided ideal, a kernel of a homomorphism and a vector-space kernel answer different questions. Algebra becomes useful for interactive reconstruction precisely when the requested invariant is named before its computation.

## Definitions

For a unital ring R, a quotient R/I is defined by a two-sided ideal I; multiplication of classes is [a][b]=[ab].

For a finite monoid S, K(S) denotes its unique minimal nonempty two-sided ideal. It is not a matrix nullspace.

For k≥1, L_k={0,…,k} has operation a⊕b=min(a+b,k); k is absorbing.

For a bijection φ:X→Y, transported multiplication is x⋆y=φ⁻¹(φ(x)φ(y)), with all other required operations transported likewise.

## Theorem

For a finite monoid S and positive integers k_1,…,k_r, K(S×∏L_{k_i})=K(S)×{(k_1,…,k_r)}. Thus adding saturated counters preserves the cardinality of the minimal ideal, although it enlarges the monoid.

## Proof

Let P=S×∏L_{k_i}. The displayed product J is a nonempty two-sided ideal: multiplication preserves K(S), while every capped coordinate remains at its maximum. Let I be any nonempty two-sided ideal of P and choose (s,a)∈I. Multiplication by (1,k_1,…,k_r) puts (s,k_1,…,k_r) in I. The set of x∈S for which (x,k_1,…,k_r)∈I is a nonempty ideal of S. It contains K(S), hence I contains J. Therefore J is the unique minimal ideal. For completeness, existence and uniqueness of K(S) in a finite monoid follow by choosing a minimal nonempty ideal and observing that the product of any two nonempty ideals is nonempty and lies in their intersection. Minimality then forces the two chosen minimal ideals to agree. This proof concerns multiplication ideals only; no vector-space structure is used.

## Worked example: setup

Take S=C₂={1,g}, g²=1, and one counter L₂. Also compare the finite commutative ring Z/12Z.

## Worked example: calculation

The six monoid elements are (1,0),(g,0),(1,1),(g,1),(1,2),(g,2). Their product is (g^a,i)(g^b,j)=(g^{a+b mod2},min(i+j,2)). Exactly (1,0) and (1,2) are idempotent. The minimal ideal is {(1,2),(g,2)}, a copy of C₂. Exhaustive multiplication proves associativity on all 216 triples and finds no smaller nonempty ideal. In Z/12Z the maximal ideals are (2)={0,2,4,6,8,10} and (3)={0,3,6,9}; their quotients have two and three elements. These outputs distinguish minimal monoid ideals from maximal ring ideals rather than relying on the shared word ideal.

## Worked example: meaning

A small exact multiplication table can certify an invariant of the declared model. It cannot identify an unnamed historical transition object with that model without a multiplication-preserving map.

## Limits

No unspecified M29 table is reconstructed. Transport is an isomorphism construction. Enumeration scales exponentially, and finite maximal-ideal search proves no algorithmic result for arbitrary infinite rings.
