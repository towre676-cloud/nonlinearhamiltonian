## Central question

What exact claim does each certificate establish, and which dependencies remain outside its proof boundary?

## Synopsis

The archive repeatedly reaches the same useful discipline from different directions: separate a search procedure from a small exact checker, and state the checker’s theorem with its quantifiers and input type. A feasible point proves existence; a primal–dual equality proves optimality; a rank minor plus factorization proves rank; a Bézout identity proves inconsistency; a complete Gröbner replay proves an ideal basis. These witnesses are not interchangeable. Nor does checking many examples establish a universal identity unless a degree bound or other completeness theorem reduces the universal statement to those examples. This family turns that discipline into a reusable proof interface. It begins with truth tables and bound variables, continues through arithmetic and floating-point envelopes, and reaches exact algebraic and finite-state certificates. The goal is constructive: make a solver’s useful discoveries portable across a trust boundary while exposing the precise assumptions on which the conclusion rests.

## Definitions

A certificate is data c for an explicitly specified predicate Check(input,c). Soundness proves Check⇒Claim; completeness, if needed, proves every true claim has an admissible certificate.

A witness establishes ∃x P(x); a complete census establishes both listed⇒valid and valid⇒listed. These are distinct quantifier directions.

A polynomial over F_q reduced to degree below q in every variable is a different object from an arbitrary formal polynomial or a polynomial function modulo x_i^q−x_i.

A proof dependency record states the encoded instance, arithmetic domain, theorem hypotheses, checker implementation and any external mathematical theorem used.

## Theorem

If f∈F_q[x_1,…,x_n] has degree <q in each variable and vanishes on all q^n points, then f is the zero formal polynomial. Without this degree condition exhaustive functional agreement need not prove formal polynomial equality. Separately, exact primal and dual feasible points with equal objectives certify an LP optimum by weak duality.

## Proof

For the polynomial theorem, induct on n. A one-variable nonzero polynomial of degree <q cannot have q roots. Write f as a polynomial in the last variable. At every fixed earlier tuple all q evaluations vanish, so every coefficient evaluates to zero. Each coefficient retains the coordinate degree bounds, and induction makes all coefficient polynomials zero. Without the bound, x_1^q−x_1 vanishes everywhere but has nonzero coefficient dictionary. For LP minimization Ax=b,x≥0 and dual maximization bᵀy with Aᵀy≤c, every feasible pair satisfies bᵀy=yᵀAx≤cᵀx. Equality therefore sandwiches the optimum. The proof uses feasibility, not closeness of objective values. A floating candidate can guide search, but exact rational feasibility or a rigorous residual enclosure must justify the final theorem.

## Worked example: setup

Compare polynomial functions over F₃⁴ and certify min{x+2y:x+y=3,x,y≥0}.

## Worked example: calculation

There are 81 assignments. The identity (x_1+x_2)^3=x_1^3+x_2^3 is true formally in characteristic three by coefficient cancellation. The expression x_1^3−x_1 vanishes at all 81 assignments but is not the zero formal polynomial, so an evaluator alone confuses two types. For the linear program, the primal point (3,0) has value 3, and dual scalar λ=1 satisfies λ≤1 and λ≤2 with dual value 3. This exact pair proves the optimum. As a different certificate, 1=y·x−(xy−1) proves that x=0 and xy−1=0 have no common solution over a field; it does not certify a census of a different polynomial system.

## Worked example: meaning

Certificate types make the conclusion and its omitted obligations inspectable, enabling exact replay of useful solver output without trusting the solver’s search strategy.

## Limits

Formal proof claims require an actual proof term or complete trusted replay, not a label such as Lean bridge. Two-prime agreement, fitted residuals and public recursion consistency each establish narrower statements. Hardness requires an input distribution, adversary model and reduction; constructing a lattice encoding alone does not produce an efficient decoder. Sequential work and cryptographic provenance are not inferred from an algebraic identity.
