## Central question

Which identities genuinely characterize modular arithmetic, rather than an arbitrary prime-indexed statistic?

## Synopsis

A modular-form assertion includes the group, weight, character, normalization and transformation law. Once those are fixed, dimensions, Hecke recurrences and Euler factors become exact algebraic constraints. The number 24 enters the logarithmic derivative of Dedekind eta through a specific product normalization; it is not interchangeable with a root count or an abstract dimension. Similarly, a genuine Frobenius trace is tied to a point count and determinant p. Its symmetric square has three eigenvalue products, not a guessed two-factor denominator. This family places q-expansion calculations beside local arithmetic while retaining the distinction between an Eisenstein series, a cusp eigenform and an elliptic-curve L-function. Modular symbols supply a boundary algebra that can later support periods, but their elementary relations alone do not execute a modular-symbol algorithm or establish modularity.

## Definitions

For the full modular group over C, the graded ring of holomorphic modular forms is C[E₄,E₆], with weights 4 and 6. Δ=(E₄³−E₆²)/1728 has weight 12.

For weight k, a normalized Hecke eigenform satisfies a_(p²)=a_p²−p^(k−1), with suitable unramified and normalization hypotheses.

For an elliptic local factor, α+β=a_p and αβ=p. The symmetric square eigenvalues are α²,p,β².

## Theorem

The weight-38 full-level modular-form space has basis E₄⁸E₆, E₄⁵E₆³, E₄²E₆⁵ and dimension three; its cusp subspace is ΔM₂₆ of dimension two. For elliptic eigenvalues α,β, the symmetric-square denominator is 1−(a_p²−p)t+p(a_p²−p)t²−p³t³.

## Proof

A monomial E₄^aE₆^b has weight 4a+6b. Solving 4a+6b=38 in nonnegative integers gives b=1,3,5 and a=8,5,2. Polynomial independence in C[E₄,E₆] proves that these monomials form a basis. Multiplication by the nonzero Δ identifies cusp forms of weight 38 with weight-26 forms, whose equation 4a+6b=26 has solutions (a,b)=(5,1),(2,3). The structural description of the modular-form ring and its cusp ideal are classical inputs, while the dimension calculation is elementary. For the symmetric square, α²+β²=(α+β)²−2αβ=a_p²−2p. Adding the middle eigenvalue p gives sum a_p²−p. The sum of pairwise products is p(α²+β²)+p²=p(a_p²−p), and the triple product is p³. Expanding the three linear factors gives the stated polynomial. The determinant p is essential: substituting an unrelated prime-power ratio destroys this derivation.

## Worked example: calculation

The normalized nonconstant coefficients of E₄ are σ₃(n). At p=2, σ₃(2)=9 and σ₃(4)=73=9²−2³. The constant coefficient of E₄ is also compatible with T₂E₄=9E₄ under the stated weight-four convention. For p=2 and a_p=−2, the symmetric-square denominator becomes 1−2t+4t²−8t³. For eta, its defining product gives q d(log η)/dq=1/24−Σ_(n≥1)σ₁(n)q^n=E₂/24. The translation phase of an eta quotient must still be tested; a q-series with appealing coefficients is not automatically invariant under τ↦τ+1.

## Worked example: meaning

Small exact coefficient tests make the hypotheses auditable, while the theorem of transformation or modularity remains a separate structural obligation.

## Limits

The sources do not support a full four-year continuous modular-form project. This family records the supported 2024–2025 treatments. Full-level ring formulas cannot be applied unchanged to arbitrary level or character. A finite q-expansion match requires a proved comparison bound before it identifies two forms.
