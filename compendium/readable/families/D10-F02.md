## Central question

How can random selection be transported into an exact expectation of a combinatorial or topological observable?

## Synopsis

Random filtrations connect probability with topology by making the event that a face is present explicit. The source trajectory runs from elementary denominators and collision probabilities to fractal measures, random birth times and weighted fixed-size samples. A random complex cannot be understood solely by calling it random: its vertex law, face rule, dependence and filtration parameter must be declared. Once those inputs are fixed, indicator expansion often turns a seemingly global expected invariant into a short exact sum. This family organizes three scales of the same idea: finite subset enumeration, order statistics of random birth times, and weak approximation of measures by coupled partial constructions.

## Definitions

For positive weights w_1,…,w_N and fixed size m, assign a subset S probability proportional to ∏_{i∈S}w_i. The normalizer e_m(w) is the degree-m elementary symmetric polynomial.

For a prescribed face F with b vertices, the inclusion indicator is 1_{F⊆S}. A simplicial complex is induced by the selected vertices only after the face rule is specified.

The Euler characteristic of a finite complex is χ=Σ_F(−1)^{dim F}; expectation commutes with this finite sum.

A filtration K_t is increasing in t. Independent uniform vertex birth times create correlated face births via the maximum over each face.

## Theorem

Under weighted fixed-size sampling, P(F⊆S)=w_F e_{m−b}(w_{F^c})/e_m(w), taking e_r=0 outside valid degrees. Consequently Eχ(K[S])=Σ_F(−1)^{dim F}P(F⊆S). In the equal-weight case this becomes binom(N−b,m−b)/binom(N,m).

## Proof

Every selected subset containing F is uniquely F∪T with T⊆F^c and |T|=m−b. Its unnormalized mass is w_F∏_{i∈T}w_i. Summing over T gives the numerator; summing over all size-m subsets gives the denominator. The Euler formula follows by inserting one inclusion indicator per face and using linearity, with no independence assumption between faces. The normalizers satisfy E_{j,r}=E_{j−1,r}+w_jE_{j−1,r−1}, because each size-r subset either excludes j or includes it. This dynamic program is an exact coefficient computation, not a Monte Carlo estimate. Equal weights reduce both symmetric polynomials to binomial coefficients and recover the hypergeometric law.

## Worked example: setup

Select two vertices from four with weights (1,2,3,4). Separately, let the four vertices of a cycle appear at independent uniform times, with edges appearing when both endpoints are present and the filling two-cell arriving at time one.

## Worked example: calculation

Here e_2=2+3+4+6+8+12=35. Vertex one is selected with probability (2+3+4)/35=9/35; the pair {1,2} is selected with probability 2/35. If K is the four-cycle, Eχ=Σ_iP(i∈S)−Σ_edgesP(edge⊆S)=2−(2+6+12+4)/35=46/35. In the birth-time model, the loop appears at B=max(U_1,…,U_4), hence P(B≤t)=t^4. Its barcode is [B,1), and E(1−B)=∫_0^1t^4dt=1/5.

## Worked example: meaning

The expected Euler characteristic needs only face inclusion probabilities, whereas expected Betti numbers usually require joint rank information. The barcode example is exactly solvable because the topology has one possible loop. The distinction prevents a facewise expectation formula from being mistaken for a general persistent-homology estimator.

## Limits

The fixed-cardinality law is different from independent Bernoulli selection or sequential probability-proportional-to-size sampling. Faces are strongly dependent. Expected χ can conceal simultaneous changes in several Betti numbers. Random birth-time claims need their continuity and independence assumptions; ties, delayed edges or an early filling cell change the barcode. Weighted expected persistence is not implemented by the atlas.

## Open questions

Derive exact expected Betti curves for a supplied small complex by grouping subsets according to boundary rank.

Compute weighted barcode distributions with rational weights and compare coefficient dynamic programming with exhaustive enumeration.
