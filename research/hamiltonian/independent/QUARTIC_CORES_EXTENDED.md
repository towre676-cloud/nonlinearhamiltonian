# Quartic cores extended: degree 24, the equianharmonic case, and inhomogeneous collapse depths

This package extends the wave-one quartic dynamic-core tables (the wave-one tables, in this directory as `quartic_dynamic_cores.*`) in three directions: the homogeneous tables are pushed from degree 16 to degree 24 (with the repository's third distinct-root representative added), the equianharmonic class $\lambda^2 = -12$ — whose core depth the repository left open beyond its degree-$\le 12$ per-degree certificate — is computed explicitly in both of its readings, and a first classification of inhomogeneous collapse depths for quadratic perturbations is computed, with the pattern it suggests stated precisely and tested on cases not used to formulate it. Everything is exact rational arithmetic (with one exact quadratic-field computation, below); the cloned repository was not edited; this package is submitted on the branch `muse/quartic-cores-extended`.

The definition is the repository's own, unchanged from wave one: $D_\infty(H) = \{G \in \mathbb{Q}[q,p] : C_k D^r G = 0 \text{ for all odd } k \ge 3 \text{ and all } r \ge 0\}$ with $D = \{H, \cdot\}$ and $C_k = T_k(H, \cdot)$ (`programmes/DYNAMIC_CORE_RESULTS.md`). For $\deg H = 4$ the only nonzero gate is $C_3 = T_3(H,\cdot)$. The refinement spaces are $S^R_n(H) = \{G \in V_n : T_3(H, D^r G) = 0,\ r \le R\}$ in the homogeneous setting, and $K_R^{\le N}(H) = \{G : \deg G \le N,\ T_3(H, D^r G) = 0,\ r \le R\}$ in the inhomogeneous setting, always with $D^r$ images kept as full unprojected polynomials. Since $D_\infty(H)$ is contained in every $S^R_n$ and every $K_R^{\le N}$, computed dimensions are rigorous upper bounds on core dimensions; where they meet an explicitly known subspace of the core, the corresponding piece of the core is determined exactly.

## 1. The homogeneous tables to degree 24

All seven homogeneous representatives now have full refinement tables for $n \le 24$, $R \le 8$ in `quartic_cores_ext.json`. The seven are the six of wave one — $q^4$, $q^3p$, $qp^3$, $q^2p^2$, $q^4+p^4$ ($\lambda=0$), $q^4+q^2p^2+p^4$ ($\lambda=1$) — plus $q^4-q^2p^2+p^4$ ($\lambda=-1$), the third distinct-root representative in the repository's §3c table, which wave one did not tabulate. In every case the truncated core dimensions in the new degrees $17$–$24$ continue the proved families exactly, and the stabilisation behaviour of wave one persists without exception: the overall stabilisation depth is $0$ for $q^4$ (its partner space is already the strip $\mathbb{Q}[q] \oplus p\mathbb{Q}[q] \oplus p^2\mathbb{Q}[q]$) and $1$ for each of the other six — a single bracket reduces the partner space to the core in every degree $\le 24$ where any reduction occurs. Concretely, the core dimensions in degrees $17$–$24$ are $3,3,3,3,3,3,3,3$ for $q^4$ (basis in degree 24: $q^{24}, q^{23}p, q^{22}p^2$); $2$ throughout for $q^3p$ and $qp^3$; alternating $0,1$ for $q^2p^2$ (basis in degree 24: $q^{12}p^{12}$); and $0$ throughout for the three distinct-root cases, whose only core pieces in degrees $\le 24$ remain $1$ in degree $0$ and $H$ in degree $4$. The exceptional static monomials of the $(3,1)$ types recur on schedule in the new range: the partner space is $3$-dimensional exactly in degrees $n \equiv 2 \pmod 4$ (degrees $18$ and $22$ in the new range), and direct spot computation confirms $q^4p^{14}$ and $q^5p^{17}$ are killed by $T_3(H, D\,\cdot)$ for $H = q^3p$, as are their mirrors $q^{14}p^4$, $q^{17}p^5$ for $H = qp^3$ — they survive $R=0$ and die at $R=1$, as in every lower degree. The static Hilbert formula $\dim E_n = 2 + \mathbf{1}_{e \mid (n-2)}$ of `quartic/proofs.md` is reproduced in all degrees $\le 24$ for every case, $D$ maps each truncated core $S^8_n$ into $S^8_{n+2}$ throughout the computed range, and the degrees $\le 16$ tables agree with wave one on all $102$ case-degree refinement sequences.

## 2. The equianharmonic case, settled explicitly

The repository's normal form for distinct-root quartics is $H_\lambda = q^4 + \lambda q^2p^2 + p^4$ (`quartic/proofs.md`, §3c), and the equianharmonic class is $\lambda^2 = -12$, the $j = 0$ stratum. Because $\lambda = 2\sqrt{-3} \notin \mathbb{Q}$, the convention question is real, and both readings were computed. Reading A is the repository's own rational representative $H = q^4 + qp^3$: `DYNAMIC_CORE_RESULTS.md` §3c names it as the rational form of the class, and the repository's test `test_equianharmonic_exception_matches_rational_form` asserts that the family's number-field ranks at $\lambda^2 = -12$ agree with direct computation on $q^4 + qp^3$. Reading B is $H_\lambda$ itself at $\lambda^2 = -12$, computed in exact arithmetic over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$ with coefficients $a + b\sqrt{-3}$, $a,b \in \mathbb{Q}$, and $\lambda$ represented as $2\sqrt{-3}$. The two computations are genuinely different — different Hamiltonians, different coefficient fields, different linear algebra — and they return **identical refinement dimension tables in every degree $n \le 24$ and every depth $R \le 8$**, which is the computed content of the $GL_2(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})$-covariance the repository asserts.

The outcome is that the equianharmonic class is **not exceptional in its core** — the truncated core is $1$ in degree $0$, $1$ in degree $4$ (spanned by $H$), and $0$ in every other degree $\le 24$, exactly the $\operatorname{span}\{1, H\}$ of the repository's distinct-roots theorem — but it **is exceptional in its depth profile**, and the depths the repository's certificate recorded in degrees $\le 12$ persist unchanged through degree $24$. The minimal depth needed to reach the core is $R = 3$ in degree $1$, $R = 2$ in degree $3$, $R = 1$ in every other positive degree $\le 24$, and $R=0$ in degree $0$. The refinement sequences make the mechanism visible: in degree $1$ the *whole* partner space survives two brackets, $\dim S^R_1 = 2, 2, 2, 0$ for $R = 0, 1, 2, 3$ (survivor basis $\{q, p\}$ at $R \le 2$, in both readings); in degree $3$ the whole partner space survives one bracket, $\dim S^R_3 = 2, 2, 0$ (survivors $qp^2$ and $q^3 + \tfrac14 p^3$ in reading A; $q^2p - \tfrac{\sqrt{-3}}{3}p^3$ and $q^3 + \sqrt{-3}\,qp^2$ in reading B — the classical cubic partners $q^2p - \frac{\lambda}{6}p^3$ type of `quartic/proofs.md` at $\lambda^2 = -12$). No other degree $\le 24$ needs more than one bracket, so the "exceptional depth" of the equianharmonic class is confined to degrees $1$ and $3$ in the full computed range, and nothing exceptional lives in its core at any degree $\le 24$: there are no extra core elements, only extra *long-lived ghosts* — partner elements that survive several brackets before the gate kills them. Since $\operatorname{span}\{1, H\} \subseteq D_\infty(H)$ holds for every $H$ elementarily, the per-degree cores in degrees $\le 24$ are determined by this computation alone, without appeal to the all-degree theorem.

## 3. Inhomogeneous classification: which quadratic terms collapse the core, and how deep it goes

The third computation tabulates $K_R^{\le N}$ for $R \le 12$ at $N = 8$ (and $N = 10$ for the $t = 1$ and test cases) for the near-top families $qp^3 + tq^2$ and $q^3p + tp^2$ with $t \in \{\tfrac12, 1, 2\}$, for $q^4 + q^2$, for $q^2p^2 + qp$, and for two test cases discussed below. The headline sequences at $N = 8$ are: $19, 12, 6, 4, 2$ (then constant) for every member of both near-top families — collapse to $\operatorname{span}\{1, H\}$ at depth $4$; $24$ at every $R$ for $q^4 + q^2$ — no refinement at all, the truncated space is already the strip; $21, 5$ (then constant) for $q^2p^2 + qp$ — one bracket reaches the truncated tower $\mathbb{Q}[qp]$; and for the test cases, $21, 3, 2$ for $q^2p^2 + q^2$ and $19, 17$ for $qp^3 + qp$. At $N = 10$ the same depths occur ($24, 14, 6, 4, 2$ for the near-top family; $30$ for $q^4+q^2$; $26, 6$ for the tower; $21$ for $qp^3 + qp$), so in the computed range the collapse depths do not grow with the truncation bound. Whenever the final dimension is $2$, the equality $D_\infty(H) \cap \{\deg \le N\} = \operatorname{span}\{1, H\}$ is proved by the computation alone; whenever it is a family dimension, the slice is identified by dimension count plus the verified containment of the family, with the family's membership in $D_\infty(H)$ being the repository theorem cited below.

The pattern these computations support has three clauses, of increasing subtlety. **(P1) Gate blindness.** A quadratic perturbation is invisible to the gate: third derivatives of a quadratic vanish, so $T_3(H, \cdot) = T_3(H_{\ge 3}, \cdot)$ and all $R = 0$ data — truncated spaces and per-degree partner spaces alike — depend only on the top part. This is visible in the tables: the three near-top cases share $K_0$ dimension $19$, the two balanced cases share $21$, and $q^4 + q^2$ has $K_0$ already equal to the $24$-dimensional truncated strip. Everything a quadratic perturbation does, it does through $D$ alone, at depths $R \ge 1$. **(P2) The collapse dichotomy is by stratum, and within a stratum by one monomial.** Writing the top part in adapted coordinates — $\ell$ the multiple-root (or pure) form, $m$ a complementary form — the near-top stratum collapses exactly when the $m^2$ coefficient is nonzero: $q^2$ for $qp^3$ (where $m = q$), $p^2$ for $q^3p$ (where $m = p$); the remaining quadratic monomials do not collapse it — $\ell m$ is the test case $qp^3 + qp$ below, and $\ell^2$ is the repository's $qp^3 + p^2$ row of §3, both keeping $\mathbb{Q}[p] \oplus q\mathbb{Q}[p]$ — in agreement with the repository's theorems (§3 and Theorem Q: within $qp^3 + aq^2 + bqp + cp^2 + dq + ep + f$ the unique exceptional locus is $a = 0$). The pure stratum is preserved by every perturbation in $\mathbb{Q}[\ell] + \mathbb{Q}\ell m + \mathbb{Q}m$ — no $m^2$ term — which is Theorem P⁺ cases 1–2 and is what $q^4 + q^2$ exhibits in its strongest form (depth $0$); an $m^2$ term collapses it by Theorem Q′ (repository certificate: $q^4 + p^2$). The balanced stratum is preserved exactly by perturbations keeping $H \in \mathbb{Q}[G]$ for a quadratic $G$ with top $Q$ — here $q^2p^2 + qp = G^2 + G$ with $G = qp$ — and collapsed by any perturbation leaving all such $\mathbb{Q}[G]$, by Theorem B. The unifying weight reading is that, for the weight adapted to the stratum (e.g. $w = (3,1)$ on $(q,p)$ for the $qp^3$ near top, under which $qp^3$ and $q^2$ both have weight $6$ while $qp$ has weight $4$ and $p^2$ weight $2$), the collapsing monomial is exactly the one that shares the top weight, so the weighted top part is already a finite-core binomial and the top-weight lemma (§3b) forces $\dim D_\infty \le 2$; the balanced stratum is the honest exception to the weight reading — no positive weight makes $q^2$ share the top weight of $q^2p^2$ — and there the operative criterion is the $\mathbb{Q}[G]$-membership of Theorem B, not weight. **(P3) Depth is a property of the stratum and the channel, not of the perturbation's size.** For the near-top $m^2$ channel the truncated collapse depth is exactly $4$ for every $t \ne 0$ and at both truncation bounds. The $t$-independence is proved, not merely observed: for $t \ne 0$ the substitution $(q, p) \mapsto (t^2 q, tp)$ sends $qp^3 + tq^2$ to $t^5 (qp^3 + q^2)$, preserves total degree, and conjugates $D$ and $T_3$ by nonzero scalars ($GL_2$ covariance), so every $K_R^{\le N}$ dimension is the same for all $t \ne 0$ and every $N$; the mirror map $(q,p) \mapsto (tq, t^2p)$ does the same for $q^3p + tp^2$, and the swap $q \leftrightarrow p$ conjugates the two families to each other — all confirmed by the identical computed sequences. The balanced off-tower collapse is shallower (depth $2$), and its mechanism is visible in the bases: $S^1$ for $q^2p^2 + q^2$ is $\operatorname{span}\{1, qp, H\}$, so the tower's generator $qp$ survives exactly one bracket as a ghost before dying. Every non-collapsing case stabilises at depth $\le 1$.

The two test cases were chosen after the pattern was formulated, with their predictions recorded in `inhomogeneous_cores.py` before computation, and both came out as predicted. $q^2p^2 + q^2$ was predicted to collapse by (P2)'s balanced clause — $H \notin \mathbb{Q}[G]$ for every quadratic $G$ with top $qp$, since $G^2$ with a $q^2$ term forces a $q^2p$ term that $H$ lacks — and it collapses at depth $2$ at both $N = 8$ and $N = 10$, proving $D_\infty \cap \{\deg \le 10\} = \operatorname{span}\{1, H\}$ for this $H$ from the computation alone. $qp^3 + qp$ was predicted not to collapse, by the $a = 0$ locus of the §3b theorem, and its truncated slices are exactly $\mathbb{Q}[p] \oplus q\mathbb{Q}[p]$ truncated ($17$ at $N=8$, $21$ at $N=10$), reached at depth $1$.

## Cross-checks

The computations were verified by independent routes at every level. The degrees $\le 16$ homogeneous tables reproduce wave one exactly ($102$ of $102$ case-degree refinement sequences). Partner spaces agree in span with both the independent Moyal checker (`improvements/moyal_partner_checker.py`, the PR #3 machinery) and the repository's star-product engine (`research/hamiltonian/independent/engine.py`) in every degree $\le 12$, and in dimension in every degree $\le 24$, for all seven homogeneous cases and the equianharmonic rational form; correction-free verdicts on all monomials of degree $\le 6$ agree across all three implementations. Refinement dimensions on samples were recomputed with the repository's own Weyl-operator machinery (`extensions/hamiltonian/weyl_receipts.py`, via the truncated-kernel construction of its `test_quartic_rigidity.py`) and agree everywhere sampled, including depths $0$–$3$ in equianharmonic degree $1$. A third, fully independent implementation in sympy (`crosscheck_sympy.py`: sympy differentiation and rational linear algebra, no shared code with the main engine) reproduces $52$ sampled dimensions with zero disagreements — including the equianharmonic $\lambda$-form over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$ via a doubled rational system, the exceptional-degree refinements of $q^3p$ at $n = 18, 22$, and the complete truncated sequences of both $qp^3 + q^2$ ($19, 12, 6, 4, 2, 2$) and the test case $q^2p^2 + q^2$ ($21, 3, 2, 2, 2, 2$) at $N = 8$. Finally, every inhomogeneous final basis was verified element-by-element through both star-product engines: for each basis element $G$, both engines confirm $D^r G$ is correction-free for $r \le 4$.

## What is proved, what is computed, and what is not claimed

**Proved here** (elementary, self-contained): the $t$-independence of all truncated dimensions in the two near-top families for $t \ne 0$, by the explicit torus conjugations of (P3); the equality of the truncated core slices with $\operatorname{span}\{1, H\}$ in every collapsing case listed, from the dimension-$2$ computations plus the elementary inclusion $\operatorname{span}\{1,H\} \subseteq D_\infty(H)$; and the per-degree core dimensions and minimal depths in degrees $\le 24$ for all distinct-root representatives including both equianharmonic readings, by the same inclusion. **Computed** (exact, cross-checked as above, but finite-range): all refinement tables in degrees $\le 24$ and depths $\le 8$; the inhomogeneous tables at $N \le 10$, $R \le 12$; the identity of the two equianharmonic readings' tables; the collapse and stabilisation depths as quantities of the stated truncations. **Repo-proved, used but not re-proved**: the all-degree core descriptions (the strip, the affine strips, $\mathbb{Q}[qp]$, $\operatorname{span}\{1,H\}$ for distinct roots, and the perturbation theorems §3, §3b, P⁺, Q, Q′, B quoted in §3). **Conjectured**: nothing in §§1–2 needs a conjecture label; in §3 the *general* form of (P2)–(P3) beyond the computed cases — e.g. that the near-top $m^2$ collapse depth is $4$ at every truncation bound $N$, or a depth formula for other strata — is a pattern supported by the computed range and the repository's theorems, stated as a pattern, not proved here. **Not claimed**: anything about perturbations of degree $\ge 3$ (which change the gates themselves), about depths beyond $R = 12$ in the inhomogeneous truncations, about degrees above $24$, or about any quartic outside the listed representatives beyond what the repository's root-type theorems already give; and no literature-priority claim is made.

## Files

`quartic_cores_ext.py` (the seven homogeneous tables to degree $24$ and both equianharmonic readings, with the wave-one, Moyal, engine, and Weyl cross-checks) writes `quartic_cores_ext.json`; `inhomogeneous_cores.py` (the perturbation tables at $N = 8, 10$, $R \le 12$, with family-containment checks and engine verification of final bases) writes `inhomogeneous_cores.json`; `crosscheck_sympy.py` is the independent sympy route and writes `crosscheck_sympy.json`. The `.log` files are the captured outputs of the actual verified runs (elapsed: $194\,\text{s}$, $145\,\text{s}$, $208\,\text{s}$ respectively). All scripts locate the repository and checkers relative to their own path and use only the standard library, except `crosscheck_sympy.py`, which requires sympy.
