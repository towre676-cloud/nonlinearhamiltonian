# Sextic Families: Moyal Partner Kernels and Dynamic Cores

*Exact computation over $\mathbb{Q}$, $\mathbb{Q}(a)$ and quadratic number fields. All scripts, JSON tables and logs are in this directory . Nothing here modifies the cloned repository; this package is submitted on the branch `muse/sextic-families`.*

## Conventions and method

Throughout, the convention is the flat Weyl–Moyal one of the nonlinearhamiltonian repository: a polynomial $G$ is a **correction-free partner** of $H$ when $(H\star G - G\star H)/(i\hbar) = \{H,G\}$ exactly, and for homogeneous $H, G$ this is equivalent to the vanishing of the odd transvectants $T_3(H,G)$ and $T_5(H,G)$ (the gates $C_3, C_5$); higher gates vanish identically for degree reasons when $\deg H = 6$. The **static partner space** $E(H)$ is the space of correction-free partners, $E_n(H)$ its homogeneous degree-$n$ piece, and the **dynamic core** is $D_\infty(H) = \{G : C_k D^r G = 0 \text{ for all odd } k\ge 3 \text{ and all } r\ge 0\}$ with $D = \{H,\cdot\}$, computed by the refinement sequence $S^0 \supseteq S^1 \supseteq \cdots$ in which $S^{R+1}$ consists of the elements of $S^R$ whose image under $D$ remains in $S^R$. Everything is computed in exact rational arithmetic (`fractions.Fraction`); where a parameter value is algebraic (the loci $a^2=-50$ and $a^2=-5$) the arithmetic is carried out in the number field $\mathbb{Q}[t]/(t^2+50)$ resp. $\mathbb{Q}[t]/(t^2+5)$ and the claimed bases are re-verified by evaluating the gates inside the field. The generic family computation is done over $\mathbb{Q}(a)$ by specialisation and interpolation: for each degree and each charge class (see below) one nonzero maximal minor of the gate matrix is computed as a polynomial in $a$, factored over $\mathbb{Q}$, and every irreducible factor is then tested by exact specialisation, so the exceptional loci below are complete over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ in the computed degree range, not merely observed at sample points. Dynamic cores are **certified**, not just computed: exact rational dimensions are taken at depths $R\le 2$, rigorous upper bounds come from ranks of the stacked gate rows modulo three large primes ($\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{Q}} \ge \mathrm{rank}_p$), and a degree is certified when the bound meets the dimension of an exhibited subspace of $D_\infty$ whose members are verified gate-killed after $D^r$ for all $r\le 10$. Every kernel dimension reported below was cross-checked by up to four independent routes (this session's gate machinery, the independent $D$-operator checker `moyal_partner_checker.py`, the repository's `independent/engine.py`, and the repository's `partner_lab.py` command-line tool); the combined suite `cross_checks.py` runs 157 checks with **0 failures**, including agreement of monomial-partner verdicts on all 270 monomial pairs $(c,d)$ with $c+d\le 8$ against `partner_lab.py`'s static logic. A structural fact used throughout is that the charge $c-e$ of a monomial $q^cp^e$ is additive under every transvectant and under $D$; since every monomial of $H_a$ has charge in $\{6,0,-6\}\equiv 0 \pmod 6$, all partner and refinement systems block-diagonalise by charge modulo 6, which is what makes the $\mathbb{Q}(a)$ analysis per charge class possible.

## 1. The family $H_a = q^6 + a q^3p^3 + p^6$ over $\mathbb{Q}(a)$

For generic $a$ the partner space is small and completely explicit. The nonzero homogeneous dimensions in degrees $0\le n\le 30$ are exactly $n=0$ (dimension $1$), $n=1$ (dimension $2$), $n=2$ (dimension $3$), $n=4$ (dimension $1$), $n=6$ (dimension $1$) and $n=8$ (dimension $1$), with bases $1$; $q, p$; $q^2, qp, p^2$; $(qp)^2$; $H_a$ itself; and at degree $8$ the form $G_8(a) = q^7p + qp^7 - \frac{7a}{20}(qp)^4$. Degrees $0,1,2$ are partners of *every* Hamiltonian for a trivial reason (each gate differentiates $G$ at least three times), so the content is in degrees $4$, $6$ and $8$. The universality of the degree-$4$ partner has a one-line explanation summand by summand: $T_k(q^6,(qp)^2)$ vanishes because the only term of the transvectant that does not differentiate $(qp)^2$ in $p$ leaves a $p$-derivative of order $k\ge3$ of a quadratic in $p$, $T_k(p^6,(qp)^2)$ vanishes symmetrically, and $T_k((qp)^3,(qp)^2)$ vanishes by the balanced antisymmetry $\psi_k(c,c)=0$ recorded in the repository's notes. The degree-$8$ partner is, by contrast, **specific to this family**: it is a genuine cancellation between the three summands of $H_a$, and §5 shows it disappears for both non-family probes even though they have the same root type. Note also that $G_8(0) = qp\cdot H_0$, while for $a\ne 0$ the partner is not a multiple of $H_a$ by anything (the quotient would have to be a degree-$2$ partner, and the space of those is spanned by $q^2, qp, p^2$, whose products with $H_a$ one checks are not in the kernel except through this combination).

## 2. Exceptional loci: where the kernel jumps, and by how much

Over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$, in degrees $n\le 30$, the kernel is larger than generic exactly at the following loci, and nowhere else. At $a=0$ there are two jumps: degree $3$ gains dimension $2$, with basis $\{q^2p,\, qp^2\}$, and degree $10$ gains dimension $1$, spanned by $q^8p^2 + q^2p^8$. At $a=\pm 20$ degree $3$ gains dimension $1$, spanned by $q^3+p^3$ when $a=20$ and by $q^3-p^3$ when $a=-20$ (in both cases $q^3 + \frac{a}{20}p^3$). At $a=\pm\frac{40}{7}$ degree $5$ gains dimension $1$, spanned by $qp^4 - q^4p = qp\,(p^3-q^3)$ when $a=\frac{40}{7}$ and by $qp^4+q^4p$ when $a=-\frac{40}{7}$ (in both cases $qp^4 - \frac{7a}{40}q^4p$). At $a^2=-50$ two degrees jump: degree $4$ rises to dimension $3$ over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-50})$, with basis $(qp)^2$, $q^3p - \frac{t}{10}p^4$ and $q^4 + \frac{t}{5}qp^3$ where $t^2=-50$, and degree $14$ gains dimension $1$, spanned by the palindromic form $q^{13}p + qp^{13} + \frac{13t}{20}(q^4p^{10}+q^{10}p^4) + \frac{39}{2}(qp)^7$. At $a^2=-5$ degree $12$ gains dimension $1$ over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5})$, spanned by $q^{12} + p^{12} + 11t\,(q^3p^9+q^9p^3) + 33q^6p^6$ with $t^2=-5$. There are **no exceptional loci in degrees $15\le n\le 30$**, so $E_n(H_a)=0$ for every $a\in\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ and $15\le n\le 30$. Every specialisation at the 21 rational grid points $\{0, \pm\tfrac12, \pm1, \pm2, \pm3, \pm\tfrac32, \pm\tfrac52, \pm\tfrac23, \pm\tfrac13, \pm4, \pm5\}$ agrees with this classification, and in particular **every grid point except $a=0$ has exactly the generic pattern**.

Two negative results are as informative as the jumps. First, the discriminant of the binary sextic $x^6 + ax^3 + 1$ is $729(a^2-4)^3$, so $a=\pm2$ is where the root configuration degenerates: indeed $H_2 = (q^3+p^3)^2$ and $H_{-2} = (q^3-p^3)^2$ (root type $(2,2,2)$, machine-verified), while every $a\ne\pm2$ gives six distinct roots (type $(1^6)$). **The kernel does not jump at $a=\pm2$ in any degree $\le 30$** — degeneracy of the root configuration alone does not create partners. Second, the classical invariant $I_2$ of the family satisfies $I_2 \propto 25920\,(40 - a^2)$ and vanishes at $a^2=40$, where **no** degree jumps in the computed range. The invariant $I_4$ behaves differently and this is the clean invariant-theoretic hit of the computation: $I_4 \propto 2579890176\,(a^2+50)^2$, so **$I_4$ vanishes exactly on the degree-$4$ and degree-$14$ exceptional locus $a^2=-50$**. The remaining loci have no invariant explanation yet: the degree-$3$ loci $\{0, \pm20\}$, the degree-$5$ locus $\pm40/7$ and the degree-$12$ locus $a^2=-5$ are not zero loci of the discriminant, of $I_2$, of $I_4$, or of the degree-$6$ transvectant scalars computed here (the combinations $((f,A)_1,(f,A)_1)_8 \propto (a^2+50)^2$, $((B,f)_2,(B,f)_2)_{10} \propto (a^2+50)^2(5a^2-56)$ and $(B,B)_8 \propto a^4 - 320a^2 + 400$, with $A=(f,f)_4$ and $B=(f,f)_2$); whether $a^2=-5$ is the vanishing locus of the genuine degree-$6$ invariant $I_6$ is left **open**, because the scalars computed only constrain $I_6$ restricted to the family up to combinations of $I_2I_4$ and $I_2^3$. One remark, recorded as such and not as an explanation: at $a^2=-5$, writing the roots through $t$ with $t^2 + at + 1 = 0$ (so the six roots are the cube roots of $t$ and of $1/t$), one finds $t = \pm i\varphi^{\pm 2}$ with $\varphi$ the golden ratio, i.e. the two root triangles sit at golden-ratio-related radii; no extra Möbius symmetry of the configuration was found, so if this is meaningful it is a resonance of moduli rather than of symmetry.

Assembling the computation with the repository's binary-static certificate (a sextic with a simple root and $E_n\ne 0$ for some $n\ge 17$ must be of root type $(5,1)$ or $(4,1,1)$) gives an **all-degree theorem for every $a\ne\pm2$**: since $H_a$ then has root type $(1^6)$, $E_n(H_a)=0$ for all $n\ge17$, and degrees $15$ and $16$ vanish by the computation above, so the full partner space $E(H_a)$ is exactly the generic space of §1 plus the exceptional extras of §2 whenever $a\in\{0,\pm20,\pm40/7\}\cup\{a^2=-50\}\cup\{a^2=-5\}$. For $a=\pm2$ the certificate does not apply (there is no simple root), and the partner space is determined here only through degree $30$, where it coincides with the generic pattern.

## 3. Dynamic cores of the family: the core never jumps

For $a \in \{0, \pm\tfrac12, \pm1, \pm2, 3, 20\}$ — a grid that includes the discriminant locus, the degree-$3$ and degree-$10$ exceptional values, and generic points — every degree $n\le 16$ is certified, and the answer is uniform: **$D_\infty(H_a)\cap V_n$ is spanned by $1$ at $n=0$, by $H_a$ at $n=6$, and is zero in every other degree $n\le16$, for every tested $a$.** The dynamic core is therefore $\mathrm{span}\{1, H_a\}$ throughout the family; it does not jump at the static exceptional loci, at the discriminant locus, or anywhere else tested, in agreement with the repository's Theorem W4 (root types $(1^6)$ and $(2,2,2)$ both have at least three distinct roots). The sharper computed phenomenon is the **depth**: at every tested $a$, the static excess over the core — the universal partners in degrees $1, 2, 4, 8$, the extra partners in degrees $3$ and $10$ at $a=0$, and the extra degree-$3$ partner at $a=20$ — is killed at refinement depth exactly $R=1$, i.e. a single application of $D$ already throws every non-core static partner out of the partner space, and $S^1_n = D_\infty(H_a)\cap V_n$ in every certified degree. The static jumps of §2 are thus dynamically invisible: they are partners that do not survive one Poisson bracket with $H_a$.

## 4. Monomial sextics

The partner tables for all monomials $q^jp^{6-j}$ were computed in degrees $0$–$24$, and in every degree the kernel is spanned by monomials (verified by comparing the full kernel against the span of the individually-tested monomial partners). The pure powers $q^6$ and $p^6$ have dimensions $1, 2$ and then $3$ in every degree $n\ge2$, the strips $\{q^n, q^{n-1}p, q^{n-2}p^2\}$ and their mirror. The monomial $q^5p$ has dimensions $1,2,3,2,3$ in degrees $0$–$4$ and then $2$ in every degree $\ge5$: the permanent kernel is $\{q^{n-1}p, q^n\}$, with one extra monomial $qp^3$ appearing only at degree $4$ (and degree $3$ contributes $\{q^2p, q^3\}$); $qp^5$ is the exact mirror. The monomial $q^4p^2$ has the full dimensions $1,2,3$ in degrees $0,1,2$ and then dimension $1$ in every degree $\ge3$, the single monomial $q^n$ — with the one exception of degree $6$, where the dimension is $2$ because $H$ itself joins $q^6$; $q^2p^4$ is the mirror with the line $p^n$. The balanced monomial $q^3p^3$ reproduces the repository's classification exactly: kernel monomials are those with $c=d$ or $c+d\le2$, plus $(1,11)$ and $(11,1)$ at degree $12$, giving dimension $3$ at degrees $2$ and $12$, dimension $1$ at other even degrees and $0$ at odd degrees $\ge3$. The dynamic cores, all certified in degrees $\le16$, match the repository's Theorem M in every case: the strips for $q^6, p^6$ are already cores; $q^5p$ and $qp^5$ have core dimension $2$ in every degree $\ge1$ (the extra static monomial $qp^3$ at degree $4$ dies at $R=1$); $q^4p^2$ and $q^2p^4$ have core exactly $\mathrm{span}\{1, qp, H\}$; and $D_\infty(q^3p^3) = \mathbb{Q}[qp]$, with the degree-$12$ static dimension $3$ collapsing to core dimension $1$ at $R=1$.

## 5. Two non-family probes

The probes $H = q^6 + q^5p + p^6$ and $H = q^6 + q^4p^2 + p^6$ test whether the family's pattern is forced by coarse data. Both have six distinct roots ($x^6+x^5+1$ has discriminant $-43531$ and is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$; $x^6+x^4+1$ has discriminant $-61504$ and is irreducible), and both have $I_2 = 1036800 \ne 0$; the first probe even shares the exact values $I_2 = 1036800$ and $I_4 = 6449725440000$ with the family member $H_0$. Their partner tables in degrees $0$–$24$ are $\{0{:}1, 1{:}2, 2{:}3, 3{:}1, 4{:}1, 6{:}1\}$ for the first probe — the degree-$3$ partner is $q^2p$ and the degree-$4$ partner is spanned by $qp^3 + 3q^2p^2$ — and $\{0{:}1, 1{:}2, 2{:}3, 4{:}1, 6{:}1\}$ for the second, whose degree-$4$ partner is spanned by $q^4 + 75q^2p^2 + 5p^4$ (not palindromic, correctly reflecting that the second probe is not symmetric under $q\leftrightarrow p$). Both probes have certified dynamic core $\mathrm{span}\{1,H\}$ in degrees $\le16$. The probes therefore deliver two honest negatives: **partner spaces are not determined by the pair $(I_2, I_4)$** (the first probe matches $H_0$ on both invariants and still has a different kernel, of a different shape in degrees $3, 8, 10$), and **the family's degree-$8$ partner is not a generic type-$(1^6)$ phenomenon** — neither probe has any degree-$8$ partner, so $G_8(a)$ belongs to the family's $D_3$-symmetric root configuration (roots closed under $x\mapsto 1/x$ through $t\leftrightarrow 1/t$) rather than to the root type alone.

## 6. Conjectures, graded, with their test status

The strongest statement, that for all $a\ne\pm2$ the partner space is exactly the one assembled in §2, is **proved** modulo two explicitly identified inputs: the exact computation through degree $30$ over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ reported here, and the repository's binary-static bound $N_0 = 17$, which is the repository's theorem and was used as a black box (its conclusion was independently confirmed in the overlap range: this session's table gives $E_n = 0$ for $17\le n\le 30$ at every algebraic $a$, as it predicts). What remains genuinely conjectural is the following. **(C2, the $a=\pm2$ tail).** $E_n(H_{\pm2}) = 0$ for all $n\ge 15$, so the closed form of §1–§2 holds for *every* $a\in\mathbb{C}$. Evidence: exact computation through degree $30$ at $a=\pm2$ (generic pattern throughout, and the $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ locus analysis shows no jump at $\pm2$ in that range). This is the only gap in the family's static classification, and it is a real one: $H_{\pm2}$ are perfect squares, no simple-root certificate applies, and no argument here excludes a first partner appearing in some degree $>30$. **(C3, depth-one death).** For the family and for the monomials, $S^1_n = D_\infty \cap V_n$ in *every* degree $n$: every static partner outside the core is expelled by a single application of $D$. Verified in all certified ranges (family degrees $\le16$ at nine values of $a$; monomials degrees $\le16$; both probes), with no counterexample; since the cores themselves are pinned by the repository's Theorems W4 and M, C3 is purely a statement about refinement speed, and a proof likely needs a lower bound on how $D$ moves charge classes rather than more computation. **(C4, invariant-locus principle — refuted in its strong form, retained in a weak form).** The attractive guess that kernel jumps occur exactly where classical invariants vanish fails twice over: $I_2=0$ at $a^2=40$ and $\Delta=0$ at $a=\pm2$ produce no jump at all, and the jump loci $\{0,\pm20\}$, $\{\pm 40/7\}$ and $\{a^2=-5\}$ are not vanishing loci of any invariant or covariant scalar computed here. What survives is the weaker observation that every jump locus found is a proper algebraic sublocus of the family's moduli, that one of them ($a^2=-50$, degrees $4$ and $14$) *is* the classical locus $I_4=0$, and that the exceptional partners themselves have rigid closed forms (palindromic in the $3$-step monomials $q^{n-3k}p^{3k}$, with coefficients polynomial in $a$) that hold on their locus. Any future conjecture should be formulated per-degree through the gate minors — the "partner resultants" $\rho_n(a)$ whose irreducible factors are computed in `family_partners.json` — rather than through the classical invariant ring alone.

## 7. What is proved by hand here

Three items are proved directly, without machine assistance beyond what is sketched. Degrees $0,1,2$ are partners of every $H$ because each gate $T_k$, $k\ge3$, differentiates $G$ at least three times in total, and $\deg G\le2$. The partner $(qp)^2$ is killed by each summand of $H_a$ separately, by the falling-factorial computation for $q^6$ and $p^6$ and by the balanced antisymmetry $\psi_k(c,c)=0$ for $(qp)^3$, as detailed in §1; the same argument shows $(qp)^2\in E(H)$ for the probes' summands $q^6, p^6$ too, although the probes' full degree-$4$ spaces are larger and different, which is consistent with §5. Finally $1$ and $H$ lie in $D_\infty(H)$ for every $H$, since $D1 = 0$ and $DH = \{H,H\} = 0$. Everything else in §§1–5 is **computed** in the precise sense defined in the preamble: exact arithmetic, complete locus analysis over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ in the stated ranges, prime-modulus rank certification for the cores, and four-way cross-checking with zero failures.

## 8. Limitations and open items

The static family analysis is complete over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ through degree $30$ and the core analysis is certified through degree $16$; beyond the repository's certificate degrees, the $a=\pm2$ tail (C2) is the one open static question. The identification of the degree-$12$ locus $a^2=-5$ with the classical invariant $I_6$ is unresolved for the reason given in §2, and the degree-$3$ and degree-$5$ loci $\{0,\pm20\}$ and $\{\pm40/7\}$ likewise await an invariant- or resonance-theoretic name; the golden-ratio form of the $a^2=-5$ roots is recorded but unexplained. No claim is made about cores in degrees $>16$ beyond the repository's own Theorems W4 and M, with which everything computed here agrees. A first version of the core script that attempted exact rational nullspaces at high refinement depth was too slow and was replaced by the certified prime-bound scheme; the final numbers are the certified ones.

## Files and reproduction

`sextic_lib.py` is the shared exact library (polynomial arithmetic over $\mathbb{Q}$, transvectants, $\mathbb{Q}(a)$ and number-field arithmetic, refinement machinery). `family_partners.py` produces `family_partners.json` (generic dimensions and bases over $\mathbb{Q}(a)$, all charge-class minors and their factors, every exceptional locus with its specialised table, and the 21-point rational grid) and `family_partners.log`. `exceptional_bases.py` produces `exceptional_bases.json` with the algebraic bases over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-50})$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5})$, each re-verified inside its field. `family_cores.py` produces `family_cores.json` with the per-degree certification records (exact dimensions at $R\le2$, prime-modulus bounds, exhibited $D_\infty$ bases) for the family, the monomials and the probes. `extra_partners.py` produces `extra_partners.json` with the monomial and probe partner tables through degree $24$, their monomial kernels, invariants and root data. `cross_checks.py` writes `cross_checks.json` and prints the 157-check suite result (0 failures). Each script runs standalone with `python3 <script>.py` from this directory and regenerates its JSON and log.
