# Parallel continuation interface

Codex's new contributions are confined to `research/beam_structure/` and
`research/beam_ring/`. Existing publications and programme files were not
modified. Integrate from a fresh main and inspect current status before editing
these directories; the campaign's executed source snapshots and frozen protocol
are historical evidence and should remain unchanged.

The most useful complementary physical lane is to produce valid evolving
residual-force premises cheaply. `emittance/predictor.py` already consumes
explicit bounds and composes the resulting emittance radius. Its remaining
obstruction is obtaining those bounds without first completing the expensive
PIC calculation. The compact retrospective tensor replay demonstrates possible
tightness, not a solution to that obstruction. Retain signed force/momentum
correlations, distinguish projected from full covariance, and compare with
quasi-frozen particle tracking rather than a Gaussian reset.

For observable work, the compiler/checker now supplies exact matrices and
moment reconstructions for the declared 25/9-dimensional family. The extra
control witnesses show why a generic extension is not automatically closed.
Finite-word summaries or a declared approximate residual are practical targets;
keep every allowed control in the common representation test. Classical closure
and quantum Moyal exactness remain separate claims.

For the ring lane, the next protocol must resolve a genuine momentum boundary
and qualify grid/horizon convergence before a performance headline. The first
pilot's saturated momentum score and MLP warnings are retained. Compare complete
cost at matched validation quality with production adaptive/batched and ordinary
surrogate workflows. The pinned generic mpBAX Engine is runnable, but its public
selectors are not the paper's full DAMA implementation. A new campaign needs
new output paths and a newly serialized protocol rather than editing old results.

Stable data interfaces are the source-bound oracle events, exact observable
certificates, compact centered force tensors, and finite-grid manifests. Each
has explicit units, conventions or scope in its own component documentation.
